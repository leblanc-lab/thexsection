Simulations of MAPS designs for FCC-ee

Danush Shekar et al.

Watchlist hit (collider, colliders) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Future electron-positron colliders such as the future circular collider require vertex detectors with excellent spatial resolution and a low material budget. Monolithic active pixel sensors (MAPS) are a leading candidate technology because they integrate the sensing volume and readout electronics within a single thin silicon substrate and can operate at power densities compatible with air cooling. This paper presents the design and optimization of a high-voltage MAPS (HV-MAPS) pixel structure with a thickness of 25 \(\mu\text{m}\) and a pitch of 50 \(\mu\text{m}\), developed using TCAD process and device simulations. The implantation energies and doses of the top-side implants are optimized with respect to the maximum electric field, sensor capacitance, and signals induced at each contact by minimum-ionizing particles. The optimized design reaches full depletion at a bias voltage of approximately -3 V and has an estimated breakdown voltage magnitude of 300-390 V. At a bias voltage of -100 V, the simulated charge-collection efficiency is approximately 90% outside the well regions and 80% within them and remains nearly independent of the angle of incidence.

Ultra-Fast 4H-SiC AC-LGAD for 4D Tracking Applications

Tao Yang et al.

Watchlist hit (collider) — original abstract; no AI summary.

4H-SiC low gain avalanche detectors (LGADs) combine internal gain with the high carrier saturation drift velocity, thermal stability, and low leakage current of SiC. AC-coupled readout (AC-LGAD) adds position information to the precise timing, which enables 4D tracking. This work reports the first experimental demonstration of a 4H-SiC AC-LGAD. Devices with a \(1.0\times1.0\)~mm\(^2\) active area and a \(2\times2\) array of AC-coupled pads at a pitch of 500~\(\mu\)m were fabricated with two gain-layer doping concentrations, \(4\times10^{17}\) and \(5\times10^{17}\)~cm\(^{-3}\), and characterized with a 375~nm ultraviolet transient current technique (UV-TCT) scanning setup. The hit position and time were reconstructed with the conventional discretized positioning circuit (DPC) and constant fraction discrimination (CFD) methods. They were also reconstructed with a transformer-based neural network, which processes the full waveforms of the four pads through a shared encoder and formulates timing as a per-sample segmentation task. With DPC and CFD, the best position and time resolutions are about 10~\(\mu\)m and 20~ps. The neural network mitigates the nonlinear distortions near the device edges and improves the position resolution to below 5~\(\mu\)m and the time resolution to about 15~ps, improvements of more than 60\% and 30\%, respectively. The higher-doped device reaches a high signal-to-noise ratio at lower bias and keeps good performance at low laser intensities. These results demonstrate the potential of 4H-SiC AC-LGADs for 4D tracking in future collider experiments.

Characterization of CharTPix-180A, a strip-shaped monolithic sensor for the STCF inner tracker

Ruiyang Zhang et al.

Watchlist hit (collider) — original abstract; no AI summary.

The Super Tau-Charm Facility (STCF) is a future high-luminosity electron-positron collider operating in the center-of-mass energy range of 2 to 7 GeV. The monolithic active pixel sensor (MAPS) is one of the options being considered for the STCF inner tracking detector. This application requires a MAPS capable of providing simultaneous position, time, and charge measurements under a constrained power budget of approximately 50 \(\rm mW/cm^2\). To address these requirements, a baseline sensor concept featuring a strip-shaped pixel geometry has been proposed to balance power consumption and detection performance. The first prototypes of this concept, referred to as CharTPix-180A, have been developed to evaluate the proposed design. Two prototypes with different design focuses were characterized in this study. An analog readout prototype, CharTPix-180A-c4, was used to evaluate the intrinsic sensor behavior, while a fully functional digital readout prototype, CharTPix-180A-c3, was used to investigate the overall sensor functionality on a small scale. Electrical, radioactive-source, and beam tests were performed to characterize the prototypes. The measured detection efficiency exceeded 99%, the fake-hit rate was below \(10^{-3}\) /pixel/s, and the spatial resolution was better than 10 \(\rm\mu m\). A best time resolution of 37 ns was achieved, and the power consumption was about 50 \(\rm mW/cm^2\). These results support the feasibility of the strip-shaped MAPS concept for the STCF inner tracker and provide guidance for further design optimization.

A machine-learning method for the estimation of the background from jets misidentified as hadronic tau lepton decays

CMS Collaboration

Watchlist hit (hadronic tau, LHC) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Backgrounds arising from quark- and gluon-initiated jets misidentified as hadronically decaying tau leptons constitute a major experimental challenge for analyses involving tau leptons. The MUltivariate Fake-Factor INference (MUFFIN) approach, a machine-learning-based method for estimating this background directly from observed events, is presented. Boosted decision tree reweighting is used to learn multidimensional, per-object correction factors directly from observed data, thereby modelling the jet to hadronic tau lepton misidentification probability across the kinematic and topological phase space without relying on low-dimensional parametrisations. Performance studies are presented using proton-proton collision data collected by the CMS experiment at the CERN LHC at \(\sqrt{s}\) = 13.6 TeV, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 62.4 fb\(^{-1}\). Compared to low-dimensional parametrisation methods, improved modelling of key kinematic observables is achieved, particularly in regions of high \(p_\mathrm{T}\). The improved modelling leads to a reduction of up to 70% in the systematic uncertainty associated with jets being misidentified as hadronically decaying tau leptons. The MUFFIN method provides a robust and scalable method for background estimation in precision measurements and searches for beyond-the-standard-model physics involving tau lepton final states.

Thomson Scattering Measurement of 1 keV Electron Temperature on Lawson Machine 26

P. Carle et al.

Watchlist hit (Juan Rojo) — original abstract; no AI summary.

LM26 is a magnetized target fusion experiment built to demonstrate compressional heating of a plasma with a collapsing solid lithium liner. We report the design of a Thomson scattering diagnostic for LM26 and measurements from the LMC-12 compression shot where an electron temperature of \(1090 \pm 40 \rm{eV}\) was measured with high confidence. The LM26 Thomson scattering diagnostic can measure temperature during compression at 8 times and 2 spatial points. Access limitations restricted the design to narrow f/30 views, and a forward scattering configuration with scattering angles < 20deg. The small solid angle of the scattered light view is largely compensated for by the 10 times increase in the density during compression to \(> 10^{20}\rm{m}^{-3}\). The narrow spectrum (1030-1063 nm) is advantageous for the design of optical components and to minimize noise from plasma background light. UKAEA polychromators specialized for small broadening measurements were used to analyze the scattered light. Results are also presented for an uncompressed plasma preceding LMC-12. Stray light was not a significant source of error. This design provides a strong design basis for Thomson scattering systems on future LM26 configurations.

FPGA-based muon shower identification and graph neural network tracking algorithms for HL-LHC triggers

Daniel Estrada et al.

Watchlist hit (LHC) — original abstract; no AI summary.

This work presents two hardware-accelerated strategies for the upgrade of the CMS Level-1 Trigger (L1T) to target efficiency losses due to highly energetic radiating muons and unconventional physics signatures, such as Long-Lived Particles (LLPs). Operating directly on low-level detector hits, the first strategy implements a dedicated muon shower identification algorithm in the CMS barrel region that monitors hit multiplicities in the Drift Tube chambers to generate Shower Primitives. Executing with sub-bunch-crossing latency and ultra-low resource consumption, this algorithm successfully tags showered muons and provides essential context for downstream track finders. Together, these developments pave the way for triggering on non-standard muon signatures, such as those from LLP decays, at L1T.

Optimization Using Pathwise Algorithmic Derivatives of Electromagnetic Shower Simulations

Max Aehle et al.

Watchlist hit (Michael Kagan) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Among the well-known methods to approximate derivatives of expectancies computed by Monte-Carlo simulations, averages of pathwise derivatives are often the easiest one to apply. Computing them via algorithmic differentiation typically does not require major manual analysis and rewriting of the code, even for very complex programs like simulations of particle-detector interactions in high-energy physics. However, the pathwise derivative estimator can be biased if there are discontinuities in the program, which may diminish its value for applications. This work integrates algorithmic differentiation into the electromagnetic shower simulation code HepEmShow based on G4HepEm, allowing us to study how well pathwise derivatives approximate derivatives of energy depositions in a sampling calorimeter with respect to parameters of the beam and geometry. We found that when multiple scattering is disabled in the simulation, means of pathwise derivatives converge quickly to their expected values, and these are close to the actual derivatives of the energy deposition. Additionally, we demonstrate the applicability of this novel gradient estimator for stochastic gradient-based optimization in a model example.

Point-cloud generative models for fast calorimeter simulation across particles and geometries

Thorsten Buss et al.

Watchlist hit (LHC, collider, Gregor Kasieczka) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Detailed Geant4 simulation of calorimeter showers is the largest single computing cost of collider experiments, and the High-Luminosity LHC will need about ten times more simulated events than are currently produced. We summarise recent progress in generative point cloud fast simulation for highly granular calorimeters. CaloClouds3 generates photon (electromagnetic) showers, is geometry-independent, and runs on average about 120x faster than Geant4 on a single CPU. We close with cross-geometry transfer learning, which needs two to three orders of magnitude fewer training showers while preserving the generative performance.

Our model is publicly available for feasibility and design studies.

Cross-geometry transfer and model collapse in point cloud calorimeter shower generation

Thorsten Buss et al.

Watchlist hit (wasserstein, Gregor Kasieczka) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Monte Carlo methods such as Geant4 are accurate but expensive, while most machine learning surrogates are tied to specific detector geometries and require retraining for each design change. We study cross-geometry transfer learning with CaloClouds II, a generative model that produces point clouds rather than voxels and can project onto arbitrary detector readouts. We pre-train on photon showers in the International Large Detector (ILD) and adapt to electron showers in the cylindrical CaloChallenge Dataset 3. Watchlist hit (energy flow) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Macroscopic materials function is determined not merely by equilibrium structure, but by how carriers, phonons, fields, defects, interfaces, and collective order dynamically evolve after perturbation. AllShowers: One model for all calorimeter showers

Thorsten Buss et al.

Watchlist hit (optimal transport, collider, Gregor Kasieczka) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Accurate and efficient detector simulation is essential for modern collider experiments. To reduce the high computational cost, various fast machine learning surrogate models have been proposed. We introduce AllShowers, a unified generative model that simulates calorimeter showers across multiple particle types using a single generative model. AllShowers marks a significant step towards a universal model for calorimeter shower simulations in collider experiments.
