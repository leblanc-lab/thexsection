arXiv:2611.07465 §

: Optimal-Transport Contrastive Learning for Centrality Determination in Heavy-Ion Collisions

This is the one paper today where OT is the method. The pipeline: - Distance: each UrQMD Au+Au event (√s_NN = 3.0, 5.2, 19.6 GeV) is a point cloud in 3-momentum. The distance between events is an exact Hungarian-assignment W1 with a Euclidean ground metric. - Unequal multiplicities: surplus particles are left unmatched at zero cost and the cost is averaged over the min(N_A, N_B) matched pairs. This partial-transport convention deliberately removes multiplicity from the distance. - Landmarks: each event is embedded by its W1 to 100 landmark events sampled uniformly in N_ch, computed for 2000 training events only. - Network: a PointNet (3→64→64→32, max+mean pooling, 32-d unit embedding) is trained with InfoNCE, using the k = 16 F-space nearest neighbours as positives (τ = 0.15). It then runs inference on 5–7M events. - Classes: size-constrained k-means gives 20 equal 5% classes.

Using all final-state particles, the classes align with N_part, giving narrower N_part bands and better σ/⟨N_part⟩ than N_ch slicing at all three energies. Under realistic STAR acceptance (−2.4 < η_lab < 0 fixed-target; |η| < 1 collider), “OT, charged, η” is worse than N_ch slicing. The authors attribute this to spectator protons and peripheral activity falling outside acceptance, leaving multiplicity as the main central/peripheral discriminant. The transport distance has, by construction, discarded multiplicity.

Caveats: - The unbalanced-OT choice is the crux and was not varied. A mass-penalizing unbalanced W1 is the obvious next test, and the min-normalized cost is not a metric. - The method is “unsupervised” only partially: landmark selection and class ordering both use N_ch. - Resolution values are figure-only.

: Estimating Spectrum-Free Information Bounds for Generative Surrogates Beyond Conditional Collapse

OT is tangential here. W1 appears as one of the 1D marginal divergences the paper argues against. On CMS open-data parton→reco-jet regression (~10.8M pairs, four residual targets), a condition-blind marginal sampler beats a trained MDN on all targets, by 3.75–18.02× under W1 and 3.65–22.39× under KL. It also cannot be stably separated from a conditional flow-matching model on two of four targets. The lesson for OT users: pooled-marginal W1 is blind to whether the conditioner is used at all.

The constructive content is a per-event CRPS skill score: - Floor: the exact marginal, i.e. half the Gini mean difference, equal to Hersbach’s uncertainty term, computed in O(N log N). - Ceiling: a k-NN analog-ensemble estimate, using fair-CRPS scores at k ∈ {5…100} extrapolated to zero neighbourhood radius.

Both surrogates land within ±0.005 of the ceiling ratio. A 17,280-cell lookup table recovers 98.8% of CFM skill on jet mass, 84.6% on Δφ, and 60.8% on Δη. Global versus p_T-binned skill on jet mass drops from 28.7% to 10.1%, showing how strongly global skill depends on the breadth of the test spectrum.

Caveats: - The zero-radius intercept is explicitly not a bound. - All scores are per-target 1D. - The k-NN search omits φ, which explains a 0.0014 gap on momentum response.

One observation of mine, not made by the authors: 1D CRPS is the integrated squared CDF difference against a step function, the L2 analogue of the CDF-area form of W1.

A Unified Geometric Framework for Understanding Collider Event Manifolds

Tianji Cai et al.

The authors embed each jet as its vector of distances to \(N_{\rm ref}\) randomly drawn reference jets (M3R). This puts the energy-flow W₂ metric, the spectral SW₂ metric (1D closed-form quantile matching), and the phase-space (PS) metric in a common Euclidean coordinate system. With all events as references the embedding is an exact \(\ell_\infty\) isometry, and it inherits IRC safety from the underlying metric. Cost drops from \(\mathcal{O}(N^2)\) to \(\mathcal{O}(NN_{\rm ref})\) metric evaluations, which matters for 2D W₂ solves.

Results on 10⁴ JetClass jets per class:

  • Reconstruction fidelity at \(N_{\rm ref}=100\) (JAUC / MADO) orders the metrics PS < SW₂ < W₂ in complexity:
  • PS: >0.92 / >0.97
  • SW₂: 0.82–0.88 / 0.86–0.92
  • W₂: 0.72–0.79 / 0.77–0.82
  • W₂ vs SW₂ on QCD jets: the leading two principal components align closely (|cos| ≈ 0.95). SW₂’s PC1 explains 0.85 of W₂ variance, versus 0.92 for W₂’s own PC1. This is a manifold-level echo of their known soft-collinear equivalence.
  • Top jets break this. W₂ decouples (|cos| ≲ 0.25 with both SW₂ and PS), while SW₂–PS stay aligned (≈0.85). This is consistent with spectral degeneracies for triangular three-prong configurations.
  • Asymmetric variance transfer on top jets. W₂’s PC1 explains about 0.3 of SW₂/PS variance, but the reverse transfers explain ≲0.1 of W₂’s. The authors read this as W₂ containing the shared structure plus extra.
  • Tagging is more compressible than geometry. SVM AUCs saturate by \(N_{\rm ref}\sim10\). At \(N_{\rm ref}=100\), the average relative gap to the full-metric SVM is ≈0% for W₂, 3% for SW₂ and 11% for PS, the reverse of the reconstruction ordering. This generalizes the linearized-W₂ idea.

OT is central here: two of the three metrics are OT-based, and the W₂/SW₂ comparison is the paper’s sharpest result. The framework itself, however, is metric-agnostic.

Caveats: - References are random and metric weights are set to 1. - PS uses 10 \(p_T\)-ordered exclusive subjets as a stand-in for the permutation minimization. - SVM hyperparameters are fixed. - The provided text is truncated mid-§5.2, so the multi-metric/PID combination results (§6) are not covered here.

Cross-geometry transfer and model collapse in point cloud calorimeter shower generation

Thorsten Buss et al.

Relevance to this topic is weak. Optimal transport enters only as the evaluation metric: the Wasserstein-1 distance to Geant4 on six shower observables, combined by geometric mean. There is no OT-based training, coupling, or methodology.

The paper is a proceedings summary of JINST 21 (2026) P07037. The authors pre-train CaloClouds II on 524k ILD ECAL photon showers (10–90 GeV) and adapt it to CaloChallenge DS3. The target differs in several ways at once: - electrons instead of photons; - a cylindrical rather than planar geometry, with 45 instead of 30 layers; - a log-uniform 1 GeV–1 TeV energy range; - point clouds more than three times larger at high energy.

At 10² target showers, full fine-tuning reaches a geometric-mean W₁ of 0.064±0.006, against 0.132±0.018 from scratch (about 51% lower). The advantage fades with more data: at 10⁵ it is 4.7 vs 4.5, i.e. no gain.

Among parameter-efficient methods: - BitFit: updates 17% of the diffusion parameters and lands within 5% of full fine-tuning (mean 6.2 vs 5.9 ×10⁻²). - LoRA at rank 106: underperforms (8.4). The authors attribute this to high-rank weight updates, with some layers needing rank above 200.

A self-consuming retraining study shows progressive collapse in both ShowerFlow and the diffusion model. It is reported only qualitatively via figures.

Caveats: - There is no comparison to multi-detector pre-training such as CaloDiT-2. - A residual per-observable mismatch at 10⁴ showers is hidden in the geometric mean. - The claim of adaptation to arbitrary geometry is explicitly not demonstrated here.

Similarity Pairing with Energy Mover's Distance for Self-Supervised Pre-Training at the LHC

Ho Fung Tsoi et al.

Tsoi and Rankin use EMD (the Komiske–Metodiev–Thaler optimal-transport metric on energy flow) as a replacement for data augmentation in self-supervised jet pre-training. Rather than distorting a jet to make two views, they pair two distinct real jets falling within an EMD window (0.01, 0.05) after normalizing p_T and scaling angular distances by R = 0.8 — treating “two genuinely similar events” as augmented views, so physics content and detector constraints are never violated. This extends MACK’s data/MC EMD-pairing idea to generic embedding learning. Pairs feed a jBOT/iBOT self-distillation teacher-student transformer (3 layers, 4 heads, 32-dim particle embeddings, [CLS] jet token) pre-trained on JetNet q/g jets.

Results: t-SNE shows clean class separation not only for q/g but for held-out W/Z/t. Probing anomaly detection (kNN, cosine, Mahalanobis scores against the QCD bulk), random pairing wins (combined AUC 0.8188 ± 0.0076), with same-class compatible and different-class worse — the interpretation being that unconstrained pairing broadens the invariance space. Versus the augmentation-based jBOT baseline (combined 0.8269), their cosine-score model reaches 0.8309 ± 0.0067, beating baseline on W and t individually.

For the optimal-transport topic, this is a concrete new use of EMD: not as a discriminant or a geometry probe but as the positive-pair sampler for contrastive/distillation pre-training, collapsing the large augmentation design space to a single window hyperparameter (found stable under moderate variation). Caveats: gains over the augmentation baseline are marginal (~0.4% AUC, within roughly 1σ on combined); pairwise EMD computation across the training set is O(N²) and its cost/scaling is not discussed; demonstrated only on JetNet ~1 TeV jets with ≤30 particles.

arXiv:2609.12024 §

Learning the Geometry of Collider Events with Metric-Aware Deep Sets

Lauren Hay et al.

A supervised Deep Sets surrogate (MA-PFN) for the Energy Mover’s Distance that bakes in three metric axioms — non-negativity, exchange symmetry, zero self-distance — while leaving the triangle inequality and IRC safety unconstrained, then measures how much of the remaining geometry is recovered “for free.” The construction uses the Multiset Deep Sets theorem: separate shared-encoder latent sums z_E, z_G, recombined into Σ=z_E+z_G, Δ=z_E−z_G, with the predicted distance factored as an exact latent pseudometric ρ=‖Δ‖₁/L (which identically satisfies the triangle inequality) times a symmetrized positive gauge g=softplus(a_sym). Any metric violation is carried entirely by the pair-dependent g.

Results are the interesting part for OT-in-HEP. Against a matched unconstrained PFN, MA-PFN gets 0.89% MAPE (vs 1.12%), a ~20% error reduction. The enforced axioms improve the unenforced triangle inequality: across 10⁶ held-out triplets, violations drop 199→2 and the max violation 149.5→5.8 GeV; exchange symmetry goes from 0.1% to ~100% of pairs within tolerance. IRC safety is only partially learned — under soft-emission and 50/50 collinear splits both networks plateau at a nonzero floor rather than tracking the exact EMD, though MA-PFN follows the exact response to smaller perturbation scales. Timing: amortized single-encode-per-event gives a flat pair-stage cost (17–26M pairs/s on one A40), with the advantage over the fastest 48-core exact solver growing from 1.5× at multiplicity 4 to 1.8×10³ at multiplicity 202; it Pareto-dominates Sinkhorn (ε=10⁻³, 3.4% error) and sliced-Wasserstein.

Caveat as authors state: identity of indiscernibles, the triangle inequality, and IRC safety are not guaranteed, and relative error grows at small EMD — so it remains an approximate surrogate, not a drop-in metric for downstream nearest-neighbor/clustering work. Timing is not hardware-normalized (GPU vs 48 CPU cores).

Note both papers share the LeBlanc/Brown-IAIFI group and the same EMD samples appear in the cell-resampling reference — the OT paper is squarely about OT, not tangential.

Transferable Fast Calorimeter Shower Generation via Multi-Geometry Pre-training

Thorsten Buss et al.

Be aware up front: OT is instrumental here, not the subject. The paper is a transfer-learning study — pre-training a point-cloud calorimeter shower generator (the AllShowers flow-matching backbone) on synthetic geometry variation (SimpleBox, 10⁴ box calorimeters spanning sampling fraction f_s ∈ [0.01,0.05] and N_layers ∈ {15,…,45}) and fine-tuning on unseen detectors. OT enters in two distinct roles.

First, on the training side: the coordinate stage is conditional flow matching “with an exact optimal-transport coupling between the prior and the data points.” This is inherited unchanged from the AllShowers release (Ref. 52) — no new OT methodology, and the OT coupling noise is regenerable and not released.

Second, and more relevant, OT distances are the evaluation currency, and the paper makes a methodologically useful distinction. They use W1/σ_ref (Wasserstein-1 normalized by the Geant4 reference std) on pooled histograms, and separately a sliced Wasserstein distance (256 random 1D projections, normalized by the Geant4 cloud’s RMS dispersion) on per-shower vectors. Because each SWD projection mixes bins, it is sensitive to inter-bin correlations and shower-to-shower fluctuations that pooled W1 or mean-profile comparisons cannot see. This is exactly what exposes the key physics result: on the held-out FCCee-ALLEGRO target, the LEMURS prior’s longitudinal SWD falls to 0.016 at D=10³ then rises to 0.059 at D=10⁵ (an “ossification”-like imprint), invisible in mean occupancy and only detectable via the distributional (SWD) metric. Ablating the multiplicity stage (Geant4 counts) restores monotonic convergence toward the 0.0079 statistical-resolution floor, and a KL cross-check reproduces the effect — so the SWD finding is metric-robust, localizing the residual to PointCountFM rather than the OT-coupled coordinate model.

Headline transfer numbers (aggregated SWD geometric mean d̄): at D=10³, both priors beat from-scratch, reducing d̄ to Geant4 by 8.0× (LEMURS) and 5.2× (SimpleBox); synthetic pre-training overtakes the realistic prior at larger D. Caveat for this topic: no new OT technique is introduced — the value is a careful demonstration that per-shower sliced-Wasserstein is the diagnostic that catches transfer pathologies pooled metrics miss. Also restricted to EM photon showers only.

Centrality caveat up front: this is a fast-simulation/normalizing-flows paper, not an OT paper. “Optimal transport” enters only through the evaluation metric — the mean 1-Wasserstein distance between per-photomultiplier photon-count distributions (Eq. 2, averaged over m=5 checkerboard-mask channels via inverse-CDF integration). Optimal-Transport-Based Cell Resampling for Negative and Pathological Event Weights

Regan Doherty et al.

This paper replaces the modified-Euclidean metric in Andersen et al.’s cell-resampling afterburner with two OT-based metrics — the Energy Mover’s Distance (EMD, i.e. the first Wasserstein distance with β=1, R=11.64) and the Spectral EMD (sEMD) — to redistribute negative MC weights among phase-space neighbors. The central OT payoff is IRC-safety by construction: because EMD/sEMD are insensitive to soft/collinear radiation, resampling can run directly on particles without prior jet clustering, eliminating the jet-definition dependence that contaminated all downstream observables in the Euclidean approach. They test on NLO MC@NLO Z+jets (37.6% negative weights) and tt̄ (22.8%), 10⁵ events each, resampling at three generation stages (hard-scatter, post-shower, post-hadronization).

Concrete results: hadronization-level resampling gives the lowest bias across all observables and the lowest ΣMD (see below). At f_rw=0.25 and 0.75, 1/f_ESS drops from 16 to 12 and 6 respectively for Z+jets — a real statistical-power gain. On the β scan, β=0 and β→∞ (which reduce to H_T-only distances, ignoring intra-event energy geometry) introduce ~50% biases in H_T/N_jets; β=0.5,1,2 are comparable, with β=1 chosen for accuracy/speed. EMD modestly outperforms sEMD; both clearly beat the Andersen Euclidean metric, whose ΣMD is nearly double EMD’s at high f_rw.

Second OT contribution: they repurpose the Cross-Section Mover’s Distance (ΣMD) — an event-level EMD used as ground metric in a sample-level OT problem — as an unbinned, observable-agnostic figure of merit for reweighting bias, handling negative weights via a constant-offset (generalized-Wasserstein cancellation) trick requiring no solver modification. This is a genuinely reusable tool beyond this application.

Caveats stated by the authors: the Euclidean metric was designed for much larger samples, so its poor showing here may partly reflect the sparse 10⁵-event metric space; exact EMD scales as O(N³log N), and runtime optimization is deferred. Bias at fixed f_rw is small but exaggerated relative to production-scale samples.

Studying baryon number transport dynamics via hyperon-kaon correlations in p + Au collisions at √(s__NN)=20, 39 and 62 GeV

This paper uses the Wasserstein distance (via the Python Optimal Transport library) to quantify the correlation strength between hyperons and kaons in \(p + \mathrm{Au}\) simulations (AMPT and UrQMD). This work moves the topic forward by applying optimal transport metrics specifically to characterize the rapidity dynamics of BNT. By quantifying the displacement of hyperon-kaon pairs in relative-rapidity space, the authors establish a baseline for identifying the gluon junction mechanism; the EMDp provides a directional signature that distinguishes between models where the baryon number carrier is either “slowed down” (baryon junction) or “speeded up” (valence-quark fragmentation).

Unfolding with a Wasserstein Loss

Katy Craig et al.

Watchlist hit (optimal transport, wasserstein, sinkhorn, transport map) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing.

Data unfolding – the removal of noise or artifacts from measurements – is a fundamental task across the experimental sciences. Of particular interest are applications in physics, where the dominant approach is Richardson-Lucy (RL) deconvolution. The classical RL approach aims to find denoised data that, once passed through the noise model, is as close as possible to the measured data in terms of Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence. This requires that the support of the measured data overlaps with the output of the noise model, a hypothesis typically enforced by binning, which introduces numerical error. As a counterpoint, the present work studies an alternative formulation using a Wasserstein loss. We establish sharp conditions for existence and uniqueness of optimizers, answering open questions of Li, et al., regarding necessary conditions for uniqueness in the case of transport map noise models. We then develop a provably convergent generalized Sinkhorn algorithm to compute approximate optimizers. Our algorithm requires only empirical observations of the noise model and measured data and scales with the size of the data, rather than the ambient dimension. Numerical experiments on one- and two-dimensional problems inspired by jet mass unfolding in particle physics demonstrate that the optimal transport approach offers robust, accurate performance compared to classical RL deconvolution, particularly when binning artifacts are significant.

AllShowers: One model for all calorimeter showers

Thorsten Buss et al.

Watchlist hit (optimal transport) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing.

Accurate and efficient detector simulation is essential for modern collider experiments. To reduce the high computational cost, various fast machine learning surrogate models have been proposed. Traditional surrogate models for calorimeter shower modeling train separate networks for each particle species, limiting scalability and reuse. We introduce AllShowers, a unified generative model that simulates calorimeter showers across multiple particle types using a single generative model. AllShowers is a continuous normalizing flow model with a Transformer architecture, enabling it to generate complex spatial and energy correlations in variable-length point cloud representations of showers. Trained on a diverse dataset of simulated showers in the highly granular ILD detector, the model demonstrates the ability to generate realistic showers for electrons, photons, and charged and neutral hadrons across a wide range of incident energies and angles without retraining. In addition to unifying shower generation for multiple particle types, AllShowers surpasses the fidelity of previous single-particle-type models for hadronic showers. Key innovations include the use of a layer embedding, allowing the model to learn all relevant calorimeter layer properties; a custom attention masking scheme to reduce computational demands and introduce a helpful inductive bias; and a shower- and layer-wise optimal transport mapping to improve training convergence and sample quality. AllShowers marks a significant step towards a universal model for calorimeter shower simulations in collider experiments.
