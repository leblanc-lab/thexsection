arXiv:2610.10734 — The hidden sunrise in the energy-energy correlator (Matthew D. Schwartz et al.)

This paper completes the analytic NNLO EEC in 𝒩=4 SYM. Ref. [1] had left one unevaluated two-fold integral F_R(ζ); this work evaluates it.

Polylogarithmic pieces. - Partial fractioning in the inner variable splits F_R into three parts: F_red₁ (purely polylogarithmic), F_K₁, and F_R₂. - F_R₂ has to be kept as a whole, because red₂ and K₂ separately diverge like (1−z̄)⁻² log²(1−z̄). - The polylog remainder P(ζ) = F_red₁ + Δ consists of HPLs of weight ≤5 on the alphabet {ζ, 1−ζ}. Notably, the √ζ letter present at NLO is absent, and the weight is non-uniform.

Elliptic sector. - The two square-root discriminants Q₁ and Q₂ are related by the Möbius map w = z̄/(z̄−1). Both are isomorphic to the equal-mass sunrise curve under S = (ζ−1)/ζ, and the j-invariants match identically. - Under this map the physical region 0<ζ<1 lands in the Euclidean sunrise region. The odd-looking singular point ζ = −1/8 is the 9m² threshold. - The coupled system for F_K₁, F_R₂ and a companion integral S₀ involves 𝓛 = ζ∘L_PF∘ζ⁻¹, which is second order. This bounds the result at elliptic: no K3 or Calabi–Yau geometry can appear. - Subtracting the coupled equations shows Δ = F_R₂ − F_K₁ is polylogarithmic, giving F_R = 2F_K₁ + P. - S₀ is written as weight-5 iterated Eisenstein integrals for Γ₁(6). The letters are ω₀ and ω₁ plus two new modular-weight-3 letters, ψ₁ω₀ and ζψ₁ω₀.

Numerics and the collinear limit. - Evaluation is by truncated q-series, with the collinear limit ζ→0 at q→0. About 75 terms give 50 digits at ζ = 3/11 (q₀ ≈ 0.17). - Per their error estimate N_dig ≈ N log₁₀(1/q₀) − 3, convergence degrades toward back-to-back, where q→1.

How central this is to the topic. The relevance to jet substructure is indirect: this is a full-angle, fixed-order result in 𝒩=4. The value for jets is mainly a clean collinear-limit expansion and a template for the QCD elliptic sector, which the authors assert but do not compute.

Caveats. - The provided text is truncated before the bootstrap section, so I can’t assess those results. - Much of the technical work was done with AI under human supervision.

arXiv:2610.12408 — A compact, interrogable Tree Tensor Network Jet Tagger on Lund declustering trees (Fabrizio Napolitano et al.)

Architecture. A single shared trilinear core C ∈ ℝ^{6×χ×χ×χ} is contracted recursively along the C/A declustering tree. Inputs are the five LundNet5 coordinates in a projective-sphere encoding, plus a shared MLP residual. Optional QCD gates (α_s and splitting functions) and Sudakov gates modulate the tree message. arXiv:2610.09320 — A Unified Geometric Framework for Understanding Collider Event Manifolds (Tianji Cai et al.)

The authors embed each jet as its vector of distances to \(N_{\rm ref}\) randomly drawn reference jets (M3R). This puts the energy-flow W₂ metric, the spectral SW₂ metric (1D closed-form quantile matching), and the phase-space (PS) metric in a common Euclidean coordinate system. With all events as references the embedding is an exact \(\ell_\infty\) isometry, and it inherits IRC safety from the underlying metric. Cost drops from \(\mathcal{O}(N^2)\) to \(\mathcal{O}(NN_{\rm ref})\) metric evaluations, which matters for 2D W₂ solves.

Results on 10⁴ JetClass jets per class:

  • Reconstruction fidelity at \(N_{\rm ref}=100\) (JAUC / MADO) orders the metrics PS < SW₂ < W₂ in complexity:
  • PS: >0.92 / >0.97
  • SW₂: 0.82–0.88 / 0.86–0.92
  • W₂: 0.72–0.79 / 0.77–0.82
  • W₂ vs SW₂ on QCD jets: the leading two principal components align closely (|cos| ≈ 0.95). SW₂’s PC1 explains 0.85 of W₂ variance, versus 0.92 for W₂’s own PC1. This is a manifold-level echo of their known soft-collinear equivalence.
  • Top jets break this. W₂ decouples (|cos| ≲ 0.25 with both SW₂ and PS), while SW₂–PS stay aligned (≈0.85). This is consistent with spectral degeneracies for triangular three-prong configurations.
  • Asymmetric variance transfer on top jets. W₂’s PC1 explains about 0.3 of SW₂/PS variance, but the reverse transfers explain ≲0.1 of W₂’s. The authors read this as W₂ containing the shared structure plus extra.
  • Tagging is more compressible than geometry. SVM AUCs saturate by \(N_{\rm ref}\sim10\). At \(N_{\rm ref}=100\), the average relative gap to the full-metric SVM is ≈0% for W₂, 3% for SW₂ and 11% for PS, the reverse of the reconstruction ordering. This generalizes the linearized-W₂ idea.

OT is central here: two of the three metrics are OT-based, and the W₂/SW₂ comparison is the paper’s sharpest result. The framework itself, however, is metric-agnostic.

Caveats: - References are random and metric weights are set to 1. - PS uses 10 \(p_T\)-ordered exclusive subjets as a stand-in for the permutation minimization. - SVM hyperparameters are fixed. - The provided text is truncated mid-§5.2, so the multi-metric/PID combination results (§6) are not covered here.

arXiv:2610.07145 — The Back-to-Back Plateau of the Energy-Energy Correlator from Weak to Strong Coupling (Hao Chen et al.)

Relevance. This is only loosely a substructure paper. It studies the back-to-back (Sudakov) endpoint of the full-event EEC in 𝒩=4 SYM, not the collinear region inside jets. QCD jet measurements appear only as motivation. The overlap with the topic is energy-correlator technology.

What they did. Chen, Moult, Wang and Zhu resum both leading-power (LP, y⁻¹) and next-to-leading-power (NLP, y⁰) back-to-back logarithms through N³LL, including 1/N_c² terms. The method is the large-spin and twist-conformal-block pipeline of Chen–Zhou–Zhu, in five steps: - twist-2 anomalous dimensions γ₁…γ₄ and OPE-coefficient corrections r₁…r₃ are put into a finite-difference large-spin basis; - these are lifted to the twist-conformal-block basis; - crossing of Φ(u,v) is used, which avoids needing twist-4 data; - a Mellin/light transform is applied; - the coefficients are matched to fixed-order data.

Results. - NLP towers and checks. They give closed forms for the generic NLP coefficients C_k^NLP(n). Only C₂^NLP(1) and C₄^NLP(2) need fixed-order input; the remaining expansion terms agree with the known fixed-order expansion through three loops. - Structure of the NLP function. The resummed NLP function G contains an erf(√a L_y/√2) tower. This term survives as y→0, giving a finite NLP shift to the plateau beyond the essential-singular LP term P_LP ~ a^{-1/2}e^{1/(2a)}. - Interpolation. A two-parameter interpolation (p=2.34, q=1.81) is built to match the weak-coupling LP asymptotics and the strong-coupling result 1+1/a. It is fit to planar bootstrap points.

Caveats. - The residual endpoint term H₀(a) is still undetermined beyond these logarithms. - The perturbative description visibly degrades by a ~ 1/4, and the string expansion only reaches down to a ~ 0.5. The window in between is covered only by the fit. - The interpolation is phenomenological, by the authors’ own description. - Transfer to QCD (running coupling, hadronization) is posed as an open question, not addressed.

Agentic workflow. The calculation used a human-in-the-loop setup with GPT-5.5. Humans had to fix the truncation depth (Step 5), working backward from the final observable. In an independent test of seven models on 15 steps, the best scored 10/15. No model solved the large-spin expansion, the explicit twist-block sum, or the three-loop check. The aggregate pass counts in the text are unfilled placeholders (“x out of 28”).

arXiv:2610.05104 — Boosted top tagging via lepton-in-jet topology for vector-like quark searches in FCC-hh (Sanchari Bhattacharyya et al.)

This paper builds a lepton-seeded tagger for boosted t→bℓν at √s=100 TeV. Everything is defined in a ΔR<0.4 cone around a non-isolated lepton with p_T>100 GeV. The study uses PYTHIA plus Delphes with the FCC-hh card. The tagger is a four-class classifier (t_lep, t_had, b, c) trained separately in seven lepton-p_T bins from 0.1 TeV to above 3 TeV.

The two taggers take different inputs: - DNN: engineered observables, including displaced-track ΔR and multiplicity, Q_κ jet charge, tower isolation, jet mass, and τ_N ratios. - ParT: nine features per constituent (tracks, towers and the lepton) and pairwise angular attention biases.

Main results - Leptonic-top AUC: ParT gives 0.994–0.997, flat in p_T. The DNN rises from 0.981 to 0.996. - Efficiency at score >0.4: ParT reaches 94% to 97%, the DNN 89% to 96%. - High-p_T crossover: above ~2 TeV the DNN has lower mistag rates for all three background classes. The authors read this as constituent information helping only while the decay products stay angularly resolved. - Track-only inputs (1.0–1.5 TeV bin): ParT 0.981, GNN 0.964, DNN 0.942. The DNN drops from 0.992 with full inputs, so the high-level approach relies heavily on calorimeter information, which is the part most exposed to pile-up. - Cone size: shrinking the cone to 0.2 leaves performance essentially unchanged. - Event level: feeding the tagger into a B′→tW search gives 5σ discovery reach of ~7 TeV for Γ/m<25%, or ~10 TeV for Γ/m≲50%, at 30 ab⁻¹.

Caveats - There is no pile-up overlay. The only proxy is raising the track threshold from 5 to 20 GeV, which leaves AUCs unchanged. - Light-flavour jets are excluded from tagger training. - Diboson events with boosted Z or W→cs produce a peak near tagger score 1. - The event classifier is trained only at 5 TeV. - The ParT+DeepSVDD anomaly result (AUC 0.894) treats the leptonic top itself as the nominal class, so its relevance to anomaly searches is limited.

arXiv:2610.03472 — Three-loop rapidity anomalous dimension for jet-veto cross sections (Samuel Abreu et al.)

This paper supplies the last missing ingredient for N³LL resummation of the colour-singlet \(p_T^{\rm jet}<p_T^{\rm veto}\) cross section. The authors compute \(\gamma_\nu^{(3)}(R^2)\) from the three-loop SCET soft function, using the exponential rapidity regulator, for anti-\(k_t\), C/A and \(k_t\). The two-loop beam and soft functions, four-loop cusp and three-loop non-cusp anomalous dimensions were already known.

Method. The key device is a non-physical “Reference Simplified Variable” (RSV). It applies the veto to the total \(p_t\) of each correlated cluster in the non-abelian-exponentiation decomposition, so it is \(R\)-independent and factorises cluster by cluster.

  • \(\ln\mathcal{S}_{\rm RSV}\) is a Bessel-kernel transform of the TMD soft function. This gives \(\gamma_{\nu,\rm RSV}=\gamma_{\nu,\perp}+(\alpha_s/4\pi)^3\,\tfrac{32}{3}C_R b_0^2\zeta_3\) at three loops.
  • The remainder \(\Delta\mathcal{S}\) vanishes for single emission, so the two-loop single-real amplitude is never needed. Its singularity structure also drops by two orders: at three loops it has only one-loop-type poles.
  • The remainder splits into III, CCI and CCC pieces, which scale at small \(R\) as \(R^4\), \(R^2\ln R^2\) and \(\ln^2R^2\) respectively. Integrations are done semi-numerically with Cuba, cross-checked with VegasFlow.

Results. Three loops is the first order at which the result depends on the clustering algorithm, but the spread between the three algorithms is very mild. For \(n_f=5\), \(C_R=C_A\), the three-loop coefficients are:

\(R\) Three-loop coefficient Two-loop coefficient (for comparison)
0.1 77165 ± 0.36‰ 2174.69
0.4 28537 ± 0.38‰ 1135.60
0.8 6645 ± 0.97‰ 498.59

At \(p_T^{\rm veto}=30\) GeV the three-loop term is a 15–40% correction to \(\gamma_\nu\), largest at small \(R\).

Checks and caveats. - IR poles cancel numerically in all colour structures; for \(C_RC_AT_Rn_f\) this requires combining quark and gluon final states. - The known LL \(\ln^2R^2\) coefficient is reproduced. - The LL small-\(R\) approximation is poor even at very small \(R\); a fit including the NLL \(\ln R^2\) term is good up to \(R\sim0.4\), where it overshoots by ~10%. This implies a large NLL small-\(R\) coefficient. - Results are numerical rather than analytic. - Coherence-violating effects are noted but enter only at five loops beyond leading \(N_c\). - The provided text truncates before the phenomenological impact discussion.

The RSV subtraction strategy is pitched as reusable for other clustering-dependent three-loop soft functions.

arXiv:2610.03489 — CMS search for \(T\bar T\to(\phi t)(\phi t)\), \(\phi\to\gamma\gamma\), boosted all-hadronic tops (CMS Collaboration)

This is a substructure application rather than a methods paper. It uses 138 fb⁻¹ at 13 TeV.

Selection. - Two AK8 jets with \(p_T>350\) GeV and soft-drop mass (\(\beta=0\), \(z_{\rm cut}=0.1\)) \(m_{\rm SD}>50\) GeV, with \(|\Delta\eta|<1.6\). - Both jets ParticleNet top-tagged at the 0.5% light-jet mistag working point (~40% top efficiency). - Two photons; the signal region requires both to pass the WP80 identification, and a control region requires both to fail it.

Background estimate and fit. Background is extrapolated from the control region with an exponential transfer function in \(m_\phi^{\rm reco}\). The fit is two-dimensional in \((m_T^{\rm reco},m_\phi^{\rm reco})\) over \(m_\phi=100\)–300 GeV and \(m_T=800\)–1800 GeV.

Results. - The largest local excess is 2.7σ (1.5σ global) at \(m_T=900\), \(m_\phi=150\) GeV. It is a single event against a predicted \(7\times10^{-6}\). - Upper limits are set on \(\sigma\,\mathcal{B}(T\to\phi t)^2\,\mathcal{B}_{\gamma\gamma}(2-\mathcal{B}_{\gamma\gamma})\).

Jet-specific systematics. The analysis is statistics-dominated. The ParticleNet uncertainty has a 1.0% impact on \(r\), and JES/JMS/JER/JMR are smaller still; FSR (3.0%) leads.

Caveats. - The second \(\phi\) is left undecayed in simulation; the authors note this may slightly underestimate signal acceptance. - Jet–diphoton pairing is correct only 50–75% of the time (~40% at the lowest \(m_T\)). - Validation is statistically limited (VR \(p\)-value 0.14).

arXiv:2610.00514 — Unified description of longitudinal and transverse parton dynamics in cold nuclear matter (Weiyao Ke)

Relevance to this topic: This paper is only peripherally about jets. It concerns parton propagation in cold nuclear matter for SIDIS and Drell–Yan. The link to substructure is the final section on the one-point energy correlator (OPEC) in DIS.

What they did. Ke combines two strands of SCET_G work into one TMD-level matching coefficient, Eq. (1): - Longitudinal part. Impact-parameter-dependent medium-induced evolution M^med, building on Ke–Vitev’s RG analysis of collinear SIDIS. The coupled quark/gluon equations (2)–(3) are in the dilute limit υ ≃ α_s ρ_G L²/(4E) ≪ 1. They run over ξ² ≲ μ² ≲ min{2E/L, μ_b²}. Finite b therefore truncates the coherent-radiation window, so energy loss depends on the transverse resolution. - Transverse part. Broadening through the exponentiated Glauber cross section Σ_i(b,Y). - Fix to the collision kernel. The kernel is BFKL-evolved in rapidity, but leading-order BFKL “produces excessively strong radiative broadening.” It is tamed with a Kwieciński-type kinematical constraint (following Deak et al.) and a Kovchegov–Weigert-style running coupling.

Results. One parameter set (ρ_G = 0.5 fm⁻³, ξ² = 0.12 GeV²) reproduces the main trends of HERMES Xe/d multiplicity ratios and E866 Fe/Be and W/Be Drell–Yan ratios. The paper also gives EIC e+Pb/e+d predictions at √s = 60 GeV, Q² = 20 GeV², x_B = 0.1 and 0.4. Attenuation is stronger at x_B = 0.4, because the rest-frame parton energy E ≃ Q²/(2m_N x_B) is lower.

The jet-substructure-relevant point. Energy weighting kills the leading final-state energy-loss enhancement via the momentum sum rule. For the OPEC at √s = 141 GeV and 10 < Q < 15 GeV: - the dynamical cold-nuclear-matter correction is below 1% in the back-to-back region; - the roughly 30% nuclear modification comes from the nPDFs.

So for nuclear energy correlators, the final-state medium effect largely cancels at this order. This makes the OPEC an nPDF probe rather than a medium probe, and is worth knowing before interpreting eA energy-correlator data as jet quenching.

Caveats. - Proceedings-length text; data agreement is described only as “main trends,” with no fit quality quoted. - Vacuum nonperturbative TMD input is used, and in-nucleus hadronization is explicitly excluded. - The υ ≪ 1 limit is stretched at low-E HERMES and EIC kinematics.

arXiv:2609.39515 — Probing jet quenching via the correlation of groomed jet substructure observables in Pb–Pb and pp collisions (ALICE Collaboration)

arXiv:2609.40224 — In medium evolution of the energy-energy correlators (Weiyao Ke)

These are Ke’s proceedings, summarising Ke–Meçaj–Vitev (JHEP 04 (2026) 155).

Framework. In SCET\(_G\) at first order in opacity, the regime is thin, dilute media with \(p_T/L\gg\alpha_s\rho_G L\sim m_D^2\). The EEC jet function receives two kinds of medium correction:

  1. Non-contact fixed-order correction. It changes character at \(\theta_{\rm LPM}=\sqrt{8\pi/(p_T L)}\) and carries a Coulomb log. It depletes the EEC below \(\theta_{\rm LPM}\) and enhances it above.
  2. Medium-induced anomalous dimension. This is a matrix \(\boldsymbol\Gamma^{\rm med}(N)\) acting in the window \(\Lambda_{\rm QCD}/p_T\ll\theta\ll\theta_{\rm LPM}\). Its strength is set by \(w_{\rm med}=4\pi\alpha_s\rho_{\rm eff}L_{\rm eff}^2/(2p_T)\). The diagonal entries grow linearly in \(N\), which motivates ratios of higher-point correlators to the EEC. Its effect is extra small-angle suppression, stronger for gluon jets.

The medium enters only through two path-integrated quantities, \(\rho_{\rm eff}\) and \(L_{\rm eff}\); coherence means the local density profile is not resolved at this order. The switch from vacuum to modified evolution occurs at \(\mu_m\sim\sqrt{p_T/L_{\rm eff}}\).

Phenomenology.

  • p–Pb: compared with preliminary ALICE minimum-bias data for \(R=0.4\) jets in charged-jet \(p_T\) bins of 20–80 GeV. The calculation gives small-angle suppression that weakens with \(p_T\), consistent with the data.
  • O–O: projections are given for O–O collisions at 5.36 TeV.

Caveats.

  • Charged jets are approximated by \(p_T^{\rm ch}\simeq\tfrac23 p_T^{\rm full}\), with no track functions.
  • The uncertainty bands vary only the matching scale, not the medium parameters.
  • Hadronization dominates at small \(\theta\), and recoil and medium response matter near \(\theta\sim R\).

Connection to the ALICE paper. Both are probing medium-modified angular structure, but in opposite regimes. ALICE uses dense central Pb–Pb with hard grooming, where survival bias dominates. Ke works in the thin-medium limit, where a modified anomalous dimension is a cleaner, calculable target. The \(p_T\) and system-size scaling of \(w_{\rm med}\) offers a falsifiable test that groomed \(z_g\) currently does not.

arXiv:2609.36523 — Non-global logarithms and fiducial transverse-momentum-dependent observables in deep-inelastic scattering (Shuo Lin et al.)

Lin and Zhou replace the lepton–jet imbalance with a jet-free fiducial imbalance, qT = l′_T + Σ{h∈|η|<Δη/2} p_hT. By momentum conservation this equals minus the total p_T outside the window, so the rapidity window plays the role of the jet boundary. At one loop, the jet-radius coefficients c₀(R), c₁(R) are replaced by analytic B₀(Δη) = −Δη − 2ln(1−e^{−Δη}) and B₁(Δη) = 4 atanh(e^{−Δη/2}) − 2e^{−Δη/2}. For reference, inclusive-k_T at R=1 gives c₀ = −0.253 and c₁ = 0.782.

The non-global treatment has three parts: - They derive closed-form two-loop angular coefficients I₀ and I₁ (dilogarithm expressions). - The azimuthal-average NGL U₀ is resummed at LL, large-N_c, with a BMS dipole-shower Monte Carlo using a 1 − J₀(bk_T) weight. - The cos φ harmonic NGL is kept only at O(α_s²). It is single-log, U₁⁽²⁾ ~ ᾱ_s² I₁ ln(P_T/μ_b), whereas U₀⁽²⁾ is double-log.

Headline numbers: - ⟨cos φ⟩ at q_T = 1 GeV roughly halves from Δη = 1 to Δη = 2. For HERA it goes 0.280 → 0.134 → 0.074 for Δη = 1, 2, 3. - At the EIC, |A_UT| and |A_LT| grow with Δη and peak near q_T ~ 1–1.5 GeV. - A_LT at Δη = 1 and q_T = 1 GeV is ≈0.25 at EicC versus ≈0.02 at EIC.

For this topic, the paper gives an algorithm-independent recoil-control knob with analytic boundary dependence. It is a direct comparator to the H1 ML-unfolded lepton-jet measurements, and scanning Δη within one dataset tests the global and non-global contributions together.

Caveats: - NGLs are LL and large-N_c only, and the first-harmonic NGL is not resummed. - The S_NP inputs are borrowed from Drell–Yan fits (halved). - Twist-3 evolution of T_F and g̃ is approximated by unpolarized DGLAP. - The calculation is at parton level, with no hadronization or detector acceptance effects.

arXiv:2609.30357 — Optimizing Cell-Based Negative Weight Mitigation with Optimal Transport (Lauren Hay et al.)

This paper replaces the modified-Euclidean metric in Andersen–Maier cell resampling with IRC-safe OT metrics: event-level EMD in (η,φ) with p_T weights, and the spectral EMD (SEMD). A negative-weight seed grows a hypersphere in metric space until the enclosed net weight is positive, then weights are redistributed as w_i → (Σw/Σ|w|)|w_i|.

The test sample is MG5_aMC@NLO+Pythia 8.3 Z+jets at 13 TeV, with 37.6% negative weights and O(10⁵) events. The reweight fraction is set deliberately high at f_RW = 0.75. Findings: - β choice: β = 1 and 0.5 give the least bias. β = 1 was chosen on cost grounds. - Stage: resampling after hadronization beats parton-shower and Born-level resampling. - Metric comparison: EMD ≳ SEMD, both better than the original metric, most visibly in H_T and leading-jet p_T. - ΣMD: the distance to the original sample stays below the sample’s statistical uncertainty (δσ/σ = 0.0127). - Statistical gain: all variants take 1/f_ESS from 16 to 6. The metric therefore controls bias, not the statistical gain.

For jets specifically, the relevant change is that IRC safety comes from the metric itself. The previous jet-clustering step, and its dependence on algorithm and R, is no longer needed. Jets otherwise enter only as anti-k_t R = 0.4 validation observables, so this is not a substructure paper.

Caveats: - This is proceedings-length and the evidence is mostly histogram comparisons. - Only one low-statistics sample is tested. - The ΣMD figure of merit (arXiv:2609.30418) uses the EMD as its ground metric. That may flatter the EMD-based resampler relative to the Euclidean baseline; this is my inference, not addressed by the authors.
