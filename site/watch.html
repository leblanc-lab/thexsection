arXiv:2609.25153 — Search for boosted VBS \(W^\pm W^\pm H\) production with same-sign leptons and \(b\)-jets (ATLAS, 140 fb⁻¹) (ATLAS Collaboration)

First ATLAS search for EW \(W^\pm W^\pm Hjj\), targeting the quartic \(WWHH\) coupling via \(\kappa_{WW}\). The physics result is a null: observed (expected) 95% CL exclusion of \(\kappa_{WW}\) outside \([-2.1,4.1]\) (\([-1.9,3.9]\)), statistically limited (data stat impact 0.22 vs total 0.26 on \(\mu\) for the \(\kappa_{WW}=-2\) benchmark). Weaker than the recent CMS inclusive \(VVH\) result (\(\kappa_{2V}\in[0.4,1.6]\)), but a different channel.

For substructure, the interesting content is the analysis architecture. The boosted \(H\to b\bar b\) candidate is a single anti-\(k_t\) \(R=1.0\) jet built from UFOs (Track-CaloClusters), groomed with Soft-Drop + Constituent Subtraction + SoftKiller, \(250<p_T<3000\) GeV, \(m_J>40\) GeV. Tagging uses GN2X, the transformer \(H\to bb\)/\(cc\)/QCD tagger, with the discriminant in Eq. (1) at \(f_{Hcc}=0.02\); the working point is a mass-flat 1.55% multijet mistag, giving ~80% \(H\to bb\) efficiency. Notably, GN2X calibration is a leading systematic — flavor tagging (signal) contributes 0.050 to the \(\mu\) impact, second only to the fake-factor uncertainty (0.084) and well above jet energy/mass scale (0.014). The VBS forward-jet topology is captured by an RNN tagger on small-\(R\) four-momenta (VBF-RNN) rather than cut-based \(m_{jj}/\Delta\eta_{jj}\), retaining ~80% signal at score >0.2, and explicitly excludes small-\(R\) jets within \(\Delta R<1.4\) of the large-\(R\) Higgs candidate. This is a clean demonstration that mass-decorrelated boosted-\(X\to bb\) taggers plus ML VBS tagging are now the default toolkit for rare EW processes; the caveat is that at these yields (9 observed, 12.5 expected background) the boosted approach is entirely statistics-limited and the resolved topology added negligible sensitivity.

arXiv:2609.25903 — Spacetimes of maximal proper acceleration and muon mean lifetime (Ricardo Gallego Torrom'e)

Tangential match — this is a classical-geometry theory paper, and “muon collider” enters only as a proposed testing ground. The claim: in a spacetime with a maximal proper acceleration \(A_{\max}\), the proper-time functional acquires a factor \((1-a^2/A_{\max}^2)^{1/2}\), reducing the muon lifetime by \(\Delta \approx -\tfrac12 a^2/A_{\max}^2\). Given the measured lifetime precision (\(\sim10^{-8}\)), an observable effect requires \(\tfrac12 a^2/A_{\max}^2 \gtrsim 10^{-8}\).

Among the \(A_{\max}\) candidates, only identifying \(A_{\max}\) with the muon Schwinger acceleration \(A_S[\mu]\sim10^{27}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\) gives a testable window: a \(10^4\) GeV circular muon collider with \(R\sim10^4\) m yields proper acceleration \(a\sim10^{23}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\), hence a \(\sim10^{-8}\) lifetime shift. High-order-jet electrodynamics (\(A_{\max}\sim8\times10^{34}\)) and Caianiello (\(\sim10^{31}\)) give far-too-small effects. Caveats are self-flagged and severe: the Schwinger identification is a heuristic effective model, sits exactly at the acceleration scale where the classical geometric framework breaks down, and the whole construction presumes a well-defined classical worldline for the muon. Of no practical relevance to muon collider design or accelerator physics — file under exotic-physics phenomenology that happens to name-check the machine.

arXiv:2609.22899 — Exact Amplitude Reconstruction in the Real Common-Phase Sector of Small-x Diffractive Energy Flow (Sanskriti Agrawal et al.)

This is a formal small-x/EIC tomography paper; the jet connection runs through energy-flow operators and coherent diffractive dijet production rather than jet substructure in the collider-QCD sense. The authors set up an operator-level map from the impact-parameter-dependent dipole amplitude \(T_Y(r,b,\phi)\) (C-even, real, even cosine harmonics \(T_{2n}\)) to the gluon Wigner harmonics \(W_{2n}\) (via a rotationally diagonal Bessel transform, \(\propto\int r\,dr\,J_{2n}(kr)\tilde T_{2n}\)) and to the longitudinal-photon diffractive dijet amplitude harmonics \(A_{2n}\propto\int\!\int \psi_L T_{2n} J_{2n}(Pr)J_{2n}(\Delta b)\). Their central claim: in the Born leading-eikonal, longitudinal, C-even sector all harmonics share a common overall phase, so \(A_{2n}/A_0\in\mathbb{R}\), and in a node-free bin the normalized amplitude is recovered exactly as \(\sqrt{p_E(\theta)}\), with harmonic ratios from a direct Fourier projection (Eq. 25). For generic complex amplitudes the energy-flow intensity only yields autocorrelations \(s_N=\sum_l a_{l+N}a_l^*\) — the standard phase-retrieval non-uniqueness.

The genuinely new content for energy-flow observables: they treat the diffractive dijet energy-flow distribution as an intensity and give an explicit deconvolution (generating function \(X(t)=\sqrt{1+\mathcal C(t)}\), plus a logarithmic/cumulant representation) that systematically subtracts reducible lower-harmonic mixing — e.g. clarifying that the measured \(\cos4\phi\) moment contains \(2x_2+x_1^2\), so the “true” \(A_4\) requires subtracting the elliptic \(x_1^2\). Caveats are stated forthrightly: the common-phase property is not an all-order theorem — nodes leave a sign ambiguity, odderon/C-odd exchange, dispersive real-part corrections with harmonic-dependent slopes \(\lambda_{2n}\), and \(\mathcal O(\alpha_s)\) radiation all break it. No numerical EIC projections are given; this is a formalism/feasibility paper.

arXiv:2609.24334 — Multitask Jet Analysis with Vision-Language Models: A Physics-Informed Four-Panel Representation (Lu Zhang et al.)

Squarely on jet tagging/substructure, but the novelty is representational rather than physics-reach. Each JetClass jet is rendered as a \(224\times224\) RGB image of four aligned \((\Delta\eta,\Delta\phi)\) panels encoding: (P1) \(p_T\) flow all/charged/neutral; (P2) charged-hadron/neutral-hadron/EM composition; (P3) \(p_T\)-weighted transverse and longitudinal impact-parameter significances plus displaced-track multiplicity (\(s\ge2\)); (P4) signed-track \(p_T\) densities and local \(p_T^{\kappa}\)-weighted (\(\kappa=0.5\)) jet-charge asymmetry. Jets are canonicalized (centroid, PCA rotation for \(a_{\rm PCA}\ge0.05\), deterministic reflections), Gaussian-deposited (\(\sigma=0.03\)) and log-compressed. Four open VLMs are LoRA-adapted for three tasks (10-class tagging, 6-field attribute prediction, panel-consistency/localization).

Results: zero-shot is at chance (3.33–10.57% macro recall); after adaptation on 200k jets (0.2% of JetClass) Gemma4-E4B hits 75.05% macro recall / 74.87% \(F_1\), 93.31% Task-2 field-mean, 99.88%/99.75% consistency/localization. Panel ablations are the physics-relevant payoff: relative to \(p_T\)-flow alone (57.50%), adding species→61.29%, then displacement→67.47% (the dominant handle, via \(b/c\) lifetime), while jet-charge (P4) specifically lifts the near-degenerate hadronic \(Z\to q\bar q\) (Zqq 22.5→34.2%). Fixed-budget WZ reallocation buys up to +9.83 pts on Zqq at ~constant macro recall. Transfer to JetClass-II and real-data Aspen Open Jets holds (>70% Task 1, >92% Task 2/3 with 3k target jets). Caveat, stated by the authors: this is not competitive with ParticleTransformer/ParticleNet on discrimination — 75% 10-class recall is well below dedicated taggers — and the appeal is a single instruction-conditioned multitask interface, not raw performance. Pixelization also discards constituent-level information by construction.

arXiv:2609.16276 — PSICHE: Probabilistic, Structure-Intrinsic Clustering with a Hierarchical Embedding (Margaret Lazarovits et al.)

This introduces a jet clustering algorithm that abandons fixed cone size and fixed multiplicity, instead learning both jointly. The core is a variational Gaussian mixture model (vGMM) embedded inside Bayesian Hierarchical Clustering: BHC handles jet-level associations via tree-consistent merges (restricted to Voronoi/Delaunay nearest neighbors, giving N ln N scaling as in FastJet), while each embedded vGMM models subcluster substructure. Key design choices for substructure: energy-proportional data weights ωₙ = ω·Eₙ preserve four-vector sums (Eqs. 10–11) and give IRC safety at jet level; a subcluster-inheritance + L2-overlap-based merge scheme regulates subcluster multiplicity and provides “IRC protection” at subcluster level (tested by injecting ghost subclusters, none survive). The transfer factor ω = 4 GeV acts as a soft lower resolution bound rather than a hard cone. Notably, PSICHE clusters in space and time (η, φ, t), natively ingesting per-cell measurement precisions Λ*ₙ matched to simulated energy/timing resolutions (σ_t ≈ 100 ps beam spread, CMS-ECAL-like geometry).

Concrete results: on Pythia 8.3 dijets it reproduces AK4/8/15 multiplicity and jet mass/energy/time spectra; on tt̄ it dynamically shrinks jet size ∝ m_jet/p_T, recovering a W/top admixture that no single AK radius captures. On single boosted W it consistently finds two-subcluster structure where a vGMM seeded on AK constituents fails to regulate multiplicity.

For this topic, the significance is a fully unsupervised, probabilistic framework that unifies clustering and substructure resolution with timing and experimental uncertainties — a genuinely different paradigm from sequential recombination + grooming. Caveats: the Λ* addition breaks inverse-Wishart conjugacy (handled approximately); the subcluster IRC argument is empirical, not proven; benchmarks are idealized single-detector simulation, and the pileup-mitigation demonstration is only sketched (text truncated). No timing/throughput numbers versus FastJet are given.

arXiv:2609.18102 — Cat’s Cradle diagrams for substructure studies (Peter Skands)

Skands proposes a bookkeeping/visualization framework (“cat’s cradle diagrams”) plotting the parametric size αₛ^A L^λ of each perturbative coefficient versus a p_T-like substructure resolution scale, for a back-to-back qq̄ dipole at √s = 200 GeV (α_s = 0.11). The physics point: in the “resolved jet substructure” region (roughly 5 ≲ p_T ≲ 20 GeV here), neither fixed-order (N^nLO) nor soft-collinear (N^nLL) power counting gives systematic convergence — each delivers only ~half an effective order per full order (Eqs. 9–10 define R_acc and n_eff = log_{αₛ} R_acc). He introduces an intermediate counting N^nLJ, assigning terms with equal 3A−2λ the same order, nested as N^nLO ⊂ N^nLJ ⊂ N^{n+1}LL, with 1/3-order increments.

The concrete claim for substructure: conventional LL p_T-ordered dipole/antenna showers (Pythia, Vincia defaults) actually include the αₛL term beyond pure LL (Eq. 12/15), making them “LJ+LL” accurate — about an order of magnitude better than pure LL resummation across the resolved-substructure region, with a broad plateau of constant n_eff. Azimuthal averaging further compensates the local-recoil αₛ²L² violation by a factor ~1/3 (2/3 vs 1/3 random-walk argument), keeping angular-averaged observables near NLL down to a few GeV. Matrix-element corrections extend this to NLO+LJ+LL, flat over the whole range.

For this topic it offers a quantitative language for what “substructure accuracy” means midway between hard and soft regimes — useful for judging shower/grooming accuracy claims. Appendix A gives exact Z→qgq̄ Ariadne-p_T Sudakov integrals and shows running-coupling coefficients stay O(1) with ~20% per-order growth. Caveats the author states plainly: exponent-level only (not cross-section), one-prong observable tuned to the shower ordering variable, order-unity coefficient assumption tested only at lowest order, final-state qq̄ dipole only, and he explicitly claims no new physics results — the diagrams and N^nLJ counting are the deliverable, and N^nLJ is close to existing double-log counting.

Optimal transport

arXiv:2609.17738 — Similarity Pairing with Energy Mover’s Distance for Self-Supervised Pre-Training at the LHC (Ho Fung Tsoi et al.)

Tsoi and Rankin use EMD (the Komiske–Metodiev–Thaler optimal-transport metric on energy flow) as a replacement for data augmentation in self-supervised jet pre-training. Rather than distorting a jet to make two views, they pair two distinct real jets falling within an EMD window (0.01, 0.05) after normalizing p_T and scaling angular distances by R = 0.8 — treating “two genuinely similar events” as augmented views, so physics content and detector constraints are never violated. This extends MACK’s data/MC EMD-pairing idea to generic embedding learning. Pairs feed a jBOT/iBOT self-distillation teacher-student transformer (3 layers, 4 heads, 32-dim particle embeddings, [CLS] jet token) pre-trained on JetNet q/g jets.

Results: t-SNE shows clean class separation not only for q/g but for held-out W/Z/t. Probing anomaly detection (kNN, cosine, Mahalanobis scores against the QCD bulk), random pairing wins (combined AUC 0.8188 ± 0.0076), with same-class compatible and different-class worse — the interpretation being that unconstrained pairing broadens the invariance space. Versus the augmentation-based jBOT baseline (combined 0.8269), their cosine-score model reaches 0.8309 ± 0.0067, beating baseline on W and t individually.

For the optimal-transport topic, this is a concrete new use of EMD: not as a discriminant or a geometry probe but as the positive-pair sampler for contrastive/distillation pre-training, collapsing the large augmentation design space to a single window hyperparameter (found stable under moderate variation). Caveats: gains over the augmentation baseline are marginal (~0.4% AUC, within roughly 1σ on combined); pairwise EMD computation across the training set is O(N²) and its cost/scaling is not discussed; demonstrated only on JetNet ~1 TeV jets with ≤30 particles.

Model-independent EFT sensitivity study of the tau-neutrino diagonal magnetic moment μ_ττ through the photon-induced subprocess μ⁻γ → μ⁻ν_τν̄_τ, with the initial photon treated via the Weizsäcker–Williams EPA. Using Asimov significance with 5-year luminosities scaled as L=10 ab⁻¹(√s/10 TeV)², the 5σ reach is μ_ττ=7.52×10⁻⁷ μ_B (3 TeV, 0.9 ab⁻¹), 2.10×10⁻⁷ (10 TeV), and 5.21×10⁻⁸ μ_B (30 TeV, 90 ab⁻¹, 1% systematics) — up to an order of magnitude below the DONUT direct bound (3.9×10⁻⁷ μ_B) and beating LHC γp (~2×10⁻⁶) and CLIC (~9×10⁻⁷) projections.

For this topic, the paper is a straightforward extension of the authors’ own earlier PLB study of dimension-7 ννγγ operators (ref [26]) to the dimension-5 dipole operator, reusing the identical process and simulation setup. arXiv:2609.17530 — Vanilla Scotogenic Model at the Future Muon Collider (Niloy Mondal et al.)

Central to the topic. The authors revive the vanilla scotogenic model (inert doublet + 3 RHNs, Z₂-odd) by exploiting a Casas-Ibarra choice (z₂₃=0+7i) that inflates 2nd/3rd-generation Yukawas to O(0.1), boosting the N₁ CP asymmetry and lowering successful leptogenesis to m_N₁≳1.4 TeV — set by the interplay of sphaleron decoupling (m_N₁/10≳131 GeV) and ΔL=2 washout (fails for λ″_ηH≲10⁻⁴). This brings the spectrum into muon-collider range while remaining consistent with LZ-2025, MEG II (BR(μ→eγ)<1.5×10⁻¹³), and relic density (DM mass pinned to ~550–650 GeV, or heavier with large Yukawas via t-channel ηη†→LL̄).

arXiv:2609.16276 — PSICHE: Probabilistic, Structure-Intrinsic Clustering with Hierarchical Embedding for Jet Reconstruction (Margaret Lazarovits et al.)

A genuinely new clustering paradigm rather than a substructure measurement. PSICHE embeds a variational Gaussian mixture model (vGMM) inside Bayesian Hierarchical Clustering, so that jet multiplicity/scale (tree level) and internal substructure (mixture level) are inferred jointly and unsupervised, with no pre-set cone size or prong count. Key novelties for substructure: (i) dynamically learned, variable jet sizes; (ii) simultaneous multi-scale learning of subclusters; (iii) clustering in space and time (η,φ,t), with per-cell energy/time measurement precisions Λ*_n folded directly into the likelihood; (iv) energy-proportional data weights ω_n=ω·E_n that give jet-level IRC safety and preserve four-vector sums. Subcluster proliferation is controlled by hierarchical inheritance (child multiplicity seeded to sum of parents) plus explicit pairwise merge comparisons via L2 Gaussian overlap. Nearest-neighbor (Voronoi/Delaunay, FastJet 3.4-based) merge restriction brings scaling to ~N ln N.

Concrete substructure results: on boosted single-W samples PSICHE consistently resolves the two-prong parton structure as two subclusters, where a vGMM run post-hoc on AK-jet constituents fails to regulate multiplicity. In tt̄, it dynamically reconstructs both resolved and boosted top/W jets, tracking jet size ∝ m_jet/p_T across p̂_T^min=200→500 GeV, whereas AK jets impose hard cone cutoffs. A top-jet decomposes into three localized prongs (bb + W→qq) plus a dispersive soft-radiation component. The hyperparameter ω (=4 GeV here) softly sets the resolved scale — this replaces the cone parameter but is not eliminated. Validation is against anti-k_T (R=0.4/0.8/1.5) on Pythia 8.3 + idealized CMS-ECAL-like calo/PF simulation; there is no comparison yet to standard substructure taggers (N-subjettiness, soft drop) or performance under realistic detector/BIB effects. The time dimension enabling new time-based substructure observables (subcluster times, space-time covariance) is the most distinctive extension for the field.

arXiv:2609.12054 — Fast BIB simulation at a future Muon Collider with generative machine learning (Radha Mastandrea et al.)

First generative-ML surrogate for beam-induced-background hits in the tracking detectors of a 10 TeV muon collider (MAIA concept), targeting the FLUKA+Geant4 bottleneck (~10⁶ HS23·hr for the ~10% bunch-crossing sample currently used for overlay, ~100 GB/beam). They train per-subdetector (6 subdetectors) models that generate the five continuous hit observables (log E, t, r, φ, z) conditioned on cell-ID indices (side, layer, module, sensor), in a local-φ basis, followed by geometry masking/rejection and KD-tree cell-ID assignment. Two architectures: a TabDDPM-based tabular diffusion model (higher fidelity, slow sampling: 6–36 hr/subdetector) and a neural circular spline flow (lower fidelity, <2 min/subdetector). Both beat full sim by >1 order of magnitude in generation time.

Validation is thorough: hit-level marginals agree well (both struggle with log E details — diffusion misses the Geant4 1e-6 GeV cutoff peak, flow overshoots high-E tail); BDT classifier AUCs ~0.52–0.56 for diffusion, up to 0.61 for two flow barrel models; after ACTS CKF reconstruction, Loose-track counts match to ~3% (1.07M vs 1.03M) and hits-per-track distributions are nearly identical. r (barrel) and z (endcap) are the hardest features, localized to sensor boundaries.

Central caveat, stated plainly: only one “unique” training event exists — itself just 10% of a real BIB event replicated via 42 rigid azimuthal rotations — so inter-event correlations cannot be modeled or validated, and the 42 copies aren’t statistically independent. Weights/code/data are released; authors flag calorimeter and post-digitization generation as follow-ups.

Optimal transport

arXiv:2609.12024 — Learning the Geometry of Collider Events with Metric-Aware Deep Sets (Lauren Hay et al.)

A supervised Deep Sets surrogate (MA-PFN) for the Energy Mover’s Distance that bakes in three metric axioms — non-negativity, exchange symmetry, zero self-distance — while leaving the triangle inequality and IRC safety unconstrained, then measures how much of the remaining geometry is recovered “for free.” The construction uses the Multiset Deep Sets theorem: separate shared-encoder latent sums z_E, z_G, recombined into Σ=z_E+z_G, Δ=z_E−z_G, with the predicted distance factored as an exact latent pseudometric ρ=‖Δ‖₁/L (which identically satisfies the triangle inequality) times a symmetrized positive gauge g=softplus(a_sym). Any metric violation is carried entirely by the pair-dependent g.

Results are the interesting part for OT-in-HEP. Against a matched unconstrained PFN, MA-PFN gets 0.89% MAPE (vs 1.12%), a ~20% error reduction. The enforced axioms improve the unenforced triangle inequality: across 10⁶ held-out triplets, violations drop 199→2 and the max violation 149.5→5.8 GeV; exchange symmetry goes from 0.1% to ~100% of pairs within tolerance. IRC safety is only partially learned — under soft-emission and 50/50 collinear splits both networks plateau at a nonzero floor rather than tracking the exact EMD, though MA-PFN follows the exact response to smaller perturbation scales. Timing: amortized single-encode-per-event gives a flat pair-stage cost (17–26M pairs/s on one A40), with the advantage over the fastest 48-core exact solver growing from 1.5× at multiplicity 4 to 1.8×10³ at multiplicity 202; it Pareto-dominates Sinkhorn (ε=10⁻³, 3.4% error) and sliced-Wasserstein.

Caveat as authors state: identity of indiscernibles, the triangle inequality, and IRC safety are not guaranteed, and relative error grows at small EMD — so it remains an approximate surrogate, not a drop-in metric for downstream nearest-neighbor/clustering work. Timing is not hardware-normalized (GPU vs 48 CPU cores).

Note both papers share the LeBlanc/Brown-IAIFI group and the same EMD samples appear in the cell-resampling reference — the OT paper is squarely about OT, not tangential.

arXiv:2609.10679 — Computationally Efficient Description of Medium Response to Jets in Heavy Ion Collisions (Jorge Casalderrey-Solana et al.)

Only tangential to jet substructure per se: the paper computes the hydrodynamic wake (medium response) that contaminates the soft component of reconstructed jets in heavy-ion collisions, not substructure observables directly. Its relevance is that this soft contribution cannot be groomed away for most observables, so a correct wake description is a prerequisite for computing jet/substructure observables in AA. The concrete advance is an “Efficient Wake” procedure: precompute a library of 34 template (Green’s-function) solutions to (1+1)D linearized hydro on a Bjorken background for unit energy-momentum deposition (deposition times τ_temp^d = kΔτ, Δτ=0.4 fm/c, τ₀=0.4 to τ_f=13.6 fm/c, T_f=145 MeV ⇒ T₀=446 MeV), then reconstruct wakes in a radially expanding droplet by superposing templates with suitable rotations/boosts to fold in transverse radial flow at freezeout.

Benchmarked against full nonlinear (3+1)D MUSIC hydro for 50 representative partons at two energies, they find reasonable agreement and — the headline — a speedup of “at least tens of thousands” up to a factor 200,000, while giving a much better p_T spectrum (harder, matching data’s demand for more particles above ~2 GeV) than the constant+cosine Hybrid-Model treatment. Caveats stated: the approximations are not controlled by smallness of the perturbation (validity assessed only empirically via the 50-event comparison), and implementation in a jet Monte Carlo (where 10⁵–10⁶ wakes per analysis are needed) remains future work.

arXiv:2609.11692 — Hunting the Unseen: Deep Learning Analysis for Semi-Visible Jet Tagging (Miguel A. Avenda\~no-Bernal et al.)

Squarely a jet-substructure paper. It benchmarks five DL classifiers for SVJ (Z′→dark quarks, Hidden Valley) vs QCD dijet discrimination, combining global kinematics (MET, Δφ(MET,jet), M_jj, M_T) with substructure: primary Lund Jet Plane, two-point EEC e₂^(β=1), angularity τ_{α,1} for α=0.1,0.8,1.5, and charged-hadron multiplicity. Architectures: a ViT on 50×50 LJP images (patchified 10×10, 6 encoder blocks, 4 heads), JetLOV (RegNet learning node features on the C/A tree, feeding LundNet EdgeConv), an MLP on 15 high-level observables, and two fusion networks (ViT+MLP, JetLOV+MLP). Training on 300k+300k events across 18 BPs (m_Z′=2.5, 4.5 TeV; scans of m_ψd, r_inv, Λ_dark), with three held-out 3.5 TeV BPs to test interpolation across parameter space.
