arXiv:2611.12793 — Electroweak Parton Distribution Functions for Future Lepton Colliders (Tao Han et al.)

Han, Kilian, Ma, Mękała, Reuter and Xie have put the Han–Ma–Xie LL EW PDF grids into Whizard. The grids are accessed through a modified LHAPDF interface (lhapdf_ew) with a helicity-resolved three-digit PID scheme of 59 entries. Evolution runs QED-only below μ_QCD (0.5 or 0.7 GeV), then QED+QCD up to m_Z, then the unbroken SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1) above m_Z. Longitudinal W/Z are included only at leading-order EWA. The Higgs PDF and the mixed γ/Z and H/Z₀ states are placeholders, not yet used in the convolution.

Benchmarks at √s = 10 TeV: - Mini-jet background. Inclusive QCD-induced dijets reach pb-level cross sections, with gg and gq dominant. They exceed γγ→qq̄ for p_T^j < 10 GeV. The result agrees with Han–Ma–Xie and agrees with an independent calculation to 5–10%. - HH, tt̄, XX (1 TeV scalar). EW PDF and EWA agree with the full matrix element above roughly 1 TeV. EW PDF sits about 5–20% below EWA, which the authors attribute to resummation and momentum-sum-rule preservation. - Polarization separation. HH at high subleading-Higgs p_T is dominated by W_T W_T, not W_0 W_0. This suggests an observable-based helicity separation. - Scale choice. 2H_T is preferred over √ŝ/2. With it, ντν̄τ agrees within 20%. - WW→WW. This case fails badly: the approximation overshoots by more than 10× because gauge cancellations are lost. It only recovers order-of-magnitude agreement with |η| < 2.44 (10°, detector-motivated), and the authors call that agreement “somewhat accidental”.

Why it matters: this gives an out-of-the-box parton-level generator for mini-jet backgrounds and VBF signals at a muon collider. Caveats: - Output is parton level only. Beam remnants and the shower interface are deferred. - A single mass threshold at m_Z is used. - Annihilation topologies (e.g. ZHH→ννHH) dominate the high-mass tail. The authors use an e⁻μ⁺ trick to remove them in comparisons.

arXiv:2611.11012 — Sensitivity of jet and dijet observables to medium effects in pp collisions at √s = 13 TeV using X-SCAPE/JETSCAPE (Lakshitha Fernando et al.)

The authors run two JETSCAPE/X-SCAPE studies with anti-k_T jets at R = 0.2 and 0.4.

Bjorken brick (MATTER, 10⁷ events per sample). The scan covers L = 0.5–2 fm and q̂₀ = 0.25 and 0.5 GeV²/fm. Dijet mass is the most responsive observable. Suppression is ordered in L and q̂, and is stronger for R = 0.2. The mean A_J shifts by only about +0.002, and no Δφ broadening is seen.

Realistic sample (3D Glauber + MUSIC + MATTER, 10⁶ events each, about six weeks of grid time). Vacuum and medium are compared in the top-20% event-activity class. - Medium/vacuum ratios sit slightly below 1, with no monotonic trend. - The mean A_J rises by 0.0056 ± 0.0018 (about 3σ) and 0.0057 ± 0.0020 (about 2.9σ) in the two leading-jet p_T bins. - For Soft Drop (z_cut = 0.1, β = 0, 60–80 GeV, R = 0.2), θ_g and m_g show only bin-to-bin structure. There is no θ_g narrowing of the kind ALICE sees in Pb–Pb, and the tagging fraction is about 0.99 in both samples.

The useful conclusion is the size of the expected signal. It is smaller than the multiplicity-selection effects ALICE already measures in pp and PYTHIA reproduces without a medium. Caveats: - The event-activity estimator includes hard-sector yield, so the vacuum and medium classes differ. - Soft-background subtraction is idealized (perfect). - Recoil is switched off in the realistic sample.

arXiv:2611.11600 — Threshold resummation for top-quark pair production in association with a jet at the LHC (Bakar Chargeishvili)

The main subject is tt̄+jet threshold resummation (NNLL in SCET for dσ/dM_tt̄j). Two sections matter for jet physics:

  • Jet ingredients. The factorization uses an unmeasured jet function at μ_j = p_T^j·R and a coft function at μ_c ≈ ωR_eff/2. Non-global logarithms are included via the Dasgupta–Salam large-N_c parametrization. The factorization requires ω ≪ 2E_J, which fails for a fixed jet threshold of a few tens of GeV. There μ_c > μ_j and the evolution produces an unphysical enhancement. The author therefore sets μ_c = μ_j = μ_s, keeping ln R at fixed order and making the non-global factor trivially 1. They also use a scaling threshold p_T^j > 0.15M.
  • Shower and R dependence. NLO+PS reduces the R = 0.4 cross section by 10–24% relative to NLO (shower uncertainty −6%/+14%). The reduction largely vanishes at R = 1.0, which points to ln R effects beyond what this resummation captures.

The headline physics result is negative: - The leading-power kernel tracks exact real emission only for emitted energy below about 100–200 GeV. - The correction beyond NLO ranges from 1–2% to 17–23% depending on how that region is treated, so it is not under perturbative control. NLO stays the central prediction.

Three errors are corrected in the published one-loop soft anomalous-dimension matrices for this process: s_tt̄ vs ŝ in L_β, the sign of an iπ term, and missing top–jet imaginary parts.

arXiv:2611.08579 — Observing Transverse Momentum Broadening from TEECs in Heavy Ion Collisions (Jo\~ao Barata et al.)

Setup. The authors compute the back-to-back TEEC (τ→0) for a colour-singlet qq̄ pair moving through a static, homogeneous medium. They use e⁺e⁻→γ→qq̄ at c=0 as a proxy, which deliberately removes initial-state and cold-nuclear effects. The vacuum part is NLL′ TMD resummation (b prescription, Fit-1 non-perturbative factor).

Medium modifications. These enter only through a parent-resolved TMDFF. The jet function becomes J = D_F[b; L⁺] J^vac + J^(1),med.rad. - Broadening: D_F is the harmonic-oscillator dipole. - Radiative term: the BDMPS–Z q→qg correction, computed at large N_c, from the in–in and in–out vertex-time regions. The out–out region reduces to broadening followed by vacuum splitting.

Technical results. - The radiative term obeys an energy sum rule, J^(1)(0) = 0. - Energy weighting softens the (1−z)^−3/2 BDMPS endpoint to an integrable (1−z)^−1/2, so no soft divergence enters the jet function. - It carries no scale dependence at O(α_s), so it is counted as an NLL′ constant. - The large-|p| tail combines as [D_F³ − D_F] × vacuum splitting. This tail is only conditionally convergent, so they use an η_UV regulator with the time integrals done first. - Medium response is added as a mean energy flow.

Headline (abstract-level). The extracted text cuts off in Sec. IV, so I have no numbers or (q̂, L) values. - Classical broadening only lowers the peak. - Radiative corrections suppress the peak further and populate larger δφ. - Medium response is flat across the back-to-back window, negligible at the peak and growing away from it.

Why it matters. Earlier back-to-back heavy-ion decorrelation studies kept only broadening. This paper argues that extracting q̂ from the peak requires the radiative piece.

Caveats. - They drop quark recoil against soft medium-induced gluons. That term is rapidity-divergent, needs a medium-modified soft function, and contains the known radiative p_T-broadening double logs. - The two legs are treated as propagating independently. - Large-N_c and harmonic-oscillator approximations throughout. - No realistic AA geometry.

arXiv:2611.08656 — Higher Order Component Attribution via Learned Surrogates in High Energy Physics (Lauri Laatu et al.)

Method. This is a workshop-length interpretability study. - For each jet (≤32 constituents), they fit a B-spline KAN surrogate (n→16→8→1, ≤5832 parameters) to the target model’s logit shifts under 50k local Gaussian perturbations. - The surrogate’s mixed partials (2nd- and 3rd-order tensors) serve as pair and triplet interaction scores. - These are Spearman-correlated with the top-256 pair (triplet) contributions to e₂^(1) (e₃^(1)). - Targets are ParT and vanilla MHA trained on 10-class JetClass. Best 8-block accuracies are 82.8% and 81.9%.

Results. - Pairs: ParT gives R^(2) ≈ 0.3 on 2-prong jets; MHA gives ≈ 0. - Triplets: correlations are weaker, but ParT’s error bars exclude zero. - Depth: adding blocks does not improve alignment for ParT and degrades it for MHA. The authors read this as motivation for explicitly higher-order (e.g. hypergraph) architectures.

Caveats. - Surrogate fidelity confounds the comparison. For ParT the KAN captures R² ≈ 0.99, but for 8-block MHA it is ~0.85 median and ~0.75 mean. MHA’s low alignment at depth may partly reflect a worse surrogate. - Second-order alignment is partly built in. ParT’s pair features include ΔR_ij, which the authors acknowledge. - Small and unexplained pieces. Only 50 jets per class per network. The Z-jet behaviour is “anomalous” and not explained. Per-jet spreads are large.

Fit with the TEEC paper. The only overlap is the energy-correlator language. This is a substructure-observable-as-probe for ML, not substructure physics per se.

arXiv:2611.07056: Searches for electroweak singlets at future plasma wakefield colliders

This is a wakefield-collider paper. The muon collider is a benchmark, not the subject. The authors project 95% CL reach on sin²γ for a Higgs-portal real singlet using S→hh→4b. Events are split into three categories (fully resolved, partially merged, fully merged b-pairs, ΔR = 0.4 boundary) and combined in a three-bin Poisson likelihood using Asimov datasets with no systematics. Detector assumptions are 10% jet energy resolution (~7.5% m_bb), 90% b-tag efficiency, and a double-b tagger with 80% efficiency and a 2% single-b fake rate. Additional cuts are 0.92 < m_4b/m_S < 1.1 and 0.7 < H_T/m_S < 1.09. The dominant background is VBF hh.

The muon-collider entry (10 TeV, 10 ab⁻¹) is not taken from an external study. It is the identical analysis with beam–beam effects switched off, so all luminosity sits at √s = 10 TeV. Relative to the e⁺e⁻ wakefield options, it: - performs better at high m_S and slightly worse at low m_S; - does so because the VBF cross section grows only logarithmically with √s, which rewards luminosity concentrated at the endpoint. Pair production of charged states (σ ∝ 1/M²) prefers broad spectra instead.

The headline is that e⁺e⁻ and γγ wakefield options reach “qualitatively similar” sensitivity to a 10 TeV muon collider and FCC-hh. γγ wins if e⁺e⁻ geometric luminosity falls short by more than ~3×. The intro quotes the muon collider’s indirect hWW sensitivity as sin²γ ~ 10⁻³ for context.

Caveats: - The muon proxy omits beam-induced background and any muon-specific detector effects. - The authors call EW PDFs negligible relative to beam–beam effects; that comparison has no content for a muon machine. - Numerical limits appear only in Fig. 7 and are not in the text provided. - The pair-production section (λ_HS-driven, 4j+4b channel) is truncated in the available text.

Optimal transport

arXiv:2611.07465: Optimal-Transport Contrastive Learning for Centrality Determination in Heavy-Ion Collisions

This is the one paper today where OT is the method. The pipeline: - Distance: each UrQMD Au+Au event (√s_NN = 3.0, 5.2, 19.6 GeV) is a point cloud in 3-momentum. The distance between events is an exact Hungarian-assignment W1 with a Euclidean ground metric. - Unequal multiplicities: surplus particles are left unmatched at zero cost and the cost is averaged over the min(N_A, N_B) matched pairs. This partial-transport convention deliberately removes multiplicity from the distance. - Landmarks: each event is embedded by its W1 to 100 landmark events sampled uniformly in N_ch, computed for 2000 training events only. - Network: a PointNet (3→64→64→32, max+mean pooling, 32-d unit embedding) is trained with InfoNCE, using the k = 16 F-space nearest neighbours as positives (τ = 0.15). It then runs inference on 5–7M events. - Classes: size-constrained k-means gives 20 equal 5% classes.

Using all final-state particles, the classes align with N_part, giving narrower N_part bands and better σ/⟨N_part⟩ than N_ch slicing at all three energies. Under realistic STAR acceptance (−2.4 < η_lab < 0 fixed-target; |η| < 1 collider), “OT, charged, η” is worse than N_ch slicing. The authors attribute this to spectator protons and peripheral activity falling outside acceptance, leaving multiplicity as the main central/peripheral discriminant. The transport distance has, by construction, discarded multiplicity.

Caveats: - The unbalanced-OT choice is the crux and was not varied. A mass-penalizing unbalanced W1 is the obvious next test, and the min-normalized cost is not a metric. - The method is “unsupervised” only partially: landmark selection and class ordering both use N_ch. - Resolution values are figure-only.

arXiv:2611.07826: Estimating Spectrum-Free Information Bounds for Generative Surrogates Beyond Conditional Collapse

OT is tangential here. W1 appears as one of the 1D marginal divergences the paper argues against. On CMS open-data parton→reco-jet regression (~10.8M pairs, four residual targets), a condition-blind marginal sampler beats a trained MDN on all targets, by 3.75–18.02× under W1 and 3.65–22.39× under KL. It also cannot be stably separated from a conditional flow-matching model on two of four targets. The lesson for OT users: pooled-marginal W1 is blind to whether the conditioner is used at all.

The constructive content is a per-event CRPS skill score: - Floor: the exact marginal, i.e. half the Gini mean difference, equal to Hersbach’s uncertainty term, computed in O(N log N). - Ceiling: a k-NN analog-ensemble estimate, using fair-CRPS scores at k ∈ {5…100} extrapolated to zero neighbourhood radius.

Both surrogates land within ±0.005 of the ceiling ratio. A 17,280-cell lookup table recovers 98.8% of CFM skill on jet mass, 84.6% on Δφ, and 60.8% on Δη. Global versus p_T-binned skill on jet mass drops from 28.7% to 10.1%, showing how strongly global skill depends on the breadth of the test spectrum.

Caveats: - The zero-radius intercept is explicitly not a bound. - All scores are per-target 1D. - The k-NN search omits φ, which explains a 0.0014 gap on momentum response.

One observation of mine, not made by the authors: 1D CRPS is the integrated squared CDF difference against a step function, the L2 analogue of the CDF-area form of W1.

arXiv:2611.06824 — Cross-Moment Consistency and the Fifth Adler Coefficient in Hadronic Tau Decays (Gauhar Abbas et al.)

Relevance: essentially off-topic. This paper is about perturbative QCD for inclusive hadronic τ decays (the Adler function and τ spectral moments). It contains no jets or substructure, and the keyword match is spurious. The only indirect link to your topic is α_s. The authors say high-scale QCD observables, presumably including event shapes and jet rates at FCC-ee, would test α_s running against low-scale τ determinations. They do not develop this.

For completeness, the results:

  • Transfer identity. The FOPT Adler-to-moment map is triangular with unit diagonal for normalized weights. A common error in the forecast of c₅,₁ therefore shifts every moment by the same absolute amount. A multi-weight 10% test then reduces to a single tolerance, set by the moment with the smallest coefficient (here W_P2).
  • This resolves c₅,₁ only to about ±24.
  • The CIPT analogue is far weaker: ±770.
  • Defect-sign obstruction. The defects of the direct Padé/Shanks forecast are positive and growing. Any continuation of the Adler sequence that preserves that sign therefore gives c₅,₁ ≥ c₄,₁²/c₃,₁ = 378.03. Values near 280 require extra structure that reverses the sign, such as UV renormalons.
  • Provenance of 283. The often-quoted Beneke–Jamin value of 283 is a moment-space linear extrapolation of FOPT term ratios. It is an input to their main five-parameter renormalon model, not an output of it.
  • Conditional estimate. A pure-power BJ-type ansatz with a cross-order closure test gives c₅,₁ = 248 ± 30 (struct) ± 74 (method). The central value is just the exact four-parameter fit.
  • Phenomenological impact. Δc₅,₁ = 80 shifts each FOPT moment by about 8.7×10⁻⁴, roughly 0.4% of δ⁽⁰⁾.

Caveats (stated by the authors):

  • The blind one-order-lower postdiction misses c₄,₁ by 30%.
  • Adding BJ’s full cut structure empties the admissible set, so the closure test rejects the more complete model.
  • A misplaced leading singularity at u = 3/2 still passes the closure test.

arXiv:2611.00349 — Top quark couplings at the muon collider (Nicol'as Urcelay et al.)

What they did. Urcelay, Bouzas and Larios survey six flavor-diagonal top operators in the Warsaw basis: C_tW, C_tB, C_tφ, C_φQ^(−), C_φt and C_φtb. They drop three others: C_φQ^(+) is fixed by LEP/SLC, C_tG from ttg gives only ~0.8, and C_bW is better from bb̄/bb̄h. Setup: - √s = 3, 6, 10 and 14 TeV, with ℒ = E²/10 ab⁻¹. - Six production modes, with muon acceptance |y| < 2.5, 4 or 6. - Limits from χ = S/√B = 2 on undecayed tops, with a flat efficiency K (hadronic tops, top-tag 0.81).

Key results. - Dipoles from tt̄h, not tt̄. At ≥6 TeV, tt̄h beats tt̄ despite a 24–27% cross-section uncertainty. The tt̄ amplitude is chirally suppressed by m_t²/s, and the extra Yukawa chirality flip in tt̄h lifts it. C_tWr reaches [−0.004, 0.013] at 10 TeV and [−0.002, 0.007] at 14 TeV, consistent with Han–Liu–Wang. - tt̄νν̄ for the ttZ couplings and the Yukawa. At 10 TeV: C_φQ^(−) = ±0.10, C_φt = [−0.050, 0.056] (about 40× narrower than the LHC), and C_tφ = [−1.07, 1.68], roughly 6.5% on y_t. - Forward-muon tagging matters. If muons with |y| > 2.5 go untagged, tt̄μμ leaks into the missing-momentum sample. C_φt then degrades by ~3× and C_tφ by ~1.3×; C_φQ^(−) is unaffected. - Single top. This gives the best C_φtb: [−0.35, 0.31] at 10 TeV with |y| < 6. That is about a third of the HL-LHC V_R projection (|C_φtb| < 1.22); it is ~1.1 with |y| < 2.5.

Caveats. The limits are statistical only, use parton-level undecayed tops, include no backgrounds, and are individual (marginalized only for the C_tW–C_tB plane). The appendix adds a 2% systematic. This mostly hurts high-rate channels: single top at 14 TeV goes from 0.6% to 4.0% total uncertainty. Treat this as a roadmap, not a projection.

arXiv:2611.01117 — LHμC Concept: Energy-Frontier Antimuon–Proton Collisions at CERN (Arda Goroglu et al.)

Relevance. These are workshop proceedings on a μ⁺p machine, not a μ⁺μ⁻ collider. They matter here because they push μTRISTAN-style ultra-cold μ⁺ technology, which needs no ionization cooling, as an earlier route to TeV muon beams.

Concept. - 1 TeV μ⁺ on 7 TeV HL-LHC protons gives √s = 5.3 TeV. - Peak ℒ is 8.8×10³³ cm⁻²s⁻¹ with a μTRISTAN-type booster, or 7.2×10³³ by reusing the LHeC ERL tunnel (50 turns, ~1.4 ms). - The survival fractions are now 0.82 and 0.68. The original μLHC paper had 0.38 and 0.16 because of an incorrect muon mass, so its luminosities rise by about 2× and 4×.

Physics claims. - DIS reach down to x = 3.6×10⁻⁷ at Q² = 10 GeV². - Resonant colour-octet muon production gives a 5σ reach of 4.1 (4.4) TeV with 100 fb⁻¹ (1 ab⁻¹), versus 2.3 TeV at the HL-LHC. - RPV λ′_2j1 can be probed to 0.01–0.02 for 1–3 TeV squarks.

Caveats. - The 10⁵ enhancement in μ⁺ yield over J-PARC is undemonstrated. - Fixed-field booster arcs are still needed. - Neutrino radiation from μ⁺ decays is unaddressed. - The tungsten nozzle costs 1 TeV muons about 19% of their energy and limits acceptance.

arXiv:2611.03218 — Probing aQGCs from dimension-eight operators via neutral triboson production at muon colliders (Parimah Hosseini et al.)

What they did. Hosseini et al. study the dim-8 transverse operators O_T0–O_T9, including T3 and T4, in γγγ, γγZ, γZZ and ZZZ. - Energies: 3 TeV (1 ab⁻¹) and 10 TeV (10 ab⁻¹). ZZZ is 3 TeV only, 6e only, because 10 TeV generation “proved challenging”. - Chain: MG5 → Pythia → Delphes with the muon-collider card. - Selection: single-variable cut-and-count on p_T^γ3, p_T^γ2 or m_Z2. - Statistics: χ² with 5% systematic, one coefficient at a time, dim-6 set to zero.

Key results. - Triphoton dominates. The best limits at 10 TeV are f_T8/Λ⁴ = ±2.64×10⁻⁶ TeV⁻⁴ and f_T9 = ±5.73×10⁻⁶. - For comparison, CMS γγZ gives f_T8 ≈ [−1.06, 1.10] TeV⁻⁴, and FCC-hh with 5% systematic gives ~10⁻³. - Their 10 TeV γγγ result is comparable to earlier 30 TeV muon-collider studies (~10⁻⁶). The paper doesn’t explain why it matches the 30 TeV studies. - Z channels are weaker because of leptonic branching ratios.

Caveats. - The “first T3/T4 constraints” claim is soft. In these channels T2 and T3 have identical cross-section fits, as do T0/T1 (and T4/T7 in γγγ). So T3 is not separately constrained. - The pure-BSM term grows as s³, yet there is no unitarity clipping or EFT-validity treatment. - There is no beam-induced background.

arXiv:2611.01274 — Measurement of |V_cb| using tt̄ events at √s = 13 TeV (CMS) (CMS Collaboration)

Result. With 138 fb⁻¹ of lepton+jets data, CMS measures |V_cb| = [4.59 +0.44/−0.48 (stat) +0.64/−0.76 (syst)]×10⁻² from W→cb in tt̄. That is r = 1.27 +0.46/−0.44, against an expected 4.07×10⁻². It is more precise than ATLAS (5.0 +1.1/−1.4).

Jet-relevant methods. - Two-stage XGBoost jet–parton assignment. The first BDT deliberately omits the DeepJet scores of the W-daughter candidates, so it doesn’t pull g→bb̄ radiation into the W. The second BDT uses those scores only to resolve up/down ordering. - Assignment performance. Correct-assignment efficiency is 67% for cb versus 86% for ud. Radiative heavy-flavor contamination is 6–10%. - Discriminant. A TabNet discriminant is driven mainly by the W-daughter B scores. There is no dedicated c-tagger, and the c-jet scale factor is fixed to 1 with conservative uncertainties decorrelated in three p_T bins. - Gluon-splitting proxies. min m_bb and min ΔR_bb rank fifth in importance and below.

Systematics. The leading term is μ_R/μ_F (±0.25 on r), mostly from the 4FS tt̄bb̄ prediction. The 4FS and 5FS fits differ by Δr = 0.19, which is 0.88σ in a bootstrap test. For a jets person, the interest is that g→bb̄ and heavy-flavor modelling limit a flavor-physics measurement.

arXiv:2610.36030 — A Practical Guide to Sensitivity Projections for Beyond the Standard Model Physics (Francesca Acanfora et al.)

How central it is: this is a statistics pedagogy paper. The muon collider is one worked example, not the subject. The core point is that BSM projections should use a hypothesis test, not a goodness-of-fit test. The authors show that on Asimov data both test statistics reduce to Pearson χ²_A. What differs is the reference distribution: - Goodness-of-fit: χ² with k = n_bins degrees of freedom. - Hypothesis test: ½χ²₁, using a μ-interpolated alternative H(μ) = μH_BSM + (1−μ)H_SM.

The practical recipe is to require χ²_A ≥ 2.71 for 95% CL, or ≥ 3.84 for 95% CLs.

The muon-collider example: - Process: μ⁺μ⁻ → V⁰ → e⁺e⁻ at √s = 10 TeV with 1 ab⁻¹. - Model: heavy vector triplet model A, tree level, with SM interference. - Binning: six uniform |η_e| bins over [0, 2.5]. - Efficiency set to 1; no detector or beam-induced-background treatment.

At the benchmark (m_V, g_V) = (1.9 TeV, 1.9), the two tests disagree sharply: - Goodness-of-fit: q_A = 9.06, p ≈ 0.16, so the point is not excluded. - Hypothesis test: q_A = 9.62, p ≈ 0.0013 (Monte Carlo) or 0.00096 (asymptotic). χ²_A = 9.33 gives 0.0011. The point is excluded.

The (m_V, g_V) exclusion contours (thresholds 12.6 vs 2.71) differ substantially. The text describes them only qualitatively; no numerical reach is quoted. Using 3.84 plus systematics reproduces the CLs contours of the earlier muon-collider HVT study (Ref. [49]). Uncorrelated systematics added in quadrature give almost the same contours as a nuisance-parameter treatment.

Takeaway for the topic: there is no new muon-collider reach here. The useful part is a calibration warning: any published muon-collider projection that used a “χ² test” with n_bins degrees of freedom is conservative by a large factor in coupling reach. arXiv:2610.36073 — Regression on high-dimensional phase space (Larkoski) (Andrew J. Larkoski)

Larkoski develops a 1/n expansion of uniform massless phase space through O(n⁻²) for m-particle correlators. At leading order, phase space factorizes into Boltzmann factors n²z e^{−nz} with isotropic angles. The main result is a proof that the covariance of any two EFPs on uniform phase space is O(n⁻²).

  • Why the O(n⁻¹) term vanishes: the 9ml/2n, −6ml/n and +3ml/2n pieces cancel. This needs permutation invariance, momentum conservation and rotational invariance together.
  • What breaks it: dropping momentum conservation (pure Boltzmann sampling) restores an O(1/n) covariance.
  • Numerical check with RAMBO: Var(EFP₂⁽²⁾) = (2/5)n⁻² on Lorentz-invariant phase space versus (32/9)n⁻¹ for Boltzmann sampling.
  • Corollary: EFP expansion coefficients of an IRC-safe observable are n-independent, so the n⁻² covariance extends to any IRC-safe pair.

Consequence for regression. Fitting |M_n|² with EFPs by mean-squared error forces coefficients c_β = O(n²). The normalization sum rule then requires the O(n²) and O(n) pieces to cancel exactly. That is a structurally ill-conditioned linear system at high multiplicity.

Larkoski proposes imposing these constraints order by order in n. He also suggests building non-analytic structure (e.g. Sudakov factors) into the basis. For the e⁺e⁻ → hadrons example he uses KNO scaling to keep only the leading ⟨n⟩ term, with O(α_s) EFP expectation values on the right-hand side.

Caveats: - The measure is uniform phase space. - No closed-form covariance exists for general EFP pairs; only numerical results are shown. - The text truncates before the e⁺e⁻ numerical results. - The minimal loss involves ⟨(|M_n|²)²⟩, which is not computable at fixed order.

This is relevant to anyone doing linear or EFP-based fits on high-multiplicity jets.
