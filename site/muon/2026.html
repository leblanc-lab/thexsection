Neutrino trident process at a muon collider

Setup. The authors estimate trident rates in a forward neutrino detector (“MuColν”): - Detector: 10 kg of tungsten (R = 10 cm, L = 1.65 cm), sited 500 m downstream of the IP. - Beam: 5 TeV μ beams, with a tree-level decay spectrum. - Flux: N_ν = 5×10¹⁷ ν_μ per year plus an equal number of ν̄_e. This assumes about 10% acceptance of decays along the 500 m straight section. - Cross section: the Altmannshofer et al. full 2→4 generator (no equivalent-photon approximation), adapted to W with a Woods–Saxon form factor. Coherent and incoherent contributions are both included.

Mixed-flavour channels are enhanced because only the W diagram contributes, so there is no destructive W/Z interference. Coherent scattering dominates (Z² scaling).

BSM reach (Δg_V, Δg_A). - The 95% CL contours greatly tighten the CCFR ring, whether statistics-only or with 5% systematics. - For L_μ−L_τ Z′, the projections open new parameter space near m_Z′ ≈ 4 GeV and above the electroweak scale. - A 10-ton detector is claimed to reach g′ ~ 10⁻⁴ for m_Z′ ≳ 10⁻² GeV.

What this adds to the muon collider topic. It contributes another entry to the emerging forward-ν physics case, alongside Marzocca et al., Adhikary et al., and Kling et al. Its distinctive argument is that the clean, calculable flux makes a percent-level trident measurement plausible.

The flux uncertainty is only tree level, and the beam divergence (0.6 mrad) enters just through the acceptance estimate. - Statistical framing is inconsistent. The Fig. 4 caption mixes “95% CL” with “±1σ.” - The 10-ton projection is statistics-only.

The LHC is not enough: the LZ High-Recoil Event at FCC-hh and a Muon Collider

Benedikt Maier et al.

Maier and Spannowsky take the LZ 248 keV nuclear-recoil candidate (2.6σ global) as motivation for a class of collider targets: pure \(Y\neq0\) electroweak multiplets with endothermic \(Z\)-mediated scattering and sub-\(2m_e\) neutral splitting. That splitting makes the neutral transitions invisible. Thermal masses run from 0.58 TeV (scalar doublet) to 11.5 TeV (scalar quintuplet). Within the leading-operator EFT, a charged-state trace sum rule caps \(\Delta_{\rm min}\lesssim 0.17\) GeV for the quadruplets and quintuplets, which implies \(c\tau\gtrsim 18\)–27 mm. Most of the new quantitative work is FCC-hh: a disappearing-track rescaling model, plus monojet and Drell–Yan arguments. The muon-collider section is a compilation of published reaches (mainly Refs. [29, 32, 67]) mapped onto this benchmark set. There is no new muon-collider simulation.

The muon-collider case, as mapped here: - Why it suits these targets: lifetime-independent mono-γ/mono-W recoil searches. Disappearing-track reach approaches \(\sqrt{s}/2\) for long-lived states but only ~\(0.3\sqrt{s}\) for the mm-scale doublet. Beam-induced-background-inclusive disappearing- and long-track studies cover the thermal Higgsino and wino at 10 TeV. - Coverage table: - fermion doublet: ≥3 TeV - scalar doublet: 6 TeV (disappearing tracks) - fermion triplet: ≥10 TeV - scalar triplet: 14 TeV (missing invariant mass; needs sub-percent systematics) - fermion quadruplet: ≥14 TeV (recoil channel needs sub-percent systematics, otherwise tracks) - fermion quintuplet: ≳20 TeV - scalar quintuplet: not reached at 30 TeV - Headline: 30 TeV covers the full fermionic family.

The useful framing is that recoil channels settle the doublet and triplet without knowing the charged spectrum, which is unpredicted once custodial-breaking operators are allowed.

Caveats: - Recoil projections assume 0–1‰ systematics, with 1% quoted only where it changes the conclusion. - The HTML I received truncates mid-§VI, so the detailed muon-collider discussion and caveats section were not available. - The authors themselves note that a full-density thermal Higgsino is in tension with solar-capture limits (\(\delta m_0\gtrsim0.51\) MeV) and with the empty LZ high-energy sideband.

Spacetimes of maximal proper acceleration and muon mean lifetime

Ricardo Gallego Torrom\'e

Tangential match — this is a classical-geometry theory paper, and “muon collider” enters only as a proposed testing ground. The claim: in a spacetime with a maximal proper acceleration \(A_{\max}\), the proper-time functional acquires a factor \((1-a^2/A_{\max}^2)^{1/2}\), reducing the muon lifetime by \(\Delta \approx -\tfrac12 a^2/A_{\max}^2\). Given the measured lifetime precision (\(\sim10^{-8}\)), an observable effect requires \(\tfrac12 a^2/A_{\max}^2 \gtrsim 10^{-8}\).

Among the \(A_{\max}\) candidates, only identifying \(A_{\max}\) with the muon Schwinger acceleration \(A_S[\mu]\sim10^{27}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\) gives a testable window: a \(10^4\) GeV circular muon collider with \(R\sim10^4\) m yields proper acceleration \(a\sim10^{23}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\), hence a \(\sim10^{-8}\) lifetime shift. High-order-jet electrodynamics (\(A_{\max}\sim8\times10^{34}\)) and Caianiello (\(\sim10^{31}\)) give far-too-small effects. Caveats are self-flagged and severe: the Schwinger identification is a heuristic effective model, sits exactly at the acceleration scale where the classical geometric framework breaks down, and the whole construction presumes a well-defined classical worldline for the muon. Of no practical relevance to muon collider design or accelerator physics — file under exotic-physics phenomenology that happens to name-check the machine.

arXiv:2609.26614 §

Thermomechanical rf breakdown from magnetically focused field emission in high-gradient normal-conducting cavities

Dillon Merenich et al.

Watchlist hit (muon-collider) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Normal-conducting radiofrequency (rf) cavities for muon-collider ionization cooling must operate at high accelerating gradients in strong solenoidal magnetic fields, where rf breakdown can be enhanced by the magnetic focusing of field-emitted electrons. In this work, field-emitted electrons were tracked in the realistic field maps of rectilinear cooling-lattice cavities to test the validity of the previously developed localized-bombardment picture with simplified field maps. Under comparable reduced-field assumptions, the tracking results show good agreement with previous results. The full rf eigenmode fields and nonuniform solenoidal fields modify the idealized beamlet structure, producing rf phase dependent centroid shifts and broadened impact distributions from solenoid fringe fields. Nevertheless, the original model remains a useful framework for estimating limits on operating gradients. The thermal response is evaluated analytically using this model, with material properties varied to assess the coupled effects of heat transport and thermomechanical damage threshold. These results can inform cavity testing in strong solenoidal fields, muon ionization cooling channel designs, and other applications requiring high-gradient rf operation in magnetic fields.

arXiv:2606.16761 §

Impedance of multilayer cylindrical structures with a material-filled beam region

Erik Kvikne et al.

Watchlist hit (muon collider) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Beam-coupling impedances in material media are relevant for ionization-cooling channels of a future muon collider, where the beam propagates in matter rather than vacuum. We extend the cylindrical field-matching formalism for multilayer structures to the case of a material-filled beam region surrounded by external layers with arbitrary electromagnetic properties. Relative to the vacuum formulation, the usual factor \(1/γ^2\) is replaced by the material-dependent factor \(F=1/\varepsilon_1-μ_1β^2\), and the radial propagation constant is modified accordingly. Analytical expressions are obtained for the monopolar longitudinal and dipolar transverse impedances, with the surrounding structure encoded through reflection coefficients determined by field matching. The formalism reduces to the known vacuum and perfectly conducting limits in the appropriate cases. Representative calculations are presented for absorber-relevant configurations, illustrating the dependence on the material properties of both the beam region and the surrounding layers. In particular, a finite beam-region conductivity can generate a real impedance component and reverse the sign of the imaginary space-charge impedance, while sufficiently high permittivity can give rise to resonant structures associated with oscillatory radial fields. The effect of finite-conductivity surroundings is also examined, showing the approach towards the perfectly conducting limit as the external conductivity is increased. The applicability of the infinite-length approximation is also discussed by comparison with finite-length mode-matching results.

Fast BIB simulation at a future Muon Collider with generative machine learning

Radha Mastandrea et al.

First generative-ML surrogate for beam-induced-background hits in the tracking detectors of a 10 TeV muon collider (MAIA concept), targeting the FLUKA+Geant4 bottleneck (~10⁶ HS23·hr for the ~10% bunch-crossing sample currently used for overlay, ~100 GB/beam). They train per-subdetector (6 subdetectors) models that generate the five continuous hit observables (log E, t, r, φ, z) conditioned on cell-ID indices (side, layer, module, sensor), in a local-φ basis, followed by geometry masking/rejection and KD-tree cell-ID assignment. Two architectures: a TabDDPM-based tabular diffusion model (higher fidelity, slow sampling: 6–36 hr/subdetector) and a neural circular spline flow (lower fidelity, <2 min/subdetector). Both beat full sim by >1 order of magnitude in generation time.

Validation is thorough: hit-level marginals agree well (both struggle with log E details — diffusion misses the Geant4 1e-6 GeV cutoff peak, flow overshoots high-E tail); BDT classifier AUCs ~0.52–0.56 for diffusion, up to 0.61 for two flow barrel models; after ACTS CKF reconstruction, Loose-track counts match to ~3% (1.07M vs 1.03M) and hits-per-track distributions are nearly identical. r (barrel) and z (endcap) are the hardest features, localized to sensor boundaries.

Central caveat, stated plainly: only one “unique” training event exists — itself just 10% of a real BIB event replicated via 42 rigid azimuthal rotations — so inter-event correlations cannot be modeled or validated, and the 42 copies aren’t statistically independent. Weights/code/data are released; authors flag calorimeter and post-digitization generation as follow-ups.

On-Detector Machine Learning for Beam-Induced Background Rejection at a 10 TeV Muon Collider

Daniel Abadjiev et al.

This is the smart-pixels concept ported from HL-LHC to the muon collider BIB problem, and it’s squarely on-topic for detector R&D at √s = 10 TeV. The concrete driver: innermost vertex layer occupancy of O(10⁴) hits/cm²/BX at 30 kHz translates to >50 Gbps/chip (2×2 cm², 40-bit hits) — ~10× the HL-LHC pixel readout ceiling. The pitch is to reject BIB in the pixel front-end using cluster shape rather than relying solely on O(30) ps timing, which they argue is fragile at 25×25 µm² pitch (needs ~10× front-end power reduction) and vulnerable to calibration/radiation drift. Physics handle: collision particles hit at IP-consistent incidence angles (x_size grows with z_global), whereas BIB e± loop in at large angles regardless of position, giving distinct cluster sizes/profiles.

They train three quantized NN classes — engineered scalars (Model 1), 1D charge profiles (Model 2), full 21×13 CNN (Model 3) — via QKeras QAT, Pareto-scanned over architecture and 3–10 bit weights, with hls4ml→Vitis (FPGA) and Catapult/SAED32 (32 nm ASIC) resource estimates. Headline: 88–90% data reduction at 99% signal efficiency for Models 1–2 with ASIC area <1 mm² and 160–250 ns latency; Model 3 reaches 90.5% cluster rejection / 94.4% data reduction but at prohibitive area (~8 mm²). Crucially, cluster-shape rejection is shown orthogonal to timing — BIB is rejected across the full t_corr spectrum, and stacking a [3σ,5σ] window pushes data reduction to 98.5–98.9%.

Caveats are stated plainly and are significant: PIXELAV assumes a single isolated cluster in a clean ROI (no overlapping/warped BIB clusters, no edge effects, ROI-finding assumed upstream) — unrealistic at true muon-collider occupancy. Incoherent pair production is absent from the BIB sample (they argue IPP clusters look similar and should be rejectable if trained on). Signal is single muons, not physics events; geometry is MuColl_v1 / lattice v0.4; ASIC numbers are open-library first-pass, not manufacturable. So: a credible feasibility demonstration and benchmark (88–90% @ 99% eff, sub-mm² ASIC), not yet a validated occupancy-realistic result.

C. Barbagallo et al.
