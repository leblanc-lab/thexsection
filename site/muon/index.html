arXiv:2608.15656 §

Probing the ALP–Photon–Dark Photon Interaction through the Mono-Photon plus Missing-Energy Signature at a Muon Collider

Chong-Xing Yue et al.

A detector-level (MadGraph5+Pythia8+Delphes generic MC card) study of the dark axion portal coupling \(g_{a\gamma\gamma'}\) via \(\mu^+\mu^-\to a\gamma'\), \(a\to\gamma\gamma'\) at \(\sqrt{s}=1.5\) TeV, \(\mathcal{L}=500\) fb\(^{-1}\), assuming \(m_a\gg m_{\gamma'}\) so both dark photons escape and the signal is mono-photon + MET. Production runs through \(s\)-channel \(\gamma\) and \(Z\), with the EW-gauge-invariance relation \(g_{aZ\gamma'}=-\tan\theta_W\,g_{a\gamma\gamma'}\). The dominant background is \(\mu^+\mu^-\to\nu\bar\nu\gamma\), whose radiative-return peak (\(E_\gamma\approx750\) GeV, \(Z\to\nu\bar\nu\) on-shell) is killed by cuts on MET, \(|\eta_\gamma|<1.2\), and recoil mass. Projected \(2\sigma\) reach spans \(g_{a\gamma\gamma'}\sim1.7\times10^{-5}\)–\(9.1\times10^{-5}\) GeV\(^{-1}\) over \(1\le m_a\le1200\) GeV for the fully polarized \(P(\mu^+,\mu^-)=(-100\%,+100\%)\) beam, and \(\sim1.0\times10^{-4}\)–\(4.5\times10^{-4}\) GeV\(^{-1}\) unpolarized.

For the muon-collider topic this is a first look at the dark axion portal at a muon machine (previously only \(e^+e^-\)/hadron colliders), and its distinctive contribution is reach in ALP mass up to \(\sim\)1.2 TeV — well beyond the \(\mathcal{O}(10\text{–}80)\) GeV windows of LEP/BaBar and the \(m_a\lesssim50\) GeV coverage of the FCC-ee projection. That said, in raw coupling sensitivity it is outperformed everywhere by FCC-ee (\(10^{-6}\)–\(10^{-7}\)), STCF (\(10^{-6}\)), and Belle II/BESIII (\(10^{-5}\)); the value claimed is complementarity via high-mass coverage, not depth.

Two visible caveats blunt the headline. The strong (\(10^{-5}\)) numbers rely on ideal \(\pm100\%\) beam polarization, which the authors concede is unrealistic; the unpolarized significances collapse to \(SS=1.8\to0.35\) across \(m_a=100\to1000\) GeV, i.e. below \(2\sigma\) over much of the range. Prompt \(a\) decay is assumed throughout — a long-lived/displaced treatment (left to future work) would change both signal efficiency and background.

arXiv:2608.04399 §

Production of the top partner T of the Bestest Little Higgs Model at the future muon collider

E. Cruz-Albaro et al.

Abstract only. A BLHM phenomenology study computing decay widths and branching ratios of the heavy top partner \(T\) and its production at a \(\mu^+\mu^-\) collider through both pair production, \(\mu^+\mu^-\to(\gamma,Z,Z',h,H)\to T\bar T\), and single/associated production, \(\mu^+\mu^-\to(\gamma,Z,Z',h,H)\to \bar t T + t\bar T\). Cross sections are scanned over several \(\sqrt{s}\) values; the headline conclusion is that pair production is the most promising discovery channel. No numbers, mass points, or luminosity/reach figures are given in the abstract.

For the muon-collider track this is a standard added-physics-case exercise rather than a machine or analysis advance: the novelty is applying the \(s\)-channel neutral-current mediator set (including the BLHM-specific \(Z'\), \(h\), \(H\)) to top-partner production, but the abstract offers no sensitivity benchmark or comparison against hadron-collider limits. Judge relevance once the full text provides the actual \(\sigma(\sqrt s)\) curves and the assumed \(T\) mass range relative to the \(Z'/H\) resonance thresholds — the associated \(tT\) channel is the more collider-specific observable and worth checking whether it stays subdominant across all \(\sqrt s\) or only near threshold.

Wednesday, August 5, 2026

Two independent, cross-referencing papers appeared today (each cites the other as “note added”/companion) on the forward neutrino flux from muon decay at a 10 TeV MuC. They agree on the headline O(10⁹) interactions/year at a forward detector but differ substantially in emphasis, geometry, and — importantly — on whether an energy–angle “prism” correlation survives beam dynamics. Read together they define the emerging quantitative baseline for a MuC forward neutrino program.

arXiv:2608.02714 §

Neutrino Fluxes at a Muon Collider

Francis M. Burk et al.

Comprehensive re-evaluation of all neutrino sources for the v0.8 IR design. Key number: beam-muon-decay flux is ~10¹⁹ ν/year in the forward direction, ~120× higher than the ISU/IMCC estimate, driven by integrating over the full ~500 m straight section (not just 10 m) and increasing collimation away from the IP. They also quantify three previously-neglected subdominant sources: (i) primary μ⁺μ⁻ collisions, dominated by γγ→τ⁺τ⁻ (~0.4–1 nb, ~10¹⁰ ν_τ at 10 ab⁻¹, cross-checked between DGLAP-resummed PDFs and fixed-order WHIZARD to O(10%)); (ii) Michel-electron EM showers in the tungsten shielding, where Bethe–Heitler γN→τ⁺τ⁻/cc̄ and photo-nuclear γg→cc̄ give ~10¹² ν/year of all flavors — this is the leading source of ν_τ; (iii) ν-induced showers in rock (~10¹¹/year). For a 100 kg MuColν detector at z=500 m, r=25 cm (0.5 mrad acceptance, >80% acceptance for beam-decay ν), Table 1 gives 1.1·10¹⁰ ν_μ + 5.2·10⁹ ν̄_e per 10 yr, but only ~2.5 ν_τ+ν̄_τ events total. Physics case: 10¹⁰ DIS/ton/yr enables PDF/CKM (V_cd ×44, V_ub ×3), sin²θ_W to 0.03%, ν trident, NSI improved ~10× over their earlier projection, sterile Δm²~10³ eV².

Caveat, stated explicitly: absolute flux carries an O(1) machine-design uncertainty — v0.6 vs v0.8 differ by ~2–5× depending on chicane treatment. The tiny ν_τ yield is the weak point of the physics case at this detector size.

arXiv:2608.02718 §

The Forward Neutrino Flux and its Secondaries at a 10 TeV Muon Collider

Ju-Yeol Choi et al.

Introduces MINT, a public MC that propagates the central muon orbit through a MAD-X hybrid v0.6+v0.9 lattice including Twiss-based beam envelope and divergence (though not full particle tracking). Central result that qualitatively updates prior work: the beam divergence (O(0.1–1) mrad over the straight section) is ~50× larger than the intrinsic 1/γ≈0.02 mrad, so the energy–angle “prism” correlation touted in preliminary studies is washed out — recovering it would require β increasing ~100×. Benchmark: 3.2 t fiducial (gas Ar TPC + vertex tracker) at 5 km, ~60% acceptance, ~7·10¹⁸ ν/year crossing the face, O(10⁹) interactions/year.

Their distinctive contribution is the secondary flux from ν interactions in the upstream rock. Secondary muons: 6.5·10¹²/yr (μ⁺ beam) and 1.2·10¹³/yr (μ⁻) produced in the last 2.5 km, ~6·10¹¹/yr reaching the face at ⟨E_μ⟩≈1.2 TeV — ~2 muons per bunch crossing, ~40–45× the neutrino interaction rate, a serious background requiring event-by-event (not per-bunch) vetoing, and >99.9% polarized (potential feature). Secondary ν_τ: charm→D_s→τ dominates but only ~0.2 events/year total (peaks near L=3 km) — consistent with 2714’s conclusion that ν_τ are scarce, and they note photoproduction in the ring (2714’s channel) likely dominates. Wrong-sign ν: O(4)/yr (μ⁻), O(0.7)/yr (μ⁺). BSM section estimates HNL reach (mixing |U_μN|²~2·10⁻¹¹ at m_N≈15 GeV; dipole d~4·10⁻¹⁰ GeV⁻¹ at m_N≈6 GeV) via ν upscattering, exploiting √s≈75 GeV(E_ν/3TeV)^½ to exceed D/B-meson kinematic ceilings.

Caveat, emphasized by the authors: HNL curves are event-yield contours before backgrounds/efficiencies, not sensitivity projections; the secondary-muon background and vertexing/timing requirements are the gating issues. MINT omits individual optics tracking.

Net for the topic: the ~120× flux upward revision (2714) and the demonstration that beam dynamics, not 1/γ, sets the beam profile and kills the prism effect (2718) together reset the design assumptions. Both agree ν_τ are rare (~0.2–2.5 events), tempering the “tau factory” framing; the real leverage is the ~10⁹–10¹⁰ ν_μ/ν_e CC sample and the secondary-muon background now quantified for the first time.

arXiv:2607.26020 §

Probing Lepton-Flavor-Violating Four-Lepton Operators at a Muon Collider

Sukanta Dutta et al.

Abstract only. A SMEFT study of charged LFV from dimension-six four-lepton operators in \(\mu^+\mu^-\to e^\pm\tau^\mp\), \(e^\pm\mu^\mp\), \(\mu^\pm\tau^\mp\) at \(\sqrt{s}=3,10,14\) TeV, with beam polarization and hadronic \(\tau\) reconstruction. Using an optimal-observable analysis of angular distributions and a global fit over the relevant operator set, they project sensitivities of \(C/\Lambda^2\sim(0.6\text{–}1.6)\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-2}\), up to an order of magnitude beyond current limits. The multi-energy, multi-polarization combination is highlighted as the lever that breaks correlations among Wilson coefficients — the concrete methodological point for anyone tracking how a muon collider’s LFV reach depends on running scenario.

For this topic the paper is squarely on-target: it’s a projection specific to a multi-TeV muon collider, exploiting the \(s\)-channel LFV production that only a muon-beam machine gives directly. Caveats limited by abstract-only: no statement of assumed integrated luminosity per energy point, no detail on the \(\tau\)-reconstruction efficiency/background model behind the quoted reach, and the sensitivities are presumably from a truth- or fast-sim level fit rather than full detector simulation. The “exceeds current limits by an order of magnitude” claim should be read against which specific low-energy LFV constraint (e.g. \(\mu\to e\), \(\tau\to\ell\)) each operator direction is compared to.

arXiv:2607.19463 §

Higgs Couplings at a Future Wakefield Collider

Katherine Fraser et al.

Abstract only. The paper’s actual subject is 10 TeV wakefield colliders (e⁺e⁻, e⁻e⁻, γγ) and their Higgs coupling reach; the muon collider appears only as a benchmark for comparison, not as an object of study. The one relevant claim: a 10 ab⁻¹ γγ dataset yields “qualitatively similar sensitivity” to Higgs electroweak couplings as 10 ab⁻¹ at a muon collider (and to 1 ab⁻¹ at an e⁺e⁻ wakefield machine). So the muon collider here serves as the established high-energy reference point against which the wakefield alternatives are calibrated.

For your tracking purposes this is tangential — it does not present new muon-collider physics, detector, or luminosity work. The takeaway is external context: it positions the muon collider’s 10 ab⁻¹ Higgs program as roughly equivalent in coupling sensitivity to a γγ wakefield option that sidesteps positron acceleration, and an order of magnitude behind a clean e⁺e⁻ wakefield collider per unit luminosity. Caveat flagged in the abstract itself: the wakefield conclusions hinge on measuring/calculating the luminosity spectra to high accuracy, given the beam-beam interactions — a caveat that does not apply symmetrically to the muon-collider benchmark. No numbers on individual couplings are given in the abstract.

arXiv:2607.16904 §

The muon collider: expected physics, technological solutions, and the prospect of a 21 km ring at the UNK site

L. V. Dudko

Single-author review (Dudko) that is 90% a consolidation of the existing IMCC/MuCol case, with one genuinely new element: a site-specific proposal to reuse the excavated-but-never-equipped 20,772 m UNK tunnel at Protvino as a muon-collider ring. The physics and technology sections are a competent restatement of known numbers (MuCol reference parameters; 0.75% on σ(H→bb̄) at 3 TeV full-sim; Higgs width pinned to (−0.41%,+2.1%) at 10 TeV via forward-μ ZZ-fusion tagging with |η|<6; δλ₃ ~4% at 10 TeV, ~1–1.5% at 30 TeV; thermal wino covered with ~70 fb⁻¹, higgsino needing ~8 ab⁻¹; MICE transverse-cooling demonstration; HTS/REBCO magnet roadmap). Nothing new there — treat it as a reference index.

The novel content is Section 4. Using the rigidity relation with R≈3306 m and a dipole packing factor calibrated to the IMCC 10 TeV lattice (f≈0.66; as-built UNK-II gives f≈0.59), the author maps arc fields onto √s: conventional NbTi/Nb₃Sn (7–8.3 T) → ~8–11 TeV, HTS (10–16 T) → ~12–21 TeV. The one physically interesting argument is the luminosity scaling: at fixed √s, ℒ ∝ B̄ ∝ 1/C, so the 20.8 km ring delivers only ≈0.55× the 11.4 km reference luminosity at 10 TeV — a deficit that closes only by running the fixed ring at the top of its energy range (B̄≈10.5 T at 21 TeV).

Caveats are stated plainly by the author: no CDR exists, the entire front end (driver, target, 6D cooling, acceleration) must still be built (and dominates cost), the reach table is pure extrapolation of published curves at assumed design luminosity, and the neutrino-flux constraint worsens with energy — the 20–60 m tunnel depth is shallower than IMCC dose studies assume, with decay-plane neutrinos surfacing 16–28 km out in populated regions. Also flagged: institutional (CERN–Russia cooperation suspended, IMCC closed to Russian labs). Bottom line for this track: no new physics or accelerator result, but a concrete tunnel-reuse scenario and a clean 1/C luminosity argument worth noting alongside the LHC/SPS reuse options.

arXiv:2607.15356 §

Multiboson Signatures of Doubly Charged Scalars at a Same-Sign Muon Collider

Mariana Frank et al.

Frank, Fuks, Majumdar & Senapati study a same-sign μ⁺μ⁺ collider (the μTRISTAN 2 TeV / 1 ab⁻¹ configuration) as a probe of Type-II seesaw doubly charged scalars in the large-triplet-vev regime (v_t ≃ 1 GeV), where Δ^{±±}→W^±W^± dominates (BR >90%) rather than the dilepton mode. Production is VBF-like pair production μ⁺μ⁺→μ⁺μ⁺Δ⁺⁺Δ⁻⁻ with two spectator muons, and they target the fully hadronic 4W channel giving a μ⁺μ⁺+8j final state (~20% combined hadronic BR). Combinatorics are resolved via a global χ² minimization over the 105 ways of forming four non-overlapping dijets from the 8 leading jets, with σ_W=12 GeV. Backgrounds (μ⁺μ⁺+2j/4j, μ⁺μ⁺W⁺W⁻) are driven to the ~1–8 ab level by the N_j≥8 requirement. A cut-and-count analysis with three nested “tight-W” regions gives 3.7–4.7σ for m_{Δ}=200–300 GeV, dropping below 2σ near 400 GeV — the fall driven by the steeply falling production cross section, not reconstruction. An XGBoost BDT (per-mass classifiers, kinematic + reconstructed-W + spectator-muon + multi-W-mass observables) extends the 2σ reach to 425–430 GeV, a few tens of GeV beyond the cut-based result.

For this topic, the concrete takeaway is a benchmark reach number for a genuinely same-sign (μ⁺μ⁺) machine in a decay regime where LHC diboson searches only reach ~350 GeV (pair) / ~230 GeV (associated) — so the improvement over LHC is modest but comes from a qualitatively different production mode and clean initial state. Caveats stated explicitly: no ME-to-PS matching/merging (backgrounds treated conservatively/independently), no beam-induced-background or dedicated detector study, and the LO Delphes treatment with a generic muon-collider card. The extra “missing-energy” BDT variables are acknowledged as event-balance proxies, not genuine invisible-particle handles. Overall the paper is fully and centrally about the muon-collider capability, not a tangential keyword match.

arXiv:2607.03775 §

Probing Fermion-Portal Scalar Dark Matter through Charged Vector-Like Fermions at Future Muon Colliders

Songshaptak De et al.

This study investigates a dark matter (DM) model featuring a real singlet scalar (\(s\)) and vector-like singlet/doublet charged fermions (\(E_S, F_D\)), where the DM is stabilized by a \(Z_2\) symmetry. By introducing Yukawa interactions (\(Y_f\)), the authors successfully move away from the minimal Higgs-portal scenario, which is currently under extreme pressure from LZ direct-detection limits. The model reproduces the observed relic density via \(t\)- and \(u\)-channel processes (\(s s \to \nu \ell \bar{\nu} \ell\)) and co-annihilations involving the new fermions.

The paper provides a detailed collider sensitivity analysis for the process \(\mu^+ \mu^- \to E_1^+ E_1^- \to e^+ e^- + \cancel{E}_T\). Using detector-level simulation (Delphes), the authors demonstrate that a 3 TeV muon collider (\(\mathcal{L}=1\) ab\(^{-1}\)) can reach a \(5\sigma\) discovery for charged fermion masses up to \(\sim 1.5\) TeV. Scaling to 10 TeV (\(\mathcal{L}=10\) ab\(^{-1}\)) extends this reach to \(\sim 2\) TeV, covering almost the entire parameter space favored by current relic density and direct-detection constraints.

The results are significant because they highlight the complementarity of a muon collider in probing “compressed” or weakly-coupled regions that the HL-LHC cannot reach, specifically for \(Z_2\)-odd states. A notable caveat is the reliance on the electron channel; the muon-associated Yukawa couplings are intentionally suppressed to evade \((g-2)_\mu\) and \(\mu \to e \gamma\) constraints, which justifies the focus on electron-rich final states but constrains the model’s flavor structure.
