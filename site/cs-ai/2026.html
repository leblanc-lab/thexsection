Correctness Conditioning as Selection in Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness Evaluation

Yueer Pan et al.

Watchlist hit (collider) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Restricting chain-of-thought (CoT) faithfulness evaluation to correctly answered instances changes the population being measured. We study this selection step in a seven-variable structural causal model and derive the exact observed gap \(\Delta = \operatorname{Cov}(A, C)/P(C=1)\). The model distinguishes difficulty confounding from shortcut-induced collider bias and states the conditional-independence assumption needed to express the gap through latent response propensities. A protocol review of 18 papers distinguishes answer-correctness restrictions from agreement-based selection, benchmark construction, and training-data filtering. We evaluate four sub-ceiling deployments on arithmetic tasks and observe positive selection gaps in all four. The primary GLM-5.3 run yields a +7.4 percentage-point gap (\(n = 1000\), \(p < 0.0001\)), and pooled Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel analysis gives OR = 1.70 (\(p < 10^{-6}\)). An assumption-free covariance decomposition assigns 42.8% of the GLM-5.3 gap to between-stratum variation in interventionable-operation count, leaving the majority unattributed. When counterfactual compliance is observable for every instance, evaluating all traces directly estimates population faithfulness without correctness filtering.

What the Primary Lund Plane Omits: A Model-Stitching Diagnosis of a Jet-Tagger Representation Gap

Saurabh Rai et al.

Watchlist hit (Lund plane, Cambridge/Aachen, jet taggers, quark/gluon, particle transformer) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Lund-plane and Particle Transformer (ParT) jet taggers can differ in performance because one representation is weaker, or because a weaker downstream computation reads an adequate one. Standalone performance cannot separate these. Model stitching can: it links the frozen early layers of one network to the frozen late layers of another through a single trained linear map. As a positive control we use an incomplete Lund representation, the primary declustering branch, whose deficit is known independently. Stitched into ParT’s downstream, it reaches a background rejection at least as much as its own downstream recovers, yet far below ParT’s background rejection: the deficit is representational, not computational. A hypergraph reader over splitting triplets does no better, ruling out higher-order correlations as the cause. The gap is invisible in AUC, which stays within 1\% of its target value, and appears only in background rejection at low signal efficiency. It localizes to 0.6\% of background jets, at and above the top mass, with more constituents but fewer primary splittings than typical background. Rebuilding the representation on the full Cambridge/Aachen declustering tree repairs the deficit as predicted: standalone rejection rises, closing 72\% of the gap. Masking nodes at inference shows the repaired network still relies almost entirely on the primary branch; the secondary splittings make the representation learnable rather than supplying information it reads. Along the primary branch, the widest-angle splitting, near the top and \(W\) opening angles, is worth nearly twice a random one. For quark/gluon tagging the primary branch is far less deficient, retaining 77.6\% of ParT’s rejection (46.9\% for top), and the full tree closes only 33\% of the gap (72\% for top).

RPSD: Retrieval-Privileged On-Policy Self-Distillation with Optimal Transport

Dongyue Li et al.

Watchlist hit (title: optimal transport) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Sequential recommendation predicts a user’s next interaction from historical behavior. Recent LLM-based methods recast this task as generative recommendation and increasingly adopt reinforcement learning such as GRPO for post-training. However, these methods suffer from two limitations, namely credit-blind optimization, where a sequence-level reward is shared across all generated tokens, and collaborative-blind supervision, where each training signal is derived from a single user and ignores behavioral patterns shared across similar users. To address both issues, we propose Retrieval-Privileged on-policy Self-Distillation (RPSD). For credit-blind optimization, we introduce on-policy self-distillation (OPSD), in which a teacher with privileged knowledge re-evaluates the student’s on-policy response, and the token-level confidence difference between the student and teacher reshapes the shared sequence-level advantage into token-specific credit. However, the privileged knowledge in vanilla OPSD is still derived from a single user, leaving collaborative-blind supervision unresolved. We therefore ground the teacher in collaborative behavioral evidence from similar users. Specifically, we use temporally aware optimal transport (OT) to compare interaction trajectories across users by jointly considering item similarity and temporal structure, and further apply diversity-aware selection to retain complementary trajectories as privileged evidence. In this way, on-policy self-distillation provides fine-grained token-level credit, while OT enriches the teacher with structured cross-user evidence. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to bring on-policy self-distillation to sequential recommendation and to use cross-user behavior as its privileged signal. Extensive experiments across multiple real-world datasets demonstrate that RPSD achieves state-of-the-art performance.

Trail-Weighted Influence Scores for Approximate Outcome Prediction in Bayesian Networks

Sahil Kumar Rajput et al.

Watchlist hit (collider, colliders) — original abstract; no AI summary.

We study trail-weighted pruning of evidence for Bayesian-network prediction. A traversal enumerates the active trails from each evidence node to the outcome, scores each trail by the product of its edge strengths, and drops evidence whose score falls below a threshold. For collider-free networks (chains and forks) over binary variables, we show that the trail score equals the exact correlation between a trail’s endpoints, that a chain and a fork with equal edge strengths have identical joint distributions, and that the pruned posterior is within a computable sum of per-trail terms of the exact posterior when each dropped trail lies in its own branch of the outcome node. We prove this bound, check it exhaustively on all small trees, and show that it fails when dropped trails share a branch. In simulations on random trees, thresholding trail scores gives 1.4 to 13 times lower error than random pruning of the same size. We also apply the score, as an unproven heuristic, to seven bnlearn networks with colliders and loops. Keeping the top half of the active evidence by trail score gives lower error than random pruning on all seven, but it is not clearly better than a mutual-information ranking, and scoring costs more than the inference it saves on six of the seven, so we show no speedup. Models trained on trail, graph and information features and tested on networks they never saw do not beat the trail score or mutual information, but trail features carry most of what they use and clearly outperform generic graph features. A bound for general networks is left to future work.

Representation Alignment from a Few Paired Data via Distilled Optimal Transport

Julien Kordes et al.

Watchlist hit (title: optimal transport) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Aligning two independently pretrained unimodal encoders typically demands millions of paired examples. In this paper, we study the regime where only \(N \sim 100\)–\(500\) pairs are available, while unpaired data on either side is abundant. Our method expresses every representation by its similarities to the \(N\) paired anchors, which places both modalities in a common coordinate system; matches the two point clouds with a single entropic optimal transport problem solved over the entire pool, whose marginal constraints counteract the hubness that defeats nearest-neighbor matching; and distills the resulting plan into two lightweight encoders, so that the correspondence becomes inductive. We additionally put this marginal constraint in relation with the one-step hub corrections of the bilingual lexicon induction literature. Pairing is required only for the anchors: the unpaired collections may be drawn independently from the two marginals, so that no item outside the anchor set needs to have both of its modalities observed. We evaluate on image-text and molecule-text alignment, where our method outperforms existing approaches at the same anchor budget on Flickr30k, MS-COCO and ChEBI-20.

Local Diagnostics of Learned Event Representations for Anomaly Detection at the HL-LHC

Donatella Genovese et al.

Watchlist hit (LHC, Large Hadron Collider, collider) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Anomaly detection, the identification of collision events that deviate from Standard Model expectations, is one of the most promising model-agnostic strategies for new-physics searches at the Large Hadron Collider. Searching for rare events in learned low-dimensional representations removes the need to commit to hand-crafted observables a priori, but sacrifices the immediate physical interpretation those observables provide. For anomaly detection to enter a discovery pipeline, flagged events must therefore be diagnosed locally, both to distinguish potential new physics from mismodeling or detector effects and to identify observables for targeted follow-up searches. We evaluate supervised and self-supervised neural embedding strategies on COLLIDE-2V, a 750-million-event dataset simulated under realistic High-Luminosity LHC conditions, using the reconstruction error of an autoencoder trained in the learned latent space as the anomaly score. Across the configurations and benchmark signals considered, supervised objectives, which exploit simulation process labels during training, generally outperform their self-supervised counterparts and, in their best configurations, improve on an autoencoder trained directly on kinematic features, raising the signal efficiency at 10% background acceptance on all held-out processes. We further introduce a local diagnostic pipeline that fits a Gaussian Mixture Model to the SM latent space, profiles each component through high-level observables without process labels, localizes the flagged events, and ranks observables by their local discriminating power. The ranking recovers the defining kinematics of the held-out processes, while correlating the anomaly score with the same observables quantifies how closely the score tracks them, revealing cases where the most discriminating observable is not the one the score follows most closely.

Uncovering and Fixing Collider Bias in Bayesian PINNs

Michael Obermayr et al.

Watchlist hit (collider) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Bayesian physics-informed neural networks (B-PINNs) are a popular framework for parameter and state inference from sparse or noisy observations. They are commonly formulated via a collider structure, in which physical and trajectory parameters are assumed to be a priori independent and become coupled through virtual likelihoods on differential-equation residuals that enforce physical consistency. We show that this modeling choice can induce severe systematic bias in the posterior over physical parameters: even when the prior is favorably centered on the ground-truth parameters, the resulting posterior can drift away and concentrate far from them. As a remedy, we advocate a hierarchical chain model in which physics generates trajectories, which in turn generate observations. The chain model does not suffer from this posterior bias, but it poses a harder, so-called doubly intractable, inference problem due to a physics-dependent normalization constant. This challenge can be resolved by discretizing the underlying stochastic dynamics, after which the chain posterior can be sampled exactly with particle MCMC. We identify two distinct mechanisms characterizing the collider bias, derive analytical approximations of their magnitudes, and establish diagnostic criteria for predicting when standard B-PINNs remain reliable. Experiments confirm the predicted bias and show that the chain formulation successfully avoids it.

Accurate but Not Humble: Evaluating Epistemic Humility in LLM Agents under Knowledge Conflict

Kaiser Sun et al.

Watchlist hit (Jingyu Zhang) — original abstract; no AI summary.

When retrieved evidence contradicts an agent’s prior beliefs, does it revise its answer, acknowledge uncertainty, or persist with an incorrect conclusion? Existing evaluations of agentic systems focus primarily on task success, offering limited insight into how agents handle such conflicts. We propose to evaluate agents on epistemic humility (EH): the agent’s willingness to recognize, act on, and communicate uncertainty during task execution. We operationalize EH through three trajectory-level behavioral dimensions: Identify, Solve, and Escalate (ISE). Through knowledge conflict, situations where the backbone language model’s parametric knowledge contradicts the evidence it encounters, or where two contextual sources disagree, we evaluate two conflict settings: (1) controlled conflict and (2) naturally occurring conflict during multi-step agentic execution, each paired with matched no-conflict controls. Evaluating four agents, we find that higher task accuracy does not necessarily correspond to greater epistemic humility: some high-accuracy configurations recognize conflicts during execution but do not communicate unresolved uncertainty in their incorrect final answers. Trajectory-level analysis further reveals that agents frequently detect conflicts in early steps of execution but fail to maintain or resolve them in later steps. Finally, we show that model-level interventions can improve EH, but often at the cost of task accuracy, suggesting that epistemic humility emerges from the interaction among the backbone model, agent harness, and evaluation environment.

Babak Barazandeh et al.

Watchlist hit (title: optimal transport) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Agents are deployed in applications from trip planners and stock trading to IT incident triage. In most cases, LLM agents work autonomously with minimal rule-based safeguarding, leading to cost and safety issues from irreversible actions. Recent works resolve this either by using a safeguard agent to monitor behavior or evaluating logs post-hoc. The first adds cost and latency to every step; the second delivers its verdict after the run, when tokens are burned and damage is done. WASD: Wasserstein-based Knowledge Distillation for Large Language Models

Byeonghu Na et al.

Watchlist hit (title: wasserstein) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Autoregressive large language models (LLMs) have rapidly advanced in capability, but their increasing scale comes with substantial computational and memory costs at inference time. Knowledge distillation (KD) offers a practical solution by transferring knowledge from a large teacher model to a smaller student model via alignment of discrete probability distributions. However, existing KD methods for LLMs primarily rely on divergences that evaluate discrepancies through probability values at each vocabulary index, without explicitly leveraging token-level semantic information. We propose Wasserstein-based knowledge distillation (WASD) for LLMs, which incorporates token-level semantic information via the Wasserstein-based distance with a cost matrix derived from token embeddings. Our results highlight the importance of semantic information encoded in the token space for effective distribution alignment in LLM distillation. LLM agents are increasingly capable of executing complex tasks and of recursively improving themselves on easy-to-verify objectives such as software engineering and mathematics. Bowen Han et al.

Destructive single-cell measurements provide unpaired population snapshots rather than observations of the same cells across conditions. We present \textbf{FlashSinkhorn~2} (FS2), a solver for squared-Euclidean cost on low-dimensional point clouds that solves large discrete EOT problems to a prescribed marginal residual on a single GPU by coupling two stages. A coarse stage solves on cell centroids, lifts the potentials to every point and, when a sampled marginal check rejects the lift, continues on the centroids, replacing most point-level updates. For reproducibility, we release an open-source implementation at https://github.com/ot-triton-lab/flash-sinkhorn

Sung Ho Jo et al.

Watchlist hit (title: optimal transport) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Machine learning models often suffer performance degradation under subpopulation shift, particularly when spurious correlations cause models to rely on shortcut features that fail to generalize across subgroups. A recent line of work mitigates this issue by using loss-based signals to identify informative samples, but these signals can become severely distorted under label noise: mislabeled samples may also incur large losses and contaminate subsequent reweighting or retraining. Despite its practical importance, this intersection remains largely underexplored. We propose POTER, a reweighting framework based on optimal transport that derives sample importance from the transport geometry between the training distribution and a reference distribution constructed from limited validation group annotations. Across standard benchmarks and noisy-label settings, POTER achieves state-of-the-art worst-group accuracy, including cases where label corruption is concentrated within minority subgroups.

Discrete Wasserstein Flows for One-Step Generative Modeling

Alessandro Micheli et al.

Watchlist hit (title: wasserstein) — original abstract; no AI summary.

We introduce a new framework for one-step generative modelling on finite state spaces. To extend drifting beyond continuous domains, we use discrete Wasserstein geometry to define a target-relative KL gradient flow over the transitions of a reversible Markov kernel. We realize this probability flow at the particle level through Markov jumps and amortize the resulting transport updates into a latent-conditioned generator, so that the iterative dynamics are required only during training while inference remains one-step. In a controlled setting where the underlying distributions and transport dynamics can be computed exactly, we verify KL dissipation, consistency between the particle dynamics and the probability flow, and the predicted numerical scaling. These results validate the basic construction and provide a foundation for scaling Discrete Drifting to structured discrete data.

Optimal Transport Meets Reinforcement Learning: A Survey

Yujie Zhu et al.

Watchlist hit (title: optimal transport) — original abstract; no AI summary.

Reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms frequently compare probability distributions, such as state visitation distributions induced by policies and experts, action distributions from learned policies and offline datasets, or transition distributions from learned models and environments. However, commonly used divergences may become ineffective when these distributions overlap weakly, which is frequently encountered in imitation learning, offline RL, and deployment under distribution shift. Optimal transport (OT) offers an alternative by measuring the cost of \emph{moving} probability mass from one distribution to another under a ground cost that encodes task geometry. This survey covers how OT is used inside RL objectives and algorithms.
