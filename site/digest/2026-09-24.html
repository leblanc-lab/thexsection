No discoveries, one careful fit

After three days of experimental spectroscopy, today brings no new data. The most useful paper is a reanalysis. Woodward et al. arXiv:2609.26965 fit MiniBooNE and MicroBooNE jointly within 3+1 using simulation-based inference, with all data-driven factors removed. The preference for a sterile neutrino drops to 2.7σ, and the two experiments disagree with each other at ≥2.5σ inside the model. Elsewhere, the day’s cosmology papers find BBN deuterium constraining supercooled phase transitions, and the LZ papers now mostly work around solar capture rather than fit the event.

Supercooling, baryons, and deuterium

This thread is new. Tanaka arXiv:2609.28348 finds that deuterium disfavors NANOGrav-compatible first-order transitions if the baryon asymmetry predates them. Deep dives

arXiv:2609.26965 — Tension between MiniBooNE and MicroBooNE within a 3+1 Sterile Neutrino Framework using Simulation-Based Inference (Julia P. Woodward (Massachusetts Institute of Technology) et al.)

With MiniBooNE’s f_π pion-flux correction removed, adding MicroBooNE BNB+NuMI data lowers the trials-calibrated 3+1 preference from ≥3.6σ (MiniBooNE alone) to 2.7σ. The two experiments sit in ≥2.5σ parameter-goodness-of-fit tension inside 3+1. The frequentist fit uses an SBI likelihood-ratio estimator (DNRE) with a normalizing-flow posterior (SNPE-C) to locate the best fit. The new ingredient is an SBI analogue of the Maltoni–Schwetz PG statistic, built from Δχ² differences relative to null. With it, 48,000 null trials become affordable.

The Wilks’ comparison shows why calibration matters. Without f_π, Wilks gives 7.1σ for MiniBooNE, 5.2σ for the joint fit, and 4.4σ tension. With f_π kept, the SBI joint preference drops to 1.8σ, but the tension rises to ≥3.2σ. In every f_π combination the BNB-only Wilks tension stays above 2σ, so the disagreement is not an artifact of inconsistent flux corrections.

Caveats: - Trial cap. 48k trials cap resolvable significance at ~4.25σ, so anything above 3.5σ is quoted as a lower bound. - Reconstructed NuMI prediction. MicroBooNE has released no event-level MC. The authors rebuilt the NuMI prediction from g4numi/PPFX/GENIE with a tuned approximate detector response (10-knot efficiencies per sample). Along the way they fixed a decay-at-rest flux bug worth up to 10% below 250 MeV. - Suspicious goodness of fit. MicroBooNE’s own χ²/dof of 86.62/179 is anomalously low. - Unexplained ν_μ excess. The low-energy ν_μ excess seen in both detectors is not understood, and the authors warn that quantitative comparisons should be read with care.

arXiv:2609.28457 — Sterile Neutrino Dark Matter Cries for GeV Heavy Neutral Leptons (Marco Drewes et al.)

This is the first νMSM scan using full quantum kinetic equations from baryogenesis through dark-matter production. The kinetics include chirality-flip effects, lepton-asymmetry back-reaction, and, decisively, the charge-odd (MSW-like) part of the HNL Hamiltonian. At the M₁ = 7 keV benchmark, the requirement to produce the QCD-epoch lepton asymmetry needed for Shi–Fuller conversion confines the HNL pair to a closed island.

Viable mass ranges: - Normal ordering (NO): the resonant-decay mechanism survives for 4 ≲ M ≲ 50 GeV. The 50 GeV upper bound is new. - Ignition mechanism (new): a self-amplifying MSW-driven instability that boosts μ_α by ~3 orders of magnitude, to |μ_α| ≃ 10⁻³. It reopens M < 5 GeV down to just above the kaon mass. - Inverted ordering (IO): only 0.8–7 GeV survives.

Experimental reach: - In the meson-accessible range, U² sits at least an order of magnitude above the seesaw floor. - SHiP covers the entire region below 2 GeV and much of 2–5 GeV. HL-LHC and Tera-Z machines cover the rest. - The predicted ΔM_phys adds a DM-specific test. For NO, HNL oscillation lengths can fall inside the SHiP decay volume; for IO, the pair looks Dirac-like.

Caveats: - A single DM mass is studied. - The scan uses momentum-averaged equations and neglects asymmetry depletion by N₁ production; both are checked only on the island boundaries. - Rates come from Fermi theory with free quarks, so the mass edges are uncertain below ~500 MeV and above ~50 GeV. - Structure-formation bounds on the computed spectra are deferred.

arXiv:2609.28348 — Big Bang Nucleosynthesis as a Probe of First-Order Phase Transitions (Masanori Tanaka)

Tanaka adds the reheating (α_n) dependence to BBN bounds on baryon inhomogeneity from supercooled transitions. The local reheating map is nonlinear, the spectral shape comes from the finite-bubble transition-time power spectrum, and proton/neutron diffusion is included. The result is imposed through the deuterium bound ε_BBN < 0.28 (1σ).

Results: - No bound exists for α_n < 0.1 with an optimistic k_cut = ∞, or for α_n < 0.5 with a conservative cut at the mean bubble separation. - For large α_n and 0.5 < T_n < 100 GeV, the disfavored region is β/H_n ≲ 5–6 (conservative) or ≲ 10–13 (optimistic). - Above ~10² GeV, diffusion weakens the bound.

NANOGrav: the NANOGrav-compatible region survives the conservative cut. It is largely disfavored only if the spectrum holds at sub-bubble scales, though much of it is still excluded keeping just modes with k < 2k_th. Evading the bound requires baryogenesis after reheating, or during the transition with baryon production tracking local entropy injection. That is the basis for the digest’s “baryogenesis timing” claim.

Caveats: - The result is 1σ only. - The baryon power spectrum is an admitted effective parametrization, and the bound is highly sensitive to the cutoff. - It assumes a pure exponential nucleation rate and energy release into the SM sector. - The paper is internally loose on bubble-wall baryogenesis. The abstract allows it if production tracks entropy, while Sec. 3 says the bound is “not evaded in general.” - A concurrent linearized analysis (Elahi et al.) finds β/H ≳ 10.3 α/(1+α), which Tanaka calls qualitatively consistent.
