Toponium, confirmed

The day’s anchor is unambiguous: CMS observes an excess at the \(t\bar t\) production threshold in the single-lepton channel at 6.1σ (arXiv:2608.19546), a 5.1±0.9 pb cross section consistent with a color-singlet pseudoscalar toponium — and an independent confirmation of the dilepton-channel excess. A theory interpretation lands the same day. Around it, CMS also posts its first global top-FCNC EFT fit, the flavor-numerology genre resurfaces, and Ke et al. offer a clean way to read hadronization off conserved-current flows. A collider-heavy day after a week that ran on flavor and heavy ions.

Themes

The \(t\bar t\) threshold excess, now in single leptons

arXiv:2608.19546 uses relative \(t\bar t\) velocity plus two spin/parity-sensitive angular observables to pin the excess to a simplified NRQCD pseudoscalar toponium, cross-checking the dilepton result in a statistically independent channel. Matsuoka arXiv:2608.19651 takes the elementary-scalar reading: identify the enhancement with a top-philic Type-III 2HDM CP-odd Higgs, and ask whether the same region drives electroweak baryogenesis — finding explicit CP violation scheme-dependent and no viable transitional branch. Two readings of one bump, worth watching as more channels report.

CMS in the top-EFT sector

arXiv:2608.19547 is the first global top-FCNC search constraining all 24 relevant SMEFT operators simultaneously in one fit — the most stringent limits to date on two-quark–two-lepton FCNC decays. This continues the drift the memory has tracked toward EFT as the default collider language (last seen in the 08-20 aTGC merged-jet bounds). The tooling backbone arrives alongside: Aebischer et al. arXiv:2608.19316 give basis-independent two-loop current–current renormalization for arbitrary dimension-six four-fermion operators, a step toward complete NLO anomalous dimensions for general EFTs.

Flavor-mixing numerology returns

The genre that dominated early in the week (the Barger commensurate-mixing papers) is back. Rousselle arXiv:2608.19277 derives the Koide relation from a \(U(3)\to U(1)\times\mathbb{Z}_3\) topological condensation locking the flavor angle at \(-2/9\); Sharma et al. arXiv:2608.19691 fix a rigid TBM deformation to the Cabibbo angle, predicting \(\theta_{23}=49.19^\circ\) and a near-\(\pi\) Dirac phase with sharp falsifiability. Parametrization, not dynamics — but each stakes out testable numbers.

Hadronization from conserved currents

Ke et al. arXiv:2608.19708 show color triality forces jet charge, baryon-number, and strangeness flows to obey a Gell-Mann–Nishijima relation, with the net jet baryon number directly measuring the diquark-to-quark vacuum production ratio. This shares the logic of the LEP one-point charge correlator that ran through last week — new precision observables built from conserved currents rather than jet algorithms.

Deep dives

arXiv:2608.19546 — Observation of an excess at the top quark pair production threshold in the single-lepton channel (CMS Collaboration)

(Abstract only available.) CMS reports a \(6.1\sigma\) excess near the \(t\bar t\) production threshold in single-lepton (\(e/\mu\) + jets) final states using the full Run 2 dataset (138 fb⁻¹ at 13 TeV). The analysis exploits the relative top–antitop velocity plus two angular observables sensitive to the parity and spin of the \(t\bar t\) system, comparing to an NNLO QCD baseline. The excess maps onto an observed total cross section of \(5.1\pm0.9\) pb and is consistent with a simplified NRQCD-motivated color-singlet pseudoscalar toponium state.

The significance here matters as much as the central value: the single-lepton channel is statistically independent of the dilepton measurement that first flagged the threshold enhancement, so this constitutes a genuine confirmation rather than a re-analysis of the same events. With two channels now converging on the same \(\sim\)5 pb, pseudoscalar-consistent signal, the “toponium” reading moves from a single anomaly toward a coherent picture — and sets up the competing elementary-scalar interpretation (Matsuoka’s top-philic 2HDM CP-odd Higgs) as the live alternative to test. The caveat is intrinsic to the modeling: the “toponium” is extracted against a fixed NNLO \(t\bar t\) prediction and fit to a simplified single-state NRQCD template, so the inferred cross section and its pseudoscalar identification are only as robust as that threshold-region theory baseline.

arXiv:2608.19547 — Search for top quark FCNC in multilepton final states using an EFT approach (CMS Collaboration)

CMS performs the first global top-FCNC search in which all 24 relevant dimension-six SMEFT Wilson coefficients (12 for \(tu\), 12 for \(tc\)) are constrained simultaneously in one likelihood, using 138 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV data in same-sign dilepton and trilepton final states. Signal is probed in both single-top production and \(t\bar t\) decay via Higgs-, \(Z\)-, photon-, gluon-, and directly lepton-pair-mediated FCNC vertices; BDT discriminants (binary for \(2\ell\)ss, multiclass jigsaw-reconstructed for \(3\ell\)) feed a maximum-likelihood fit where signal yields are quadratic polynomials in the WCs. No excess is seen; 68% and 95% intervals are set on all 24 coefficients and translated to branching-fraction limits.

The headline is methodological and archival: this is the first simultaneous treatment of the complete FCNC operator set — including the first-ever direct constraints on two-quark–two-lepton FCNC operators — rather than the usual single-mediator-at-a-time approach that ignores interference. The resulting \(\mathcal{B}(t\to Hq)\) and \(\mathcal{B}(t\to Zq)\) bounds are quoted as similar to or more restrictive than prior anomalous-coupling results, and the framework is explicitly pitched as the reference for future LHC combinations. This fits the digest’s EFT-as-default-language theme. Caveats are candid: chiral partners are assumed equal-strength and lepton flavors share common WCs to keep the fit tractable; gluon (\(c_{tG}\)) and photon (\(c_{tA}\)) operators are included only “for completeness” given the multilepton channel’s weak sensitivity; and a 50% uncertainty on \(t\bar tW\) reflects a known modeling tension.

arXiv:2608.19708 — Tracing Vacuum Hadronization with Conserved Currents (Weiyao Ke et al.)

Ke, Li, Li, Liu, and Shao argue that color triality — the requirement that an anti-fundamental Wilson-line endpoint be screened by matter of conjugate triality — forces the net electric-charge, baryon-number, and strangeness flows into a jet (or celestial region) to satisfy a jet-level Gell-Mann–Nishijima relation, \(\langle Q_{\rm flow}\rangle_f \simeq I_{3,f} + \tfrac12(\langle S_{\rm flow}\rangle_f + \langle B_{\rm flow}\rangle_f)\). Because these are moments of conserved currents, they are IR-safe and renormalization-scale independent at leading power: any shift from the initiating parton’s charge is purely nonperturbative “defect-charge” screening. The net jet baryon number directly reads out the diquark-to-quark vacuum ratio, \(\langle B_{\rm flow}\rangle_f \simeq r_{qq}/(1+r_{qq})\), and the strangeness flow encodes the strange-to-light ratio \(r_s\).

Pythia 8.3 (Lund string) and Herwig 7.3 (cluster) validate the relations in \(e^+e^-\) dijets across \(E_{\rm CM}=50\)–1000 GeV: parton-level flows recover \(Q_f\); hadron-level flows shift but obey the Gell-Mann–Nishijima diagonal in both generators. Scanning probStoUD recovers \(r_s\approx\) the input value across three independent slope projections; scanning probQQtoQ gives \(\langle B_{\rm flow}\rangle\) largely independent of the strange parameter, as predicted. This is the conserved-current-observable program (cf. the DELPHI one-point charge correlator) applied to hadronization microphysics, offering a flavor-resolved probe complementary to inclusive yield fits. Honest caveats: the extraction rests on a minimal one-quark/anti-diquark screening truncation (validated only to \(\sim\)9% by the event-level \(r_{topology}\) check), assumes light-flavor symmetry and independent flavor sampling, and the extracted \(r_{qq}\) deliberately does not equal probQQtoQ because of the state-dependent fragmentation history. Everything so far is generator-level; experimental tests are left to future work.

Citation radar

This week’s radar catches two very different flavors of “quietly essential” physics. One is a theory proposal riding the surging quantum-information-at-colliders wave; the other is the kind of infrastructure measurement that underwrites nearly every cross section the LHC produces. Both are accumulating citations fast for reasons that say something about the field’s current priorities: extending quantum-correlation studies to new systems, and pinning down the reference numbers everyone builds on.

arXiv:2606.30759 — Quantum Information of Photon Pairs at Lepton Colliders (Kun Cheng et al.)

Cheng, Han, Li, and Yan extend the collider quantum-information program to photon pairs, a system that pioneered entanglement and Bell tests at low energy but had not been tackled at high energy. Their trick is to exploit near-on-shell photon conversion (γ*→ℓ⁺ℓ⁻): in the small-virtuality limit the conversion factorizes from the hard process and the two transverse photon polarizations form an effective qubit, with linear polarization reconstructed from the dilepton azimuthal distributions. Using existing Belle data they project a 7.4σ CHSH violation, plus measurements of quantum discord (5.6%) and nonstabilizerness/magic (1.6%), and demonstrate consistency with the kinematic approach for virtual photons.

At ~7 citations in about a month, the pace reflects a crowded, fast-moving subfield rather than a singular result—the reference list itself is dominated by 2025–2026 quantum-information-at-colliders papers, many by overlapping author groups. The appeal here is a concrete, data-ready proposal in a new channel (photons) with a clean qubit reduction that the authors note extends to h→γγ and other spin-1 systems.

arXiv:2606.26832 — Precision luminosity measurement in proton-proton collisions at √s = 13 TeV with the CMS detector (Arzunik Gevorgyan et al.)

This is CMS’s legacy Run 2 luminosity measurement, delivering 0.73% precision on the full 2015–2018 pp data set (140.22 ± 1.02 fb⁻¹)—described as the most precise ever at a bunched-beam hadron collider. The paper details the van der Meer calibration machinery and a battery of new refinements: residual orbit-drift corrections from BPM data, a two-step tracker-to-nominal length-scale calibration, an improved luminous-region treatment of transverse nonfactorization, better beam-beam modeling, and combination of four independently calibrated luminometers (HFET, HFOC, PLT, PCC). Long-term stability is validated against the Z→μμ rate, with the extracted σ_fid = 708 ± 12 pb consistent with theory.

The citation logic here is structural, not provocative: luminosity is the normalization for every cross section and, as the paper itself notes, the dominant systematic in several CMS measurements. At ~5 citations in six weeks, it is on track to become the standard reference that Run 2 CMS analyses cite for their luminosity uncertainty—the number everyone downstream simply has to quote. Its sub-percent milestone also sets the baseline going into HL-LHC.
