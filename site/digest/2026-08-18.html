An old dataset, a new observable

The most distinctive work today is a three-paper package from Zhang et al. proposing and measuring the one-point charge correlator — a jet-free reformulation of the electroweak forward-backward asymmetry — extracted from archival DELPHI LEP-1 open data (arXiv:2608.14779, arXiv:2608.15357, arXiv:2608.15367). Around it sits a heavy hadron-spectroscopy load, from a lattice continuum-limit exotic-\(B_s\) result to Dudek’s review of gluonic excitations, and the usual dark-matter/axion spread. The day’s wild card is Gharibyan et al. arXiv:2608.14703, claiming evidence for parity violation in gravitational fields from HERA Compton-polarimeter spectra — an extraordinary claim that will draw extraordinary scrutiny.

Themes

A new electroweak observable from LEP open data

Zhang et al. formulate the charge flux of hadronic \(Z\) decays as a parity-odd \(\sin(2\theta)\) modulation measured directly against the beam axis, with no jet charge or reconstructed quark direction (arXiv:2608.14779 the theory Letter, arXiv:2608.15357 the first measurement, arXiv:2608.15367 the analysis note). The measurement, from just 61 pb⁻¹ of DELPHI data, agrees with PYTHIA and is pitched explicitly at the longstanding hadronic-asymmetry tensions. Nothing in recent memory anticipated this — it’s a genuinely new handle on precision EW, and the archival-data route is itself the point.

Deep dives

arXiv:2608.15357 — First measurement of the one-point charge correlator in \(e^+e^-\) collisions at \(\sqrt{s}=91.2\) GeV with DELPHI Open Data (Jingyu Zhang et al.)

Zhang et al. deliver the first-ever measurement of the one-point charge correlator — the net electric-charge flux \(\mathcal{Q}(\theta)\) into the detector as a function of polar angle relative to the \(e^-\) beam — from 61 pb⁻¹ of archival DELPHI Open Data (46 pb⁻¹ in 1994, 15 pb⁻¹ in 1995). This is a jet-free, hemisphere-free, IRC-safe reformulation of the hadronic forward-backward asymmetry: the parity-odd \(\cos\theta\) coupling structure shows up directly as a \(\sin(2\theta)\) modulation in the charge-weighted track density (tracks with \(p_T>2\) GeV, \(20°\le\theta\le160°\)). Detector effects are handled in two stages — a PYTHIA 8.3-based correction for track-finding, fakes and charge misID, then a data-driven tag-and-probe measurement of the residual data–MC charge-misreconstruction bias in \(Z\to\tau^+\tau^-\) events, where charge conservation fixes the probe’s true sign. The fully corrected result exhibits the expected \(\sin(2\theta)\) shape, negative in the forward hemisphere, crossing zero at 90°, and agrees with PYTHIA 8.3 within uncertainties.

Why it matters: this establishes experimental viability of a genuinely new precision-EW handle, one whose relation to the \(Z\) couplings is not mediated by jet-charge model dependence — the very ambiguity blamed for the persistent \(A_{FB}^b\) tension. The construction extends to heavy-flavor currents where tensions are largest, and prefigures Tera-\(Z\) programs at FCC-ee/CEPC. The honest caveat is prominent: this is a proof-of-principle, not a competitive extraction. Systematics (27.6%/35.6% at \(\theta=55°/125°\)) dominate over statistics and are set by the τ control-sample size; the parity-odd bias component is bounded, not measured to vanish. Sagitta and photon-conversion calibrations are the named next steps.

arXiv:2608.14703 — Evidence for parity violation in gravitational fields (V. Gharibyan et al.)

Gharibyan et al. analyze 314,896 Compton spectra from the HERA transverse polarimeter (2004–2007), tagged by 514.5 nm laser helicity, and report a laser-helicity-dependent asymmetry in the Compton-edge energy (\(\omega_{max}=13.9\) GeV) of \(A=(4.89\pm0.52)\times10^{-5}\), claimed at \(9\sigma\). Within a phenomenological framework where the gravitational coupling acquires a helicity-dependent term \(G_s=G+(\mathbf{s}\cdot\mathbf{P}/P)\Delta G_{\mathcal P}\), the modified energy-momentum relation shifts \(\omega_{max}\) oppositely for the two helicities, and the \(\gamma^2\) amplification factor sets the sensitivity. They translate the asymmetry into a helicity-dependent gravitational-potential difference \(\Delta U_{\mathcal P}=(1.71\pm0.036\pm0.187)\times10^{-14}\), i.e. \(\Delta G_{\mathcal P}/G=(5.7\pm0.6)\times10^{-10}\) using summed local potentials (dominated by the Virgo Supercluster). The signal is isotropic in right ascension (constant fit checked against 64 bins), which they argue excludes Lorentz-violating vector theories, and they estimate instrumental false asymmetries from transverse/longitudinal beam polarization and linear-light contamination to be sub-dominant.

Why it matters: if real, this would be the first high-energy test of a discrete symmetry in gravity showing violation, with speculative implications for galactic rotation curves via a chiral-gravity shift of the 21 cm line — potentially bearing on dark matter. But this is an extraordinary claim resting on a \(5\times10^{-5}\) edge shift in a polarimeter never built to measure absolute energy asymmetries. The authors themselves flag “hidden systematic factors”: helicity-dependent detector gain (estimated opposite-sign from bremsstrahlung fits, but uncorrected due to unknown beam offsets on an inhomogeneous calorimeter) and helicity-correlated laser spatial jitter convolved with the lepton phase space. The digest’s “read skeptically” is well-earned.

arXiv:2608.16875 — Truncation of the Radial Ladder in Heavy Quarkonia (Wen-Xuan Zhang et al.)

Zhang, Luo and Liu ask whether the radial excitation ladder of quarkonia terminates, solving a screened relativized Godfrey–Isgur Hamiltonian for \(c\bar c\) and \(b\bar b\) via the Gaussian expansion method (\(N_{max}=20\), \(r_{max}=30\) fm) up to \(n=14\). Screened confinement replaces the linear term’s unbounded rise with a plateau \(M_\infty=m_1+m_2+b/\mu+c\), giving limiting masses of 4.74 GeV (\(c\bar c\)) and 11.67 GeV (\(b\bar b\)). Crucially, while masses saturate — adjacent gaps dropping below 10 MeV — RMS radii keep diverging to \(\sim\)10–50 fm, an order of magnitude past the confinement scale. This “mass–radius decoupling” defines a breakdown of the \(q\bar q\) picture. A threshold criterion (min gap \(<\)10 MeV with radius increments \(>\)3/2 fm), corroborated by CUSUM/two-segment changepoint analysis of \(\log(\Delta R/\Delta M)\) and a resolvability index \(\chi_n=\Delta M_n R_n\) collapsing below unity, locates operational upper limits at \(n\approx8\)–10 for charmonium (\(D\)-waves first, then \(P\), then \(S\)) and \(n\approx12\)–13 for bottomonium. Staged \(\chi^2\) fits give RMS mass residuals of 34/24 MeV (10/4 MeV for low-lying states), with the charmonium screening length \(\mu^{-1}\approx1.4\) fm inside the lattice string-breaking range.

Why it matters: this is the first quantitative answer to a long-standing spectroscopy question, and it is directly testable — no new charmonia should appear above \(\sim\)4.7 GeV, and BESIII/Belle II/LHCb can probe this. Caveats are candidly stated: high-\(n\) bottomonium shows round-off artifacts; the thresholds are empirical; and the truncation value is model-input-dependent (alternative fits give 4.61–4.83 GeV). Formal parameter errors are not quoted. The manuscript was substantially AI-drafted (Grok, GPT-5.4), per the authors’ declaration.
