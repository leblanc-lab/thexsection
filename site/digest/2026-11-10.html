LEP’s neutrino count, recounted

Madison arXiv:2611.12265 rebuilds LEP beam–beam effects in a new framework, FeeBL, and finds a ~2% bias in the published beam-deflection simulations behind the Bhabha luminosity.

  • The correction: \(N_\nu=2.9965\pm0.0040\), against \(2.9963\pm0.0074\). The central value barely moves and the error nearly halves.
  • The condition: the smaller error holds only under an assumed common rescaling of experiment-specific uncertainties, which the author says needs further scrutiny.
  • FCC-ee: monochromatizing toward s-channel Higgs would need ~10 m of horizontal dispersion. That is a sobering number for last week’s FCC-ee-as-arbiter thread.

The rest of the day is spread thin: lattice nucleon structure, light-ion quenching, an 11σ strange vector, and several papers that say exactly where their own fits stop being trustworthy.

Themes

Fits that state their limits

  • Lattice estimators. Moerschbacher et al. arXiv:2611.10773 find that covariance-optimized background-field estimators gain 1–2% in precision. Their weights, however, carry 10³–10⁴× more leverage on omitted response modes than fixed stencils do.
  • Glueball sum rules. Kang et al. arXiv:2611.12073 separate “a pole fits” from “this region is required.” For the \(0^{-+}\) current, the band containing X(2370) can be empty while a broader band must carry ~44% of the spectrum.
  • \(t\bar tj\) resummation. Chargeishvili arXiv:2611.11600 corrects the \(t\bar tj\) soft anomalous dimensions, then keeps NLO as his central prediction because his own NNLL kernel fails beyond 100–200 GeV of emitted energy.

This is last week’s identifiability-audit habit (Serao’s blind directions, Shen’s magic measures), now spreading into lattice and perturbative QCD.

One QGP, oxygen to lead?

Karmakar et al. arXiv:2611.12404 run DREENA with a single parameter set across O, Ne, Xe and Pb and reproduce the observed suppression ordering. They predict that \(v_2^{\rm SP}/(1-R_{AA})\) is nearly flat in \(p_\perp\) for light ions, which is a clean test.

Yang et al. arXiv:2611.11347 describe φ and Ω \(v_2\) by recombination from 7.7 GeV to 5.02 TeV. At LHC energies, though, the extracted strange-quark \(v_2\) exceeds the NCQ-scaled value. That is worth remembering before reading ALICE’s O–O φ result (10-28) through simple scaling.

Strange vector, compact \(T_{cc}\)

BESIII arXiv:2611.11328 observes a \(1^-\) \(K_1^*(1870)\) in \(K\eta'\) at 11.0σ, with Γ≈167 MeV, and pitches it as input for hybrid searches. Peng et al. arXiv:2611.11472 fix the contact interaction from the \(T_{cc}\) pole and find the \(X(3872)\) comes out 5–12 MeV too deeply bound. Their Feshbach analysis instead reads \(T_{cc}\) as mixing with a compact \(cc\bar u\bar d\) state.

Nucleon structure, lattice-anchored

  • Charges. Alexandrou et al. arXiv:2611.10816 get \(g_A^{u-d}=1.263(34)(26)\) from four spacings at physical masses, including disconnected isoscalar pieces.
  • PDFs. Gao et al. arXiv:2611.12730 find the isovector PDF matches phenomenology for \(0.25\lesssim|x|\lesssim0.75\). For the first time, the imaginary part agrees too.
  • Transversity. Dong et al. arXiv:2611.12745 add \(x\)-dependent lattice transversity to a dihadron fit, and the down-valence distribution moves past the Soffer bound.

Worth a look

  • arXiv:2611.12265 (Madison): A legacy precision number re-examined with new code. Read the uncertainty argument before quoting ±0.0040.
  • arXiv:2611.11328 (BESIII): A new strange \(1^-\) at 11σ, in a channel built for hybrid hunting.
  • arXiv:2611.12774 (Takeuchi et al.): Broadband receivers instead of a cavity give \(\chi<(3.1\)–\(17)\times10^{-12}\) over 18–38.5 GHz. That is more than 10× better over most of 74–110 μeV, plus first direct limits up to 159 μeV.
  • arXiv:2611.10773 (Moerschbacher et al.): Exact decuplet ratios serve as built-in controls. The correlated fit misses them by 3–17%, while the fixed stencils pass, so precision and robustness are separate axes.
  • arXiv:2611.10115 (Fei et al.): A training-free greedy search recovers Parke–Taylor from all 1024 Feynman-diagram forms. It generally beats published ML simplifiers on solve rate, at 0.02–0.3 s per case with finite-field checks.

Deep dives

arXiv:2611.12265 — Beam-beam effects on measurements at electron-positron colliders: LEP neutrino counting and Higgs factories (Brendon Madison)

The bias is real but small in \(N_\nu\); the error-bar argument is the real claim. FeeBL, a Guinea-Pig++ descendant, reproduces the analytic field-integration Bhabha deflections at LEP: inner 13.12±0.08 vs 13.09 μrad, outer 11.49±0.06 vs 11.49 μrad. The published Guinea-Pig values sit 2.2±0.5% and 2.6±0.6% low. This moves the acceptance correction from −0.10425% to −0.10671% and shifts \(N_\nu\) by only +0.0002. The author cannot identify the origin of the deficit without the original settings.

The error-bar change is a separate, statistical argument. The four LEP determinations scatter less than their quoted errors allow, with a χ² lower-tail probability of 2.05%. Rescaling only the experiment-specific uncertainties by a common factor gives σ = 0.00401, with a 68% interval of [0.00391, 0.00485]. The paper explicitly says this needs each experiment’s uncertainty budget to be redone before anyone can defend quoting it. The suspected culprit is the \(\sigma^0_{\rm had}\)–\(R_\ell\) correlation.

The paper also does two other things: - Beam-pair fixes. It corrects implementation defects in Guinea-Pig++ v1.2.2 pair production. The Born normalization is 1.319% low, small-angle sampling is truncated, and virtual-virtual Landau–Lifshitz collisions are dropped. - Monochromatization. Opposite horizontal dispersion narrows the ILC/LCF and HALHF peaks about 2× at constant luminosity. It reaches sub-MeV FWHM, but only at about 0.07% of nominal luminosity. FCC-ee at |D| = 175 mm is still at 57 MeV FWHM, roughly 14× wider than 4 MeV.

The ~10 m figure for FCC-ee is an extrapolation from a rigid-Gaussian model. That model already misses the FCC-ee simulation by 55% in luminosity and 22% in width. Convergence is explicitly not claimed anywhere in the paper.

arXiv:2611.11328 — Observation of a strange \(J^P=1^{-}\) resonance \(K^{*}_1(1870)\) in \(\chi_{c1}\to K^+K^-\eta^{\prime}\) decays (BESIII Collaboration et al.)

A new \(1^-\) state appears in \(K\eta'\) in BESIII’s ψ(2S)→γχ\(_{c1}\) data. The sample is 2.71×10⁹ ψ(2S), with η′ reconstructed in both ππγ and ππη. The state is required beyond the PDG-based amplitude set, with M = 1869.0±14.6±18.6 MeV and Γ = 167.3±34.0±24.2 MeV. Its significance is 11.0σ (statistical) for \(1^-\), against 3.2σ for \(0^+\) and 3.7σ for \(2^+\). The product branching fraction is (1.48±0.30±0.33)×10⁻⁵, and B(χ\(_{c1}\)→K⁺K⁻η′) = (7.90±0.48)×10⁻⁴ supersedes the earlier BESIII value.

Why it matters: this is the strangeness slot of the \(1^{-(+)}\) nonet that the π₁(1600) and η₁(1855) candidates point to. One scenario predicts a strange hybrid at 1.83–1.90 GeV that couples to \(K\eta'\). Quark-model \(3^3S_1\) expectations sit near 1.95 GeV with \(K\eta'\) suppressed, or else are implausibly broad.

Caveats: - The quoted significance is statistical only. - Several neighbours are fixed to PDG values, including K(1680) at 1718/320 MeV, so correlations with the new \(1^-\) are only partly probed. - \(J^P=1^-\) alone cannot separate a hybrid from a conventional excited K. The collaboration says further study is needed.

arXiv:2611.12774 — Search for Dark Photon Dark Matter at 74–159 μeV/c² Using Broadband Cryogenic Receivers (Hiroki Takeuchi et al.)

No signal is seen, giving χ < (3.1–17)×10⁻¹² at 95% CL over 99.8% of 18–38.5 GHz. The setup is a metal plate viewed by cryogenic K- and Ka-band horns, with system temperatures of about 40–120 K. The readout is dSpec, a 4 GHz, 15.6 kHz-bin spectrometer that gives 2000× more integration per bin than the collaboration’s previous spectrum analyzer, for 4.8×10⁵ s per bin. The largest local excess is 4.96σ at 37.585 GHz, which becomes a global p = 0.38 once the look-elsewhere effect is included (\(N_{\rm eff}\) = 1.94×10⁶).

Why it matters: the result improves the collaboration’s own limits more than 10× over most of 74–110 μeV. It also gives the first direct limits over most of 110–159 μeV, about two orders below cosmological bounds. That is the mass range where inflationary DPDM points, and resonant searches have to tune step by step to cover it.

Caveats: - The limits assume DPDM is all of the local dark matter (ρ = 0.39 GeV/cm³) with randomly oriented polarization in a single-polarization receiver. - Coherent conversion from other metal surfaces has an unknown phase. \(A_{\rm eff}\) is therefore set to its minimum over that phase, which costs up to 44% in area. - Window geometry contributes up to about 25% systematic uncertainty on χ. - Clock-harmonic spurs are excluded. In those excluded intervals the conservative limit degrades to 3.8×10⁻¹⁰.
