LHCb’s light beauty-strange surprise

The day’s weight sits with the experiments, and specifically with spectroscopy. LHCb reports a new beauty-strange resonance at above 7σ arXiv:2609.25178 — unexpectedly light and narrow, “a substantial mass deficit” versus the quark model, billed as the first nonconventional single-beauty hadron. BESIII adds the heaviest light meson observed to date, the pseudoscalar η(2600) arXiv:2609.25158, and pins the Ω_b⁻ lifetime to twice the prior precision arXiv:2609.25370. This follows straight on from Sunday’s \(\Omega_{cc}^+\) discovery — the experiments are filling in (and now breaking out of) the hadron spectrum at a steady clip. Underneath, the collider-QI program and the long-running LZ saga both keep moving, and a large instrumentation cluster lands.

Collider quantum information

The entanglement-to-magic program the season tracked reaches its strongest form yet. Afik et al. arXiv:2609.25214 argue \(\tau^+\tau^-\) from \(e^+e^-\) at Belle II and FCC-ee can certify Nonlocal Advantage of Quantum Coherence — the strongest quantum correlation studied at colliders — with a single dedicated witness, and estimate the required precision (~1% at Belle II). Del Gratta et al. arXiv:2609.25217 supply the sober counterpoint: standard two-qutrit tomography of \(H\to4\ell\) can return a non-positive-semidefinite operator at NLO, though a robust concurrence lower bound survives. Two sides of the same maturing toolkit.

LZ, now an elastic form-factor story

The register shifted again. After Monday’s confining-sector readings, today’s crop explains the 248 keV recoil elastically, via composite form factors rather than inelastic splittings. Jung et al. arXiv:2609.25723 use a screened neutral four-dark-quark baryon whose charge form factor acts as a momentum band-pass filter; Khan et al. arXiv:2609.25114 use a toroidal electromagnetic moment through a dark photon. Deep dives

arXiv:2609.25178 — Discovery of an unexpectedly light and narrow beauty-strange state (LHCb collaboration et al.)

LHCb reconstructs \(B_s^0\pi^0\) candidates in 9 fb⁻¹ of Run 1–2 data across three \(B_s^0\) channels (\(D_s^-\pi^+\), \(J/\psi\phi\), \(D_s^-\pi^+\pi^+\pi^-\)), finding a narrow peak at a common mass in all three. A simultaneous fit yields \(343^{+44}_{-41}\) signal events at \(M=5698.9\pm1.5\pm0.6\) MeV/\(c^2\), with local (global) significance exceeding 8 (7)σ. The width is consistent with zero, \(\Gamma<9.8\) (11.8) MeV at 90% (95%) CL. Interpreted as the \(0^+\) scalar \(B_{s0}^*(5700)^0\) — the beauty counterpart of \(D_{s0}^*(2317)^+\) — its \(B_s^0\) splitting of \(332.0\) MeV/\(c^2\) tracks the charm-sector \(349.2\) MeV/\(c^2\), and sits well below quark-potential predictions, the same “mass deficit” seen in charm. This is billed as the first observation of a nonconventional single-beauty hadron, extending the exotic-dynamics pattern (chiral doubling, \(BK\) molecules, or compact tetraquarks) into the open-beauty frontier that had lacked a counterpart.

The main caveat is spin-parity ambiguity: the structure is equally compatible with the \(1^+\) axial-vector partner decaying to \(B_s^{*0}(\to B_s^0\gamma)\pi^0\) with the photon unreconstructed (then \(M=5748.3\) MeV/\(c^2\)), or a superposition of both. Resolving the \((0^+,1^+)\) doublet requires the \(B_s^0\gamma\) spectrum and awaits the larger post-2022 upgrade dataset. The state also enables the first data-driven \(0^+\) pole for \(B_s^0\) heavy-to-light form factors, and predicts light narrow partners for \(\Omega_{cc}^+\) and broad \(B\)-sector states.

arXiv:2609.25158 — Observation of η(2600) and Threshold Enhancements in the ΛΛ̄ System (BESIII Collaboration et al.)

Using 2712.4 million \(\psi(3686)\) events, BESIII performs a model-independent partial-wave analysis of \(\psi(3686)\to\gamma\Lambda\bar\Lambda\), exploiting the self-analyzing weak decays of \(\Lambda/\bar\Lambda\) to extract spin correlations and cleanly separate \(0^{-+}\) from \(0^{++}\). The near-threshold region (97.6% purity) is dominated by the \(^1S_0\) (\(0^{-+}\), 13.2σ) and \(^3P_0\) (\(0^{++}\), 5.4σ) waves, both showing strong threshold enhancements. In the \(^1S_0\) wave a new pseudoscalar \(\eta(2600)\) emerges at \(M=2625\pm26\pm6\) MeV/\(c^2\), \(\Gamma=140\pm58\pm46\) MeV, significance 7.0σ — mass-consistent with the \(X(2600)\) seen in \(J/\psi\to\gamma\pi^+\pi^-\eta'\), and the heaviest light meson to date. Its production relative to \(\eta_c\) (12–22%) matches the \(X(2600)/\eta_c\) ratio (31%) in the other channel, supporting a common identity. The \(^3P_0\) wave gives a \(2262\pm34\) MeV/\(c^2\), \(215\pm35\) MeV state near \(f_0(2200)\).

The finding supports theoretical suggestions of a pseudoscalar glueball-like state near 2.6 GeV, and the \(\eta(\Lambda\bar\Lambda)\) threshold enhancement invites hexaquark/baryonium interpretations (a predicted pseudoscalar \(\Lambda\bar\Lambda\) bound state at ~2.27 GeV/\(c^2\)). Caveats: the \(\eta(2600)\) mass and width depend on lineshape choice (BW vs K-matrix), the \(\eta(\Lambda\bar\Lambda)\) pole position is model-unstable (BW mass 2169 vs KM 1899 MeV/\(c^2\)), and the dominant systematic on the \(^3P_0\) branching fraction reaches −22.8% from additional-wave assumptions.

arXiv:2609.25214 — Nonlocal advantage of quantum coherence in tau-lepton pairs from electron–positron collisions (Yoav Afik et al.)

Afik et al. argue that \(e^+e^-\to\tau^+\tau^-\) is uniquely suited to certify Nonlocal Advantage of Quantum Coherence (NAQC) — the strongest collider quantum correlation studied — because, unlike top pairs at the LHC where NAQC survives only in unmeasurably thin phase-space slivers, tau pairs exhibit NAQC over broad angular windows. For unpolarized \(T\)-states with symmetric correlation matrix, they show NAQC reduces to \(\sum_i|C_i|>\sqrt6\) and construct a single-observable witness \(W_{\rm NA}=\sum_i|C_{ii}|\) (maximized in the diagonal basis), measurable via a pion opening-angle distribution \(D_i=\mathrm{Tr}[P_i\mathbf C]/3\), with \(D_i\ge\sqrt{2/3}\) sufficient for NAQC. At Belle II (\(\sqrt s=10.58\) GeV) NAQC peaks at \(\mathfrak C^{l_1}_{\rm NA,max}=2.59\) within \(|\cos\Theta|<0.23\), containing \(f_{\rm NAQC}=0.18\) of events; at the FCC-ee \(Z\) pole, \(2.96\) within \(0.39\), \(f=0.31\). A 5σ observation needs ~1% precision at Belle II, a few percent at FCC-ee.

The result matures the collider-QI program from entanglement toward a certifiable NAQC hierarchy (\(\text{NAQC}\subset\text{Bell}\subset\ldots\)), and includes the first proposed steering-game implementation in a collider — with asymmetric steering flagged as a future CP-violation probe. Caveats are honest: event counts and NAQC signal are anti-correlated (both peak/dip at \(\cos\Theta=0\)); estimates omit detector acceptance and reconstruction efficiencies; and the analysis is LO electroweak with an ultrarelativistic \(T\)-state approximation. The general proof that NAQC implies Bell nonlocality remains missing (numerical evidence only).
