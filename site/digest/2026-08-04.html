Distances without photons

The most novel single result is a cosmology paper hiding in the neutrino literature: Herrera et al. arXiv:2608.00923 build the first distance ladder from high-energy astrophysical neutrinos, using 12 IceCube-detected Seyferts and the disk-corona \(L_\nu\)–\(L_X\) correlation to get \(H_0=49^{+40}_{-30}\). The error bar is enormous and the value consistent with everyone, but it is a genuinely propagation-systematic-free probe — a fresh entry against the week’s steady drumbeat of tension walls, this time proposing a new rung rather than kicking away an old one. The day’s actual bulk sits in QCD and hadron structure (reviews, spectroscopy, lattice), and the cleanest new experimental thread is oxygen-collision data from LHCb and ALICE.

Themes

Oxygen collisions and small-system QGP

Run 3 OO data is starting to land. LHCb arXiv:2608.00182 finds the \(\Upsilon(2S)\) suppressed by ~2× in OO — far beyond the slight \(p\)O effect — as evidence that deconfinement-like screening switches on even in this small system. ALICE arXiv:2608.02161 (Di Costanzo) reports the first baryon-meson \(v_2\) splitting in the charm sector and extends prompt-D flow down into central OO. Together they push the “how small can QGP be” question onto real Run 3 measurements.

Deep dives

arXiv:2608.00923 — Measurement of the Hubble constant with high-energy neutrinos (Gonzalo Herrera et al.)

The first-ever \(H_0\) determination from astrophysical neutrinos: Herrera, Kamp and Argüelles build a distance ladder from 12 X-ray–selected Seyferts with IceCube per-source excesses, exploiting the disk-corona relation \(L_\nu=\kappa L_X^\beta\). NGC 1068 (Cepheid+TRGB, \(10.93\pm0.74\) Mpc) anchors the absolute scale; the 11 Hubble-flow sources supply \(H_0\) through the \(d_L^{2(\beta-1)}\) lever arm that only exists when \(\beta\neq1\). Re-extracting signal counts from the 14-year public point-source data with SkyLLH and profiling \(\kappa\) analytically, they find \(H_0=49^{+40}_{-30}\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}\) and \(\beta=0.67^{+0.16}_{-0.25}\), with the calorimetric \(\beta=1\) disfavored at \(\sim2\sigma\) — the first population-level slope measurement, pointing to subcalorimetric coronae (Bethe-Heitler or synchrotron suppression).

Why it matters: this is a genuinely new distance rung immune to dust, absorption, scattering and radiative-transfer systematics — the entire error budget lives in source physics. As the digest frames it, it proposes a new rung rather than kicking away an old one. Caveats are candid and severe: the \(H_0\) uncertainty is enormous and consistent with both Planck and SH0ES at \(1\sigma\); the value swings dramatically with the NGC 1068 intrinsic luminosity choice (\(H_0=109^{+29}_{-37}\) if the high value is adopted) and with adding NGC 4151 as a second anchor (to \(H_0\approx109\), a \(\sim15\%\) residual tension the authors attribute to intrinsic \(L_\nu\)–\(L_X\) scatter). Selection on neutrino significance can also imprint a spurious correlation. IceCube-Gen2 could reach \(\sigma(H_0)\approx16\)–20.

arXiv:2608.01289 — Combination of vector boson scattering measurements in leptonic final states at \(\sqrt{s}=13\) TeV (CMS Collaboration)

CMS delivers its most comprehensive VBS combination: seven analyses over the full 138 fb\(^{-1}\) Run 2 dataset, spanning same-sign and opposite-sign WW, WZ, ZZ, plus semileptonic WV/ZV channels, fit simultaneously across 48 phase-space regions and 1525 bins. A 4-POI model returns signal strengths all consistent with unity — \(\mu_{\mathrm{SSWW}}=1.05^{+0.15}_{-0.14}\), \(\mu_{\mathrm{OSWW}}=1.11\pm0.20\) (\(6.4\sigma\)), \(\mu_{\mathrm{WZ}}=1.21^{+0.28}_{-0.25}\) (\(7.0\sigma\)), \(\mu_{\mathrm{ZZ}}=1.15^{+0.44}_{-0.37}\) (\(4.0\sigma\)) — with the combination sharpening OSWW and WZ uncertainties by up to 15% over the best individual inputs. A charge-separated 6-POI model probes the expected production charge asymmetry from the proton’s valence content, finding \(\mu_{W^+W^+}=1.13^{+0.17}_{-0.16}\) versus \(\mu_{W^-W^-}=0.84^{+0.28}_{-0.24}\).

Why it matters: this is the consolidated stress-test of the electroweak-scattering program, coherently accounting for cross-channel correlations that single measurements cannot — the reference point against which unitarity-violating BSM effects in longitudinal boson scattering would appear as event excesses. Everything is SM-consistent, so it functions as a benchmark and a clean baseline for the EFT/anomalous-coupling recasts that follow. No tension is flagged; the correlations among signal strengths are mild.

arXiv:2608.00182 — Modification of \(\Upsilon\) production in \(p\)O and OO collisions at LHCb (LHCb collaboration et al.)

Abstract only. LHCb measures \(\Upsilon(2S)\) and \(\Upsilon(3S)\) yields relative to \(\Upsilon(1S)\) in \(pp\), \(p\)O, and OO collisions. The \(pp\) ratios match earlier LHCb results; \(p\)O shows only slight relative suppression of the excited states, but OO suppresses the \(\Upsilon(2S)\) by a factor of \(\sim2\), with evidence for \(\Upsilon(3S)\) suppression. The jump from a small \(p\)O effect to significant OO suppression is read as the onset of additional mechanisms — color screening in a deconfined medium — with QGP-formation models successfully describing the OO data.

Why it matters: alongside ALICE’s charm-sector \(v_2\) splitting, this pushes the “how small can QGP be” question onto real Run 3 oxygen data, with sequential melting appearing in a system this small. The caveat, visible even from the abstract, is disentangling cold-nuclear-matter effects from genuine deconfinement screening — the \(p\)O reference is precisely what makes the OO excess interpretable, but the model dependence of the QGP interpretation remains.
