The vibe today

A big, sprawling day — 66 papers with no single collaboration bombshell but several genuinely satisfying results scattered across the whole map. If last week ended with theorists reclaiming the floor, today the experiments quietly reclaimed a corner of it: LHCb reports two new excited \(\Sigma_c\) baryons, BESIII pins down a new \(\eta_c\) decay, T2K delivers its first joint carbon–oxygen kinematic-imbalance cross sections, and DarkSide-50 sets a \(^{36}\)Ar double-electron-capture limit. Real numbers, real detectors. And underneath the data papers, the theory currents that have run all fortnight kept flowing: strong CP, an ever-diversifying dark sector, and — yet again — the anxiety over what could masquerade as new physics in long-baseline neutrino oscillations.

The intellectual center of gravity, if there is one, is the strong CP problem, approached today from two very different altitudes. A lattice collaboration hands us a fresh, high-precision neutron-EDM coefficient, while a single-author essay quietly asks whether the problem is even a problem — arguing that the whole \(\theta\)-vacuum edifice rests on an optional topological assumption. That juxtaposition, computation and foundational skepticism landing the same morning, is the day in miniature: the field simultaneously sharpening its tools and questioning its premises.

The rest is a healthy diffusion. Dark matter candidates multiply into ever-stranger corners — heavy charged relics settling into the Earth’s crust, primordial quark pellets, reactor-produced light DM via the Migdal effect. Machine-learning reconstruction reaches two new detector types. And there’s the usual quiet stream of femtoscopy, Regge trajectories, and formal QCD-in-magnetic-fields work keeping the foundations tended.

Themes

Strong CP, examined and quantified

The most striking pairing of the day. Blum et al. arXiv:2607.10606 present a lattice-QCD calculation of the neutron EDM using a static background electric field, exploiting a Feynman-Hellmann-motivated single-time-slice sampling of the topological charge to sharpen precision dramatically, and — crucially — solving a non-Hermitian GEVP to strip excited-state contamination from the deformed nucleon. Their physical-point result, \(d_n = -0.0050(4)_{\rm stat}(8)_{\rm sys}\,\bar\theta\,e\,\)fm, is exactly the kind of controlled number the \(0\nu\)-and-EDM programs need. Against it, Williams arXiv:2607.10272 pushes in the opposite direction, arguing that the conventional strong CP problem is contingent: the local observables (topological susceptibility, the anomalous Ward identity, Witten-Veneziano) follow from gauge invariance and locality, while the global topological classification that produces \(\theta\)-vacua is an added assumption no known observable requires. This is a provocative counterpoint to last week’s “strong CP from three directions” flurry — where the field was busy solving the problem via Nelson-Barr and accidental PQ symmetries. Around them orbit the darkly-charged ALP EFT of Arenaza et al. arXiv:2607.09852 and Choi et al.’s dynamical suppression of extra-dimensional axion isocurvature arXiv:2607.09969. (One should treat the tabletop “axion in an optical trap” claim arXiv:2607.11589 with considerable caution.)

And Choi et al. arXiv:2607.11887 add a genuinely new detection window — stochastic diffractive lensing of GWs by \(10\)–\(10^4\,M_\odot\) halos, a “dark timbre” imprint LISA could stack.

Neutrino degeneracies and cross-section inputs

The fake-CP thread that dominated a fortnight ago resurfaces in a new guise. Koranga et al. arXiv:2607.10417 show that approximating Earth’s density with a single path-averaged value (rather than spatially resolved PREM) fabricates a degeneracy in \((\delta_{\rm CP},\Delta_{\rm CPT},\theta_{\rm CPT})\) that, at \(L\gtrsim5000\) km, becomes indistinguishable from genuine BSM physics — a DUNE-like exposure could report a spurious \(\pm3\sigma\) CPT violation. It’s the same lesson the week of degeneracy papers kept teaching, now aimed at the matter profile itself. On the input-precision side, T2K delivers its first double-differential kinematic-imbalance cross sections simultaneously on carbon and oxygen arXiv:2607.10638, arXiv:2607.10644, finding that no current generator describes the data across phase space — essential common systematics work for Hyper-K. And two double-beta papers land: ab-initio \(^{48}\)Ca NMEs from VS-IMSRG showing the enlarged \(d_{3/2}pf\) space matters arXiv:2607.11733, and DarkSide-50’s first \(^{36}\)Ar \(2\nu2\)EC limit arXiv:2607.11476.

Deep dives

arXiv:2607.10606 — The Neutron Electric Dipole Moment from Lattice QCD using a Background Electric Field (Thomas Blum et al.)

Blum et al. extract the θ̄-induced neutron EDM from the energy shift of a neutron in a static, imaginary-valued Euclidean electric field, arriving at \(d_n = -0.0050(4)_{\rm stat}(8)_{\rm sys}\,\bar\theta\,e\,\)fm at the physical point. Two methodological moves drive the precision. First, motivated by Feynman-Hellmann, they sample the topological charge density on a single time slice rather than integrating the global charge \(Q_{\rm top}\) — the disconnected topological correlator is the noise bottleneck, and localizing it plus a full-volume low-mode average sharpens the signal dramatically. Second, because the background field deforms the nucleon and mixes parity, the naive positive-parity projector suffers large excited-state contamination; they instead solve a non-Hermitian (PT-symmetric, \(S\)-pseudo-Hermitian) GEVP in the space of the nucleon, its parity partner and excitations to isolate the true ground state. Reassuringly, EDM values agree across chiral-covariant and non-covariant interpolators and across gluonic vs. pseudoscalar-density definitions of the topological charge, and the global-charge method gives results consistent with (but far noisier than) the new estimator.

This is the kind of controlled number the EDM and \(0\nu\beta\beta\) programs need to convert experimental limits into bounds on \(\bar\theta\) and BSM CP-violating operators, and the local-charge + GEVP toolkit extends naturally to the Weinberg and chromo-EDM operators. Caveats are stated frankly: the calculation uses a single lattice spacing (\(a\approx0.11\) fm) and pion masses of 340–576 MeV, so discretization, finite-volume and chiral-extrapolation systematics are deferred to future work; the electro-quenched approximation is assumed benign (~1% for magnetic moments). It also lands amid ongoing dispute over whether the θ-term generates a nonzero nEDM at all.

arXiv:2607.10272 — On the Origins of the Strong CP Problem (Anthony G. Williams)

Williams argues that the strong CP problem is contingent, not mandatory. He defines “minimal QCD” — the standard Lagrangian plus local gauge invariance, causal locality, and the functional integral — and shows that essentially every established nonperturbative result attributed to topology follows within it without assuming the gauge-field configuration space decomposes into integer-valued sectors. The topological susceptibility emerges as \(\chi=\langle\hat Q^2\rangle/V=\partial^2\varepsilon_0/\partial\theta^2\) with \(\theta\) treated purely as a source parameter for the local operator \(q(x)\); the axial anomaly identifies the invariant \(\bar\theta=\theta+\arg\det M\); and the ’t Hooft vertex, Witten–Veneziano (\(m_{\eta'}^2=2N_f\chi_{\rm YM}/f_0^2\)) and Leutwyler–Smilga (\(\chi=\Sigma/\sum_f m_f^{-1}\)) relations all rely only on local operators, semiclassical saddle points, or the large-\(N_c\) expansion. The integer-valued \(Q\in\mathbb{Z}\), the \(2\pi\)-periodicity, the sector weighting \(e^{i\theta Q}\), the physical θ-vacuum and hence the fine-tuning problem appear only after supplementing minimal QCD with smoothness/boundary assumptions (\(\pi_3(SU(3))=\mathbb Z\)) — assumptions, he stresses, that typical distributional (non-differentiable) functional-integral configurations need not satisfy, and that lattice topology recovers only via added constructions (gradient flow, index theorem).

If this framing holds, the “problem” becomes a question about which additional structure Nature actually requires, reframing the recent Nelson-Barr/PQ solution flurry as answers to a possibly-optional question. The honest caveat is baked in: this is a foundational/logical clarification, not a new prediction. Williams explicitly does not claim a θ-term is forbidden by symmetry, does not dispute the θ↔pseudoscalar-mass equivalence, and concedes future theory or data could yet demand the global structure — in which case the entire conventional literature immediately reapplies.

arXiv:2607.10595 — Observation of new excited \(\Sigma_c^0\) states in \(B^-\to\Lambda_c^+\bar p\pi^-\) (LHCb collaboration et al.)

An LHCb amplitude analysis of \(B^-\to\Lambda_c^+\bar p\pi^-\) using the full 9 fb\(^{-1}\) Run 1+2 dataset (\(37.0\times10^3\) signal decays, 5.1% combinatorial background) resolves the \(\Lambda_c^+\pi^-\) spectrum into the known \(\Sigma_c(2455)^0\), \(\Sigma_c(2520)^0\) and \(\Sigma_c(2800)^0\) plus two new states, \(\Sigma_c(2900)^0\) and \(\Sigma_c(3200)^0\) — the first new excited \(\Sigma_c^0\) baryons in over two decades. The two-resonance description of the ~2.8 GeV region (splitting the old BaBar \(\Sigma_c(2800)^0\) into overlapping states) is favored at \(7.5\sigma\), and the \(\Sigma_c(3200)^0\) is observed at \(12.0\sigma\). Combining the two solution families, they measure \(m_{\Sigma_c(3200)^0}=3.186\pm0.006\pm0.015\) GeV/\(c^2\), \(\Gamma=0.133\pm0.018\pm0.046\) GeV. The \(\Sigma_c(2520)^0\) spin-parity is pinned to \(3/2^+\) (>6σ) and the \(\Sigma_c(2455)^0\) spin to \(1/2\). Candidate interpretations span 2S/1P for the \(\Sigma_c(2900)^0\) and 2P/3S for the \(\Sigma_c(3200)^0\).

This materially expands the sparse \(\Sigma_c\) sector — only three states were previously established — providing masses and widths to confront the crowded quark-model predictions for 2S/1D/2P λ- and ρ-mode excitations, part of today’s experiment-anchored spectroscopy cluster. The central honest caveat is that spin-parity cannot be unambiguously fixed: multiple \(J^P\) assignments give nearly degenerate likelihoods, yielding two solution groups (A and B) statistically indistinguishable (\(2\Delta\ln\mathcal L<1\)) yet giving very different masses, widths and fit fractions for the \(\Sigma_c(2800)^0\)/\(\Sigma_c(2900)^0\) pair — group A matches Belle’s \(\Sigma_c(2800)^0\) (which sees no \(\Sigma_c(2900)^0\)), group B does not. Only the \(\Sigma_c(3200)^0\) parameters are robust across solutions.
