Can the CKM matrix do baryogenesis?

A theory-heavy day, with the weight in early-universe model building and the sharpest single claim a provocation: Bigaran et al. arXiv:2608.05269 argue that the usual “SM CP violation is insufficient for baryogenesis” verdict has a counterexample — a leptoquark with minimal-flavor-violating couplings whose decays generate the asymmetry from CKM phases alone, no time-varying parameters required. It anchors a three-paper baryogenesis cluster that ties directly to the flavor/CP top-down thread the memory has tracked for two weeks. On the experimental side the clean results are elsewhere: BESIII pushes a CLFV limit down three orders of magnitude, and ALICE turns three-proton femtoscopy into an effective 3→3 scattering experiment.

Themes

Baryogenesis and leptogenesis, three routes

Beyond Bigaran et al.’s CKM/MFV scenario arXiv:2608.05269, Kuckenberg et al. arXiv:2608.05279 revisit axion-driven spontaneous leptogenesis with fully flavor-dependent Weinberg-operator rates, finding standard Majoron misalignment under severe tension from late-decay dilution while strong/weak-sphaleron couplings survive near the GUT scale. Ogawa et al. arXiv:2608.05950 add dimension-8 CP-violating operators to sphalerogenesis and show loop-order counting can matter as much as mass-dimension counting. Three different mechanisms, all circling the same target — the origin of the baryon asymmetry — and the CKM-phase entry is the one worth arguing about.

Dark matter: fundamental limits and exotic signatures

Barker et al. arXiv:2608.05282 derive material-agnostic upper bounds on DM-electron scattering rates from condensed-matter sum rules, fixing a fundamental ceiling on any density-coupled direct-detection experiment from just \(\omega_p\), \(\rho_T\) and the static dielectric function. Davoudiasl et al. arXiv:2608.05284 propose matter-induced DM decay giving non-collinear double-muon vertices in IceCube/KM3NeT, and CMS arXiv:2608.05323 posts the first constraints on lepton-enriched semivisible jets, excluding a \(Z'\) mediator up to 4.7 TeV. Bounds and signatures from both ends of the same problem.

Entanglement reaches Compton scattering

Hatta et al. arXiv:2608.05330 study photon-nucleon spin entanglement in Compton scattering, proving a no-go theorem (no entanglement in unpolarized scattering with real amplitudes) and then finding maximally entangled Bell states across the polarized kinematic plane — with the neutron’s electric/magnetic polarizabilities controlling whether entanglement exists at all. The collider-QI program the memory has followed from \(t\bar t\) tomography to hyperons now has a low-energy nucleon-structure entry, with entanglement pitched as a probe of nucleon EM properties.

Deep dives

arXiv:2608.05269 — Baryogenesis via the CKM Matrix with Minimal Flavor Violation (Innes Bigaran et al.)

Bigaran, Krnjaic, Langhoff and Xiao construct an explicit counterexample to the folklore that SM CP violation is too small for baryogenesis. The model adds a scalar leptoquark \(\mathcal{S}\sim(\bar{\mathbf 3},\mathbf 1)_{1/3}\times\mathbf 3_{\bar d}\) whose baryon- and lepton-violating couplings are built entirely from Yukawa spurions under MFV, so the only CP phase is the Jarlskog invariant \(J\). Flavor breaking splits the three \(\mathcal{S}_i\) masses (\(m_i^2=M^2(1+\gamma y_{d_i}^2)\)), which lets tree–propagator interference in \(\mathcal{S}_i\) decays generate a CP asymmetry per decay that peaks near resonance at \(\epsilon_1^{\max}\approx 0.15\) — vastly above the naive \(Y_B\sim 10^{-20}\) estimate. Crucially, no time-varying parameters are needed, distinguishing it from Mesogenesis. Two cosmologies are offered: low reheat (\(T_{\rm RH}<130\) GeV) with non-thermal \(\mathcal{S}\) production, requiring \(m_{\mathcal S}\lesssim10^{11}\) GeV; or high reheat with right-handed-neutrino \(\Delta L=2\) washout converting \(B{+}L\) into surviving \(B{-}L\), viable for \(\epsilon_i\gtrsim10^{-7}\).

Why it matters: it directly challenges the “SM CP violation is insufficient” verdict that motivates much BSM baryogenesis, and anchors the digest’s flavor/CP top-down thread. The honest caveats are prominent: the vertex-correction contribution vanishes and the entire asymmetry rides on the propagator term, which dies at mass degeneracy; the \(B{-}L\)-preserving decays require an extra ingredient (a late-decaying scalar or RH neutrinos) to escape sphaleron washout; and proton decay forces \(M\gtrsim10^{14}\) GeV for electron couplings (relaxed to \(\sim6\times10^9\) GeV for \(\tau\)). The authors explicitly frame it as a proof of principle, not a classified model.

arXiv:2608.05330 — Photon-nucleon entanglement in Compton scattering at low and high energies (Yoshitaka Hatta et al.)

Hatta and Martínez-Fernández extend the collider quantum-information program to elastic Compton scattering, treating the final photon helicity and nucleon spin as two qubits. Their central analytic result is a no-go theorem: in unpolarized scattering off any spin-\(\tfrac12\) target with real amplitudes, the partially-transposed density matrix cannot have a negative eigenvalue, so no entanglement is possible — proven generally, extending an earlier numerical electron-only observation. To generate entanglement they turn to polarized initial states. Below the pion threshold (\(\omega<140\) MeV) all six helicity amplitudes are real, yet with polarized beams they find a rich pattern of maximally entangled Bell states (and unitary equivalents, including a cyclic-anticorrelation \(\Psi^{\circlearrowright}\)) distributed across the \((\theta,\omega)\) plane, with proton and neutron showing distinct structure. They also compute the high-energy wide-angle regime at NLO in pQCD via GPD-based soft form factors, where an \(\mathcal{O}(\alpha_s)\) imaginary part gives entanglement everywhere but never maximal (\(\lambda_{\min}\approx-0.36\) at best).

The striking physics point is diagnostic: for the neutron, where the Thomson term is absent, the electric and magnetic polarizabilities \(\alpha_E^n,\beta_M^n\) control not just the pattern but the very existence of entanglement — setting \(\alpha_E^n=\beta_M^n=0\) makes the \((\hat x,\hat x)\) configuration separable. This positions entanglement as a probe of nucleon EM structure, measurable at MAMI, JLab and a future EIC. Caveat: measuring final-state spin correlations is acknowledged as experimentally challenging, and the free-neutron extraction relies on deuteron subtraction, left to future work.

arXiv:2608.05708 — Three-baryon femtoscopy as an effective 3→3 scattering experiment (ALICE Collaboration)

ALICE measures the three-proton correlation function in 13.6 TeV pp collisions (Run 3, 53.1 pb\(^{-1}\), with dedicated software triggers selecting triplets at \(Q_3<1.4\) GeV/\(c\), found once per 30000 minimum-bias events). A data-driven decomposition isolates the genuine p–p–p signal (79% of triplets) from \(\Lambda\)/\(\Sigma^+\) feed-down and misidentification backgrounds, with the source size \(\rho_0=(2.54\pm0.04)\) fm fixed by the companion two-proton measurement (\(r_0=1.27\) fm). Comparison to hyperspherical-adiabatic three-body continuum calculations (AV18 + Coulomb, three-body force negligible by Pauli blocking) reproduces the data at \(n_\sigma=2.5\). The headline sensitivity result: removing the NN \(p\)-wave interaction produces a \(22.2\sigma\) disagreement, proving the correlation function resolves the partial-wave structure of the nuclear force. Two novel continuum signatures emerge — the nuclear interaction stays relevant up to grand-angular momentum \(K\le7\) (far slower convergence than the two-body case, since large \(K\) still admits pairs in \(l_x=0\)), and the correlation stays above unity as it approaches the uncorrelated limit, the first experimental signature of an effective long-range attraction (\(\propto\zeta/\rho^3\), with \(\zeta\) tied to the two-body scattering length).

This establishes femtoscopy as a genuine 3→3 scattering experiment and gives the first direct access to the isospin-3/2 three-body continuum, previously reachable only in isospin-1/2 via nucleon–deuteron scattering. Caveats acknowledged by the collaboration: the three-body Coulomb problem is treated approximately (no exact solution), feed-down modeling could be sharpened, and the \(2.5\sigma\) agreement leaves room for theory improvement. The clear payoff is extension to p–p–\(\Lambda\), p–p–\(\Sigma\), p–p–\(\Xi\) for the strangeness sector.

Citation radar

This week’s radar leans toward methodological reckoning rather than new measurements. Two entries are LLM/ML tooling for physics workflows (one autonomous ROOT agent, one scaling-law study for jet generation); two are theory papers that reframe or correct existing formalisms (a control theorem for string inflation, a partonic rethink of bubble-collision particle production); and one proposes a new collider observable for quantum-correlation tests. The common thread: papers offering filters, corrections, and invariants that let the community decide which existing approaches to trust.

arXiv:2605.17318 — RooAgent: An LLM Agent for Root-Based High Energy Physics Analysis (Aman Desai)

RooAgent wraps PyROOT routines (inspection, cutflows, fitting, significance, CLs) as tools an LLM invokes from plain-language prompts, supporting both a LangGraph agent (GPT-4.1 via Copilot, DeepSeek-V3 via Ollama) and an MCP server for Claude. The paper benchmarks it on simulated \(pp\to ZH\) events, a toy statistical search, and ATLAS \(H\to ZZ^*\to 4\ell\) open data, reporting 19/20 single-task successes with runtimes of 7–225 s and consistent numerical results across backends.

With ~6 citations since posting, its pull is timing rather than a headline result: the reference list shows a burst of “agentic AI for HEP analysis” papers in late 2025–2026, and RooAgent is a concrete, PyPI-installable entry in that fast-moving conversation. It is the kind of tool paper that accumulates citations by being cited alongside peers as evidence that LLM-driven analysis is becoming practical. The candid documentation of failure modes (case-sensitive tree lookups, model-dependent binning choices) also makes it a useful honest datapoint.

arXiv:2605.10197 — Controlled Penumbral Inflation from Monodromic Valleys (Pirzada et al.)

The paper derives a covariant “control theorem” for single-field inflation in the penumbra — the near-boundary region of complex-structure moduli space. It shows that a monodromy-preserving odd branch term rotates the light trajectory saxionically and produces a plateau when \(\Delta\equiv p+2\nu-q>0\), with single-clock control requiring \(p<2\) (or \(p=2\) with a large entropy-mass prefactor). The benchmark predicts \(r\sim10^{-3}\) with correlated running \(\alpha_s\simeq-r/2\), a target for LiteBIRD/CMB-S4, and provides an exactly solvable “invariant attractor equation.”

At ~6 citations in a few months, the appeal is that it turns a qualitative regime into a decision principle: a local EFT filter that flags which monodromic valleys deserve expensive global string embedding before that work is attempted. That framing — “filtered search” over the landscape — plus a falsifiable observable corridor gives both string phenomenologists and CMB modelers something to cite. Honestly, with such a specialized construction and dense formalism, some early citations are likely from the immediate collaborators/community around the cited penumbra literature (Lanza–Westphal, Grimm) rather than broad uptake.

arXiv:2605.13250 — Determining the Spin-Analyzing Powers via Invariants of the Spin Correlation Matrices and Probing the Bell Non-Locality at the Lepton Colliders (Dianwei Wang et al.)

For two-fermion production via single-mediator exchange at \(e^+e^-\) colliders, the authors prove that \(\mathrm{Tr}[C]\) of the spin correlation matrix is a rotation-invariant, scattering-angle-independent quantity, equal to \(+1\), \(+1\), \(-3\) for gauge-boson, CP-even scalar, and CP-odd scalar exchange respectively. This lets one fix the product of spin-analyzing powers \(\alpha_{F_a}\alpha_{F_b}\) and reconstruct \(C\) from angular correlations alone — thereby evading the no-go theorem that otherwise blocks Bell tests at colliders. They apply it to \(e^+e^-\to J/\psi\to\Lambda\bar\Lambda\) at BESIII, finding CHSH-Horodecki violation for \(|\cos\theta_\Lambda|<0.44\).

The ~5 citations reflect a hot, crowded subfield (the reference list runs past 130 collider-entanglement papers) where the no-go theorem on measuring spin-analyzing powers is the central obstruction. A claimed clean invariant that circumvents it is exactly the kind of tool the community was waiting for. Given the authors’ own dense self-citation cluster (Pei, Wu, Li et al.), a share of early citations is internal, but the observable’s generality gives it broader traction potential.

arXiv:2605.28940 — Neural Scaling Laws for Jet Generation (Oz Amram et al.)

This is the first systematic study of neural scaling laws for generative jet modeling, training the autoregressive OmniJet-\(\alpha\) on ~180M real jets from the CMS-derived Aspen Open Jets dataset. Clean power-law scaling appears with model size \(N\) (with the SWD physics metric monotonically tracking the next-token-prediction loss), but scaling with dataset size and compute is weak and saturates early. The authors introduce a learnable window diagnostic and show it is dramatically smaller for jet NTP (\(W=3.2\) nats, effective perplexity 1330) than for language (\(W=8.7\), perplexity ~5), attributing the rapid saturation to QCD stochasticity and the mismatch between \(p_T\)-ordering and parton-shower causality.

At ~5 citations, the draw is a genuinely surprising negative-ish result on real detector data: scaling laws — the organizing principle of modern foundation models — behave qualitatively differently for generic QCD jets. That directly informs anyone planning large pretraining runs on collider data, and the “learnable window” concept is a portable, quotable diagnostic. The authorship overlap with the AOJ dataset and OmniJet ecosystem also naturally seeds citations from that active foundation-model community.

arXiv:2607.15279 — Particle production from bubble collisions (Anish Ghoshal et al.)

Ghoshal, Pal, and Strumia argue that the standard “off-shell” Schwinger-like formalism for heavy-particle production in ultra-relativistic bubble collisions is unphysical: the off-shell propagator \(\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi(p^2)\) depends on field-space parameterization (manifesting as gauge dependence for vectors) and can give nonzero rates even in free theories, while the perfectly-elastic collision assumption parametrically overestimates hard production. They propose instead a partonic picture — Lorentz-contracted walls undergo near-free passage, and hard production arises from on-shell scatterings among wall quanta, giving rates suppressed by \(1/\gamma^2\) in the hard regime. They recompute scalar/fermion/vector/graviton cross sections and revisit dark matter, leptogenesis, and gravitational-wave implications, finding substantially reduced (though not negligible) yields.

The ~5 citations in a couple of months signal a provocative correction to a whole line of work (13 papers are grouped as using the flawed approach). Any claim that a widely-used mechanism was overestimated — and that DM/leptogenesis/GW signals must be revised downward — forces responses from exactly those authors, which is a reliable citation driver.
