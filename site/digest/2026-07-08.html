The vibe today

A theory-heavy, model-builder’s day — the kind where the phenomenologists outnumber the collaborations and the whiteboard energy is all about structure: mass matrices, discrete symmetries, seesaws, and the stubborn puzzle of why the QCD vacuum angle is zero. After a week dominated by precision machinery and neutrino-oscillation stress tests, the field pivoted today toward the deep flavor questions that never really go away. There’s no single bombshell, but there is a clear intellectual center of gravity, and it sits at the intersection of flavor hierarchies, CP violation, and strong CP.

Two quieter continuities run underneath. The joint QCD+QED factorization program that the memory has been tracking for two weeks — lepton distribution functions, sub-percent precision beyond pure-QCD factorization — got its most ambitious application yet, and the proton-mass/gravitational-form-factor thread from earlier this week kept building. Meanwhile the dark sector stayed in its now-familiar diversifying mode: winos, dark neutrons, massive gravitons, multipolar freeze-out, and a genuinely eye-catching (if unconfirmed) dark-photon hint from a tabletop experiment.

And a couple of papers reached for the deep water: a first collider bound on Born-rule violations, and a pair of reflective reviews tracing how QCD and cosmology grew into precision sciences. A cerebral day, more about frameworks than discoveries.

Themes

Flavor, neutrinos, and the geometry of mixing

The densest cluster today is pure flavor model-building. Xing proposes a “neutrino cuboid” parametrization that accommodates tribimaximal mixing and near-degenerate normal-ordered masses in a cubic limit, offering a testable correlation for JUNO/DUNE/T2HK (arXiv:2607.07311). Hao et al. deliver the first comprehensive \(\Delta(96)\) Modular Littlest Seesaw study, scanning 35 viable breaking patterns that go beyond the usual TM\(_1\) paradigm (arXiv:2607.07865). Yang derives the CKM CP phase perturbatively from rephasing invariants of hierarchical mass matrices (arXiv:2607.06985), and Darricau et al. revisit cLFV asymmetries in scotogenic “T1-2-A” models (arXiv:2607.07484). Bonilla et al. tie neutrino mass to dark-matter stability in minimal \(S_3\)/\(D_4\) three-Higgs-doublet models (arXiv:2607.07853). Also here, though a matter-physics paper: Chaulagain et al.’s algebraic dissection of three-flavor oscillations via Cayley–Hamilton and DMP resummation (arXiv:2607.07213), which was already flagged in the weekly memory as a degeneracy-breaking tool and separates genuine from fake CP in closed form.

Strong CP, from three directions at once

A striking coincidence: three quite different attacks on the strong CP problem land the same day. Baer et al. continue their storyline — the memory has tracked it all week — arguing that once the MSSM \(\mu\)-term is forbidden by anomaly-free \(Z_n^R\) symmetry, an accidental Peccei-Quinn symmetry falls out and, via Kim-Nilles, “serendipitously” delivers a DFSZ axion that solves strong CP while regenerating \(\mu\) (arXiv:2607.07921). Fileviez Perez et al. take the Nelson-Barr route: a universal seesaw with spontaneous CP violation keeps \(\bar\theta=0\) at tree level while generating the CKM phase and the whole charged-fermion hierarchy (arXiv:2607.07794). And Figueroa et al. flip the sign of the question, using a dark-sector \(\theta\)-term as a feature — sourcing dark-neutron self-interactions and CP-violating dark electric dipole moments visible in direct detection (arXiv:2607.07406). Strong CP as bug, as solved problem, and as dark-matter engine, all in one morning.

QCD+QED factorization goes inclusive

The lepton-distribution-function program the memory has been following reaches a milestone: Qiu et al. present the first calculation of single-inclusive hadron and jet production at large \(p_T\) in lepton-hadron scattering within joint QCD+QED factorization, with DGLAP kernels in both couplings and a strategy to extract nonperturbative LDFs at JLab and the EIC (arXiv:2607.07664). Its companion in spirit, Mantry et al., shows QED radiation gives order-one corrections to DIS cross sections but leaves longitudinal spin asymmetries robust at the few-percent level — the observable of choice for SMEFT extraction at SoLID/EIC (arXiv:2607.07811). Marzocca et al. push the parallel “joint fits” theme, showing PDF profiling degrades high-mass Drell-Yan SMEFT bounds in a strongly flavour-dependent way — worst for first-generation operators tied to high-\(x\) valence luminosities (arXiv:2607.07791), a direct sequel to the PDF-absorbs-new-physics warning from last week. Sterman’s review (arXiv:2607.07908) frames the whole enterprise historically.

The gluonic origin of mass

Meziani et al. lay out an experimental roadmap to the gluonic trace anomaly — the dominant source of the proton mass — reconstructing the scalar gluonic trace form factor from DVCS, near-threshold quarkonium production, and the sigma-term, with consistency checks against lattice and the instanton-liquid model (arXiv:2607.07969). Deng et al. complement this from theory, computing the pion’s \(A\) and \(D\) gravitational form factors in light-front holographic QCD (arXiv:2607.07305). Together they extend the gravitational-form-factor thread that ran through this week’s proton-structure papers.

Dark matter keeps diversifying

No WIMP renaissance, just a widening search. Frolovsky et al. connect Starobinsky supergravity to the MSSM and land on a 3 TeV thermal wino with a disappearing-track signature for a 100 TeV collider (arXiv:2607.07193); Bose et al. show an early matter-dominated epoch reopens excluded multipolar-DM parameter space (arXiv:2607.07956); Megias et al. offer massive-graviton DM from a gapped continuum (arXiv:2607.07295). On the experimental side, Belle II sets the world’s best ALP–photon limits over most of \(0.17\)–\(5\) GeV (arXiv:2607.07800), while PHELEX reports a possible diurnal dark-photon signal at 10–40 eV (arXiv:2607.07074) — intriguing, but explicitly awaiting independent confirmation.

Deep dives

arXiv:2607.07664 — Single inclusive hadron and jet production in lepton-hadron scattering (Jian-Wei Qiu et al.)

The first calculation of high-\(p_T\) single-inclusive hadron (\(\pi^\pm,K^\pm\)) and jet production in \(e+h\to H+X\) within joint QCD+QED collinear factorization, treating collision-induced QED radiation on equal footing with QCD. The cross section factorizes into hard parts (kept at NLO) convolved with universal lepton distribution functions (LDFs), PDFs (JAM20/CT18) and FFs (JAM20/MAP1.0), with DGLAP kernels carrying mixed QCD+QED splitting functions. Because a photon can split to \(q\bar q\) nonperturbatively, LDFs are genuinely nonperturbative objects; the authors start evolution at \(\mu_0=m_c\) (not \(m_e\)) and build a “default” LDF set from a Beta-function ansatz constrained by number and momentum sum rules, then evolve to LHAPDF6-compatible grids. Headline numbers: collision-induced QED radiation reshapes the hadron \(p_T\) spectrum by up to ~50% (depleting high-\(p_T\), enhancing low-\(p_T\)); jet spectra drop ~10–20%; scale uncertainty runs ~20–25%, but the LDF (\(\mu_e\)) piece is only a few percent. Nuclear modification factors show EMC suppression below \(p_T\sim10\) GeV and Fermi-motion enhancement above.

Why it matters: this is the LDF program’s most concrete deliverable — a unified treatment of leptoproduction and photoproduction with no ad hoc \(Q^2\) cut or “resolved photon” structure, exactly the framework the digest flags as sub-percent-precision machinery aimed at JLab and the EIC. The key caveat is honest and stated repeatedly: the LDFs are still model defaults (quark/gluon content set to zero at input, so those are lower limits), and existing data carry traditional radiative corrections and cannot be used to fit true LDFs until reanalyzed à la ZEUS. Dominant theory uncertainty is currently the FF choice, not the scales.

arXiv:2607.07800 — Search for axion-like particles decaying to two photons at Belle II (Belle II Collaboration et al.)

Using 408 fb\(^{-1}\) of Belle II data, a search for \(e^+e^-\to\gamma a\), \(a\to\gamma\gamma\) reconstructs the ALP as a narrow diphoton peak over \(0.17<m_a<9.80\) GeV/\(c^2\), assuming photon-dominant coupling (\(\mathcal{B}(a\to\gamma\gamma)=1\)). Three-photon events are selected with a helicity-angle cut (\(|\cos\lambda|<0.74\)) to suppress the dominant \(\gamma\gamma(\gamma)\) background, a kinematic fit sharpening the mass resolution to 10–16 MeV/\(c^2\), and a 19-feature neural-network classifier. No significant excess: the largest local significance is \(3.3\sigma\) near \(m_a=0.22\) GeV/\(c^2\), reducing to \(1.4\sigma\) global after look-elsewhere. The resulting 95% CL limits on \(g_{a\gamma\gamma}\) reach \(\sim10^{-4}\) GeV\(^{-1}\) and are the most restrictive to date over nearly all of \(0.17<m_a<5.00\) GeV/\(c^2\) (except near 0.22 GeV/\(c^2\)), improving on the prior Belle II result by up to a factor 9.

Why it matters: this cleanly extends the dark-sector portal coverage flagged in today’s digest, closing an intermediate-mass gap between beam-dump/forward limits and heavy-ion light-by-light constraints with a single tabletop-clean channel. Improvements over the 2020 Belle II analysis come from the kinematic fit, the NN, and the larger sample. Caveats are modest and disclosed: the coupling extraction ignores possible energy dependence of \(\alpha_\text{QED}\) and \(g_{a\gamma\gamma}\), and \(\eta/\omega\) mass windows are vetoed for peaking backgrounds. The mild \(0.22\) GeV/\(c^2\) bump is statistically unremarkable.

arXiv:2607.07969 — Experimental access to the gluonic origin of the proton mass (Zein-Eddine Meziani et al.)

A concrete reconstruction scheme for the scalar gluonic trace form factor \(G_N(t)\) — the dominant, trace-anomaly source of the proton mass — from three measurable inputs. Their central identity (Eq. 14) writes \(G_N(t)=G_{s,g}(t)+G_{s,q}(t)-\sigma(t)/M\): the quark scalar gravitational form factor from DVCS, the gluon scalar GFF from near-threshold \(J/\psi\) photoproduction (extracted via holographic QCD from JLab data), and the nucleon sigma-term form factor. At zero momentum transfer this reduces to \(G_N(0)=1-\sigma(0)/M_N\). Plugging in the phenomenological \(A_q(0)=0.576\pm0.011\) and lattice dipole/tripole GFF fits, the reconstructed \(G_N(t)\) falls rapidly with \(-t\) and agrees, within bands, with an independent lattice-QCD trace-anomaly determination — and, for \(-t\lesssim1\) GeV\(^2\), with the interacting instanton liquid model, supporting a picture where the long-distance scalar gluonic structure is dominated by semiclassical topological glue.

Why it matters: this converts an abstract mass-decomposition question into a defined experimental program, tying together DVCS, near-threshold quarkonium, and \(\sigma\)-term measurements — exactly the gravitational-form-factor thread the digest has tracked this week. The lattice consistency is a genuine cross-check rather than an input. Caveats: the result relies on holographic-QCD modeling to extract \(G_{s,g}\) from \(J/\psi\) data and on lattice/phenomenological fits for the quark piece, so it is a proof-of-principle reconstruction rather than a fully data-driven extraction; ILM–lattice agreement degrades above \(-t\simeq1\) GeV\(^2\) where short-distance gluon dynamics enter.
