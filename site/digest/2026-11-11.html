A 1.9σ ordering, taken apart

The combined reactor plus \(\nu_\mu\)-disappearance fit disfavors inverted ordering at 1.9σ. Machado et al. arXiv:2611.13629 show that number is a balance of small pulls that disagree with each other. Reactor+T2K and reactor+NOvA point to opposite orderings, and the two experiments’ \(\Delta m^2\) values differ by ~3.5%, about the size of the ordering effect itself. More JUNO data tops out near 3σ. Beyond that, the limiting factor is \(\nu_\mu\) energy reconstruction, which means neutrino–nucleus modeling at the percent level, so today’s interaction-physics papers matter more than usual.

Themes

Interaction modeling sets the limit

Two papers sit right behind Machado’s conclusion. Ježo et al. arXiv:2611.13618 compute heavy-quark \(F_4\) and \(F_5\) at NLO in APFEL++. They find these structure functions suppress the CC \(\nu_\tau\) cross section by up to 40% near the kinematic boundary, the first such estimate for SHiP, IceCube and DUNE. Fatima et al. arXiv:2611.13602 find that medium effects and final-state interactions substantially suppress \(\eta\) production on C and Ar. Both extend the 11-02 generator-rebuilding thread, which now has a concrete percent-level target.

Light-field bounds, recalibrated

  • Pseudo-Goldstone couplings. Banerjee et al. arXiv:2611.14473 point out that linear-coupling bounds fail once a pNGB reaches \(O(f)\) near its source. As a result, terrestrial fifth-force and equivalence-principle tests lose their low-mass reach.
  • SPHEREx axion limits. Hainje et al. arXiv:2611.13090 set the first SPHEREx ALP limits over 0.5–3.3 eV, overlapping the window JWST closed to QCD-axion dark matter in September. Their best limit is \(g_{a\gamma\gamma}<2.1\times10^{-11}\) GeV⁻¹, and their own Cramér–Rao estimate says better methods alone could improve it more than 10×.
  • Helioscope mirrors. Frerick arXiv:2611.13073 shows that helioscope mirrors convert the longitudinal solar dark-photon flux that conventional searches miss.

Precision plumbing

  • IR finiteness without dim reg. Capatti et al. arXiv:2611.13184 build an initial-state KLN construction compatible with collinear factorisation. It produces IR-finite partonic cross sections in four dimensions by Monte Carlo, demonstrated at NLO for Drell–Yan and \(t\bar t\).
  • A published correction. The same group’s comment arXiv:2611.13087 argues that a 2024 PRL on vacuum-amplitude cross sections gets both its central claims wrong.
  • \(B\to X_s\gamma\) uncertainty. Bartocci et al. arXiv:2611.14318 complete NLO for the resolved \(Q_1\)–\(Q_{7\gamma}\) term at \(m_c\to0\). This is one of the leading uncertainties in the SM \(B\to X_s\gamma\) prediction, which August’s exact-\(m_c\) NNLO work had brought into line with data.

  • arXiv:2611.14473: Above a few×10⁻¹² eV, nothing constrains a >10-decade span of nucleon coupling. The paper names a ground–space clock comparison as the test.
  • arXiv:2611.13617: Dutta’s MeV lepton-number-breaking scalar doubles as the SIDM mediator and decays to neutrinos at a rate fixed by neutrino masses. It is fit to dwarf-to-cluster data, not lensing, yet lands near Gilman’s 11-02 neutrinophilic-scalar corner. It prefers normal ordering.

  • arXiv:2611.13184: Local Unitarity reaches hadron colliders with a universal \(\overline{\rm MS}\) conversion.

Deep dives

arXiv:2611.13629 — Where does the neutrino Mass Ordering preference come from? (Pedro A. N. Machado et al.)

The combined reactor + \(\nu_\mu\)-disappearance fit disfavors IO at 1.9σ (CL\(_s\), pseudo-experiments), but the pieces disagree. JUNO’s 207 days alone favor NO by \(\Delta\chi^2=1.84\). Forcing JUNO from its low global minimum (\(1.96\times10^{-3}\) eV² in NO) onto Daya Bay’s branch near \(2.5\times10^{-3}\) costs 5.6 units in NO and 4.0 in IO, so reactors alone give \(T_{\rm MO}=0.21\), which is no preference. Each \(\nu_\mu\) experiment pulls a different way. Reactor+NOvA disfavors IO at 1.4σ, while reactor+T2K disfavors NO at 0.8σ. The T2K–NOvA gap in \(|\Delta m^2_{31}|\) is about 3.5% (~1σ), comparable to the 1.6–3.2% \(\Delta m^2_{ee}\)–\(\Delta m^2_{\mu\mu}\) separation that distinguishes the orderings. In a stress test, sliding T2K onto NOvA’s best fit gives a 1.8σ IO rejection, while the reverse gives a 1.7σ NO rejection. The \(\cos\delta_{\rm CP}\) term contributes at most 0.4%, so it cannot explain the gap. Asimov projections stall the reactor contribution: 1500 more JUNO days yield 2.8σ, and a perfect reactor measurement asymptotes at 2.9σ.

Why it matters. Past ~3σ this channel is limited by \(\nu_\mu\) energy reconstruction at the percent level. That is the target for today’s interaction-modeling papers, and the same requirement applies to HK. DUNE’s matter-effect route sidesteps it.

Caveats. The shift exercise simply sums \(\chi^2\) curves and ignores the experiments’ shared \(\delta_{\rm CP}/\theta_{23}\) dependence. The known appearance-channel disagreement may leak into the \(\Delta m^2\) values. The statistics also matter: naive \(\sqrt T\) would claim a 3.1σ IO tension where toys give 1.8σ.

arXiv:2611.14473 — Running out of Field Range: Nonlinear Sourcing of Light Scalars (Abhishek Banerjee et al.)

For a pNGB with periodic potential and coupling, matter induces \(m_\rho^2\propto dQ\rho/f\) and drives \(\theta\to\pi/2\). This caps the sourced field at \(\pi f/2\) and suppresses the local linear coupling as \(\cos\theta\). For the photon coupling the Earth saturates at \(d_e\simeq2.2\times10^{-9}(f/10\,{\rm MeV})\), about \(10^4\) below the MICROSCOPE linear limit. The two become comparable only near \(f\sim100\) GeV, so existing bounds sit outside their own validity regime. At \(f=10\) MeV the recast results are:

  • MICROSCOPE: at orbit \(\cos\theta\simeq0.16\), and the limit relaxes to \(d_e\lesssim2.8\), a factor \(7\times10^4\) weaker. It closes again near \(3\times10^9\) as the satellite walls screen the field.
  • Electron and nucleon couplings: \(d_{m_e}\lesssim170\) and \(d_{m_N}\lesssim6\), weakened by \(2\times10^5\) and \(2\times10^6\).
  • Eöt-Wash uranium test: develops a vertical wall below \(m_0=3\times10^{-11}\) eV once the air is sourced. MICROSCOPE loses reach above a few×\(10^{-12}\) eV, leaving an unconstrained mass window between the two.
  • Stellar bounds: linear cooling bounds vanish because cores also saturate. Only quadratic pair emission survives, e.g. SN 1987A gives \(d_e\lesssim4\times10^9\).

A ground–ISS clock comparison measures field values rather than gradients. With ACES Cs/H-maser it would reach \(d_e\gtrsim4\times10^4\) above \(1/h_{\rm ISS}\), with a ceiling near \(10^6\) set by the ISS’s own sourcing. A \(^{229}\)Th clock would reach \(\sim3\times10^{-2}\).

Caveats. The upper edges depend on geometry. The lab rock opens a notch in the uranium exclusion, the satellite is a toy model, and LAGEOS–Lunar is ambiguous at this \(f\). Naturalness also needs a \(\mathbb Z_N\) to avoid vacuum sourcing.

arXiv:2611.13617 — Self-interacting dark matter and neutrino masses from MeV-scale lepton-number breaking (Manoranjan Dutta)

One MeV-scale L-breaking vev generates \(\mu_S\) for the inverse seesaw and a pseudo-Dirac splitting for DM. A structural result follows: any central coupling satisfies \(|g|\simeq\Delta m_\chi/(2v_\phi)\), so a vev-generated splitting cannot support strong inelastic SIDM. In the viable symmetric regime, \(\Delta m_\chi\simeq2g_{11}v_\phi\) is tens of MeV and scattering is elastic.

With \(\alpha_{11}\) fixed by the relic density (decoupled, hotter dark sector) and exact partial waves including Majorana statistics, the fit is:

  • Main band: \(m_s\simeq1.9\,{\rm MeV}\times(m_\chi/{\rm GeV})\), with best fit \(m_\chi=7.6\) GeV, \(m_s=14.6\) MeV and \(\chi^2/{\rm dof}=2.04\). The data’s own floor is ~1.9, since a free power law gets 1.92.
  • Resonant islands: appear at higher mass.
  • Overall range: 3–70 GeV at 95% CL, with \(m_s\) = 6–28 MeV.

The mediator decays to \(\nu\nu\) in roughly 40 ms at a rate fixed by \(\sum m_\nu^2\). That lifetime and SN 1987A confine \(v_\phi\) to roughly 130–390 MeV. NO is preferred because its \(\nu_3\)-dominated couplings weaken the SN bound by \(F=5.7\), while IO (\(F\simeq1\)) forces \(m_s\lesssim6\) MeV, against \(N_{\rm eff}\).

Why it matters. It is a concrete NO-predicting model, landing as Machado shows how fragile the current oscillation NO hint is. CMB-S4-level \(N_{\rm eff}\) would probe \(m_s\lesssim11\) MeV.

Caveats. The 70 GeV cap depends on thermal history; it falls to ~60 GeV at a common temperature. The SN treatment is one-zone and ignores flavor conversion in the envelope.
