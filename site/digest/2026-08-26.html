Strong CP, one rung down

A diffuse, methods-heavy day whose clearest through-line extends yesterday’s strong-CP result. Li et al. arXiv:2608.23685 give the first direct lattice-QCD determination of the θ-induced CP-violating pion-nucleon coupling — the hadronic matrix element sitting one rung above the nEDM — landing at \(\tilde g_{\pi NN}=0.0306(56)(124)\,\bar\theta\), consistent with the indirect estimate from the strong \(n\)–\(p\) mass splitting. Coming a day after Blum et al.’s nEDM extraction, it fills in the coupling side of the same calculation. Around it: an exclusive \(|V_{cb}|\) update that keeps the \(R(D^*)\) tension alive, neural amplitude analysis, and a rethink of the look-elsewhere effect.

Flavor: |V_cb| and a persistent R(D*)

Deep dives

arXiv:2608.23685 — Direct lattice QCD calculation of the θ-induced CP-violating pion-nucleon coupling (Chuan-Yang Li et al.)

The χQCD collaboration reports the first direct lattice evaluation of the θ-induced CP-odd pion-nucleon coupling, extracting the forward proton matrix element of the isovector pseudoscalar density weighted by the topological charge to first order in \(\bar\theta\). Working with overlap valence fermions on three RBC/UKQCD \(2+1\)-flavor domain-wall ensembles at a single spacing (\(a=0.1105\) fm), they use the index-theorem-consistent overlap definition of \(Q\) and cluster-decomposition error reduction (CDER) to tame the volume-enhanced noise, while explicitly including the \(\bar\theta\)-induced parity mixing of the external nucleon (the \(2\alpha_1 g_P\) term). A key simplification over their earlier nEDM work: the ratio at \(q^2=0\) directly isolates the full physical \(\tilde g_P\) with no zero-recoil extrapolation or separate \(\alpha_1\)/\(g_P\) calculation, and \(Z_mZ_P=1\) for overlap removes renormalization. An AIC-weighted average over 7 partially-quenched extrapolation forms yields \(\tilde g_{\pi NN}=0.0306(56)(124)\,\bar\theta\); the full-volume cross-check gives \(0.0305(87)(140)\,\bar\theta\).

This supplies the hadronic-coupling rung above the nEDM, complementing yesterday’s Blum et al. extraction and testing the chiral matching relations that underpin EDM interpretations of light nuclei and diamagnetic atoms. The result is consistent with the indirect \(n\)–\(p\) mass-splitting estimate \(0.0155(20)(16)\,\bar\theta\), though the central value sits somewhat higher. The honest caveats are stark: the dominant second error is the extrapolation-form spread from heavy sea pions (no dedicated partially-quenched χPT expression exists), and the single lattice spacing means discretization effects are not quantified. Lighter-pion ensembles are underway.

arXiv:2608.24332 — Exclusive Determination of \(|V_{cb}|\) from Semileptonic Decays \(B\to D^{(*)}\ell\nu_\ell\) (Xue-Wen Chen et al.)

Chen et al. perform a frequentist BGL global fit combining the latest Belle II \(B\to D\ell\nu\) and \(B\to D^*\ell\nu\) spectra, updated lattice form factors (FNAL/MILC, HPQCD, JLQCD), and correlated large-recoil SCET/LCSR predictions with NLO and next-to-leading-power corrections, plus strong unitarity bounds from four heavy-to-heavy channels with HQET power corrections to \(\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon_c^2)\). Three nested scenarios show LCSR constraints dramatically shrink high-order form-factor uncertainties. The full global fit yields \(|V_{cb}|=(39.18\pm0.47)\times10^{-3}\), with the SM predictions \(R(D)=0.3069\pm0.0080\) and \(R(D^*)=0.2548\pm0.0043\) from the lattice\(\oplus\)LCSR theory fit. The correlated 68% theory ellipse in the \((R(D),R(D^*))\) plane shows “only modest overlap” with individual experimental measurements and a substantial deviation from the experimental average.

The takeaway for today’s flavor theme: even a state-of-the-art SM benchmark leaves the LFU tension intact, against a month where flavor has otherwise consolidated on nulls. The paper is candid about internal frictions — visible tension between the HPQCD \(B\to D^*\) dataset and other lattice results, and mild lattice-vs-LCSR discord, though points overlap within errors. Their \(|V_{cb}|\) sits \(1.7\times10^{-3}\) below a recent Bayesian angular analysis, attributed to methodological choices (correlated LCSR inputs, dropping electron-mode data with known \(e\)/\(\mu\) tension, frequentist vs. MCMC). Local angular deviations from Belle II exist but carry no significance. Whether the residual \(R(D^{(*)})\) gap is theory systematics or new physics remains open pending better lattice/LCSR control and Belle II statistics.

arXiv:2608.23750 — S-matrix informed neural networks for amplitude analysis (Wyatt A. Smith et al.)

The Joint Physics Analysis Center introduces SINNs — neural representations of partial-wave amplitudes on the real axis that enforce S-matrix principles by construction. A shared trunk feeds channel-specific heads, with threshold barrier factors (\(q^{2\ell+1}\)) and the inelasticity map imposed algebraically in postprocessing (guaranteeing \(0\le\eta\le1\) and elastic unitarity below thresholds), while analyticity and crossing enter numerically through a twice-subtracted Roy-equation loss evaluated over \(2m_\pi<\sqrt{s}<1.1\) GeV. Training uses a six-phase curriculum with UPGrad multi-objective gradient handling to prevent the dispersive integrals from collapsing the fit to trivial solutions. Applied to \(\pi\pi\) scattering, the framework recovers the expected \(\sigma/f_0(500)\), \(\rho(770)\), and \(f_0(980)\) features and yields scattering lengths \(a_0^0=0.222^{+0.014}_{-0.014}\), \(a_0^2=-0.041^{+0.005}_{-0.006}\,m_\pi^{-1}\), consistent with Colangelo et al. and García-Martín et al. (with honestly larger bands, reflecting genuine amplitude-space sampling).

The methodological headline is “spectral response clustering”: each experiment is upweighted inside constrained ensembles, a symmetrized \(\chi^2\)-response matrix is spectrally embedded and Ward-clustered, and groups in tension are pruned automatically — replacing heuristic data selection. Two rounds cut the historically inconsistent \(\pi\pi\) database to 18 experiments/621 points, dropping the deep-dip Protopopescu and several Grayer/Hyams solutions, lowering median loss from 7.2 to 3.1. Robustness is well-documented: a 100-architecture sweep shifts no central value by more than \(0.42\delta x\); closure tests recover a known CFD amplitude (\(|z|<0.4\)); and an ablation shows removing Roy constraints leaves data description untouched but degrades scattering lengths outside the universal band. Caveats acknowledged: the selected dataset implicitly depends on the selection architecture, unknown experimental systematics likely dominate, and the \(K\bar K\) cusp in the \(S_0\) phase is deliberately not imposed. The workflow is presented as transferable to \(\pi K\), \(\pi N\), and beyond.
