Neutrino mixing doesn’t run observably

Barenboim et al. arXiv:2610.28744 argue that a growing literature on observable RG running of neutrino mixing makes one mistake. That literature proposes zero-distance flavor change, shifted CP phases and odd astrophysical flavor ratios. In the authors’ account, mixing is fixed by the on-shell self-energy, and setting \(\mu=Q\) only reintroduces large logs that undo the running. They check this in the MSSM, two seesaw models and SMEFT, and add a data bound: short-baseline experiments already limit any angle change between production and detection to 0.7°–2.4°. The day’s other result with direct consequences is a Gaia-anchored halo tail that undercuts last week’s halo rescue of the LZ Higgsino.

LZ: the halo tail gets measured

X. Zhang et al. arXiv:2610.28731 combine a Gaia-reconstructed Milky Way with FIRE-2’s best LMC analog. They find no local dark matter faster than ~900 km/s, and the inferred LZ splitting moves by up to ~130 keV depending on the tail. That matters because Graham et al. (10-16) could rescue the thermal Higgsino from the solar-capture bound only with a tail extended to 1100 km/s. If this reconstruction holds, that tail is not there.

Two papers sit around it: - Ibe et al. arXiv:2610.29335 avoid the Sun by going heavy, to a 10²–10⁴ TeV pseudo-Dirac doublet. Deep dives

arXiv:2610.28744 — The renormalization-group running of neutrino mixing is undetectable (Gabriela Barenboim et al.)

Barenboim, Bustamante and Liu argue that every experiment measures one physical mixing matrix \(\hat U\). They define it by the eigenvectors of the on-shell physical mass matrix \(m_{\rm eff}=m(\mu)+\Sigma_M-\tfrac12(\Sigma_K^Tm+m\Sigma_K)\), which does not depend on \(\mu\). Momentum transfer \(Q\) never flows through the external-leg self-energy. If one sets \(\mu=Q\), that self-energy picks up \(\ln(Q^2/M_X^2)\) terms that rotate \(U(Q)\) back to \(\hat U\). The premise in the prior literature keeps \(U(Q)\) but drops these logs, which the authors call an inconsistent truncation.

Only vertex and box corrections carry both flavor and \(Q\) dependence, and they lack the \(1/(m_i-m_j)\) degeneracy enhancement. The paper gives their sizes in each setting: - MSSM: a flavor-diagonal ~0.7% rescaling of the \(\nu_\tau\) coupling at 10 TeV. - GeV HNL: at most \(4\times10^{-9}\). - Light-scalar seesaw: zero at one loop.

For Model 2 of Babu et al., the claimed running disappears once the Dirac masses are allowed to run along with \(Y_N\).

Independently of the theory argument, NuTeV and NOMAD limits on zero-baseline transitions would require \(|\Delta\theta_{23}|<0.7^\circ\), \(|\Delta\theta_{12}|<2.3^\circ\), \(|\Delta\theta_{13}|<2.4^\circ\) and \(|\Delta\delta_{\rm CP}|<16^\circ\) between \(m_\pi\) and a few GeV, if the premise held. This bound excludes the \(\beta_\delta=0.15\) used to illustrate a proposed reactor signal.

Caveats: - The data bounds assume one parameter shifts at a time, so correlated shifts could partly compensate. - They do not apply when running starts above a few GeV, as in the MSSM case or the ~5 GeV double-bang configuration. There only the theory argument applies. - Off-diagonal vertex terms beyond the SM, up to ~\(10^{-2}\), survive and act as non-standard charged-current interactions. - Two of the targeted papers are the authors’ own, including a step-at-\(Q=M\) HNL paper they now say also used an incorrect \(\delta m\). That self-correction gives the refutation more weight.

arXiv:2610.28731 — Simulation-Informed High-Velocity Dark Matter in the Effective Theory of Direct Detection (Xiuyuan Zhang et al.)

Zhang, Herrera, Thoyas and Necib build a local dark matter (DM) velocity distribution from two pieces: - a Gaia DR3 reconstruction, combining Gaia–Sausage–Enceladus stars convolved with a simulation-calibrated kernel and a generalized-Gaussian diffuse component; - the m12b LMC analog from FIRE-2, which contributes 32 particles at 738–846 km/s and 0.3% of the local density.

The resulting laboratory-frame endpoints, and the maximum accessible splitting \(\delta_{\max}\) for a 1 TeV particle on xenon, are:

Distribution Endpoint (km/s) \(\delta_{\max}\) (keV)
Standard Halo Model (SHM) 793 381
Reconstruction alone 835 423
Reconstruction + LMC 846 434
Auriga-25 (Au-25) 975 575

Limits across all 14 non-relativistic EFT operators coincide below \(\delta\approx200\) keV and then fan out. Velocity-suppressed operators separate first.

For \(\mathcal O_1\), the region that fits the LZ 248 keV event plus empty sidebands sits at different splittings: - SHM: \(\delta=304\)–360 keV. - FIRE-based: 320–390 keV. - Au-25: 376–448 keV, with mass confined to 171–468 GeV.

In the digest’s context, a tail reaching 1100 km/s would need support well beyond anything here.

The authors are explicit that the result is limited: - The tail rests on 32 particles. - The Sun has to be placed out of the disk to match the LMC geometry. With the observer in the disk, the LMC particles come out slower. - The Milky Way-only endpoint has a 16–84% posterior spread of 773–912 km/s. - Their counting likelihood differs from LZ’s published limits by up to ~3×. - They conclude that a single LMC analog cannot define the tail uniquely.

arXiv:2610.28742 — Radiative corrections to double-β decay and their impact on experimental analyses (Jordy de Vries et al.)

This is the full companion to the authors’ earlier Letter. In a heavy-nucleus EFT with explicit \(1^+\) intermediate states, de Vries, Mereghetti, el Morabit and Sandner compute all \(\mathcal O(\alpha)\) virtual and real corrections for arbitrary excitation energy \(\omega_n\). They also give new closed-form heavy-propagator triangle and box integrals via an auxiliary-mass trick.

Main structural results: - No \(Z\)-enhanced terms survive. The \(\mathcal O(\alpha Z)\) and \(\mathcal O(\alpha Z^2)\) terms cancel class by class, except the static Coulomb piece already contained in the Fermi functions. - A new topology matters. The electron–electron box has no single-β analogue and brings in dependence on the two-electron invariant mass \(s\). - The correction factorizes at large \(\omega_n\). At leading order in \(1/\omega_n\) it is the tree-level rate times a “double-weak Sirlin function” \(f_{2\nu}(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_{12})\). This function differs from the sum of two single-β Sirlin functions, and its \(\mu\) dependence cancels against \(g_V^4\) (\(g_V-1\approx1.9\)–2.0% at \(\mu=2E_0\)). - One new nuclear input appears at next order. It is \(\zeta_{31}\), a log-weighted \(M_{GT}\) ratio, which the authors find negligible. - There is a non-trivial check. The total-visible-energy spectrum has no \(\ln m_e\) dependence as \(m_e\to0\), as the Kinoshita–Lee–Nauenberg theorem requires.

Because the electron-only and calorimetric (\(\epsilon_{\rm tot}\)) spectra differ, the correction depends on how a detector handles the real photon. The authors provide the fully differential photon spectrum and ask experiments to simulate it. For \(0\nu\beta\beta\), the inclusive rate rises by ~5%, independent of the nuclear matrix elements and \(m_{\beta\beta}\).

The extracted text ends before the numerical \(\xi_{31}\)-bias results, so their size cannot be quoted here.

Acknowledged approximations: - \(\mathcal O(\alpha ZRE_e)\) finite-size terms are not yet mapped rigorously. - The Fermi function \(F\) is also used for the \(\beta_1\beta_2y_{12}\) term. - The \(1/\omega_n\) expansion holds for \(^{136}\)Xe, but the authors cite larger deviations for \(^{82}\)Se.
