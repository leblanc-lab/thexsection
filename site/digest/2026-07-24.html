Tension solutions hit two walls

The day’s coherent weight is cosmological, and it runs against the tension-solvers rather than for them. Two independent papers close off room for raising \(H_0\): Poulin et al. arXiv:2607.20635 show that the primordial-deuterium discrepancy grows to \(\sim3\sigma\) precisely in the early-dark-energy models that were supposed to help, while Sabogal et al. arXiv:2607.21244 erect a percent-level “shape wall” against late-time modifications independent of the known normalization wall. That’s the natural next beat after last week’s \(H_0\) World Cup verdict — the referees are now poking holes in the winners. The other genuine milestone is LHCb’s demonstration of real-time processing at 32 Tbps arXiv:2607.21492, the direct descendant of the July 17 GPU-Kalman-filter work.

Themes

New walls for the Hubble tension

Poulin et al. arXiv:2607.20635 find that using the CMB baryon density, standard BBN sits in \(\sim2\sigma\) tension with the latest deuterium measurement — worsening to \(3\sigma\) under EDE, which they resolve only by invoking a very early dark-energy component (\(\Delta H/H\simeq0.087\) during deuterium burning), reading it as a hint of yet another light scalar. Sabogal et al. arXiv:2607.21244 show late-time \(E(z)\) modifications can raise \(H_0\) by at most \(\sim2\%\) against the required \(\sim8\%\). Both sharpen, rather than solve, the picture the World Cup left; da Silva Pereira et al.’s O(3) no-scale interacting-DE model arXiv:2607.20320 is the model-building counterpoint targeting DESI’s phantom crossing.

Deep dives

arXiv:2607.21492 — A converged architecture for processing 32 Tbps of physics data in real-time at the LHCb experiment (Roel Aaij et al.)

The upgraded LHCb now reads out its entire detector — roughly 30 MHz of non-empty pp collisions, 32 Tbps — with no hardware low-level trigger, a factor-40 increase in data rate over the previous system. The engineering result: the full stream is filtered in real time on just 164 nodes (2× AMD EPYC 7502, 2× NVIDIA RTX A5000 each), applying a 30:1 reduction in HLT1 entirely on GPU. The design rests on three moves: commercial-off-the-shelf networking (an InfiniBand HDR fat-tree treating event-building as an all-to-all personalized exchange), second-scale host-memory buffering that eliminates incast without deep-buffer switches, and a “converged” node hosting readout, event-building, and GPU filtering together via zero-copy/RDMA. Algorithmically, a multi-event GPU scheduler batches ~600–800 events per sequence, with mixed-precision reconstruction (single precision for a truncated-region Kalman filter; half precision for VELO storage and SciFi seeding). They report 41 MHz peak combined throughput, 36% headroom over the 30 MHz requirement, and demonstrate a full EB+HLT1 integration test sustaining 160 GB/s memory and 115 GB/s PCIe per node with no performance drops.

Why it matters: this is the production endpoint of the month’s real-time-reconstruction wave (the July 17 GPU-Kalman work), and the comparison table makes the claim concrete — the highest software-processed data rate of any experiment, exceeding ATLAS/CMS (1.6 Tbps into software after a 300× L1 cut) and ALICE (7.2 Tbps). Removing the low-level trigger reduces selection bias by considering the whole event. It’s an engineering demonstration paper rather than a physics measurement; the throughput figures are commissioning-phase benchmarks.

arXiv:2607.20635 — What could an emerging Big Bang Nucleosynthesis discrepancy be hinting at? (Vivian Poulin et al.)

Using PRIMAT (rewritten in Python) with the Cooke et al. deuterium measurement (10⁵D/H = 2.527±0.030), the BBN-inferred baryon density ω_b ≃ 0.0219 sits ~2.1σ below the ΛCDM CMB value and ~3.1σ below the higher ω_b = 0.02272±0.00014 preferred in EDE fits — early-dark-energy solutions to H₀ generically raise ω_b and thus worsen the deuterium problem. The authors test three expansion histories against the EDE ω_b target: ΛCDM, a ΔN_eff extension, and a transient “very early dark energy” (vEDE) component peaking during deuterium burning. Constant extra radiation fails (Q_DMAP = 3.1σ, ΔAIC ≃ +1.2): it raises Y_p too efficiently at weak freeze-out. vEDE succeeds, because a boost localized at T_D ≃ 0.03 MeV shortens the residual deuterium-burning window (raising D/H at large ω_b) while barely touching ⁴He synthesis. The combined fit gives ΔH/H(T_D) = 0.087⁺⁰·⁰³⁶₋₀.₀₃₇, no residual tension (Q_DMAP = 0.7σ, ΔAIC ≃ −5.3).

Why it matters: this is the natural referee’s-follow-up to the H₀ World Cup — the very models that win on H₀ lose on primordial deuterium, and the resolution demands yet another light scalar, framed as an axiverse window at the BBN epoch. Caveats are candidly stated: the CMB is compressed to a single Gaussian ω_b likelihood (not a joint fit); nuclear-rate uncertainties enter only through tabulated MC errors; the discrepancy is partly rate-driven (PArthENoPE agrees better with the data), and the updated D/H = 2.508 softens the ΛCDM tension to 2.7σ.

arXiv:2607.21244 — Hubble tension: the shape wall (Miguel A. Sabogal et al.)

The standard no-go against late-time H₀ solutions is a normalization wall (BAO fixes r_d·H₀). This paper erects an independent shape wall: at fixed acoustic scale θ_s and sound horizon, δH₀/H₀ ≃ δI/I, where I ≡ ∫dz/E(z). Using Gaussian-process reconstructions of the dimensionless expansion rate E(z) from unanchored SNeIa (PantheonPlus, DESY5Dvk, Union3) and DESI DR2 BAO — marginalizing over the r_d·h amplitude to retain only shape information — they find E(z) can deviate at most ≈5–7% from fiducial ΛCDM. The directly-constrained low-redshift integral I_< (z ≲ 2.3) is pinned to the percent level: δI_</I_< ≲ 0.5% (68%), ≲1.5% (95%) across SNeIa+BAO combinations, mapping to (δH₀/H₀)_< ≲ 0.8% at 95%. Adding a model-guided estimate of the unconstrained high-z tail I_> (from ΛCDM/wCDM fits) yields a total δH₀/H₀ ≲ 2% (conservative) to ≲3.7% (maximally permissive) — versus the ≈9.3% needed to close the tension, leaving ≳4.3σ residual.

Why it matters: alongside Poulin et al., this is the day’s second wall against tension-solvers, and it is statistically stronger than the normalization wall since it survives redshift-independent BAO recalibration. The key methodological point — pointwise E(z) envelopes overstate freedom because integrating I preserves cross-redshift correlations — is well argued. Honest caveats: I_> bounds are model-dependent (not nonparametric); flat FLRW, smoothness, and the distance-duality relation are assumed. The explicit surviving loophole is new physics at z ≪ 0.01, below SNeIa reach.

arXiv:2604.25726 — Imprint of domain wall annihilation on induced gravitational waves (Rishav Roshan)

Roshan studies a minimal Z₂-symmetric real-scalar extension of the SM in which domain walls, made unstable by a small bias term, annihilate and dump most of their energy into the long-lived scalar. If that scalar dominates before decaying, it drives an early matter-dominated era that dramatically amplifies scalar-induced GWs (working in the non-linear regime via the Fernandez et al. simulation framework), while its eventual decay injects entropy that dilutes the direct domain-wall signal. The headline result is a distinctive double-peaked spectrum — a higher-frequency peak from wall annihilation, a lower-frequency peak from enhanced induced GWs — with both peaks potentially visible to THEIA and μARES, so detection in one band predicts the other.

The paper’s appeal is the correlated multi-band signature from a single symmetry-breaking event, a cleaner falsifiable target than isolated-source studies. ~5 citations in roughly two months, likely from the active domain-wall/induced-GW community (many self-citations to King, Borah, and collaborators are visible), reflecting momentum rather than settled impact.

arXiv:2606.30354 — Solution of Canonical Differential Equations for Integrals on Arbitrary Geometries (Michał Czakon et al.)

Czakon and Tancredi tackle a real bottleneck: canonical ε-factorized systems beyond the polylogarithmic case have connection matrices containing transcendental functions — elliptic or Calabi-Yau periods — for which closed forms are often unavailable, blocking numerical evaluation. Their key observation is that these functions are not arbitrary: since the original master integrals obey rational differential equations, so do the transcendental building blocks. Adjoining these auxiliary equations yields an enlarged purely-rational system solvable by standard numerical ODE methods, with correct analytic continuation guaranteed automatically. They demonstrate on the 220 two-loop masters for di-jet and γ+jet production through a heavy-quark loop — 16 square roots plus elliptic periods — and ship a lightweight, commercial-software-free C++ package built on Boost’s Bulirsch-Stoer solver, achieving sub-second double-precision evaluations.

~5 citations since late June. The draw is a general, deployable recipe that sidesteps the hardest obstacle to using canonical bases in realistic amplitudes: no special-function implementation needed, universally applicable to any Feynman integral. Given the surge of parallel efforts (Baur–Duhr, Liu–Zhang) it references, this lands in a hot and crowded space, positioned as the production-oriented option.
