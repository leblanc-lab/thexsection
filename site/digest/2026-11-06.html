No neutrino route to birefringence

Reig arXiv:2611.07578 tests the proposal that photons couple to the cosmic neutrino-asymmetry current, and finds it cannot produce the hinted isotropic CMB polarization rotation. That hint stands at 4.8σ, or 3.5σ after dust, and has been read as an axion Chern–Simons effect if real. The argument runs in three steps:

  • Gauge invariance forces a Kalb–Ramond field dual to a massless axion.
  • The signal then fixes \(g_{a\gamma}/f_\ell\) at \(\Lambda_*\simeq10\) TeV.
  • \(SU(2)_L\) fixes an electron coupling that \(g_e-2\) plus light-shining-through-wall searches already exclude, as does stellar evolution.

The day’s main data paper is ALICE asking how low in multiplicity partonic flow survives. A two-generator QED study also addresses the radiative corrections behind the BaBar ππ dispute.

Themes

Small systems, at the floor and at the nucleus

ALICE arXiv:2611.08327 finds baryon–meson \(v_2\) splitting consistent with partonic collectivity in pp and p–Pb down to \(N_{\rm ch}\geq25\). In the 15–25 bin the ratio keeps its shape, but significance drops to ~2σ (p–Pb) and <1σ (pp). After last week’s φ-flow result in O–O, this is the other end of the question: where the effect stops being measurable.

Duguet et al. arXiv:2611.08122 work from the nuclear side. Coupled-cluster emulators show that O and Ne quadrupole eccentricities respond linearly to all 16 N²LO chiral LECs, and that Ne–Ne mostly probes the spin-orbit force. The light-ion runs past oxygen become nuclear-force data, not just geometry inputs.

QED radiation, done twice

Delorme et al. arXiv:2611.07067 implement resummed QED corrections to \(e^+e^-\to\pi^+\pi^-(\gamma)\) in both KKMC and SHERPA. The two agree to ≲0.04% at first order, and the paper adds a new rule for radiative return, where first-order corrections are O(1). That is the regime of BaBar’s ISR ππ result and its ~8×10⁻¹⁰ internal spread (11-04). One limit: the pions are still point-like scalars dressed with a form factor.

Two companion papers: - Abudinén et al. arXiv:2611.07366 add matrix-element-corrected QED antenna showers for hadron decays in Pythia+Vincia. - Gauld et al. arXiv:2611.08221 find EW corrections to neutrino CC DIS can exceed 10%. Above 10⁴ GeV, photon-initiated and coherent-\(W\) pieces contribute sizeably and are missing from POWHEG-BOX.

Top-philic, but which spin?

Lessa et al. arXiv:2611.08459 show a coloured scalar and a vector-like fermion match onto the same EFT operators. Only above-threshold \(p_T\) shapes separate them, and not easily. Banfi et al. arXiv:2611.08388 report NLO \(t\bar t\) with 2HDM scalar/pseudoscalar–QCD interference and full spin correlations in POWHEG. It is a status report, but it is the tool the toponium-vs-CP-odd-Higgs question needs.

Deep dives

arXiv:2611.07578 — Can cosmic neutrinos induce a cosmic birefringence signal? (Mario Reig)

The Kalb–Ramond completion of the neutrino-current operator \(j_\mu A_\nu\tilde F^{\mu\nu}\) is exactly dual to a massless axion. In that dual theory the signal fixes \(g_{a\gamma}/f_\ell=1/\Lambda_*^2\) independently of the 2-form normalisation. The Yin–Komatsu fit (\(44^{+12}_{-7}\) TeV\(\sqrt{\xi_{\nu_e}}\)) gives \(\Lambda_*\simeq10\) TeV. Because \(j^\mu\) must be an \(SU(2)_L\) singlet, the axion picks up an electron coupling with \(g_{a\gamma}g_{ae}=(1-c_R)m_e/\Lambda_*^2\simeq5.3\times10^{-12}\) GeV\(^{-1}\) for \(\xi_{\nu_e}=0.05\). The bounds then close every branch:

  • Left-handed current. ALPS II requires \(g_{ae}\gtrsim1.8\times10^{-3}\). That shifts \(a_e\) by \(-2\times10^{-8}\), against agreement at \(\sim10^{-12}\). The product also exceeds the CAST helioscope bound by eleven orders of magnitude. Stellar trapping does not rescue it: trapped axions boost solar conductivity, and protection requires \(g_{ae}\gtrsim40\).
  • Electron lepton number (\(c_R=1\)). This current is anomalous. Since ALPS II caps \(f_\ell\lesssim0.3\) GeV, it predicts BR\((Z\to\gamma\varphi)\gtrsim0.1\), against L3’s \(10^{-6}\).
  • Higgs-dressed currents. These give excessive \(W\) and \(h\) decays to \(\varphi\).
  • Anomaly-free currents (e.g. \(B-L\)). These leave a contact term \(\gtrsim5\times10^5G_F\). LEP and neutrino scattering exclude it, and it would also give \(N_{\rm eff}\approx12\). Alternatively the axion velocity is set once at asymmetry generation, and relic neutrinos play no role in the rotation.

Why it matters: any confirmed isotropic rotation should be read as a light photon-coupled axion, not as a measurement of the relic-neutrino asymmetry.

Caveats: the Stückelberg alternative is dismissed on EFT-validity grounds (breakdown at ~keV, below BBN) rather than excluded by data. The stellar-trapping treatment uses order-of-magnitude solar-core estimates.

arXiv:2611.08327 — Exploring the limit of partonic collectivity in small collision systems at the LHC (ALICE Collaboration)

ALICE extracts identified-hadron \(v_2(p_T)\) in Run 2 p–Pb (198M events) and pp (226M events) in multiplicity bins. The method combines TPC–FMD long-range correlations, a template fit, and a three-subevent construction. Baryon–meson splitting over \(3<p_T<5\) GeV/\(c\):

\(N_{\rm ch}\) bin p–Pb pp
\(\geq50\) 6.13σ —
25–50 5.28σ 4.78σ
15–25 1.95σ 0.35σ

Mass ordering and the ~2.2 GeV/\(c\) crossing persist in every bin. In low-multiplicity p–Pb the baryon/meson ratios stay above unity past the crossing but return to unity at 4–6 GeV/\(c\). In low-multiplicity pp, \(v_2(\Lambda)/v_2(K)\) sits below unity.

Why it matters: this is the first scan of where coalescence-type signatures fade. The ≥25 bins in pp now look like high-multiplicity p–Pb. In the 15–25 bin the authors explicitly decline to claim presence or absence of splitting, because uncertainties dominate. It complements last week’s O–O φ-flow result from the opposite direction: the floor rather than the reach.

Caveats: - The non-flow template baseline is \(N_{\rm ch}<15\), directly adjacent to the lowest signal bin. Baseline variation is already the dominant systematic (4% p–Pb, 7% pp). - Equal \(N_{\rm ch}\) in pp and p–Pb need not mean equivalent physics. - Run 3 statistics are needed to settle the low bins.

arXiv:2611.07067 — QED corrections to \(e^+e^-\to\pi^+\pi^-(\gamma)\) with amplitude-level resummation (St'ephane Delorme et al.)

Amplitude-level YFS resummation for \(\pi^+\pi^-(\gamma)\) is implemented independently in KKMC (CEEX) and SHERPA (ACRAIC). Both include exact one- and two-photon trees, the full one-loop correction with initial–final boxes, and the exact one-loop correction to single-photon emission. First-order fiducial cross sections agree to ≲0.04% across all five STRONG2020 scenarios. The second-order theory-nuisance bands are:

  • ~0.005% at CMD
  • ~0.1% at KLOE-I
  • ~0.02% at KLOE-II
  • ~0.4% at BESIII
  • ~0.9% in the 10 GeV B scenario, the setup matching BaBar’s ISR measurement

The new radiative-return parameter handles the regime where the first-order correction \(r_1\) is O(1), up to order −1 at BESIII and B. It uses the last relative correction as its template and normalises it by the YFS exponent \(\gamma=2(\alpha/\pi)(L-1)\). At O(α) it dominates the bands (~6% at BESIII, ~9% at B) and covers the −5% and −10% second-order steps. The exception is low \(M_{\pi\pi}\), where a second hard photon enters at leading order. There an explicit \(\pi\pi\gamma\) NLO calculation is needed; it agrees with BabaYaga@NLO to ~1%, and to ~0.1% around the ρ.

At KLOE-I both generators follow BabaYaga@NLO, not PHOKHARA, in the pion polar-angle shape. GVMD loops lower the CMD-3 charge asymmetry by up to \(1.15\times10^{-2}\) near 0.78 GeV, so form-factor modelling dominates that observable.

Caveats: - Pions are point-like scalars with a factorised form factor, and vacuum polarisation is off throughout. - Two-loop boxes, the genuine two-loop pion vertex and O(α³) are missing. - The KLOE-I second-order steps differ by ~0.06%, several times the statistical error. - The radiative-return parameter underestimates the missing higher orders wherever real and virtual corrections cancel.
