YR4 retired, γ tightened

Two numbers people quote by default were replaced today.

  • Higgs cross sections. The LHC Higgs Working Group’s Report 5 inclusive cross sections (Hayes et al. arXiv:2611.26281) supersede YR4. Central values are unchanged within the old errors, and most uncertainties shrink. PDFs are now the leading uncertainty for the best-predicted modes. With no N³LO PDF set available, the gluon-fusion PDF-order mismatch uncertainty is doubled relative to YR4. ATLAS’s Run 2 combination had already become theory-limited, and this is where that limit now sits.
  • CKM γ. LHCb’s new combination (arXiv:2611.26449) gives γ = 63.2°⁺²·³₋₂.₂ and the most precise charm-mixing parameters to date. It includes the first γ measurement from 2024 data.

The rest of the day: a resummation ambiguity that dwarfs last week’s GW worries, and a third independent hit on superradiance.

Themes

GW forecasts: the weak link moves upstream

Last week the nucleation rate held up (~8% from one loop), and the blame for unreliable forecasts shifted to wall hydrodynamics. Today the effective potential itself is in question.

  • Zhao et al. arXiv:2611.26417 compare thermal resummation prescriptions in 2HDMs. Partial dressing and Arnold–Espinosa differ by up to nine orders of magnitude in GW peak amplitude, and partial dressing has its own path ambiguity worth a factor ~3.
  • Qiu et al. arXiv:2611.26303 propose an improved partial dressing scheme that removes that scheme’s overlapping-momentum divergences.
  • Guo et al. arXiv:2611.25581 find an extra \(q^{-2}\) high-frequency tail when the sound-wave source is dissipative.

Superradiance, third mechanism-level hit

Bošković et al. arXiv:2611.26541 find that self-gravity depletes superradiant clouds by orders of magnitude. It dominates over self-interactions above \(f_a\gtrsim6\times10^{16}\) GeV\((\alpha/0.1)\), essentially the crossover Foster et al. found on 11-17. Bottaro (11-19) reached a similar conclusion via self-interactions. One new wrinkle: a binary companion can partly offset the depletion, so environment now enters the black-hole spin bounds on axions.

LZ, photon-mediated

Wang et al. arXiv:2611.25931 (ALP→dark photon) and Yin et al. arXiv:2611.26274 (axino→bino) post near-identical templates. Both use endothermic photon exchange, a three-bin Poisson fit, and halo plus boosted benchmarks, with halo fits at ~1.2 TeV/346 keV and ~506 GeV/326 keV. Neither abstract mentions solar capture, the constraint every LZ model since Pospelov has been judged against.

Measured νe cross sections

  • SBND. Aliaga-Soplin et al. arXiv:2611.25098 report the highest-statistics inclusive νe CC cross section on argon: \(3.90\pm0.18\pm0.41\times10^{-39}\) cm². The data prefer generators that predict lower cross sections.
  • MINERvA surrogate. Dey et al. arXiv:2611.26271 release a surrogate MINERvA detector response that reproduces held-out 2p2h events.

Both feed the percent-level interaction-modeling target that Machado’s mass-ordering analysis set on 11-11.

  • arXiv:2611.26449 (LHCb): A joint γ/charm-mixing fit, plus \(A_{CP}(KK)\) and \(A_{CP}(\pi\pi)\) under different theory assumptions. That bears on charm CPV, where the SM still falls short. The abstract gives no asymmetry values.
  • arXiv:2611.26417 (Zhao et al.): Puts a number on how much resummation choice alone moves a GW forecast. Expect it to be quoted against any LISA projection.
  • arXiv:2611.26026 (Hager et al.): Super-leading logs at lepton colliders, arising from Glauber exchange off photon splittings. The authors conjecture that soft–collinear factorization fails even in pure QED, which is relevant to every FCC-ee non-global observable.
  • arXiv:2611.26541 (Bošković et al.): A short, independent confirmation that free-field cloud models are never realized.

Deep dives

arXiv:2611.26281 — Predictions for Inclusive Production Cross Sections of the Higgs Boson at the LHC and HL-LHC (Robin Hayes et al.)

At 13 TeV and \(m_H=125\) GeV, the R5 gluon-fusion cross section is 48.04 pb, 1.11% below YR4’s 48.58 pb. The uncertainty budget is reshuffled:

  • Shrank: \(\delta(\text{theory})\) goes from \(^{+3.41}_{-5.57}\%\) to \(^{+1.63}_{-4.67}\%\). The truncation and \(1/m_t\) terms disappear because exact N³LO HTL and NNLO top and top–bottom mass effects are now included. \(\delta(t,b,c)\) falls from 0.83% to 0.34%, and PDF+\(\alpha_s\) goes from ±3.2% to \(^{+2.69}_{-2.28}\%\).
  • Doubled: \(\delta(\text{PDF-TH})\) goes from 1.16% to 2.31%. The reason is concrete. Swapping PDF4LHC21 for the MSHT20×NNPDF40 aN³LO combination lowers σ(ggF) by about 4.7% at 13 TeV. MSHT and NNPDF aN³LO themselves disagree by about 3%, so the direct NNLO→aN³LO difference cannot be trusted as the estimate.

Conversion tables to aN³LO are supplied. QED DGLAP evolution is included for ggF (−0.74% at 13.6 TeV). An N⁴LO soft-virtual cross-check shifts the result by only −0.1%.

For VBF the two aN³LO fits agree, which makes the genuine order effect (+2.2%) meaningful. It is still left out of the central value. Users wanting a conservative band can add ±2.2% in quadrature, which takes the total from \(^{+2.5}_{-2.4}\%\) to about ±3.3%.

Why it matters: this is now the normalisation for every signal strength. For the best-predicted modes the limiting factor is a community decision on N³LO PDFs, not a missing calculation.

Caveats: - The ggF scale band widened, not narrowed. This follows from switching to an on-shell \(m_t\) and a 3-point scale variation. - The photon/QED treatment is admittedly not uniform across production modes.

arXiv:2611.26449 — Progress in measurements of the CKM angle γ and charm mixing at LHCb (LHCb collaboration et al.)

The joint beauty–charm fit gives:

  • \(\gamma=(63.2^{+2.3}_{-2.2})^\circ\), versus \((65.4^{+3.8}_{-4.2})^\circ\) in LHCb’s 2021 combination.
  • \(x_{12}=(4.2^{+0.4}_{-0.5})\times10^{-3}\) and \(y_{12}=(6.18^{+0.17}_{-0.18})\times10^{-3}\).
  • \(\phi_2^M=(4^{+11}_{-10})\) mrad and \(\phi_2^\Gamma=(19^{+23}_{-24})\) mrad.

Two technical points stand out. First, the \(B\to Dh\) inputs constrain \(\delta_D^{K\pi}\) well enough to sharpen \(y_{12}\). Second, ignoring charm mixing would bias γ by roughly the current precision, so the fit must be simultaneous. The first Run 3 input (\(B^-\to Dh^-\), \(D\to K_S^0h^+h^-\), 2024 only, 5.8 fb⁻¹) already reaches 6.7° by itself.

Why it matters: - The direct γ precision now approaches the indirect fits, quoted as \(66.2^{+0.8}_{-3.2}\)° (frequentist) and \(65.7\pm1.3\)° (Bayesian). The central value sits about 2.5° below the Bayesian number. - The mixing phases reach the ~10 mrad level, against theory estimates of 1–5 mrad. CPV in mixing is still unseen. - \(A_{CP}(KK)\) and \(A_{CP}(\pi\pi)\) are now fitted internally rather than treated as external inputs. The text supplied here stops before their values. The paper flags that the U-spin relation \(a^d_{KK}/a^d_{\pi\pi}=-1\), even with branching-fraction corrections, is fragile if the strong phases are small.

Caveats: - The baseline assumes universality of the mixing phases, which would fail if \(\cot\delta_{h^+h^-}\sim10\), and neglects CPV in CF and DCS decays. - The Run 1–2 γ inputs were not reinterpreted with the updated BESIII strong-phase inputs. - Correlations are only partially included.

arXiv:2611.26417 — Thermal Resummation Effects on Electroweak Phase Transitions and Gravitational Waves in Conformal and Non-Conformal 2HDMs (Yu-Bo Zhao et al.)

The authors apply multi-field partial dressing (PD, full thermal functions, scalar gap equations with field-dependent mixing), Parwani, and Arnold–Espinosa to identical zero-temperature 2HDM points. Any difference is therefore purely the resummation choice.

Conformal 2HDM: - PD yields \(\alpha=\mathcal O(1)\) and \(T_p/T_c\simeq0.5\), while the truncated schemes give \(T_p/T_c\gtrsim0.8\). - Wherever all three complete, peak amplitudes order as PD > Parwani > AE. - The authors’ own earlier conclusion of limited conformal supercooling turns out to be an artefact of the truncated schemes.

Non-conformal 2HDM: there is no universal ordering. Over extended regions the prescription even decides between a first-order transition, a second-order transition, and symmetry non-restoration.

Headline numbers: on benchmarks, PD and AE differ by up to about nine orders of magnitude in peak \(\Omega_{\rm GW}\) and about 50× in peak frequency.

Formal result: the dressed PD tadpole has nonzero curl (Eq. 14), contradicting the path-independence argument of Bahl et al. Changing the integration contour shifts transition temperatures by up to 7% and the GW amplitude by up to about 3×. A follow-up traces the curl to the zero-momentum sunset in the gap equation.

Why it matters: last week the blame for unreliable forecasts moved downstream to wall hydrodynamics. This moves it back upstream to the effective potential, and the spread here dwarfs both.

Caveats: - The PD used is minimal: no sunset combinatorial factors and no lollipops, and their size is untested across the scan. - Gauge bosons get only Parwani-type Debye masses. - Tunnelling is restricted to the SM-like direction, checked at a single benchmark. - The Hubble rate ignores vacuum energy at the most supercooled points. - A non-runaway energy budget is assumed.
