The vibe today

After a week spent rebuilding tools from first principles and confronting the sticker-shock of next-generation facilities, today feels like the field cashing in on that infrastructure investment. Two of the papers that the week’s syntheses flagged as landmarks — the pixel-level confirmation of the Fermi-LAT 20 GeV halo excess (arXiv:2607.08552) and the direct-neutron-readout hadronic calorimeter (arXiv:2607.08587) — actually post today, and they arrive alongside a fresh crop of software releases, a quantum-computing review, and the transformer-based cell-level calorimeter work that has been the throughline of the week’s ML story. It is a builder’s day.

The intellectual center of gravity, though, is neutrinos — specifically the Dirac-versus-Majorana question, attacked from four directions at once. A null half-life limit from AMoRE, a cosmological \(\Delta N_{\rm eff}\) handle on Dirac right-handed neutrinos, a machine-learning-assisted revisit of mass textures, and an unusually abstract algebraic reformulation of particle–antiparticle coherence all converge on the same fundamental ambiguity. Nobody resolved it today, but the sheer variety of angles — nuclear, cosmological, phenomenological, group-theoretic — is a good snapshot of how central this one question remains.

The rest of the day is healthily diffuse: hadron structure and heavy-ion phenomenology, a couple of LISA methodology papers tightening how we report sensitivity, and the usual quiet stream of formal hep-th. No single blockbuster, but a satisfying breadth.

Themes

The Majorana question, from four directions

The AMoRE Collaboration reports the first dedicated search for neutrinoless double electron capture in \(^{40}\)Ca using their \(^{40}\)Ca\(^{100}\)MoO\(_4\) cryogenic detectors, setting \(T^{0\nu}_{1/2} > 1.7\times10^{22}\) yr (arXiv:2607.08039) — a nice demonstration that a \(0\nu\beta\beta\)-optimized crystal can be repurposed for a complementary LNV channel. From cosmology, Liu et al. (arXiv:2607.08082) point out that if the right-handed neutrinos in a \(Z'\)-portal dark matter model are Dirac rather than Majorana, they contribute to \(\Delta N_{\rm eff}\), giving upcoming CMB measurements a distinct lever on the neutrino nature that reshapes the entire detectability landscape. Kitagawa et al. (arXiv:2607.08384) revisit one- and two-zero mass textures under the latest oscillation and cosmological bounds, using flow matching to map the surviving structures and their \(\delta_{\rm CP}\) predictions — the CMB+BAO mass-sum cut does most of the killing. And on the formal end, Romero (arXiv:2607.08739) reorganizes the four-dimensional particle/antiparticle–helicity space of a massive neutrino into an internal \(SU(2)\) pseudospin, recasting the Majorana condition as a projection and classifying pseudo-Dirac splittings and \(0\nu\beta\beta\) mass factors algebraically. Four papers, one question — a fair reflection of where the field’s anxiety lives as JUNO, DUNE, and Hyper-K approach.

Tooling up: software, ML, and quantum

The week’s “rebuild the tools” mood carries straight into today. AMFlow 2.0 (arXiv:2607.08477) brings a new recursion mode, a faster differential-equation solver, and support for modern IBP reducers, benchmarked on a three-loop five-point topology — squarely in the auxiliary-mass-flow ecosystem that keeps multi-loop numerics tractable. On the event-generation side, Doherty et al. (arXiv:2607.08723) attack the perennial negative-weight problem with optimal-transport metrics (Energy Mover’s Distance and a spectral variant) for cell resampling, reducing bias relative to existing techniques — a direct heir to the week’s “Watts per event” sustainability thread. Rodrigo’s overview of quantum computing in QCD (arXiv:2607.08169) surveys the same territory soberly, cataloguing where quantum algorithms might help and where NISQ hardware still can’t. And the transformer-based collimated-photon identifier from low-level calorimeter cells (arXiv:2607.08175) is exactly the calorimetry chapter of the week’s flavor-tagging-to-raw-reconstruction ML arc: cell-level beats shower-shape variables decisively, and the transformer even does direct invariant-mass regression to suppress \(\pi^0/\eta\) fakes.

Dark matter’s velocity puzzle, and a new gravitational window

The 20 GeV Galactic halo excess gets its independent stress-test today: Stenhouse et al. (arXiv:2607.08552) reproduce the cell-aggregated Fermi-LAT result and extend it to a pixel-level likelihood with PSF forward-folding, confirming the spectrum and its \(\sim\)0.55–0.72 TeV WIMP best fit. The honest punchline is that only low-velocity-enhanced (resonant Sommerfeld or Breit-Wigner) annihilation survives the joint dwarf-limit, present-day-rate, and relic-density constraints — and even that requires a resonance fine-tuned to the halo velocity. This is precisely the open question the weekly memory posed, now sharpened. Meanwhile Gan et al. (arXiv:2607.08248) show LISA can probe quadratically coupled ultralight dark matter at two characteristic frequencies and, crucially, without the screening that hobbles terrestrial searches — a genuinely new detection window rather than a reinterpretation.

Detectors and flux inputs for the intensity frontier

Two papers quietly do the unglamorous work the next decade depends on. The Pb/Gd-loaded-scintillator calorimeter (arXiv:2607.08587) reads out delayed neutron-capture energy to recover invisible hadronic energy, improving prompt resolution from 21.8% to 13.3% with no event rejection — a hardware answer to hadronic fluctuations. And Cheng et al. (arXiv:2607.08310) deliver a precision 3D atmospheric-neutrino flux built on the Honda framework, incorporating in-Earth muon propagation for the first time and reducing hadronic systematics across seven detector sites — essential common input for JUNO, Hyper-K, DUNE, ORCA, and the direct-detection neutrino fog.

Deep dives

arXiv:2607.08552 — The 20 GeV Galactic Halo Excess: Pixel-Level Confirmation and Consistency with Sub-TeV WIMP Annihilation (Trinity Rosebud Stenhouse et al.)

Stenhouse, Ghag & Deppisch independently reproduce Totani’s cell-aggregated Fermi-LAT halo excess and extend it to a pixel-level Poisson likelihood on native 0.125° maps with energy-dependent PSF forward-folding and bright-source masking. The two methods agree on the spectrum — a peak at ~20.8 GeV — with the pixel-level normalization ~20% higher for the ρ² halo, confirming the feature is not an artifact of 10° binning. The excess is centrally concentrated (angular profile χ²/ν≃5–7 for NFW vs 30 for isotropic), spectrally and spatially distinct from the few-GeV GCE, and stable under a foreground-systematics suite and an injection-null test (a distorted-ICS mock with no halo yields ≤10% of the observed peak). Prompt s-wave fits give mχ≃0.55 TeV (W⁺W⁻) and 0.72 TeV (bb̄) with ⟨σv⟩≃1×10⁻²⁴ cm³ s⁻¹.

Why it matters: this is exactly the independent stress-test the weekly memory flagged. The headline tension is with dwarf-spheroidal limits, ℛ≃5.1 face-value, widening to 1.6–9.3 once foreground choice (GALPROP vs GIEM dominates, halving the flux) and J-factor uncertainties are folded in — so s-wave is not excluded but not comfortable. Crucially, p/d-wave over-resolves the dwarf tension but misses relic abundance by ~7 orders of magnitude; decay is disfavored by the IGRB. Only resonant Sommerfeld/Breit-Wigner survives, supplying the needed ≈45× boost — but requiring a fine-tuned above-threshold resonance and a structured dark sector. Caveat honestly stated: χ²/ν∼8 from coherent diffuse residuals, and AMS-02 antiprotons independently disfavor direct bb̄; this is “consistency,” not detection.

arXiv:2607.08587 — A Novel Hadronic Calorimeter With A Direct Neutron Readout (I. Giomataris et al.)

Giomataris et al. simulate (Geant4, benchmarked against FLUKA) a sampling calorimeter of alternating Pb absorbers and Gd-loaded LAB scintillator that reads out both the prompt scintillation and a delayed signal from neutron moderation and capture. In a six-layer prototype (3.66 λ_I) hit by 10 GeV protons, ~197 neutrons/proton are captured with a median capture time of 11.1 μs; the delayed deposited energy is almost perfectly proportional to capture multiplicity (R²=0.9998, slope 7.97 MeV/neutron), giving direct calibration. Gadolinium’s key benefit is timing, not yield: 0.5% Gd collects >85% of captures within 20 μs versus ~10% for pure LAB. Because neutron production tracks the invisible hadronic energy, a branch-wise event-by-event correction using the delayed signal improves prompt resolution from 21.8% to 13.3% — a ~63% variance reduction — with no event rejection.

Why it matters: this is a hardware attack on the invisible-energy fluctuations that fundamentally limit hadronic resolution, complementary to dual-readout. The intrinsic neutron-counting resolution (~7%) is far below the 21.8% calorimetric resolution, confirming that neutron-production fluctuations, not the detector, dominate — and can be measured out. Events binned by fixed neutron multiplicity show resolution falling to ~6%, suggesting the method scales favorably to higher energies. Caveats are explicit: a single 10 GeV energy, a shallow preliminary geometry, and no optical-photon transport, photosensor, or electronics modeling yet — the 13.3% is a proof-of-principle, not a device spec.

arXiv:2607.08175 — Transformer-based machine learning using low-level calorimeter signals for collimated photon identification (Gabriel Matos et al.)

(Abstract only.) The authors use calorimeter cell-level information to separate highly collimated diphoton signatures — from light axion-like-particle decays — from isolated single photons, systematically comparing six architectures spanning high-level shower-shape approaches and direct cell-level methods. Cell-level ML decisively outperforms shower-shape variables, with a Transformer best overall and an MLP Mixer offering a resource-constrained option for trigger-level deployment. Beyond classification, the Transformer performs direct invariant-mass regression from cells, sharpening light-resonance characterization and adding a handle to suppress π⁰ and η fake-photon backgrounds.

Why it matters: this is the calorimetry front of the week’s ML-reconstruction arc — the same raw-input-beats-engineered-features lesson seen in flavor tagging, now applied to fine-grained EM showers. Direct mass regression from cells is the notable step beyond classification. No numerical performance figures are available in the provided text.
