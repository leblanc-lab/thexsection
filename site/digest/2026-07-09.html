It is a day of concrete answers to hard questions. This pragmatic momentum is most visible at the intersection of raw detector outputs and reconstruction algorithms. The ongoing paradigm shift away from high-level, heuristic variables toward raw, low-level data has reached a fever pitch. Themes

Raw-Data Reconstruction: The Calorimetry Revolution

We are witnessing a major turning point in how detectors are read out and analyzed. For years, the community relied on high-level shower shapes or hand-crafted variables to classify calorimeter signals. Today, two papers demonstrate that we can bypass these intermediaries entirely, utilizing raw low-level signals to drastically improve resolution.

First, on the software side, Matos et al. arXiv:2607.08175 directly advance the running theme of Machine Learning Transitions from Raw Reconstruction. Following the recent successes of the GN2 flavor-tagger, this work processes raw, fine-grained electromagnetic calorimeter cells to distinguish collimated diphotons (typical of light axion-like particles) from isolated single photons. The authors show that a Transformer architecture not only delivers vastly superior classification over conventional shower-shape methods but is capable of direct invariant mass regression from the raw cell energy deposits. This is a crucial tool for pulling light resonances out of stubborn \(\pi^0\) and \(\eta\) backgrounds.

On the hardware side, Giomataris et al. arXiv:2607.08587 provide the experimental backing for the Neutron-Capture Calorimetry turning point introduced earlier this week. They present Geant4 simulations of a sampling calorimeter utilizing alternating lead and gadolinium-loaded liquid scintillator (LAB-Gd). By directly measuring the delayed neutron capture signals, they can calibrate the “invisible” hadronic energy on an event-by-event basis. The results are striking: correcting the prompt signal with this delayed neutron information slashes the hadronic energy resolution from \(21.8\%\) to \(13.3\%\). It is a beautiful proof-of-concept that demonstrates how we can physically engineer our way out of the fundamental limitations of hadronic shower fluctuations.

Reconciling the 20 GeV Galactic Halo Excess

A major open question in the astroparticle community has been the physical reality of the 20 GeV Galactic Halo excess in Fermi-LAT data, and whether its apparent WIMP interpretation can survive the stringent limits set by dwarf spheroidal galaxies. Today, Stenhouse et al. arXiv:2607.08552 tackle this head-on.

The authors perform a rigorous, independent reproduction of the original cell-aggregated analysis and then take it a step further: they execute a native pixel-level likelihood analysis with full energy-dependent PSF forward folding. They confirm the existence of the 20 GeV halo-like component, showing it is a high-latitude, centrally concentrated feature that cannot be explained by extragalactic diffuse emission.

However, the plot thickens when fitting WIMP annihilation. Standard \(s\)-wave annihilation to \(W^+W^-\) or \(b\bar{b}\) faces a \(4\text{--}5\times\) tension with dwarf spheroidal limits. While systematic uncertainties in the foreground templates and \(J\)-factors can soften this tension slightly, the authors show that the only robust way to reconcile the dwarf spheroidal limits with the relic density is through low-velocity-enhanced annihilation (such as resonant Sommerfeld or Breit-Wigner enhancement). This provides a precise theoretical target for dark matter model builders looking to explain this highly resilient astrophysical signal.

Neutrino Structures, Textures, and Backgrounds

The neutrino community’s focus on resolving deep degeneracies and ensuring clean experimental baselines continues to grow.

In the search for leptonic CP violation, the structure of the neutrino mass matrix remains a key guide. Kitagawa et al. arXiv:2607.08384 revisit one-zero and two-zero mass textures under the latest cosmological and oscillation data. Using machine learning flow-matching techniques, they show that only the \(A\)-series of two-zero textures survives the tight CMB+BAO constraints. However, if those are relaxed slightly, the \(B\)-series textures predict a Dirac CP phase \(\delta_{\rm CP}\) clustering tightly around \(\pi/2\) and \(3\pi/2\)—well within reach of next-generation searches. To better understand the underlying symmetries of these states, Romero arXiv:2607.08739 introduces an internal \(SU(2)\) pseudospin algebra. This mathematical framework organizes the lepton-number-violating (\(\Delta L = 2\)) sector, cleanly classifying Majorana masses and pseudo-Dirac splittings without needing to assume the Majorana nature of the neutrino a priori.

Simultaneously, Cheng et al. arXiv:2607.08310 deliver a highly needed upgrade to our experimental baselines: a precision 3D atmospheric neutrino flux calculation based on the Honda model. For the first time, this framework incorporates muon propagation inside the Earth and subsequent decay or capture, using updated IGRF2020 geomagnetic fields and AMS-02 cosmic ray inputs. The calculation reveals that muon propagation inside the Earth contributes a globally significant, site-independent absolute increment to the neutrino flux below 100 MeV. This will be an essential input for JUNO, DUNE, and Hyper-Kamiokande as they push to extract CP phases and sterile limits from low-energy atmospheric events.

Cosmic Relics and Gravitational Waves

The early universe offers a playground for extreme physics, but our observational tools require highly precise calibration to avoid false claims.

For the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA), Schmitz et al. arXiv:2607.08445 point out a widespread issue in the literature: the standard formula for calculating the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of a stochastic gravitational-wave background (GWB) is only valid in the weak-signal limit. The authors derive a generalized optimal SNR expression valid at arbitrary signal strengths, showing that the GWB SNR is bounded from above by a hard ceiling (\(\text{SNR}_{\rm max} \approx 10^4\) for typical parameters). This fundamentally changes how we must plot LISA sensitivity contours for strong, early-universe phase transitions. This is paired with work by Gan et al. arXiv:2607.08248, who show that LISA can probe quadratically coupled ultralight dark matter, evading the screening effects that typically blind terrestrial experiments.

Meanwhile, Escrivà arXiv:2607.08738 takes a closer look at Primordial Black Hole (PBH) formation. By developing a finite-action framework for curvature-profile dispersion, they show that the dominant contribution to PBH abundance is not simply the average profile, nor the one with the lowest collapse threshold. Instead, PBH abundance is governed by a competition between the Gaussian probability of generating a profile deformation and the exponential gain from lowering the threshold. Broad primordial spectra and non-Gaussianity can cause rare, several-sigma coherent shape fluctuations to dominate the final abundance, significantly reducing the power-spectrum amplitude required to seed PBHs.

Deep dives

arXiv:2607.08175 — Transformer-based machine learning using low-level calorimeter signals for collimated photon identification at collider experiments

Only the abstract was available for this study. The authors implemented a Transformer-based machine learning architecture operating directly on raw, cell-level electromagnetic calorimeter signals to reconstruct highly collimated diphoton decays of light axion-like particles (ALPs). By utilizing fine-grained spatial energy deposits rather than traditional, hand-crafted high-level shower-shape variables, this approach achieves superior classification performance over five alternative architectures (including an MLP Mixer designed as a resource-constrained alternative for real-time triggers). Crucially, the Transformer model successfully performs direct invariant mass regression from the raw calorimeter cells, enhancing the reconstruction of light resonances and providing a powerful handle to reject problematic \(\pi^0\) and \(\eta\) fake-photon backgrounds.

Bypassing conventional high-level heuristics represents a significant paradigm shift in how fine-grained calorimetry data are processed at high-luminosity colliders. Without the full paper, however, the exact quantitative improvements in classification efficiency and mass resolution remain unstated, and the robustness of the model under realistic detector noise, pile-up conditions, and spatial misalignment is not detailed.

arXiv:2607.08552 — The 20 GeV Galactic Halo Excess: Pixel-Level Confirmation and Consistency with Sub-TeV WIMP Annihilation

The authors validated the contentious 20 GeV Galactic Halo excess by migrating from the original \(10^\circ\) cell-aggregated analysis to a native \(0.125^\circ\) pixel-level likelihood fit on 15 years of Fermi-LAT data, incorporating energy-dependent PSF forward folding and bright source masking. The pixel-level analysis confirms the excess, yielding a template-level fit improvement of \(2\sum_k \Delta\ln\mathcal{L} \approx 1.7\times 10^3\) and a halo spectrum shape in close agreement with the cellwise fit, albeit with a \(\sim 20\%\) higher overall normalization for a smooth Navarro-Frenk-White (\(\rho^2\)) profile. Interpreted as \(s\)-wave WIMP annihilation, the spectrum is best fit by \(m_\chi \approx 0.55\text{ TeV}\) (\(W^+W^-\)) and \(0.72\text{ TeV}\) (\(b\bar{b}\)) with \(\langle\sigma v\rangle \approx 1\times 10^{-24}\text{ cm}^3\text{s}^{-1}\). This cross section is in nominal \(4\text{--}5\times\) tension with dwarf spheroidal (dSph) limits. However, propagating the systematic budget (primarily the GALPROP-versus-GIEM diffuse templates and \(J\)-factor uncertainties) widens the tension parameter \(\mathcal{R}\) to \(1.6\text{--}9.3\), bringing the lower bound within dSph compliance.

To simultaneously satisfy dSph limits and the thermal relic density, the authors show that velocity-suppressed (\(p\)-wave) models are strictly ruled out because they underpredict the present-day Galactic halo rate by seven orders of magnitude. Instead, the dark sector must feature low-velocity-enhanced annihilation via resonant Sommerfeld or Breit-Wigner mechanisms. This requires a non-minimal dark sector with a sub-GeV mediator to saturate the enhancement between the dwarf (\(\sim 10\text{ km/s}\)) and Galactic (\(\sim 10^2\text{--}10^3\text{ km/s}\)) velocity scales. A significant caveat is the poor overall goodness-of-fit (\(\chi^2/\nu \sim 8\)), which is dominated by coherent, unmodeled spatial residuals in the diffuse models, highlighting that the signal’s properties remain highly dependent on interstellar emission templates.

arXiv:2607.08587 — A Novel Hadronic Calorimeter With A Direct Neutron Readout

The authors present a Geant4-simulated proof-of-concept for a neutron-sensitive sampling calorimeter designed to directly measure “invisible” hadronic energy on an event-by-event basis. The simulated detector consists of six layers of 10-cm lead absorbers alternating with 5-cm active layers of linear alkylbenzene loaded with 0.5% gadolinium (LAB-Gd), totaling \(3.66\) nuclear interaction lengths. When exposed to 10 GeV protons, the calorimeter records both a prompt scintillation signal and a delayed signal from thermal neutron captures. Loading with Gd reduces the median neutron-capture time from \(134.8\ \mu\text{s}\) (in pure LAB) to \(8.9\ \mu\text{s}\), enabling realistic acquisition gates, and yields a high-energy \(7.9\text{ MeV}\) \(\gamma\)-cascade signature. By characterizing the non-monotonic correlation between the prompt energy and the delayed neutron signal (which measures neutron-capture multiplicity with an intrinsic detector resolution of \(\sim 7\%\)), the authors developed an event-by-event branch-wise interpolation correction. Applying this correction to the prompt signal slashes the hadronic energy resolution from \(21.8\%\) to \(13.3\%\) without rejecting any events.

This hardware-software hybrid approach directly addresses the stochastic fluctuations of nuclear breakup and neutron production that fundamentally limit hadronic calorimetry. However, several realistic hurdles remain: the current simulation uses a thin prototype and lacks optical-photon transport, photosensor response (APDs), and front-end electronics noise. Furthermore, the non-monotonic correlation between prompt and delayed signals (which rises at low neutron multiplicities before anti-correlating at high multiplicities) may introduce reconstruction degeneracies when scaled to deep, full-containment calorimeters or complex, multi-particle hadronic jets.
