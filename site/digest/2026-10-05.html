Jet vetoes reach third order

The jet-vetoed Higgs cross section now has a full third-order prediction. Abreu et al. pair a three-loop rapidity anomalous dimension arXiv:2610.03472 with N³LO+N³LL jet-vetoed Higgs arXiv:2610.03687, landing at ~3% residual uncertainty for \(p_T^{\rm v}\sim30\) GeV. Last Wednesday the same observable got its first NLL non-global treatment (Becher et al.), so this process has advanced on two fronts in a week. Super-K’s first reactor-antineutrino observation is the day’s experimental headline. On LZ, a single paper appeared, and it reads like a consolidation of the heavy-Higgsino escape route.

Themes

Jet vetoes at third order

Abreu et al. arXiv:2610.03472 extract the anomalous dimension from a three-loop SCET soft function for generalized-\(k_t\) clustering as a function of \(R\). The semi-numerical method is offered as reusable for other three-loop soft functions. The phenomenology companion arXiv:2610.03687 finds resummation lifts the fixed-order result by ~3%. Fiore et al. arXiv:2610.02327 add the first fully differential NNLO photon+identified-hadron predictions, aimed at FF fits and as a pp baseline for photon-tagged heavy-ion studies.

First light for neutrinos and muons

Super-K arXiv:2610.03143 sees reactor \(\bar\nu_e\) at ~6σ in SK-Gd. Its reactor-only \(\Delta m^2_{21}=(9.08^{+0.55}_{-0.54})\times10^{-5}\) eV² sits about 3σ above JUNO’s value from last week, though \(\sin^2\theta_{12}=0.50\pm0.16\) shows how little this sample constrains yet. COMET arXiv:2610.02855 reports Phase-α, the first demonstration of low-momentum \(\mu^-\) transport through its solenoid. Measured yields run ~30% above Geant4.

Quantum structure, collider to plasma

Bao et al. arXiv:2610.03024 give a full-detector STCF study of \(\tau\tau\) concurrence at ~2% precision with 1 ab⁻¹, which extends the season’s tau-polarimetry thread to the detector level. Brambilla et al. arXiv:2610.02313 track Wigner negativity of bottomonium in the QGP and find that it plateaus near the 1S value. The mechanism is conditional purification: the medium dissolves the large-\(\langle r^2\rangle\) modes, leaving the surviving ensemble close to 1S.

LLM and GPU tooling

Schwartz’s BootLoops arXiv:2610.02286 packages IBP reduction, differential equations, PSLQ and ball arithmetic for any agentic LLM to operate. This continues last week’s LLMs-inside-calculations cluster and points it outward to genomics and statistics. Sule arXiv:2610.02318 puts cluster hadronisation into GAPS, generating 10⁶ hadron-level events in ~4 s on a V100. Samuel et al. arXiv:2610.03339 treat track fitting as translation, and their model matches RANSAC.

  • arXiv:2610.03143: Super-K observes reactor antineutrinos for the first time. Its \(\Delta m^2_{21}\) is high relative to JUNO and only 1.9σ from SK’s own solar data, a small new handle on the solar–reactor question.

Deep dives

arXiv:2610.03687 — Third-Order Logarithmic Resummation for Jet-Vetoed Higgs Production (Samuel Abreu et al.)

The first N³LL+N³LO prediction for the gluon-fusion zero-jet cross section gives 30.11 (+3.2%/−3.0%) pb at \(p_T^{\rm v}=30\) GeV, with \(R=0.4\) anti-\(k_t\) jets at 13.6 TeV. That is ~3% above fixed-order N³LO (29.24 pb). The shift lies outside N³LO’s narrow +0.7/−2.2% scale band, so fixed-order scale variation alone misses the logarithmic correction. Relative to NNLL+NNLO, the central value rises ~4% and the uncertainty shrinks from 6–8% to ~3%. The new ingredient is the three-loop, algorithm-dependent rapidity anomalous dimension \(\gamma_\nu^{(3)}(R^2)\) from the companion paper, combined with the four-loop cusp, three-loop \(\gamma_V\) and two-loop boundary terms, all implemented in RadISH. The authors tested the expansion against NNLOJet \(H\)+jet with \(M=1\) TeV and \(R=0.1\). With \(\Delta\gamma_\nu^{(3)}\) set to zero, the expansion visibly fails to converge, so the three-loop clustering term is numerically essential.

The physics point is that the previous NNLL+N³LO result had an accidental cancellation near 30 GeV. N³LL lifts it and restores a uniform 3–4% third-order correction for \(p_T^{\rm v}\gtrsim20\) GeV. Alternative matching schemes and log-switching prescriptions all agree within the band. The code is public (JetVHeto).

Caveats: - Missing effects: top and bottom mass effects and small-\(R\) resummation are not included. Both were 1–2% in earlier work, comparable to the quoted uncertainty. - ln R terms: the authors flag large subleading \(\ln R\) terms in \(\gamma_\nu^{(3)}\) as motivation for \(\ln R\) resummation. - Factorization breaking: coherence-violating effects, which break factorization from five loops, are explicitly set aside. The non-global/super-leading front noted in the digest therefore sits outside this calculation.

arXiv:2610.03143 — First observation of electron antineutrinos from nuclear reactors at Super-Kamiokande (K. Abe (The Super-Kamiokande Collaboration) et al.)

SK-Gd sees reactor \(\bar\nu_e\) via IBD with Gd-tagged neutrons. The exposure is 411.52 live days: SK-VI at 0.011% Gd and SK-VII at 0.033% Gd. The no-reactor hypothesis gives \(\Delta\chi^2=34.7\), about 6σ, and the oscillated fit has \(\chi^2=16.7/16\).

Reactor-only fit, with \(\theta_{13}\) fixed: - \(\sin^2\theta_{12}=0.500\pm0.155\) - \(\Delta m^2_{21}=(9.08^{+0.55}_{-0.54})\times10^{-5}\) eV²

Comparison with SK solar. SK’s own solar fit gives \(\Delta m^2_{21}=(6.10^{+1.26}_{-0.86})\times10^{-5}\) eV². The differences are 1.1σ in \(\theta_{12}\) and 2.2σ in \(\Delta m^2_{21}\). A parameter-goodness-of-fit test gives 1.9σ compatibility. The main text quotes \(\Delta\chi^2_{\rm PG}=7.25\) (p=0.027) while the appendix quotes 7.64 (p=0.022), a minor internal inconsistency. The combined SK fit is \(\sin^2\theta_{12}=0.332\), \(\Delta m^2_{21}=(8.94^{+0.46}_{-0.47})\times10^{-5}\) eV².

Why it matters: this is a new water-Cherenkov reactor handle on the solar sector, from the same detector that measures solar neutrinos. That makes it an internal CPT-motivated cross-check, and a demonstration of Gd-water for low-energy \(\bar\nu_e\).

The paper does not quantify the digest’s “~3σ above JUNO” framing. It calls its values consistent with global fits and recent reactor results, JUNO included.

Caveats: - Background model: the accidental background comes from a single low-reactor window (Nov–Dec 2020) and is statistics-limited. - Flux inputs: fluxes use electrical rather than thermal load factors. - Systematics: neutron-capture γ modeling (5.4%) and coincidence efficiency (5.5%) dominate the listed systematics.

arXiv:2610.03692 — Higgsino dark matter compatible with the LZ high-energy nuclear-recoil event and IceCube constraints (Katherine Freese et al.)

Heavy Higgsinos can explain the 248 keV LZ event without violating IceCube solar bounds, but the thermal 1.1 TeV case barely survives. The authors build a 96-bin approximate LZ likelihood digitized from LZ’s published data, calibrated with 30k toys, which reproduces LZ’s interval near δ=350 keV. They include the corrected (4× larger) inelastic cross section.

Thermal 1.1 TeV case. The LZ-compatible splitting depends on the halo model: - SHM: 360–370 keV. - SHM+LMC: 485–505 keV.

IceCube lower bounds on δ at 1.1 TeV depend on how captured Higgsinos cool in the Sun:

Cooling assumption Lower bound on δ
Capture–annihilation equilibrium imposed 566 keV
Inelastic + elastic SD 558 keV
Inelastic only 506 keV

The thermal Higgsino survives only at δ≈508 keV, and only under three conditions together: LMC tail included, all elastic cooling neglected, and the LZ sideband dropped. The authors call this a limiting case rather than a robust interpretation.

Heavier Higgsinos. The key novelty is the \(m_\chi^{-2}\) falloff of the elastic SD cross section, which weakens cooling at high mass. With it, compatible regions open at: - ~2 TeV (δ~520 keV) for SHM+LMC, no sideband and SD-only elastic cooling. - ≳10–30 TeV (δ≈350–400 keV) for the SHM. - Equilibrium imposed everywhere: ≳80 TeV for the SHM, and 13–120 TeV (465–520 keV) for SHM+LMC.

They also find Sommerfeld resonances near 6.5, 25 and 55 TeV that locally tighten the bounds.

Caveats: - Halo model: the LMC tail uses a single simulated halo with a large LMC/MW mass ratio (0.27). - Sideband acceptance: unpublished, and LZ has not calibrated its nuclear-recoil response there. - Solar treatment: cooling assumes stationary nuclei, radial orbits and a uniform-density population. - IceCube conversion: the \(W^+W^-\) limits are frozen above 10 TeV, so high-mass regions are extrapolations. - Cosmology: heavier Higgsinos need a non-standard abundance history.
