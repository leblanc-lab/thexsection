The vibe today

A sprawling, low-centralization day — 52 papers with no single collaboration bombshell but one genuine experimental milestone tucked inside: ATLAS reports the first observation of two simultaneously longitudinally polarized Z bosons (arXiv:2607.12879), a 6.5σ result that quietly extends the electroweak-symmetry-breaking program into the diboson polarization sector. Around it, the theory currents that have run all fortnight kept flowing, but two threads stand out as unusually loud today: axions, approached from what feels like every conceivable angle, and quantum information, which is steadily migrating from a niche curiosity into a working vocabulary for collider and few-body physics.

If the memory’s recurring refrain has been “the axion search is diversifying,” today is the exclamation point. A single-author LHC bound from merged photons, an SN1987A-to-Cherenkov study of maximally invisible QCD axions, a GeV axion rescued by coupling to charm, an ultralight-axion coherence probe from galaxy polarization, and a quantum-metrology sensing scheme — five papers, five completely different mass windows and detection philosophies. Nobody is converging; everybody is fanning out.

The rest of the day is healthily foundational: three-loop Higgs-plus-jet amplitudes, a functional-methods HVP determination that lands squarely on the lattice consensus, lattice quarkonium decay widths, EIC structure-function formalism, and a scattering of cosmological-background and dark-matter-phase-space work. A tool-tending, breadth-over-depth kind of morning.

Themes

Axions across every altitude

The day’s densest cluster. Knapen arXiv:2607.12004 points out — cleanly, in a short single-author paper — that for \(0.1\)–\(6\) GeV ALPs the strongest photon-coupling bound now comes from high-energy \(a\gamma\) production at the LHC, where the diphoton pair merges into a single reconstructed photon. Di Luzio et al. arXiv:2607.12025 ask the complementary question from below: how invisible can a QCD axion be if it couples only to gluons, deriving the SN1987A cooling constraint and Cherenkov-detection prospects to set a floor on detectability. Lu arXiv:2607.12956 revives the GeV-scale QCD axion by coupling the PQ scalar to charm rather than light quarks — eliminating the isospin/\(\pi^0\)-splitting problem and, at \(f_a\sim\) GeV, automatically solving the axion quality problem, with a testable negative \(\Delta N_{\rm eff}\). That quality-problem framing is the direct heir to last week’s “minimal high-quality QCD axion” and the strong-CP flurry the memory tracked. On the ultralight end, Doi arXiv:2607.12446 proposes measuring axion coherence — not just amplitude — via the 3D correlation of galaxy polarization-rotation angles, and Manshouri et al. arXiv:2607.12164 push a multi-probe quantum-Fisher-information sensing scheme for ALP-mediated spin-spin forces.

Quantum information meets colliders

A genuinely emerging theme, not something the recent memory has flagged. Cheng et al. arXiv:2607.12015 study \(Z\to\tau^-\tau^+\gamma\) as a laboratory for decoherence, finding the striking result that final-state radiation can either decohere the \(\tau\)-pair spin state or monotonically enhance its entanglement. Low et al. arXiv:2607.12053 use time-averaged entangling power as an operator-level diagnostic of hidden versus manifest symmetries in the quantum Rabi model. Jwa et al. arXiv:2607.12821 formulate strip-detector track reconstruction as a QUBO problem for quantum annealing, benchmarked on simulated DAMSA ALP-decay events. And at the far formal end, Baez et al. arXiv:2607.10833 draw a correspondence between the exceptional “bi-Cayley” hermitian Jordan triple and the Standard Model’s structure. Whether or not any of these mature, the collective mood — spin states, entanglement, and quantum hardware as legitimate objects of collider study — is worth noting.

Precision QCD amplitudes and measurements

The precision-machinery lineage continues. Guan et al. arXiv:2607.12033 deliver three-loop QCD helicity amplitudes for a Higgs plus three partons with full color, and flag a surprise: sub-leading color contributions are numerically as large as leading-color terms — a caution for anyone truncating. Miramontes et al. arXiv:2607.12918 compute the leading-order HVP contribution to \(a_\mu\) from Dyson-Schwinger/Bethe-Salpeter methods with self-consistent isospin breaking, landing at \(710.0\times10^{-10}\) in agreement with recent lattice — another continuum data point in the still-unsettled HVP saga the memory has tracked. Brambilla et al. arXiv:2607.13024 combine lattice QCD with pNRQCD to compute inclusive P-wave quarkonium decay widths from a single universal chromoelectric correlator, reproducing \(\chi_{cJ}\) and predicting \(\chi_{bJ}\). And the ATLAS longitudinal-\(ZZ\) observation arXiv:2607.12879 anchors the experimental end.

EIC structure and small-x

A steady EIC-facing undercurrent. Ee et al. arXiv:2607.12006 show that azimuthal dependence in gapped \(ep\) scattering is a pure diffractive signature — the \(F_{3,4}^{\rm gap}\) and \(F_L^{\rm gap}\) structure functions vanish at leading power for ordinary hard scattering, a clean discriminant against the hard-scattering background. Mukherjee et al. arXiv:2607.12268 complete the leading-order back-to-back dijet DIS cross section to twist-3 at arbitrary Bjorken-\(x\), extending TMD descriptions beyond the small-\(x\) eikonal regime. And Ming Li arXiv:2607.12554 derives the first closed nonlinear evolution equation for the dipole gluon distribution at subeikonal order, finding its dilute-limit growth exponent is under a fifth of the eikonal value — a step toward stitching small-\(x\) to moderate-\(x\) dynamics.

Scalar and gravitational-wave backgrounds

The cosmological-background modeling continues to broaden. de Giorgi et al. arXiv:2607.12013 propose “scalar tsunamis” — bursts released when black-hole formation abruptly removes a scalar field’s source — accumulating into a diffuse stochastic scalar background peaked at \(1\)–\(10^3\) Hz, reaching masses ten orders below individual-transient sensitivity. Budker et al. arXiv:2607.12617 constrain high-frequency GWs (100 kHz–100 MHz) via optical precision spectroscopy, covering much of the range for the first time. Lin et al. arXiv:2607.12485 use neutrino superradiance to bound asteroid-mass PBH dark matter below microlensing limits. And on the DM-astrophysics side, Huang et al. arXiv:2607.12008 find a near-universal dark-matter phase-space distribution across simulation suites, inferrable from metal-poor stars — directly relevant to direct-detection interpretation.

Deep dives

arXiv:2607.12879 — Observation of pair production of longitudinally polarized Z bosons in four-lepton final state with the ATLAS detector (ATLAS Collaboration)

(abstract only) ATLAS measures \(ZZ\) production in the \(4\ell\) channel (\(ee\)/\(\mu\mu\) pairs) using 164 fb\(^{-1}\) at \(\sqrt{s}=13.6\) TeV, extracting polarization fractions inclusively and in two \(m_{4\ell}\) regions — all consistent with the SM. The headline comes from combining with the earlier 13 TeV measurement: the first observation of two simultaneously longitudinally polarized Z bosons, at 6.5σ observed (5.6σ expected). This pushes the electroweak-symmetry-breaking program — long anchored in single-boson \(W_L\)/\(Z_L\) fractions and \(W_LW_L\) scattering — into the doubly-longitudinal diboson sector, directly probing the Goldstone-mode content of two gauge bosons at once. The caveat is structural: a genuine observation is only reached after statistical combination across two center-of-mass energies, so the 13.6 TeV dataset alone is not yet decisive, and the analysis reports agreement with the SM rather than any deviation.

arXiv:2607.12004 — Bounds on axion-like particles from fully merged photons at the LHC (Simon Knapen)

Knapen recasts the ATLAS 139 fb\(^{-1}\) high-mass diphoton resonance search (2102.13405) into an ALP-photon bound by exploiting that for \(0.1\)–\(6\) GeV ALPs the two decay photons from \(q\bar q\to a\gamma\) merge into a single reconstructed photon. Because the \(aF\tilde F\) operator is dimension-five, the parton cross section is energy-independent while the \(\gamma\gamma\) background falls as \(1/m_{\gamma\gamma}^2\), so the signal-to-background ratio grows as \((m_{\gamma\gamma}g_{a\gamma\gamma})^2\); a profile-likelihood fit to bins with \(m_{\gamma\gamma}>1.5\) TeV yields \(g_{a\gamma\gamma}\approx 4\times10^{-4}\) GeV\(^{-1}\), the strongest current bound over most of \(0.1\)–\(6\) GeV. PDF systematics are shown subdominant (~10% of the statistical uncertainty). This is a clean, reinterpretation-style result that plants an LHC flag in a mass window otherwise covered by beam dumps, FASER and \(e^+e^-\) machines — squarely today’s “axions fanning out” theme.

Honest caveats are flagged by the author himself: the limit weakens below 0.2 GeV (displaced decays) and above 1 GeV due to a deliberately conservative tight-photon merging requirement (\(\Delta\eta<0.003\)). A dedicated ATLAS analysis with one loose + one tight photon should do better. Also, under the alternative \(c_W\approx c_B\) ansatz the \(B\to Ka\) bound (\(g_{a\gamma\gamma}\lesssim5\times10^{-6}\) GeV\(^{-1}\)) is far stronger — so the result’s competitiveness is specific to the \(c_B\gg c_W\) scenario.

arXiv:2607.12033 — Three-Loop QCD Corrections to Scattering Amplitudes of a Higgs Boson and Three Partons (Xin Guan et al.)

Guan, Mistlberger and Ruf deliver the full-color three-loop helicity amplitudes for \(H+3\) partons (\(Hggg\) and \(Hq\bar qg\)) in the heavy-top EFT, using Blade IBP reduction via block-triangular forms and canonical differential equations, with results in compact generalized-polylogarithm form (full reduction ~several hundred thousand core-hours). All checks pass: infrared pole structure, collinear/soft factorization, AMFlow numerics, agreement with the prior leading-color result (2504.06490), and the striking confirmation that the leading-transcendental part coincides with the \(\mathcal N=4\) sYM \(\mathrm{tr}(\phi^2)\) form factor — now including subleading color. These are the virtual ingredients for \(H+\)jet at N\(^3\)LO and a building block toward inclusive gluon-fusion at N\(^4\)LO.

The notable phenomenological finding: at N\(^3\)LO the subleading-color contribution to the amplitude is of the same order as the leading-color term — in sharp contrast to lower loops, where subleading color is negligible. This is a direct caution against generalized-leading-color truncation at this order. The reassuring counterpoint is that, evaluated at \(\alpha_s=0.118\), the net three-loop impact on the tree-normalized interference is small (sub-percent shift in the cumulant on the \(t=u\) slice), so the series remains well-behaved even as its color decomposition becomes non-hierarchical.
