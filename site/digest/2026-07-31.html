arXiv:2607.28396 — A measurement of the Higgs boson mass in the diphoton decay channel in proton-proton collisions at \(\sqrt{s}=13\) TeV (CMS Collaboration)

CMS pins down \(m_H=125.13\pm0.15\) GeV (\(\pm0.10\,\text{stat}\pm0.12\,\text{syst}\)) using the full 138 fb\(^{-1}\) Run 2 diphoton sample — a near factor-of-two systematic reduction over their 2020 result. The key advance is the photon energy scale: rather than extrapolating electron scales from \(Z\to ee\) via simulation (previously the dominant uncertainty), they now measure residual electron-photon differences directly from \(Z\to\mu\mu\gamma\) FSR photons, and apply fine-grained \(E_\text{T}\)-dependent resolution corrections that bridge the gap between \(Z\)-electron energies (\(\approx45\) GeV) and \(H\to\gamma\gamma\) photons (\(\approx60\) GeV). The leading systematics are now the \(Z\to\mu\mu\gamma\) sample statistics (74 MeV), muon momentum scale (55 MeV), and high-\(E_\text{T}\) non-linearity (54 MeV). The signal strength \(\mu=0.95\pm0.06\pm0.07\) is SM-consistent.

Why it matters: this is the most precise single-channel \(m_H\) to date, a direct input to EW global fits and vacuum-stability arguments. Combined with CMS 7+8 TeV diphoton data it gives \(125.06\pm0.14\) GeV. One visible caveat: the 13 TeV value sits 1.2\(\sigma\) above the earlier CMS 7+8 TeV combination, and the result remains systematics-limited, with further gains tied to the statistically starved \(Z\to\mu\mu\gamma\) calibration sample.

arXiv:2607.27315 — The Very Nearly Right Theory of Flavor (Nima Arkani-Hamed et al.)

(Abstract only.) Arkani-Hamed et al. parametrize the ten-dimensional space of quark flavor data using “nine-link textures” — full-rank \(Y_{u,d}\) matrices with nine total nonzero entries and a single CP phase. Fitting all such textures, they find the CP phases cluster tightly around multiples of \(\pi/8\), driven by a “Yukawa triangle” defined by the matrix entries that coincides with the unitarity triangle at leading order in small flavor parameters. The empirical observation being explained is that \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\approx(\pi/2,\pi/8,3\pi/8)\). Crucially, the two triangles differ beyond leading order, yielding calculable deviations from these values that the next generation of unitarity-triangle-angle measurements can decisively confirm or exclude.

Why it matters: this turns a striking numerical coincidence into a falsifiable framework, and lands one day after Lin’s geometric CKM reparameterization — two independent attacks on the same pattern. A bonus: the textures are sparse with naturally real determinants, which combined with spontaneous CP violation offers a route to solving strong CP. Caveat: as abstract-only, the robustness of the “clustering” claim and the size of the predicted deviations relative to projected experimental precision cannot be assessed here.

arXiv:2607.27313 — Suppressed Quantum Effects of Weakly Coupled Waves (Yunjia Bao et al.)

Bao et al. prove two general obstructions to detecting nonclassicality in weakly coupled waves (axion DM, gravitational radiation), using the cavity haloscope as worked example. First, detectors couple not to fundamental plane-wave modes but to a coarse-grained “effective mode”; if DM occupies many independent modes, the quantum central limit theorem drives the effective-mode \(P\)-function to a nonnegative thermal Gaussian, erasing negativity regardless of occupancy. They show convergence is rapid — the purity of \(\mathcal N\) coarse-grained Fock states approaches the Gaussian value \(1/(1+2n)\) as \(\sim n(n+1)/[\mathcal N(2n+1)^2]\), fast even for \(n\sim1\). Second, even granting a maximally nonclassical effective mode, all nonclassicality signatures carry extra powers of the tiny conversion efficiency \(\eta=\sin^2(gt_m)\sim10^{-21}\): Mandel \(Q_c=\eta Q_a\geq-\eta\), squeezing \(S_c=\eta S_a\). Distinguishing a Fock from a coherent axion via number statistics needs \(N_\text{shot}\sim1/\eta^2\), i.e. \(t_\text{int}\gtrsim10^{17}\) yr — utterly impractical and unenhanced by large \(N_\text{eff}\).

Why it matters: this is a general no-go closing off the recent high-profile “quantize gravity from GW observations” program, and the cold-water counterpart to yesterday’s Blas–Oller GW\(\to\gamma\gamma\) claim. Distinguishing classical states (coherent vs. Gaussian) remains feasible, needing only \(N_\text{shot}\sim1/\langle n_c\rangle^2\). Caveat honestly flagged: the CLT can be evaded — few contributing modes (fuzzy DM, condensates), surviving inter-mode correlations, or highly monochromatic late-time sources with \(T\Delta\omega\lesssim1\) — though even then the \(\eta\)-suppression persists unless quantum resources scale as \(1/\eta\).

arXiv:2606.19425 — LeWRON: Agentic Analysis of Electroweak Phase Transitions (Isaac R. Wang)

LeWRON is an LLM-agent framework that takes a BSM Lagrangian (or a literature target) and orchestrates the full electroweak-phase-transition pipeline: constructing the finite-temperature effective potential, tracking thermal histories, computing nucleation, and predicting gravitational-wave spectra. Its design leans on role-separated model calls (deterministic coding vs. reasoning agents), CAS-assisted symbolic derivations, audited Pydantic artifacts, and human checkpoints. The paper validates in “reproduction mode” (recovering the singlet-scalar nucleation temperatures of Ref. [71] to <1%, and a 2HDM+a potential) and “discovery mode” (ALP-Higgs and 2HDM+S), where it claims to improve on prior renormalization treatments.

The ~5 citations in roughly two months reflect a surge of “hep-ph agent” papers (HEPTAPOD, MadAgents, DarkAgents, Collider-Bench, all cited here) rather than physics results per se. LeWRON is positioned as the first domain-specialized agent for end-to-end EWPT analysis, and it is being cited as part of this emerging cluster establishing what agentic workflows for particle theory can do — a claim to a niche everyone in that subfield now references.

arXiv:2606.26691 — CHESS: CHEbyshev pSeudo-Spectral transport for Feynman integral differential equations (Yuanche Liu et al.)

CHESS is a Wolfram Language package for high-precision one-dimensional transport of \(\epsilon\)-factorized differential equations for Feynman master integrals. Given a path-pulled-back connection \(B(t)\) — supplied either as constant-matrix/scalar-one-form data or as a direct numerical evaluator — it uses Chebyshev–Lobatto spectral collocation, solving the triangular \(\epsilon\)-hierarchy sequentially with one factored node matrix acting on many right-hand sides, plus residue-based regularization of spurious endpoint singularities. Benchmarks are striking: a physical-region two-loop six-point double-pentagon reaches agreement with AMFlow at the \(10^{-101}\) level; three-loop five-point families with up to 734 masters complete in under 80 s; and a fixed-protocol comparison shows 8.8–17.9× speedups over DiffExp. An elliptic non-planar-triangle example exercises the direct-matrix mode.

The ~5 citations in about a month signal a needed tool: multi-scale, high-multiplicity integral families (three-loop five-point, two-loop six-point) are exactly the current bottlenecks, and CHESS slots into the DiffExp/SeaSyde/AMFlow ecosystem with demonstrable performance gains. Its deliberately narrow scope — transport only, given a canonical basis and path — makes it easy to adopt as a drop-in propagation layer.

arXiv:2605.11332 — Reviving primordial black hole formation in slow first-order phase transitions (Wen-Yuan Ai et al.)

This paper is a direct rebuttal to recent claims that PBH formation from slow first-order phase transitions is ruled out. The problem: bubble simulations extract the density contrast in the spatially flat gauge, \(\delta^{(F)}\), whereas the collapse threshold is defined in comoving gauge, and \(\delta^{(F)}\simeq 10\,\delta^{(C)}\) at horizon re-entry — apparently killing the mechanism. Using a gauge-invariant treatment that avoids the superhorizon approximation, the authors confirm the suppression, but then argue the mechanism survives: if post-transition reheating is slow (\(\Gamma_\phi < H_*\)), the Universe enters an early matter-dominated era in which even small overdensities grow and collapse. They derive a model-independent condition (\(\beta/H_n \lesssim 18\), EMD duration \(\gtrsim 10^3 H_*^{-1}\)), extend Doroshkevich statistics to non-Gaussian \(P(\delta_k)\), and realize the scenario in a conformal \(U(1)_X\) model yielding asteroid-mass PBHs that can be all of dark matter.

The ~5 citations since May reflect its role in a live, contested debate (Franciolini et al.; Wang et al. are the foils). A “the mechanism isn’t dead after all” paper in an active controversy naturally attracts rapid engagement from both camps.
