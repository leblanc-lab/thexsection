The higgsino gets exiled to 10⁵ TeV

Four weeks on, the LZ recoil still anchors the day, but the interpretation has run out of ordinary places to hide. Langhoff arXiv:2609.09385 takes the thermal-higgsino reading to its conclusion: the empty sideband plus IceCube solar capture force the mass to \(10^5\)–\(10^6\) GeV, which cannot come from standard freeze-out — so it is produced during early matter domination via a Kim-Nilles mechanism that also solves strong CP. Meanwhile Chattaraj et al. arXiv:2609.10504 close off the mundane alternative, ruling out coherent neutrino scattering (SM, BSM, and exotic fluxes) as the source. The model zoo the memory has tracked since late August is now visibly straining against its own bounds.

Deep dives

arXiv:2609.09385 — Heavy Higgsino Interpretation of the LZ Event (Kevin Langhoff)

Langhoff performs a 2D likelihood fit of pure-higgsino inelastic scattering to the LZ 248 keV recoil plus the empty high-energy sideband (350–675 keV), then folds in recomputed IceCube solar-capture bounds. The data alone prefer a light branch, \(m_\chi\approx(240,450)\) GeV, but that region is excluded by solar capture (which sets a floor on the splitting \(\delta\) via neutrinos from captured-higgsino annihilation). The thermal higgsino at 1.1 TeV overfills the sideband (\(N_{\rm SB}\gtrsim2\), \(\Delta\chi^2\) up to 11). The only corner consistent with the event, the empty sideband and solar capture is \(m_\chi\approx(10^5,10^6)\) GeV with \(\delta\approx(330,480)\) keV — where DM dilution (\(\rho\propto1/m_\chi\)) forces small \(\delta\) and the sideband/ROI event ratio lands near 0.8–1 (versus \(\sim0.1\) light, \(\gtrsim15\) thermal), a testable prediction. Such a heavy higgsino cannot arise from freeze-out, so he builds a proof-of-concept: freeze-out during early matter domination reheating to \(T_{\rm RH}\approx1\) TeV, tied to the \(\mu\) term via a Kim-Nilles mechanism that also solves strong CP.

Why it matters: it closes today’s headline arc — the ordinary thermal reading is dead, and survival demands a \(10^7\) GeV gaugino scale plus a bespoke cosmology, connecting a direct-detection anomaly to axion/strong-CP physics. Caveats are frank: the \(\Delta\chi^2\) contours are a “heuristic guide” only, the heavy point is not the best fit, results depend on LMC-tail halo modeling, solar-capture curves bracket limiting behavior rather than a precise bound, and the model needs a tuned axion misalignment (\(\theta\lesssim0.05\)).

arXiv:2609.09318 — Nucleon Strong-Interaction Size from Charmonium Photoproduction (Xiangdong Ji et al.)

Ji, Joosten, Meziani, and Pefkou extract the nucleon’s overall strong-interaction radius by combining world near-threshold \(J/\psi\) photoproduction data (GlueX, Hall C, CLAS12) with a recent Bayesian quark-sector GFF analysis. Fitting the differential cross sections in a holographic-QCD model — \(A_g(t)\) dipole, \(C_g(t)\) tripole, \(B_g=0\) — yields the gluon stress-energy form factors (\(\chi^2/{\rm ndf}=1.005\) after dropping the lowest GlueX energy bin). Energy-momentum conservation then relates the total \(A,C\) form factors to the scalar (trace-anomaly) color-field distribution \(G^s(t)\), whose Breit-frame radius is \(\langle r_s^2\rangle^{1/2}=0.94\pm0.09\) fm — larger than the PDG charge radius (0.8409 fm) and larger than the mass radius, defining a gluonic “skin” analogous to the neutron skin in \(^{208}\)Pb.

Why it matters: it delivers a clean, quotable first experimental number for the nucleon’s strong-interaction extent, mapping the color field that generates most of the nucleon mass rather than its charge. The ordering (strong > charge > mass) agrees with lattice and dispersive determinations cited. Caveats are stated plainly: the identification of the photoproduction amplitude with the gluonic SET is framework-dependent (open-charm and alternative production mechanisms may contaminate), \(B_g=0\) is an assumption, and the result inherits both statistical and model uncertainty. SoLID and EIC/ePIC are projected to improve precision by up to an order of magnitude.

arXiv:2609.10504 — Can Elastic Neutrino Scattering Account for the LZ230616 Event? (Ayan Chattaraj et al.)

Chattaraj et al. systematically test whether coherent elastic neutrino–nucleus scattering (CEνNS) can produce the 248 keV LZ recoil. Kinematics alone require \(E_\nu\gtrsim118\) MeV, killing solar and DSNB fluxes; only atmospheric neutrinos reach that energy, but their SM rate in the signal bin (225–271 keV) is \(R_{\rm I}\simeq2\times10^{-6}\) events. Introducing BSM scalar or vector mediators tuned to give one signal-region event overshoots the low-energy bins by \(\mathcal{O}(10^5)\) events — six orders of magnitude above background — so the \(R_{\rm I}=1\) contour sits entirely inside the \(R_{\rm II}\)-excluded region. Exotic monochromatic fluxes fare no better: DM annihilation (\(\chi\chi\to\nu\bar\nu\)) requires \(\langle\sigma v\rangle\gtrsim10^{-18}\) cm³/s and DM decay \(\tau_\chi\lesssim10^{15}\) s, both far past existing bounds, while overpopulating low-recoil bins by hundreds of events. PBH evaporation is worst: the exponentially soft Hawking spectrum gives \(R_{\rm II}/R_{\rm I}\sim10^4\)–\(10^{23}\) and demands \(f_{\rm PBH}\gtrsim95\), exceeding the total DM density.

Why it matters: the useful null of the day. The broad recoil spectrum inherent to elastic scattering is fundamentally incompatible with an isolated high-energy feature, reinforcing that the LZ event demands an inelastic/threshold mechanism (higgsino, exothermic DM). Caveat: the analysis is deliberately restricted to elastic channels — inelastic neutrino processes and atmospheric-neutrino upscattering (their ref. [29]) are outside scope.
