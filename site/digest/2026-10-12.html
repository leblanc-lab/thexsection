Freedman–McLerran, one order on

Kärkkäinen et al. arXiv:2610.13873 compute the full N³LO pressure of cold, dense QED. That problem has been open since the 1977 N²LO result, and the authors call it the first thermodynamic quantity at this order in a physical gauge theory. The series converges well. Their Bayesian estimate of missing orders, however, can exceed what scale variation suggests, which is the same warning as last week’s jet-veto N³LL result. Philcox’s two-loop galaxy power spectrum has a similar mixed outcome: the extra order buys reach in \(k\) but no gain on \(\sigma_8\). BESIII supplies the day’s data, and the one LZ paper takes the solar bound as an input rather than ignoring it.

BESIII charmonium and charm

LZ via Dirac gauginos

Lee et al. arXiv:2610.13930 drop the Higgsino, which conflicts with the solar-neutrino bound they adopt. They use an \(R\)-symmetric Dirac bino with wino coannihilation and get 300–850 GeV spectra. Last week several LZ model papers skipped solar capture; this one is built around it.

Deep dives

arXiv:2610.13873 — Complete four-loop pressure of cold QED (Aapeli K"arkk"ainen et al.)

The N³LO pressure of cold, dense massless QED (jellium setup) is now complete, the first new order since Freedman–McLerran in 1977. The soft and mixed sectors were already known, so the missing piece was the hard contribution: 10 four-loop 1PI vacuum diagrams at finite μ. The authors generated them in general \(R_\xi\) gauge and confirmed that the gauge dependence cancels. Canonicalization reduces \(O(10^5)\) scalar integrals to 75 masters, compared with 114 in QCD. The methods were: - cutting rules and self-energy techniques for the factorizable topologies, - IBP plus differential equations as a cross-check on a subset, - thermal loop-tree duality (LTD) Monte Carlo for roughly 70% of the integrals, at about \(10^6\) CPU-core hours.

The hard IR pole cancels exactly against the mixed sector. The new constant term is \(p_{3,5}=1.02270(2)-5.64(5)N_f^{-1}-8.50(7)N_f^{-2}\). Two independent checks hold up: - The Bugblatter sum gives \(b=-5.44(24)\times10^{-7}\), against the literature value \(-5.545(12)\times10^{-7}\). - Renormalization-group invariance holds through N³LO.

Along the way the authors corrected two errors in their earlier work: an \(O(\varepsilon)\) normalization error in the mixed term, and a factor of two in the N²LO handling that shifted \(p_{3,4}\).

The physics result is the uncertainty story. At \(\alpha_e=0.2\), order-to-order shifts far exceed the \(X\in[1/2,2]\) scale band. The Bayesian abc error band at N²LO, by contrast, contains the actual N³LO result near its center, and the series stays predictive up to \(\alpha_e\approx0.4\). Because the physical \(\alpha\) is small, the direct phenomenology is negligible. The value is as a benchmark and as a partial integral library for cold quark matter, and hence for the neutron-star EoS. One caveat is that the Bayesian conclusion rests on modelling the coefficients as draws from a common distribution.

arXiv:2610.13097 — Threshold enhancement in \(\pi^0\pi^0\) from \(\psi(3686)\to\pi^0\pi^0J/\psi\) (BESIII Collaboration et al.)

BESIII selects \(8.4\times10^6\) events from \(2.7\times10^9\) \(\psi(3686)\) decays, using \(J/\psi\to ee,\mu\mu\) and a 7C kinematic fit. Background is about 0.1% and non-peaking. A Breit–Wigner fit to the threshold structure gives: - 2554 ± 62 signal events, at more than 10σ, - \(M=274.3\pm2.1\pm0.6\) MeV, - \(\Gamma=17.1\pm1.5\pm0.5\) MeV.

These values are consistent with the charged channel (about 283 MeV and 17 MeV). The neutral mode carries no I=1 component and no ISR background, which makes it the cleaner confirmation.

The unfolded 2D spectrum, in \(m_{\pi\pi}\) and \(\cos\theta_{\pi^0}\), discriminates between models: - ChPT: adding FSI improves χ²/ndf from 48.6 to 6.5, but the model still misses the threshold region. - QCDME: with 2S–1D mixing fixed at \(\theta_{\rm mix}=12^\circ\) and FSI included, it describes the full spectrum. - Adler zero: the dip near 0.3 GeV persists, which BESIII ties to the Adler zero.

Caveats: - The mixing angle had to be fixed because it is strongly correlated with β. - A systematic trend remains in the \(\cos\theta\) pulls. - The ChPT parameter errors are implausibly tiny (e.g. \(g_1/g_0=-0.09426\pm0.00002\)). - The introduction says a structure near the π⁺π⁻ threshold would favor a rescattering cusp, but the paper never states whether the measured mass supports that reading. That question is left to theorists.

arXiv:2610.13867 — Redshift-space galaxy power spectrum at two loops (Oliver H. E. Philcox)

(The extracted text stops partway through the numerical section; the validation numbers below come from the abstract.)

Philcox gives the first complete two-loop, redshift-space galaxy \(P(k)\) in the EFT of large-scale structure (EFTofLSS). The model is renormalized at fifth order in fields and derivatives, and its ingredients are: - 17 leading-gradient, 7 next-to-leading-gradient and 1 next-to-next-to-leading-gradient bias operators, - spatially non-local counterterms generated by the dark-matter equations of motion, - 15 redshift-space counterterm combinations, - 6 stochastic terms.

That totals 49 coefficients, against 10 at one loop. Closure under renormalization is verified exactly at every power of \(f\). IR resummation is applied without the usual factorized approximation. The same kernels fix the deterministic one-loop bispectrum and trispectrum, and 18 of the coefficients are constrained by the one-loop bispectrum.

The loop integrals are precomputed with vegas, so a likelihood evaluation takes milliseconds. On simulations, \(k_{\max}\) rises from 0.2 to 0.34 \(h\)/Mpc for an LRG sample at z = 0.5. The new biases and counterterms absorb most of the added information, however, and \(\sigma_8\) does not tighten appreciably. The author also stresses strong parameter degeneracies. On its own the result is a template and a calibration of two-loop expectations, not yet a cosmology gain. Its main promise is in joint fits with higher-order statistics.
