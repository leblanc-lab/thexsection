A helicity convention, corrected

The day’s cleanest result is Žlebčík arXiv:2609.31828. The \(\rho\to\pi\pi\) helicity convention in the time-dependent \(B^0\to(\rho\pi)^0\) Dalitz analyses does not match the one the downstream isospin analysis assumes. Correcting it drops the direct-versus-indirect \(\alpha\) tension from 2.9σ to 2.1σ. LZ produced no papers yesterday and seven today, and the new work is mostly about the velocity distribution rather than the particle content. Underneath, ML papers now read as infrastructure reports, and several precision-QCD results arrive together.

The LZ recoil meets the halo

Oliveira arXiv:2609.35650 rebuilds LZ’s inference and allows a surviving excited-state fraction \(f\). For \(f\gtrsim10^{-5}\)–\(10^{-2}\) exothermic scattering dominates, and at \(f=1/2\) the required cross section falls by factors of 80 to \(1.6\times10^4\). Das et al. arXiv:2609.35737 use a Gaia-informed halo with a suppressed high-velocity tail. Deep dives

arXiv:2609.31828 — Impact of Helicity–Isospin Convention Matching on the Extraction of α from B⁰→(ρπ)⁰ (Radek \v{Z}leb\v{c}'{i}k)

Belle and BaBar both define the ρ→ππ helicity angles in the cyclic convention, in which the ρ⁰ angle is referenced to the π⁻. They then extract α with the Lipkin–Nir–Quinn–Snyder relation \(A^++A^-+2A^0\). That relation holds only if the ρ⁰ angle is referenced to the π⁺. The mismatch amounts to a sign flip of \(A^0\) and \(\bar A^0\). Žlebčík corrects it by flipping the 12 of the 26 \(U/I\) parameters that are linear in \(A^0\) or \(\bar A^0\), along with the corresponding covariance off-diagonals. He first reproduces the public HFLAV ρπ and combined curves exactly, then reruns the scan.

The corrected ρπ curve is much flatter near 90°. Its tension with the indirect value (91.2°) drops from 2.9σ to 2.1σ. The combined result becomes \(\alpha=(87.0^{+11.8}_{-4.7})^\circ\), compared with HFLAV’s \((84.1^{+3.7}_{-3.0})^\circ\). The ππ+ρρ combination alone gives \((85.9^{+8.6}_{-3.9})^\circ\).

The paper says every phenomenological treatment it examined carries the mismatch, including the Belle and BaBar papers, HFLAV/CKMfitter and UTfit. It traces the root cause to Snyder–Quinn never specifying the reference pion.

Caveats: - The apparent precision was partly an artefact. The combined upper error roughly triples, and ρπ no longer improves precision; only the 2σ interval tightens slightly, to [79.1°, 108.1°]. - The ρπ best-fit χ² rises from 8.4 to 9.5 (17 dof), although the combined χ² improves by 1.4. - A mirror solution near 0° persists.

For Belle II and LHCb, the practical lesson is that the Dalitz convention must be fixed before the isospin fit.

arXiv:2609.35650 — The LZ event with a two-state dark matter halo (Vinicius Oliveira)

Oliveira rebuilds LZ’s inference from public material. He uses a binned likelihood from the Fig. 5 projection (3 S1c slices × 32 bins), the shell-model nuclear response, and a toy-MC Neyman construction. The Helm form factor would overestimate the rate at 248 keV by a factor of 5, which is why the shell-model response matters. The recast matches LZ’s endothermic upper limits within 26% at all 17 points with δ ≥ 50 keV, and within 5% at about half of them. Its local significances run 0.2–0.7σ low: Z = 2.5 versus 3.0 at 1 TeV and δ = 300 keV.

The recast then allows a surviving excited-state fraction \(f\). Freeze-out or freeze-in naturally gives \(f\approx1/2\) unless \(\chi_2\) decays. Exothermic scattering dominates above a critical \(f\) of \(6\times10^{-3}\), \(1.4\times10^{-3}\) and \(3\times10^{-5}\) at δ = 250, 300 and 350 keV. Above that point the required cross section scales as \(1/f\).

At \(f=1/2\) and 1 TeV: - The best-fit σ_N drops by 80, 360 and \(1.6\times10^4\) at those three splittings. - σ_N stays within \(3.5\times10^{-45}\)–\(3.6\times10^{-44}\) cm² over 250–500 keV. - The endothermic cutoff disappears. - Di Mauro’s thermal benchmark (297 keV, \(6.5\times10^{-43}\) cm²) sits 30× above the upper limit.

Caveats: - The exothermic intervals cannot be validated against any LZ result. - The likelihood is binned and the background is fixed. - Only the Standard Halo Model is used, which connects to Das et al. on halo dependence. - The paper does not impose the \(\chi_2\) lifetime bound. Radiative decay could drive \(f\to0\), which is exactly what Dutra et al. propose to test with a line in COSI. - The paper also flags unexplained internal inconsistencies in where LZ’s published lower limits turn on.

arXiv:2609.34984 — A First Glimpse at Super-Leading Logarithms in Monte Carlo Evolution (Nicolas Schalch)

Schalch extends the EFT-based Marzili framework to resum the SLL tower \(\alpha_s^{n+3}L^{2n+3}\). The tower comes from two Glauber exchanges, arbitrarily many incoming-collinear emissions, and one terminal soft emission into the gap. The calculation is at the leading nonzero colour order, relative \(1/N_c^2\). The RG path-ordered exponential is turned into a shower-like sequence of Sudakov-sampled emissions acting on paired amplitude and conjugate-amplitude colour flows.

The key technical point is that retaining \(h=d+p\le2\) bounds the flow mismatch to at most two partner swaps at any multiplicity. Glauber interference therefore survives without the colour space blowing up. In qq′, any real emission between the Glaubers gives zero. In qg, such emissions and unequal final flows contribute.

The validation is against analytic resummations at \(\Delta Y=2\), \(\xi=\alpha_sL^2=8\):

Channel Marzili Analytic
Octet (qq′) \(C_O=6.2759(47)\) 6.2754
Singlet (qq′) \(C_S=-11.6853(45)\) −11.6804
qg→qV \(C_{qg}=0.1099(247)\) 0.0971

The pronounced qg turnover is reproduced.

Caveats: - The calculation uses fixed coupling, strict-forward geometry and a single soft emission. The NGL–SLL interplay is not yet included. - There is a real sign problem in qg: the mean absolute weight is about 52× the mean signed weight, hence the ~25% error. - A mapping to CVolver’s cutoff-regulated Coulomb treatment is still open.

A parallel parton-shower effort (Helliwell) is noted.
