The higgsino window keeps narrowing

The LZ event is still the center of gravity, but the traffic has shifted from model-building to model-killing. A week after the interpretation glut converged on a ~1.1 TeV endothermic higgsino, the constraints keep arriving: yesterday Pospelov’s solar-capture bound demanded δ>566 keV, and today Rodd et al. arXiv:2609.04175 point out that a thermal higgsino with the required splitting should have deposited events in LZ’s high-energy sideband — which is empty. McCabe arXiv:2609.04181 adds a different discriminant: the same kinematics force the signal into the halo’s high-speed tail, predicting annual modulation above 50%, sometimes 100%. Away from xenon, the day’s real substance sits in a pair of lattice results — a second direct calculation of the θ-induced pion-nucleon coupling, and the first two-hadron form factor from QCD.

Themes

The LZ event, squeezed from both sides

The interpretation stream hasn’t dried up — Chattopadhyay et al. arXiv:2609.02994 fit a sub-TeV singlino, Du et al. arXiv:2609.04163 push the higgsino to intermediate-scale gauginos with a non-universal GUT boundary condition, Smirnov et al. arXiv:2609.04144 give a clean universal treatment across coupled EW multiplets, and Jeesun et al. arXiv:2609.04185 and Unwin arXiv:2609.04186 offer escape routes (atmospheric-ν up-scattering, elastic axion-portal) that dodge the halo-tail dependence entirely. But the useful papers are the ones adding pressure: Rodd’s sideband and McCabe’s modulation both give the collaboration concrete things to check, and both point the same way as yesterday’s solar bound — the higgsino reading is getting hard to sustain.

Strong CP on the lattice, a second determination

He et al. arXiv:2609.04129 compute the CP-violating pion-nucleon coupling \(\bar g_0\) from the θ-term on 2+1+1 HISQ ensembles, wrestling with \(N\pi\) excited-state contamination and landing at \(\bar g_0/(2F_\pi)=17.4(1.9)\times10^{-3}\,\bar\Theta\) via the axial Ward identity. This is a direct sibling to Li et al.’s \(\tilde g_{\pi NN}\) from late August — the same hadronic-coupling rung above the nEDM, now from a second group and method. The strong-CP thread the memory has tracked since the nEDM/lattice pair is still producing.

Lattice firsts and real-time gauge theory

Ortega-Gama et al. arXiv:2609.04142 report the first QCD determination of an energy-dependent form factor for a two-hadron scattering state (\(\pi^+\pi^+ + \gamma\)), validating the finite-volume approach against the Ward-Takahashi identity — a genuine methodological milestone toward electroweak responses of resonances. Alongside, two tensor-network papers simulate real-time string breaking in 3+1D U(1) arXiv:2609.04114 and thermalization in open non-Abelian LGT arXiv:2609.04117, extending the quantum-simulation-for-QCD strand toward physical dimensionality.

Deep dives

arXiv:2609.04175 — Confronting the Higgsino Interpretation of the LZ Event with the High-Energy Sideband (Nicholas L. Rodd et al.)

Abstract only. The empty region above the 248 keV event is the discriminant. A thermal 1.1 TeV higgsino tuned to the observed rate and spectrum — splitting δ ~ 370–490 keV depending on the halo model — would, Rodd et al. argue, generically populate a higher-energy sideband along LZ’s S1c axis with additional recoils. LZ reports zero events there, so the higgsino reading sits in mild tension with its own data. The caveat is explicit: the acceptance in that bin is not public, so the tension cannot be quantified without the collaboration’s input — precisely what the authors ask for. They note a non-thermal ~500 GeV higgsino can evade the sideband, and that heavier-target experiments could test the interpretation either way. This is the sideband complement to yesterday’s solar-capture bound: another empty region pushing the same direction, giving LZ a concrete internal cross-check rather than an external astrophysical one.

arXiv:2609.04181 — Seasonal dark matter from the LUX-ZEPLIN high-energy event (Christopher McCabe)

The high recoil energy forces the inelastic splitting against the kinematic ceiling set by the halo escape speed, and McCabe turns that into a sharp seasonal signature. A shape-only profile likelihood in the (m_χ, δ) plane confines δ to ~370–400 keV at 1σ for m_χ = 1 TeV, with small splittings disfavored because they place the spectrum below 248 keV. At δ = 350 keV the required v_min = 766 km/s sits just 44 km/s below the maximum lab-frame speed (810 km/s), so the entire signal comes from a thin sliver of the halo tail. The consequence: annual modulation amplitude A = 94% at (1 TeV, 350 keV), rising to 100% at 500 GeV — the rate literally vanishes for up to two-thirds of the year. The modulation is nearly pure timing information (97.2% of discriminating power is arrival-time, not energy correlation) and carries mass information even where spectra are degenerate. Reach: 11 events give 3σ on modulation, 31 give 5σ; XLZD at 200 t·yr reaches 7.5σ, and LZ’s own ~15 t·yr already hits 3σ for δ > 374 keV. A secondary handle is isotopic — ¹³⁶Xe supplies 47% of the rate at δ = 380 keV versus 8.9% natural abundance — though enrichment at scale is unrealistic. Honest caveat, stated twice: everything rests on the Standard Halo Model, precisely in the tail region where it is least trustworthy.

arXiv:2609.04144 — Inelastic Signatures of Electroweak Dark Matter (Juri Smirnov et al.)

Smirnov, Griffith, and Beacom show that Higgs-coupled minimal dark matter — a Majorana plus adjacent Dirac multiplet at the custodial point — reproduces the LZ event through a mechanism that is universal across the whole 3_M2_D through 13_M12_D sequence. The neutral Higgs Clebsch is exactly 1/√2 independent of representation, so both the pseudo-Dirac splitting δ ≃ y²v²/4m_χ and the off-diagonal Z coupling (σ_n^inel ≃ 1.9×10⁻³⁹ cm²) are fixed by (m_χ, y) alone. Requiring one expected inelastic event in LZ’s 2.84 t·yr defines a rate-selected δ_LZ(m_χ) ~ 350–380 keV and a universal band in (m_χ, y); intersecting it with each multiplet’s thermal relic trajectory selects a benchmark, from 3_M2_D at 1.44 TeV to 13_M12_D at 131 TeV. The payoff is a correlated, representation-dependent loop-induced elastic signal — from 1.3×10⁻⁴⁷ cm² up to 5×10⁻⁴⁵ cm² — that must lie above the neutrino floor. Existing null elastic results already disfavor 11_M10_D and 13_M12_D, leaving 3_M2_D, 5_M4_D, 7_M6_D as the preferred survivors, all testable by next-generation searches. Caveats are candidly flagged: the rate estimate uses no full detector-response/likelihood reconstruction; the relic calculation is done in the unbroken phase while the signal depends on sub-MeV broken-phase splittings (argued negligible but unchecked); and it remains a single-event excess. The distinctive value is predictivity — inelastic rate, splitting, and elastic channel are locked together rather than fit independently.

Citation radar

This week’s radar is dominated by a single new experimental anomaly and the theory rush to claim it. Both featured papers, posted the same day (September 1, 2026), interpret the LZ Collaboration’s ~248 keV high-energy nuclear-recoil “event of interest” as inelastic Higgsino dark matter — and both drew ~10 citations almost immediately. Attention is flowing hard toward the oldest, most canonical WIMP candidate suddenly being handed a possible signal, and toward the fact that the TeV Higgsino’s interaction strength is fixed rather than fitted, making it uniquely predictive against the new data.

arXiv:2609.01583 — Higgsino Dark Matter Interpretation of the LUX-ZEPLIN 248 keV Nuclear-Recoil Event (Katherine Freese et al.)

Freese and Theodosopoulos make the minimal, sharp argument: a nearly pure ~1 TeV Higgsino realizes inelastic dark matter through the off-diagonal Z coupling of two nearly degenerate neutral Majorana states split by δ, and this fixes the inelastic Higgsino–nucleon cross section at σ ≃ 1.86×10⁻³⁹ cm², rather than leaving it free. Using the LZ scalar-to-vector conversion (σ_V ≃ 3.2 σ_SI for xenon), they show the fixed Higgsino prediction falls inside LZ’s two-sided 90% interval for δ ~ 350 keV at m = 1 TeV. Honestly, the paper also concedes tension: rescaling to the thermally preferred 1.1 TeV (via an efficiency-weighted rate ratio ≈0.8) pushes the interval below the prediction, though δ slightly above 350 keV — near xenon’s kinematic ceiling — would recover consistency.

The ~10 citations in days reflect first-mover advantage on a fresh anomaly plus the authors’ prominence. The headline draw is the “no free parameter” framing: the LZ event lands where the most economical SUSY WIMP predicts. The caveats are stated plainly — the rescaling is not a proper LZ likelihood reanalysis — but the provocative claim (a classic candidate matching a real excess) is what the community is picking up.

arXiv:2609.01590 — TeV Higgsino Dark Matter from LZ Nuclear Recoil to Fermi-LAT Gamma Rays (Lei Wu et al.)

Wu, Zhang, and Zhu pursue the same inelastic-Higgsino idea far more ambitiously, welding together three signals under one MSSM benchmark. Freeze-out fixes m ≈ 1.09 TeV; a joint likelihood combining relic density, an energy-only LZ recast, and the 14-year Fermi-LAT Galactic-center excess selects δ ≈ 324 keV. They further argue that destructive interference between tree-level Higgs-exchange and electroweak-loop elastic amplitudes can suppress solar capture/thermalization, evading the 10-year IceCube bound, and that the predicted γγ/γZ line plus endpoint sits near current H.E.S.S. sensitivity and within CTAO’s projected reach. The extensive Supplemental Material builds an internally consistent Einasto–Eddington halo, an annulus-by-annulus Fermi-LAT reweighting, and a response-matched CTAO projection.

The ~10 citations track the same anomaly-driven surge, but this paper’s appeal is different: it packages the LZ interpretation into a fully testable multi-messenger program with near-term falsifiability at CTAO. The authors are unusually candid that their LZ proxy is not the official likelihood and that the IceCube cancellation band is only shown to be “parametrically possible,” not located. Its citations likely accrue as the more complete reference framework for anyone extending the Higgsino-LZ story to indirect detection.
