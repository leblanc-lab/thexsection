Deconfinement in oxygen, and CMS at large

No single anomaly anchors the day, but CMS supplies its spine — three papers ranging from heavy ions to EFT to trigger performance. The cleanest result is the first observation of baryon-to-meson enhancement in oxygen-oxygen collisions (arXiv:2608.18256), the \(\Lambda/K^0_S\) ratio showing the coalescence/radial-flow signature of a deconfined medium in a genuinely small system — the payoff the season’s OO and small-system flow program (EPOS4 scaling, ALICE ultra-central) has been building toward. Around it sit two takes on LHC Higgs-sector excesses, a collider quantum-information program that has moved past entanglement into “magic,” and a scatter of new dark-matter detection concepts.

Themes

CMS on multiple fronts

Beyond the OO result arXiv:2608.18256, CMS reports the most stringent aTGC/EFT bounds to date from WW/WZ single-lepton production with merged-jet reconstruction (arXiv:2608.18245) — another entry in the season’s steady drift toward EFT as the default language for diboson searches — and documents deep-learning heavy-flavour jet identification in the high-level trigger (arXiv:2608.18331), the unglamorous infrastructure behind the \(HH\to4b\) and VBF/\(t\bar t H\) programs.

Two roads to an LHC excess

Dobrescu et al. arXiv:2608.18506 show a 10-GeV-scale ALP with \(h^0\to A_hA_h\to(gg)(\gamma\gamma)\) can reproduce the \(3.5\sigma\) CMS diphoton excess near 13.6 GeV, with a 4g companion mode giving wide two-prong jets. Separately, Uttayarat et al. arXiv:2608.18738 propose \(H\gamma\), \(Hj\), \(Hjj\) channels to distinguish mixing from leptophilic production behind the 146-GeV \(e\mu\) excess. Two excesses, two concrete follow-up strategies — worth flagging together as the kind of loose corners current data can’t yet close.

Quantum information beyond entanglement

The collider-QI thread the memory has tracked all season takes two fresh turns. Li et al. arXiv:2608.19095 study “quantum magic” (nonstabilizerness) in QED and electroweak scattering, finding — as in earlier lab-basis work — that the physical weak mixing angle sits near the value minimizing magic production, and now tracking how the helicity basis changes the count. Yamagishi arXiv:2608.18732 argues genuine tripartite entanglement uniquely selects gauge- and diffeomorphism-invariant theories where bipartite measures were ambiguous — an extension of the entanglement-selects-symmetry line to three particles.

New handles for dark matter

Three distinct detector ideas. Matas et al. arXiv:2608.18204 give the first ab-initio calculation of DM-electron scattering in quantum dots, proposing an array of tuned morphologies whose relative rates form a “barcode” discriminating interaction operators — a genuinely new method. Scopel arXiv:2608.18609 argues JUNO’s mass plus annual modulation can reach world-leading sub-GeV spin-dependent sensitivity via the Migdal effect, and Kyriazis et al. arXiv:2608.18200 turn X-ray binary disk heating into an axion-electron probe competitive with XENONnT.

Deep dives

arXiv:2608.18256 — Observation of enhanced baryon production using strange hadrons in oxygen-oxygen collisions (CMS Collaboration)

CMS measured \(p_T\)-differential \(K^0_S\) and \(\Lambda\) cross sections in OO and pp collisions at \(\sqrt{s_{NN}}=5.36\) TeV (6.8 nb\(^{-1}\) and 1.06 pb\(^{-1}\)), reconstructing the neutral strange hadrons via displaced two-track vertices. The nuclear modification factor \(R_{OO}\) for \(K^0_S\) tracks that of inclusive charged hadrons, but \(\Lambda\) baryons are enhanced over both in the intermediate range \(2<p_T<8\) GeV; above 10 GeV all three converge and sit well below unity, consistent with nPDF effects plus parton energy loss. The headline is the \(\Lambda/K^0_S\) cross-section ratio, which shows a pronounced enhancement in OO versus pp for \(2.3<p_T<5.2\) GeV, with a significance of 5.0–6.3σ across bins after accounting for correlated systematics. The ratio’s peak also shifts from \(p_T\approx2.3\) GeV in pp to \(\approx2.8\) GeV in OO.

This is the first observation of a baryon-to-meson enhancement in OO — the quark-coalescence/radial-flow signature of a deconfined medium — in a genuinely small system, without the event-activity selections that complicate high-multiplicity pp/pPb interpretations. It caps the season’s OO program (CMS charged-hadron suppression, ALICE/ATLAS flow, Υ suppression) with a clean coalescence handle. The qualitative trends mirror PbPb and AuAu, though “less pronounced in the smaller OO system.” Caveats are honest: the \(\Lambda\) feed-down correction (∼18% at 5 GeV) relies on scaling ALICE \(\Xi/\Omega\) ratios to the OO multiplicity, and the pp comparison inherits pileup and secondary-track corrections.

arXiv:2608.18245 — Search for anomalous couplings in WW and WZ production with single-lepton final states at √s = 13 TeV (CMS Collaboration)

Using the full Run 2 dataset (138 fb\(^{-1}\)), CMS searched for dimension-six EFT effects in boosted \(WV\) (\(WW\)/\(WZ\)) production where one \(W\) decays leptonically and the hadronic boson merges into a single large-\(R\) jet, tagged with mass-decorrelated ParticleNet (43% \(V\)-tag efficiency, ∼1% mistag). The \(m_{WV}\) spectrum (\(950\)–\(4550\) GeV) is fit simultaneously with \(W\)+jets and \(t\bar t\) control regions, with the SM \(WV\) cross section floated (constrained to \(0.32\pm0.1\) times the NLO prediction — a notable tension the authors attribute to large LO-to-NLO \(K\)-factors and flag for dedicated measurement). Data are SM-consistent, and the analysis is statistics-limited.

The payoff is the strongest constraints to date on aTGC operators: in the HISZ basis the observed 95% intervals are \(c_{WWW}/\Lambda^2\in[-0.93,0.93]\), \(c_{\tilde W WW}\in[-0.37,0.49]\) TeV\(^{-2}\), improving 30–70% over the previous 36 fb\(^{-1}\) result. In SMEFT, \(c_W\) and \(c_{\tilde W}\) reach \([-0.08,0.08]\) TeV\(^{-2}\) — the tightest triple-gauge bounds available — while the light-quark operators are competitive with, but not surpassing, inclusive-jet limits. This entrenches EFT with merged-jet substructure as the default diboson language, matching the digest’s theme. The main limitation is statistical, plus the approximate treatment of SMEFT operators potentially affecting background shapes (exact only in the HISZ triple-gauge case), and strong \(c_{qq}^{(3,1)}\)–\(c_{qq}^{(3,8)}\) degeneracy from insensitivity to color flow.

arXiv:2608.18506 — Axion-like particle at the 10 GeV scale: Higgs decays to wide jets and photons (Bogdan A. Dobrescu et al.)

Dobrescu and Roy build a renormalizable model where an ALP \(A_h\) couples to the SM only through the Higgs portal (\(-\tfrac{\mu_{H\phi}}{2v_\phi}H^\dagger H A_h A_h\)), with TeV-scale charge-2/3 colored scalars generating loop-induced \(A_h\to gg\) (BR ∼99%) and \(A_h\to\gamma\gamma\) (BR ∼0.15%, fixed by \(8\alpha^2/9\alpha_s^2\)). An effective portal coupling \(\mu_{H\phi}/v_\phi\approx10^{-2}\) yields \(B(h^0\to A_hA_h)\approx10\%\), below current undetected-width limits (\(<12\%\) from ATLAS under \(|\kappa_V|\le1\)). The key claim: \(h^0\to A_hA_h\to(gg)(\gamma\gamma)\) produces an automatically boosted diphoton at \(M_A\approx13.6\) GeV that satisfies existing low-mass diphoton selections. Delphes-level cutflows give acceptance×efficiency of 12.7% (CMS) and 11.9% (ATLAS), and the model’s \(1\sigma\) bands overlap both the CMS 3.5σ excess and the milder ATLAS ∼1.8σ at the same mass — reconciling the two, which under a singly-produced-ALP hypothesis appeared inconsistent.

If it holds, the CMS excess is not a lone diphoton resonance but a Higgs cascade with distinctive hadronic activity: the second \(A_h\) yields a wide jet \(J_{gg}\) with 2-prong substructure, and combining it with the diphoton reconstructs a 125-GeV peak (broad, from their simulation) — a background-suppression handle CMS/ATLAS could exploit. The companion \(h^0\to A_hA_h\to4g\) mode (two wide jets, or (2+2)-prong when boosted) is unsearched. Caveats: the fit relies on order-of-magnitude kinematic estimates and simplified isolation proxies (large PTRatioMax to bypass rejection), not a full experimental reproduction, and the ALP mass formula requires tuned but “natural” cutoffs.
