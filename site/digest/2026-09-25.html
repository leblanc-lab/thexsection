Inclusive |V_cb| holds, LZ goes heavy

Fael et al. arXiv:2609.28752 run a global HQE fit that treats perturbative scales as correlated nuisances and get \(|V_{cb}|=(42.32\pm0.30)\times10^{-3}\). That is a tight inclusive anchor against the exclusive determination that has kept \(R(D^{(*)})\) awkward since August (arXiv:2608.24332). The fit to the full moment set is poor, though, and the authors say so. The LZ recoil still accounts for five papers. Yesterday’s move was to engineer around solar capture, and today’s is to move out of its reach, with mass scales from 250 MeV to \(10^{11}\) GeV.

Themes

LZ: escaping by mass scale

Murayama et al. arXiv:2609.28819 push the inert doublet to 11–20 TeV. The lower number density still yields the event through exponential velocity sensitivity, and the higher infall velocity suppresses solar capture. Langhoff et al. arXiv:2609.28616 keep an MRSSM sneutrino near 360 GeV and use singlet mixing to evade the capture bound. Unwin arXiv:2609.28740 ties PeV Higgsinos to the vanishing-quartic scale. At the light end, Brdar et al. arXiv:2609.30255 propose a two-step \(\nu\to N_1\to N_2\) chain that is kinematically forbidden on the oxygen and carbon targets of neutrino experiments. Kalita arXiv:2609.29462 contributes the useful null: Hawking-boosted DM overproduces low-energy recoils, so the event can instead be turned into a bound on \(f_{\rm PBH}\).

Flavor, by theory

Beyond the \(|V_{cb}|\) number, Fael et al. find that predicted \(B\) widths sit 1.1–1.2σ below measurement, while the lifetime ratio agrees with experiment. Their closing call for new correlated moment measurements is the actionable part. Fleischer arXiv:2609.29314 provides a compact status-and-prospects overview heading into HL-LHC, Belle II and FCC-ee.

Tooling: SBI, generators, UFOs

Lopes de Sa et al. arXiv:2609.30196 combine a flow reference with classifier ratios to build an exact unbinned Asimov dataset. This continues the SBI thread from yesterday’s sterile-neutrino fit. Buss et al. arXiv:2609.28661 show that CaloClouds II transfers across geometries with only 100 target showers, and they directly test for model collapse under self-retraining. Hourtz et al. arXiv:2609.28619 extend Matchete to UFO export, including a full dim-8 SMEFT. This reinforces EFT as the default language for BSM work.

Small systems: initial state isn’t enough

Faraday et al. arXiv:2609.29886 tune IP-Glasma only to HERA. It reproduces the multiplicities and \(R_{pA}\) but underpredicts the suppression measured in O+O and Pb+Pb, which points to final-state energy loss. That is consistent with the oxygen payoff the season tracked. Liu et al. arXiv:2609.29237 add ZDC neutron ratios as a probe of short-range correlations, following yesterday’s SRC-in-O+O paper.

Worth a look

  • arXiv:2609.28752: An inclusive \(|V_{cb}|\) at 0.7% with an honest leave-one-out diagnosis of the bad fit. This is the reference number for the next round of the \(|V_{cb}|\) puzzle.
  • arXiv:2609.28819: Murayama et al. give the simplest answer yet to the IceCube problem, which is to go heavier. The catch is a large Higgs coupling with roughly one decade of headroom below the Landau pole.
  • arXiv:2609.28700: Toll et al. fit a Clem vortex profile to 104 HERA exclusive \(J/\psi\) points and extract dual-superconductor lengths, finding type-II (\(\kappa=2.7\)). Their \(1/\lambda\) matches the lattice gluelump. The idea is unusual and the fit is clean.
  • arXiv:2609.29442: Gardi’s Hidden Region Finder derives the Minkowski regions that Newton-polytope methods miss from pinch/Landau conditions. It is likely to become standard method-of-regions infrastructure.

From the community

arXiv:2609.30180 Climate, Science, and the FCC: Think Clearly, Choose Wisely, Act Swiftly — Patrick Janot et al. (hep-ex). The authors take the French and Swiss public-debate objections to the FCC one at a time, using orders of magnitude. It is written for non-physicists, and worth reading for exactly that reason.

Also noted

  • arXiv:2609.29911 Plasma Effects Suppress Mixing-Induced Collisional Freeze-In — Shao-Ping Li et al. (hep-ph). They claim that SM-plasma production cannot make keV–MeV sterile-neutrino DM. The claim is broad, and it is worth reading alongside yesterday’s resonant νMSM scan.
  • arXiv:2609.28617 Half-ladder partial waves for all \(n,d\) with applications to string theory — Jeffrey V. Backus (hep-th). Multi-particle positivity singles out the open bosonic string at \(d\le26\), and Z-theory fails.
  • arXiv:2609.22470 Stress-testing the spatially flat Universe — Hannah C. Turner et al. (astro-ph.CO). A nonparametric DESI DR2 fit mildly prefers \(\Omega_K>0\).

Deep dives

arXiv:2609.28752 — Inclusive \(B\) decays, lifetimes, and \(|V_{cb}|\): a unified heavy-quark expansion analysis (Matteo Fael et al.)

The default fit gives \(|V_{cb}|=(42.32\pm0.28_{\rm fit}\pm0.10_{\rm h.o.})\times10^{-3}\). Two methodological choices produce it. First, \(\mu_{\alpha_s}\), \(\mu_{m_b^{\rm kin}}\) and \(\mu_{\overline m_c}\) are floated as fully correlated nuisances under a flat Rfit penalty. Second, no ad hoc power-correction covariance is added; missing orders are instead probed by refitting at \(\mathcal O(1/m_b^{4,5})\). Across those orders \(|V_{cb}|\) moves by 0.2%, while \(\mu_G^2\) shifts by up to 43%.

The fit quality is poor: \(\chi^2/{\rm dof}=119.5/67\), \(p=8.4\times10^{-5}\). Leave-one-out isolates the Belle \(q^2\) moments, and dropping them gives \(p=0.23\). The higher-order coefficients appear to absorb data tension rather than measure genuine power corrections. The fit also finds \(\mu_G^2\) about a factor 3 below the \(B^*\)–\(B\) splitting relation.

The central value is about 1σ above the recent \(41.64\pm0.47\), which includes \(\mathcal O(\alpha_{\rm em})\) effects that lower \(|V_{cb}|\). For the digest’s inclusive-vs-exclusive tension, this is a tighter but method-dependent inclusive number. The authors caution that: - \(\mu_{\alpha_s}=9.13\) GeV sits on the boundary of its range. - The reduced uncertainty “is inherent to the model” and not due to improved inputs. - With scales fixed, \(|V_{cb}|=43.18\) with a \(^{+1.4}_{-0.7}\) scale variation.

Lifetimes come out 13% below experiment (1.1–1.2σ), rescued only by a strongly asymmetric non-leptonic scale error. The ratio \(\tau_{B^+}/\tau_{B^0}=1.082^{+0.031}_{-0.013}\) agrees with \(1.0832\pm0.0034\). The actionable output is the call for new correlated moment and branching-fraction measurements.

arXiv:2609.28819 — Heavy Inert Doublet Reconciles LZ and IceCube (Hitoshi Murayama et al.)

Murayama and Noether reuse the Fan–Reece likelihood and find that the inert-doublet \(\phi_0\to\phi_3\) upscatter matches the higgsino phenomenology to \(\mathcal O(10^{-9})\). The LZ data fix the splitting, \(\delta_0=368^{+12}_{-8}\) keV, essentially independently of mass; they do not select a mass. The \(1/M\) number-density loss is offset by the halo integral as \(v_{\rm min}\) falls.

The mass window comes entirely from other constraints: - Lower edge, 11.3 TeV: an independent re-implementation of the Bose et al. IceCube \(\nu_\mu\) solar limit (at \(\sigma_{\rm SI}^{\rm loop}=0\)) agrees with that analysis to 1–6%. - Upper edge, 20.3 TeV: freeze-out through longitudinal \(W/Z\) requires \(\kappa\approx6.92\,(M/11.3\,{\rm TeV})\), which hits the unitarity bound \(\kappa<4\pi\).

Expected yields are 0.05–0.72 events in the 215–270 keV window, compared with 0.93 for the 1.08 TeV higgsino.

The caveats are substantive: - Solar escape requires \(|\eta|\lesssim0.013\), but \(\kappa^2\) running of \(\eta\) demands percent-level tuning of its initial condition. - The Landau pole sits at only \(\sim23M\) down to \(6M\), or \(2.6M\) at large \(\lambda_\Phi\). - Neutrino spectra were generated at 1080 GeV and reused at all masses; the authors bound the resulting yield error to a factor 1.1–1.7.

\(\Delta T\simeq0.0015\) is harmless. The model is also untestable at FCC-hh.

arXiv:2609.28700 — Measuring Dual-Superconductor Length Scales in the QCD Vacuum at HERA (Tobias Toll et al.)

Toll and Vasim replace Gaussian hotspots with Clem’s vortex profile, \(K_0(\sqrt{b^2+\xi_v^2}/\lambda)\), in a Good–Walker dipole model. Coherent and incoherent moments are computed analytically, with log-normal saturation-scale fluctuations. They fit all 104 H1/ZEUS \(J/\psi\) photoproduction points (67 coherent, 37 incoherent; \(|t|<30\) GeV², \(40\le W\le251\) GeV) and get \(\chi^2/{\rm ndf}=70.8/98\) at \(N_{hs}=8\).

The extracted vacuum parameters are: - \(\lambda=0.191(5)(19)\) fm and \(\xi_v=0.087(5)(12)\) fm. - \(\kappa=2.7(3)(7)\), which is type-II. - \(1/\lambda=1.03(11)\) GeV, compared with the lattice \(1^{+-}\) gluelump at 0.87(15) GeV. The authors note that the gluelump value is scheme-dependent.

The Ginzburg–Landau string tension reproduces \(\sqrt{\sigma_0}\) for \(\alpha_S=0.37(9)\), close to \(\alpha_S(1/\lambda^2)=0.36(2)\). Alternative tails (stretched exponential, power law) worsen the fit by \(\Delta\chi^2=30\). A floating tail exponent returns \(n=1.17(15)\), consistent with the \(K_0\) value of 1.

Caveats: - The scalar sector fits less well. \(m_S=\sqrt2/\xi=3.9(7)\) GeV is 2.4(4)× the \(0^{++}\) glueball. - The dominant uncertainty comes from the weak sensitivity to \(N_{hs}\). - A free normalization, about 1.2, is floated. - The type-II prediction of vortex repulsion cannot be tested, because saturation fluctuations are degenerate with geometric variance at \(|t|\sim1\) GeV². \(M_Y\) tagging at the EIC would break this degeneracy.

The claimed \(Q^2\) independence is a clean EIC/LHeC test.

Citation radar

The radar this week is a single story. All four papers are responses to LZ230616, the lone ~248 keV nuclear recoil LZ reported at 2.6σ global (3.4σ local) significance, and all four were posted within about 48 hours of one another. Their bibliographies overlap almost completely, and much of each count probably reflects the reflexive “see also Refs. [x–y]” citing typical of a fast anomaly literature. Still, the sequence is instructive. The first wave asked whether a TeV electroweak multiplet with a few-hundred-keV splitting fits the event. This batch shifts to whether anything like that survives the Sun.

arXiv:2609.07807 — Not so good νs for Higgsino dark matter as LZ excess: stringent limits from Super-Kamiokande and IceCube (Debajit Bose et al.)

The authors compute solar capture of Higgsino DM through inelastic scattering on heavy solar nuclei, followed by annihilation to WW/ZZ. They confront the resulting neutrino flux with angular-cut atmospheric ν_e and ν_μ data from Super-K and IceCube. For the 1.08 TeV thermal Higgsino, splittings δ ≲ 557 keV are excluded, which covers the LZ-preferred 340–360 keV band. The strongest and weakest datasets differ by only ~20%. They also map limits for non-thermal Higgsino masses, where Super-K overtakes IceCube near 100 GeV.

The paper has drawn ~39 citations since 7 September. The most likely reason is that it is an independent cross-check of Pospelov–Ramani’s solar bound using different datasets and arriving at a comparable limit. That makes it the second half of the standard “solar capture disfavors Higgsinos” citation pair. The authors are candid that the thermal Higgsino survives if the local velocity distribution is non-standard, for example through LMC motion or streams. They also flag that the Helm form factor oscillates rapidly near 250 keV in xenon. An abstract-level note that one author thinks the event is background, sitting too close to the upper energy cut, may make the paper more memorable, but that is speculation.

arXiv:2609.06571 — The Inert Doublet Model of Dark Matter and the LUX-ZEPLIN High-Recoil Event (Lei Wang et al.)

This paper realizes the endothermic transition H N → A N through the off-diagonal Z coupling of the inert doublet. Because that coupling is fixed by electroweak strength, the scattering normalization is not a free parameter. The authors impose relic density, perturbativity, oblique parameters, elastic LZ limits and dwarf-galaxy limits. A profile likelihood on the event then gives a best fit at m_H = 1080 GeV and δ = 369 keV, with a GeV-scale charged splitting and λ_L ≈ −2×10⁻⁴. They stress that the event constrains δ far more tightly than the mass.

The ~35 citations likely come from its role as the canonical scalar counterpart to the Higgsino fit, with the full IDM constraint stack applied. The paper does not address solar capture, which is the main vulnerability of fixed-coupling interpretations. Lee–Youn (below) note that an inert-doublet fit lies in the same region as the Z₂-doublet case they estimate at roughly 10⁴ times the IceCube limit. The authors also acknowledge that the LZ likelihood cannot be fully reconstructed from public information.

arXiv:2609.07800 — Inelastic Singlet-Doublet Fermion Dark Matter in light of the 248 keV LZ event (Debasish Borah et al.)

Here a singlet-doublet fermion is split into pseudo-Dirac states by a hypercharge-1 scalar triplet, and the same triplet generates neutrino masses via type-II seesaw. The Majorana lightest state has no diagonal Z coupling, so the Z-mediated inelastic rate scales as sin⁴θ and the mixing sets the normalization. The preferred region moves with mixing: - At sinθ = 0.7, the fit prefers δ ~ 310–370 keV and masses of 10⁴–10^5.8 GeV. - At sinθ = 0.0135, it prefers δ ~ 30–180 keV and masses of 10^2.5–10^3.8 GeV.

Only the two small-mixing benchmarks evade elastic LZ bounds.

The ~34 citations likely reflect membership in the standard list of LZ interpretations. It also appears to be a direct precursor to the mixing-suppression mechanism developed in arXiv:2609.09138, which cites it. On substance it is thin: - The fit is a signal-only extended likelihood. - Relic density is taken from earlier work. - There is no solar or sideband analysis.

arXiv:2609.09138 — Mixing-suppressed inelastic dark matter: a minimal model for the LZ 248 keV event (Seung J. Lee et al.)

This is the most thorough of the four. The authors show that a full-strength Z transition at the recoil-inferred splitting predicts ~4000 events. Fixed-coupling models such as the Higgsino must therefore push δ deep into the halo tail, but solar gravitational acceleration undoes that suppression. A Majorana singlet mixed with a vector-like doublet instead suppresses the rate through sin⁴θ without shifting the spectrum. The same mixing drives capture as sin⁴θ and out-of-equilibrium annihilation as sin¹²θ, and weak Higgs cooling keeps the captured population extended.

With relic density and a one-event normalization imposed, they find δ = 302–305 keV conservatively (thermalized population). Under their cooling ansatz this rises to 331–343 keV near a 730–733 GeV endpoint. Falsifiable predictions include: - a tungsten rate ~46 times the xenon rate at the nominal ceiling; - no germanium or argon signal; - June-to-December rate ratios up to ~56.

Its ~34 citations are probably earned by its common-assumption comparison table, which places the Higgsino at ~4×10³ times the IceCube limit. That makes it a natural reference point for later papers.
