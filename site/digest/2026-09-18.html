Structure again, and a direct Higgs width

The day’s weight sits where it has for three days running — nucleon structure and precision QCD — but the sharpest single result is elsewhere: ATLAS arXiv:2609.19948 sets a direct, coupling-independent bound \(\Gamma_H<307\) MeV from on-shell \(H\to\gamma\gamma\) interference, a genuinely complementary handle to the off-shell method. Deep dives

arXiv:2609.19948 — Constraints on the Higgs boson total width from on-shell signal–background interference in \(H\to\gamma\gamma\) (ATLAS Collaboration)

ATLAS extracts a direct, coupling-independent bound on \(\Gamma_H\) from the interference between the resonant \(gg\to H\to\gamma\gamma\) amplitude and the continuum diphoton background, which distorts the \(m_{\gamma\gamma}\) lineshape — an antisymmetric peak shift (~\(-70\) MeV at the SM width) plus a ~\(-1.7\%\) yield reduction, both scaling as \(\sqrt{\mu\,\Gamma_H/\Gamma_H^{\rm SM}}\). Using the full 140 fb\(^{-1}\) Run 2 dataset in two \(p_T^{\gamma\gamma}\) categories, with \(m_H\) externally constrained from \(H\to ZZ^*\to4\ell\) and \(\mu\) profiled, they set an observed (expected) 95% CL limit \(\Gamma_H<307\,(258)\) MeV, ~75× the SM value; the best fit is \(\hat\Gamma_H=13^{+75}_{-13}\) MeV and \(\mu=1.08\pm0.12\). Statistics-only, the limit tightens to 105 (87) MeV.

Why it matters: unlike the off-shell \(ZZ/WW\) method (ATLAS combined \(\Gamma_H=3.6^{+2.1}_{-1.6}\) MeV), this probe makes no assumption that on- and off-shell couplings coincide, so it remains valid under exotic or invisible decays — a genuinely orthogonal handle even at ~30× weaker sensitivity. The caveat is explicit and dominant: the measurement is systematics-limited, with the largest single contribution (49 MeV at 68% CL) from the 39% theory uncertainty on the \(gg\)-interference \(K\)-factor (\(K_I=1.64\), NLO→NNLOsv), followed by the \(m_H\) constraint (43 MeV). Because interference scales as \(\sqrt{\Gamma_H}\), the \(K_I\) uncertainty dominates entirely near the limit. The authors flag that interference calculations matched to parton showers are the needed improvement, and note only two \(p_T^{\gamma\gamma}\) bins are justified given current modelling.

arXiv:2609.20545 — Computability of GPDs near \(x=\pm\xi\) in Lattice QCD (Yushan Su et al.)

Su, Ji, Liu, and Zhang argue that the LaMET large-momentum expansion for GPDs does not require the naive EFT condition \(2|x\pm\xi|P^z\gg\Lambda_{\rm QCD}\) near the transition points \(x=\pm\xi\), where one parton momentum vanishes. Their argument parallels DVCS factorization: soft contributions associated with the vanishing scale are power-suppressed, just as photon-DA physics enters DVCS only at \(O(\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^2/Q^2)\). The condition reduces to keeping at least one hard scale, \(2(|x|+|\xi|)P^z\gg\Lambda_{\rm QCD}\), dramatically enlarging the computable window that would otherwise exclude bands of width ~0.4 around \(x=\pm\xi\) for realistic \(P^z\sim2\)–3 GeV.

Reanalyzing four existing nonzero-skewness datasets (ETMC20/21/25, MSULat23) with complete one-loop matching and a carefully ordered regulator hierarchy (\(\epsilon_2\ll|\epsilon_1|\to0\), the only convergent ordering), they recover GPDs that are continuous at \(x=\pm\xi\) but with discontinuous derivatives — the expected partonic threshold kink from soft-quark zero modes, seen on the lattice for the first time and consistent with polynomiality-preserving GPD models. The previously reported discontinuities are attributed to regulator artifacts in the matching. This yields the first first-principles prediction of GPDs in the distribution-amplitude-like region (\(|x|<\xi\)), smoothly connecting quark and antiquark PDF-like regimes — directly relevant to DVCS extractions and the GUMP global-analysis program.

Caveats: the result rests on one-loop matching applied to existing data at coarse pion masses (mostly 260 MeV) and modest momenta, and the region remains uncomputable when both \(x\) and \(\xi\) are small (near \(x=0\)) or near the endpoints \(x=\pm1\).

arXiv:2609.20154 — Observation of double \(s\bar{s}\) production in \(e^+e^-\) collision at \(\sqrt{s}=3.08\) GeV (BESIII Collaboration et al.)

BESIII reports the first systematic observation of double-\(s\bar{s}\) production in the \(e^+e^-\) continuum, tagging a prompt \(\phi\) recoiling against a second strangeness-carrying system \(X(s\bar{s}) \in \{\eta,\eta',K^+K^-,K^+K^{*-},K^-K^{*+},K^0\bar{K}^0,K^0\bar{K}^{*0}\}\). From 167 pb\(^{-1}\), they measure \(\sigma(e^+e^-\to\phi+\text{anything})=506.3\pm6.9\pm19.7\) pb (a first) plus the individual \(\phi+X(s\bar{s})\) cross sections, with \(\phi K^+K^{*-}\), \(\phi K^-K^{*+}\), \(\phi K^0\bar{K}^0\), \(\phi K^0\bar{K}^{*0}\) reported for the first time. After subtracting ISR (\(\phi\gamma_{\rm ISR}\)) to isolate the QCD-driven part, the ratio is \(R_{s\bar{s}}=(40.4\pm1.7_{\rm stat}\pm1.5_{\rm syst})\%\).

Why it matters: this large ratio directly echoes the double-\(c\bar{c}\) surprise seen by Belle and BABAR at 10.6 GeV, where \(R_{c\bar{c}}\) exceeded early NRQCD predictions and drove years of theory refinement. Extending the phenomenon to the strange sector — in the deep non-perturbative regime — supplies a clean hadronization number that challenges the frameworks tuned to \(R_{c\bar{c}}\) and opens a window on possible flavor correlations in fragmentation. The synergy of strange and charm “flavor portals” is the framing.

Caveat, stated by the authors: the measurement omits higher-multiplicity final states (e.g. \(\phi K^+K^-\pi^0\)), so \(R_{s\bar{s}}\) is a lower bound on total double-\(s\bar{s}\) rate; \(f_{s\bar{s}}\) assumptions for \(\eta/\eta'\) and \(f\)-states carry model dependence, and several sub-channels have 6–11% systematics dominated by efficiency and background modelling.

Citation radar

This week’s radar is dominated by a single experimental spark: LUX-ZEPLIN’s report of one anomalous ~248 keV nuclear-recoil event (LZ230616), with a 3.4σ local / 2.6σ global significance. All five heavily-cited papers below are theory responses posted within days of the LZ preprint, and together they map the interpretive landscape that has formed almost overnight—endothermic vs. exothermic vs. neutrino-upscattering, Higgsino vs. inert-doublet vs. axion-portal. The rapid, cross-citing cluster (these papers reference each other extensively) is itself the story: a genuine community pile-on around a possible first direct-detection signal, with everyone racing to stake a mechanism and stress-test it against solar-capture and halo-tail constraints.

arXiv:2609.04186 — Axion Portal Dark Matter and the LUX-ZEPLIN High-Recoil Event (James Unwin)

Unwin proposes an elastic explanation of the LZ event, in deliberate contrast to the inelastic Higgsino interpretations dominating the early literature. The paper UV-completes the pseudoscalar-pseudoscalar operator ℒ₄ = (χ̄iγ⁵χ)(N̄iγ⁵N)—one of the templates for which LZ reported its largest local significances—via an axion portal: Dirac fermion dark matter (m_χ ≈ 400 GeV) coupled to a GeV-scale pNGB mediator, with a single multi-TeV vectorlike quark completing the up-quark coupling. The hard recoil spectrum arises from momentum dependence (moderated by a pion pole) rather than inelastic kinematics, so it is insensitive to the uncertain high-velocity halo tail; the model reproduces the relic abundance via p-wave freeze-out and brackets the LZ significance at 2.6–3.2σ.

At ~43 citations since early September, its pull likely comes from being the first full elastic UV completion of a favored LZ operator—offering a distinct, halo-robust alternative just as the field worried the Higgsino reading was fragile. The bonus correlation with the first-row CKM unitarity deficit adds cross-sector appeal.

arXiv:2609.04163 — TeV Higgsino Interpretation of the LZ High-Recoil Event with Intermediate-Scale Electroweak Gauginos (Xiaokang Du et al.)

Du and Wang address a specific tension in the popular TeV-Higgsino reading: reproducing a sub-MeV neutral-Higgsino splitting normally pushes electroweak-gaugino masses toward ~10⁷ GeV. They show that with opposite-sign bino and wino masses, the non-universal SU(5) GUT boundary condition M₁ᴳ/M₂ᴳ = −3/5 cancels the leading dimension-five contribution, and homogeneous one-loop running preserves this down to the Higgsino scale. The residual finite-gaugino terms then yield a controlled branch with a wino near 120 TeV for a 350 keV gap, μ = 1.091 TeV, tanβ = 10. They classify the mixed SU(5) representations (1+24, 1+75, 1+200) that realize the ratio, but candidly note the small gap is far more sensitive to bino–wino alignment than to overall scale, and that the inelastic Z coupling remains unsuppressed—so the full-density thermal Higgsino stays in tension with solar-capture bounds.

The ~42 citations reflect its role as the top-down SUSY-model-building companion to the phenomenological Higgsino fits: it supplies a concrete GUT-scale mechanism others can cite when invoking small Higgsino splittings.

arXiv:2609.04185 — Atmospheric neutrino up-scattering explanation of LZ 2026 excess (Sk Jeesun et al.)

Jeesun and Majumdar break from the dark-matter framing entirely: they explain the isolated high-recoil event via atmospheric neutrinos up-scattering into a massive BSM fermion χ (ν𝒩→χ𝒩) through a scalar mediator. The key kinematic feature is that producing a ~GeV-mass χ requires a minimum recoil energy of ~200 keV, naturally cutting off the low-energy tail and mimicking an isolated event exactly where LZ sees one. They identify a closed 90% CL band—m_χ ≈ 0.7–2 GeV, coupling √(y_χy_q)/m_φ ≈ 2×10⁻² GeV⁻¹—bounded below by the low-recoil veto and above by the falling atmospheric flux, and argue collider/beam-dump/supernova limits evade this region.

At ~40 citations, this is the field’s principal non-dark-matter alternative, keeping the interpretation space honest. Its value is providing a distinct testable hypothesis (a sterile-neutrino-like signal) that upcoming low-background experiments could distinguish from the halo-DM readings.

arXiv:2609.02505 — Dark Matter as the Z₂ Partner of the Standard Model Higgs Boson (Yasunori Nomura)

Nomura connects the LZ event to an inert-doublet model previously selected—in prior work—to fit a Fermi-LAT Galactic-halo gamma-ray excess at several-hundred-GeV mass. The conceptual hook is that the inert Z₂ parity is exactly equivalent (by field redefinition) to an interchange symmetry between two Higgs doublets, making dark matter the Z₂-odd partner of the SM Higgs. Phenomenologically, gauge invariance fixes the off-diagonal Z coupling, and the halo-plus-thermal mass window (440–600 GeV) selects a narrow strip of ~350 keV neutral splittings near the kinematic boundary where inelastic Z-exchange can produce the recoil. A sharp prediction follows: because scattering probes the extreme halo tail, future events should show large annual modulation peaking near early summer—and LZ230616 occurred June 16.

The ~32 citations track the paper’s ambition to unify two anomalies (gamma-ray excess + LZ event) under one minimal, technically-natural model. The v2 note honestly concedes the solar-capture (Pospelov–Ramani) constraint may require δ ≳ 500 keV, showing the interpretation under active pressure.

arXiv:2609.05204 — Exothermic Dark Matter at LZ (Carlos Henrique de Lima)

de Lima pursues the exothermic route: a metastable excited state χ_H down-scatters (χ_H𝒩→χ_L𝒩), releasing the mass splitting δ into the recoil so the signal does not require the halo’s high-velocity tail—the chief liability of endothermic Higgsino readings. Realized in a minimal inelastic dark-photon model, the fit favors m_χ ~ 30–200 GeV and δ ~ 0.5–1 MeV (bounded by δ < 2m_e for cosmological stability), with GeV-scale mediators and ε ~ 10⁻⁶. Both secluded freeze-out (yielding a small excited fraction f_H) and low-reheating freeze-in can supply the rate. The standout prediction is target dependence: because xenon is being probed near its diffraction minimum, argon and germanium should see ~3× more events at higher recoil energies (Table 1), giving DEAP-3600 and DarkSide-20k a clean cross-check.

At ~31 citations, its appeal is offering the halo-robust, testable counterpoint to endothermic models—and explicitly evading the solar-capture bound because χ_L is kinematically inert. Its dense cross-referencing of the whole LZ-interpretation cluster makes it a natural hub in this fast-moving conversation.
