Down in the detector shop

After a fortnight where theorists and phenomenologists held the floor, today the hardware people walked back in and put their tools on the table. Six instrumentation and reconstruction papers landed at once — a production-grade Kalman filter running in LHCb’s real-time trigger, a multi-year quality-control census of ATLAS ITk strip sensors, gamma-irradiation damage studies, scintillator timing, calorimeter energy reconstruction, a Timepix4 DAQ. It reads less like a coincidence than like the HL-LHC and future-collider machines reminding everyone that all the precision phenomenology of recent weeks eventually has to survive contact with silicon and firmware.

Around that core the day spread wide but not thin. Two threads the memory has been watching kept their momentum: quantum information is still creeping from novelty into working vocabulary — three papers today treat spin states and entanglement as collider observables — and the EIC-facing structure program produced a genuinely pretty result on the proton’s gluon cloud. Dark matter, meanwhile, was in a constraining rather than model-building mood: several papers today are about bounding things, and one of them quietly kills a benchmark GUT-scale naturalness scenario.

No collaboration bombshell, but a satisfying working day: things measured, things excluded, things fitted.

Themes

Instrumentation and real-time reconstruction

The standout is LHCb’s Billoir et al. arXiv:2607.14793: a parameterised Kalman filter replacing magnetic-field-map lookups and numerical integration with fast analytical forms, deployed on ~500 GPUs in the first trigger stage, delivering a factor-of-two improvement in \(D^0\) and \(J/\psi\) mass resolution and more than doubling accidental-track rejection — all at a 2% cost in processing time. This is the real-time-reconstruction wave reaching production, the hardware counterpart to the ML-trigger papers the memory tracked earlier this month. Alongside it, Federičová et al. arXiv:2607.14691 report the ATLAS ITk strip-sensor QC census (>91% of 590+ batches accepted), and Mikestikova et al. arXiv:2607.15056 characterize bulk damage in gamma-irradiated \(n^+\)-in-\(p\) diodes — the unglamorous radiation-tolerance groundwork HL-LHC rests on. Che et al. arXiv:2607.14290 (scintillator/WLS/SiPM timing), Biesuz et al. arXiv:2607.14847 (Timepix4 DAQ), and Pasquier et al. arXiv:2607.15023 (SDHCAL hadronic reconstruction for a future lepton collider) round out an unusually dense hardware cluster.

Quantum information at colliders

Yesterday this looked like an emerging coalescence; today it consolidates. Cheng et al. arXiv:2607.12015 (cross-listed) dissect the \(\tau^-\tau^+\gamma\) spin state across phase space and find final-state radiation can either decohere the pair or monotonically boost its entanglement. ShivaSankar K. A. et al. arXiv:2607.15153 show that entanglement markers, concurrence and the CHSH parameter in \(Z'\)-mediated \(t\bar t\) at future lepton colliders carry information complementary to cross sections — with beam polarization as a direct handle on chiral \(U(1)_X\) charges. And Mao et al. arXiv:2607.14558 compute spin-resolved ALP→\(e^+e^-\) decay in strong laser fields, using pair entanglement (singlet-like near threshold, triplet-like in the strong-field regime) as a signature of the production mechanism. Spin and entanglement are becoming ordinary columns in the observable table.

Nucleon structure toward the EIC

A coherent structure cluster, all pointing at future machines. Toll et al. arXiv:2607.14955 fit the hotspot model to all HERA exclusive \(J/\psi\) photoproduction data and extract a precise proton gluon-cloud shape — a perturbative Gaussian core of 0.105 fm inside a non-perturbative 0.220 fm halo, with an excellent \(\chi^2/\mathrm{ndf}=0.77\). Bellotti et al. arXiv:2607.14964 work out gluon GTMDs in exclusive heavy-quark-pair electroproduction; Cong Li arXiv:2607.14958 proposes a cross-section-ratio inversion to extract the photon distribution function at the EIC; Nefedov arXiv:2607.14986 adds \(O(v^2)\) relativistic corrections to high-energy-resummed quarkonium photoproduction; and Jia et al. arXiv:2607.15214 compute substantial NNLO QCD corrections to pion- and kaon-induced exclusive Drell-Yan for J-PARC.

Dark matter bounds and attenuation

The mood is exclusionary. Mosbech et al. arXiv:2607.15020 run full hydrodynamical simulations plus an emulator on high-resolution Lyman-\(\alpha\) data to set the strongest direct bound to date on DM–neutrino scattering, explicitly ruling out earlier hints. Gu et al. arXiv:2607.15098 fold resonant and deep-inelastic channels into boosted-DM Earth attenuation, refining PandaX-4T constraints. Chanda et al. arXiv:2607.14253 bound coexisting PBH-plus-particle-DM minispikes with neutrino data. And in the most striking entry, Lonardi et al. arXiv:2607.14243 mount the first experimental search for the \(N\)-naturalness tower of neutrinos, excluding — for Majorana neutrinos — the \(N=10^4\) GUT-unification benchmark that had been the framework’s tidy no-fine-tuning selling point. On the model side, Chen et al. arXiv:2607.14210 embed scotogenic DM in \(SU(6)/Sp(6)\) composite Higgs, and Issifu arXiv:2607.14979 threads Higgs-portal DM through neutron-star EOSs.

Phase transitions and GW backgrounds

The early-universe modeling continues to sharpen its systematics. Branchina et al. arXiv:2607.14867 show that consistently including thermal masses in bubble-wall friction suppresses the troublesome infrared gauge-boson contribution, so wall velocities end up close to top-quark friction alone — a welcome reduction of a poorly-controlled uncertainty. Ghoshal et al. arXiv:2607.15279 argue that the standard off-shell-decay picture of particle production in bubble collisions parametrically overestimates hard production and is gauge-dependent, offering a partonic reformulation. And Dimitriou et al. arXiv:2607.15219 deliver a sobering number: reconstructing cosmic-string tensions at LISA once all astrophysical foregrounds are included demands \(G\mu\gtrsim10^{-11}\) — a factor \(\sim10^5\) worse than foreground-free forecasts.

Deep dives

arXiv:2607.14793 — Track fitting at the full LHC collision rate (Pierre Billoir et al.)

(Abstract only.) LHCb deployed a fast parameterised Kalman filter in the first trigger stage from the 2025 run, fitting charged-particle trajectories at the full 30 MHz collision rate on ~500 GPUs. The trick is to replace magnetic-field-map lookups and numerical integration with analytical parameterisations while keeping the Kalman mathematical framework intact. Against the previous partial fitter it delivers a factor-of-two improvement in \(D^0\) and \(J/\psi\) invariant-mass resolution (from improved momentum resolution), greater robustness to misalignment, and more than doubled rejection of accidental tracks at no loss of genuine-track efficiency — for only a 2% processing-time increase.

Why it matters: this is real-time reconstruction crossing from R&D into production in an all-software trigger, the hardware counterpart to the month’s ML-trigger work. Sharper online mass resolution and background rejection directly widen what LHCb’s first stage can select and store, a template for HL-LHC-era triggering. As an abstract, the numbers are headline-level; the underlying validation (efficiency turn-ons, systematics of the parameterisation across the field volume) isn’t visible here.

arXiv:2607.14955 — A Precise Determination of the Shape of the Proton’s Gluon Cloud from HERA data (Tobias Toll et al.)

(Abstract only.) An analytic leading-twist calculation of coherent and incoherent exclusive \(J/\psi\) photoproduction cross sections in the hotspot model is fit to all H1 and ZEUS diffractive data, yielding \(\chi^2/\mathrm{ndf}=0.77\). The extracted gluon-cloud profile has a perturbative Gaussian core of \(0.105(1)\) fm sitting inside a non-perturbative exponential halo of \(0.220(2)\) fm — a two-scale structure the authors report as independent of \(x_{\mathrm{Pom}}\) or hotspot number, with a stable energy dependence \(\alpha'_{\rm eff}=0.046(19)\).

Why it matters: a clean, tightly-fit picture of the proton’s small-\(x\) gluonic transverse structure, resolving core from halo, is exactly the input the EIC structure program needs, and the excellent fit lends the hotspot framework credibility as an interpretive tool. As an abstract, the key caveats — leading-twist/leading-order treatment and the model-dependence of the incoherent-to-fluctuation mapping — can’t be assessed from the text provided.

arXiv:2607.14243 — Searching for the \(N\)-naturalness tower of neutrinos (Sofia Lonardi et al.)

First experimental test of the \(N\)-naturalness neutrino tower, via a global fit of public data (Daya Bay, GERDA, LEGEND-200) plus projections (JUNO+TAO, LEGEND-1000) in the Newtrinos.jl framework. The model solves the hierarchy problem with \(N\) dark sectors of varying Higgs mass, encoding residual fine-tuning in a parameter \(r\) (\(r=1\) = no tuning); implemented via the many-mixing-partners mechanism, it generates suppressed neutrino masses and a tower of extra light states that distort oscillations and shift \(m_{\beta\beta}\). Numerically diagonalising the full \(N\)-sector mass matrix (avoiding the perturbative breakdown at small \(N\)), they set 90% CL contours. For Majorana neutrinos, current GERDA+Daya Bay data already exclude non-fine-tuned scenarios (\(r=1\)) up to \(N=10^4\) for both orderings — killing the tidy \(N=10^4\) GUT-unification benchmark that let the framework address the hierarchy problem without ad-hoc tuning. JUNO+TAO projections push lower bounds to \(N\gtrsim500\) and require \(r\lesssim0.1\) in \(500\le N\le10^4\).

Why it matters: it converts a “no-fine-tuning” selling point into a falsified benchmark using existing neutrino data, exactly the constraining mood of today’s DM-and-naturalness cluster. Caveats are honestly flagged: the Dirac case is far weaker (no \(0\nu\beta\beta\) handle, excluded only to \(N\sim50\)); results assume \(m_0=0.01\) eV and \(\eta=1+1/N\); and \(m_{\beta\beta}\) rests on a single benchmark set of nuclear matrix elements (varied only for robustness checks).

Citation radar

This week’s radar leans toward infrastructure and framing: papers that hand the community a reusable tool, a systematic classification, or a reference review rather than a single headline number. Two automation/formalism papers (finite-temperature matching, multi-axion classification), a major experimental-systematics review, and a collider quantum-information study round out a set where the common thread is enabling other people’s calculations. All four sit at five citations, consistent with their recent posting.

arXiv:2604.21332 — Disentangling new physics with quantum entanglement in \(t\bar{t}\) production at future lepton colliders (Masato Arai et al.)

Arai, Mawatari and Okada compute quantum-information observables—the entanglement marker \(D_{\min}\), the concurrence, and the maximal CHSH parameter \(B_{\max}\)—for \(l^-l^+\to t\bar t\) in the SM and three s-channel extensions: a scalar mediator, a \(U(1)_{B-L}\) \(Z'\), and Randall–Sundrum KK gravitons. Working from helicity amplitudes and the two-qubit density matrix, they find each Lorentz structure reorganizes spin correlations distinctively: the scalar reduces entanglement, \(U(1)_{B-L}\) induces energy-dependent interference distortions, and RS gravitons produce qualitatively new high-energy angular structures with multiple local extrema. Results are cross-checked against MadGraph event-by-event spin-density-matrix simulation.

At ~5 citations since April, the paper rides the fast-growing “entanglement at colliders” wave triggered by the ATLAS/CMS \(t\bar t\) entanglement observations. Its appeal is likely as a concrete worked template for applying these observables as BSM probes at future lepton colliders (ILC, muon collider)—a natural next-step reference for a subfield actively hunting for new discriminating variables. The honest read: citations reflect topical momentum more than a singular result.

arXiv:2605.06787 — The structure of multi-axion solutions to the strong CP problem (Mario Fernández Navarro et al.)

Fernández Navarro and collaborators build a general framework for multi-axion solutions, organizing the theory space by two structural conditions: whether a PQ symmetry is broken only by QCD (“QCD-PQ”) versus more generally (“general-PQ”), and whether the QED and QCD anomaly vectors \(\bm E\) and \(\bm N\) are parallel (universal \(E_i/N_i\)) or not. Relaxing the assumptions behind the known sum rule of Ref. [89], they derive a generalized \(N\)-axion sum rule (via Sherman–Morrison inversion) that admits values above or below one, and show axions can now populate regions to the left of the QCD band—not only the right. They map these regimes onto UV completions, noting e.g. that non-universal anomaly coefficients would rule out simple GUTs.

The ~5 citations in roughly a month likely reflect its value as a classification scheme: it gives the axion-search community a clean language (“where can axions sit relative to the QCD line, and why”) that motivates broadening experimental targets beyond the canonical band. Framework papers with explicit sum rules and a theory-space figure tend to become quick reference points.

arXiv:2605.28671 — CP-violation or Nuclear Excitation: Reviewing the Role of Neutrino Interaction Model Uncertainties on Accelerator-Based Neutrino Oscillation Measurements (Stephen Dolan et al.)

Dolan, Pickering, Stowell, Wilkinson and Wret provide a Reports on Progress in Physics review of how few-GeV neutrino–nucleus interaction modeling limits accelerator oscillation measurements at T2K, NOvA, Hyper-K and DUNE. It walks through neutrino–nucleon cross sections (CCQE, resonant/shallow/deep inelastic, diffractive/coherent pion production, radiative corrections) and nuclear effects (spectral functions, RPA, FSI, 2p2h), then quantifies why near-to-far-detector extrapolation does not trivially cancel systematics—different targets, detector responses, oscillated fluxes, and \(\nu_e/\nu_\mu\) cross-section differences. A recurring device is “model spread” across event generators (NEUT, NuWro, GENIE, GiBUU) as a lower bound on interaction uncertainty.

As a comprehensive, up-to-date review with extensive tables and generator comparisons, it is a natural citation anchor: authors of DUNE/Hyper-K systematics studies can point to it for context and for the argument that interaction uncertainties are “the least clear” path to control. The ~5 citations so soon after a May posting reflect the standard trajectory of an authoritative review that consolidates a sprawling literature into one referenceable document.

arXiv:2604.21972 — Matchotter: An Automated Tool for Dimensional Reduction at Finite Temperature (Javier Fuentes-Martín et al.)

Fuentes-Martín, López Miras and Moreno-Sánchez present Matchotter, a finite-temperature module inside the Matchete package that automates one-loop dimensional reduction—integrating out heavy Matsubara modes to build the purely spatial 3D EFT used for cosmological phase-transition computations—for generic Lagrangians including SMEFT. They adapt functional matching techniques to the thermal path integral (identifying the EFT with the “hard region” of the effective action), handle Lorentz-breaking sum-integrals, and additionally automate supersoft matching (integrating out Debye-massive temporal gauge bosons). The tool is validated against SU(N) Yang–Mills, the Abelian Higgs model, a Yukawa toy model, and the electroweak SMEFT, with benchmark timings from seconds to ~30 minutes.

The headline selling point is systematic inclusion of higher-dimensional operators, filling a gap left by the widely used DRalgo (restricted mostly to renormalizable interactions). Given rising interest in higher-precision phase-transition predictions for gravitational-wave observables, a public, automated code addressing a “tedious and highly error-prone” bottleneck is exactly the kind of deliverable that accrues citations quickly—~5 already—as groups adopt it for BSM phase-transition studies.
