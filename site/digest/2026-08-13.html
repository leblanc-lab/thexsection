The B anomalies come back, from data

After a week of neutrino mixing numerology, the weight today swings to flavor — but from the data side, not model-building. LHCb posts a model-independent measurement of the \(B^0\to K^{*0}\mu^+\mu^-\) transversity amplitudes arXiv:2608.12215 with the \(C_9\) deviation landing at 4.3–4.8σ depending on binning, and Bouchard et al. arXiv:2608.11298 find the \(B\to PP\) global fit under SU(3)\(_F\) off by 4.1σ, with SU(3)-breaking parameters that make no sense in the SM. Two independent hadronic/semileptonic \(B\) anomalies in one day is the story. Around them sit a precision NOvA antineutrino–hydrogen cross section, an FCC-ee Higgs-mass study reaching the natural width, and a clean Majoron EFT.

Themes

B-physics anomalies, data-driven

The LHCb amplitude analysis arXiv:2608.12215 is the one to open: rather than fitting angular observables in fixed \(q^2\) bins, they reconstruct the transversity amplitudes from Legendre polynomials, letting global fits choose their own binning and disentangling hadronic contamination from a genuine \(C_9\) shift — which survives at 4.3–4.8σ. Bouchard et al. arXiv:2608.11298 attack from the non-leptonic side: their \(B\to PP\) fit demands ~1000% SU(3)\(_F\) breaking, worsening to ~5σ under theory constraints. Different sectors, same direction. Nothing in the recent memory anticipated a data-side flare-up of this size.

Neutrino cross-sections and Majorons

NOvA arXiv:2608.12293 delivers the most precise \(\bar\nu_\mu\mathrm{H}\to\mu^+n\) CCQE cross section to date (35,509 events, systematics four times below the flux uncertainty) — directly usable to constrain the absolute antineutrino flux. That speaks straight to last week’s DUNE cross-section-systematics warning (Puhan et al.): the field is starting to pin down the very inputs that were shifting sensitivities 25–30%. On the model side, Biggio et al. arXiv:2608.11522 build a Majoron EFT from Type I/II/III seesaws with a single VEV, so invisible Higgs width, Majoron–lepton coupling and leptonic non-unitarity all become locked relations — falsifiable even if the new states sit far out of reach.

Collider precision and EFT matching

Eysermans et al. arXiv:2608.12071 show FCC-ee can reach 4 MeV Higgs-mass precision in leptonic \(ZH\) recoil — comparable to the natural width, the enabling step for resonant electron-Yukawa production. This extends the SMEFT-precision thread the memory has tracked (Haisch’s NLL EWPO, Celada’s FCC-ee accounting). Dittmaier et al. arXiv:2608.11306 give a functional one-loop matching recipe for the Higgs singlet extension, noting the emergent EFT only maps to SMEFT at the cost of fermionic operators; Deng et al. arXiv:2608.12079 resum a quasi-collinear divergence in the missing-mass spectrum of invisible dark-photon production.

Dark matter and exotic-state searches

Two NaI(Tl) hardware papers land together — temperature-dependent SiPM readout arXiv:2608.11303 and the COSINE-100U encapsulation upgrade arXiv:2608.11678, both chasing lower thresholds for low-mass DM. LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA arXiv:2608.11620 excludes ultralight scalar/vector bosons via black-hole superradiance, and a mirror-neutron search arXiv:2608.12173 sets the first lab limit on peV-scale \(n\)–\(n'\) splitting that beats the neutron-star-cooling bound.

Deep dives

arXiv:2608.12215 — Model-independent measurement of the transversity amplitudes of the \(B^0\to K^{*0}\mu^+\mu^-\) decay (LHCb collaboration et al.)

LHCb reconstructs the full set of \(B^0\to K^{*0}\mu^+\mu^-\) transversity amplitudes unbinned in \(q^2\) over \(1.1<q^2<8.0\,\mathrm{GeV}^2/c^4\), expanding each P-wave amplitude in four Legendre polynomials (32 parameters, S-wave as nuisance) fitted to 8.4 fb⁻¹. The orthogonality of the Legendre basis reduces amplitude correlations relative to earlier empirical parametrisations, and CP-averaged observables (\(S_5\), \(A_{\rm FB}\), etc.) are then built as continuous functions of \(q^2\). Systematics run 0.09–0.35 of the statistical error, dominated by kinematic/trigger corrections. Profiling in flavio with the Ref. [14] baseline binning gives \(\mathrm{Re}(C_9^{\rm NP})=-1.17^{+0.21}_{-0.20}\), a \(4.3\sigma\) pull; quartering the bins tightens to \(-1.17^{+0.19}_{-0.18}\) and \(4.8\sigma\).

The key point is methodological: because hadronic (long-distance) contributions and a genuine NP \(C_9\) shift have distinct \(q^2\) dependences, the unbinned amplitude reconstruction lets global fits choose their own binning and, in principle, disentangle the two. The \(C_9\) deviation surviving this more flexible treatment — and growing with finer \(q^2\) resolution — is exactly what one expects from a real short-distance effect rather than a mismodelled charm-loop contamination. Results agree with the latest binned angular analysis [14]. Caveat: the interpretation still fixes all other Wilson coefficients to SM values and imports SM hadronic nuisance parameters through flavio’s covariance; the significance is a one-parameter statement, and the amplitude fit assumes no scalar/timelike contributions and a uniform-in-\(q^2\) S-wave. The disentangling of hadronic versus NP awaits improved theory predictions to compare against the measured amplitudes.

arXiv:2608.11298 — Anomalies in Hadronic \(B\) Decays (Marianne Bouchard et al.)

This BEACH 2026 proceedings summarises SU(3)\(_F\) global fits to \(B\to PP\) (\(P\in\{\pi,K,\eta,\eta'\}\)) in the topological-diagram basis, using EWP-tree relations to reduce 10 complex amplitudes to seven effective diagrams (13 real parameters). Individually the \(\Delta S=0\) (\(\chi^2/{\rm dof}=1.1/2\), \(p=58\%\)) and \(\Delta S=1\) (\(1.6/2\), \(p=45\%\)) fits are excellent, but the combined SU(3)\(_F\)-symmetric fit collapses to \(\chi^2/{\rm dof}=43/17\), a \(3.6\sigma\) SM discrepancy. The cause: best-fit diagram magnitudes differ across sectors by ~10×, e.g. \(|\tilde T'/\tilde T|=13.1\pm2.1\), \(|\tilde C'/\tilde C|=8.9\pm1.3\) — nominally 1000% SU(3)\(_F\) breaking versus the ~20–30% (\(f_K/f_\pi-1\)) expected. Adding \(\eta,\eta'\) (25 parameters) worsens the fit to \(4.1\sigma\); the \(B\to VV\) extension exceeds \(7\sigma\).

Imposing theory-motivated constraints makes things worse, not better: forcing \(|\tilde C/\tilde T|=0.2\) (QCD-factorisation colour-counting) against the fitted \(0.85\pm0.12\) pushes to \(4.9\sigma\), and setting the annihilation diagram \(\tilde A=0\) (expected \(\sim f_B/m_B\)-suppressed, yet fitted \(\sim|\tilde T|\)) gives \(5.2\sigma\). The claim to near-rigour rests on the EWP-tree relations being modified only ~10% by neglected \(c_{7,8}\). This is the day’s second, independent \(B\)-anomaly hint, from the non-leptonic sector. Caveat: it is a proceedings, results drawn from Refs. [6–9]; the central assumption is exact SU(3)\(_F\) with a fixed \(\theta_\eta=19.5°\), and the authors themselves flag that whether a genuine ~30% breaking could accommodate the data “has to be checked” — work in progress.

arXiv:2608.12293 — Measurement of the \(\bar\nu_\mu\)–Hydrogen CCQE cross section using the NOvA Near Detector (The NOvA Collaboration)

NOvA isolates \(\bar\nu_\mu\mathrm{H}\to\mu^+n\) from the 10.8%-hydrogen scintillator/PVC near detector (1.2×10²¹ POT), exploiting the nuclear-effect-free kinematics of a free-proton target: two BDT discriminants (topological HitID, kinematic KineID) plus data-driven control regions yield 35,509 background-subtracted signal events — the highest-statistics (anti)neutrino–hydrogen sample ever. The result, \(\sigma=0.538\pm0.009(\text{stat})\pm0.010(\text{syst})\pm0.055(\text{flux})\times10^{-38}\,\mathrm{cm}^2\) at \(\langle E\rangle=1.9\) GeV, is the most precise total QEH cross section to date, superseding the 13±6-event BNL bubble-chamber measurement. The decisive feature: the combined stat+non-flux systematic (2.5%) is more than four times below the 10.2% flux uncertainty, so the measurement can invert the usual logic and constrain the absolute \(\bar\nu_\mu\) flux.

This lands squarely on the digest’s theme: the same cross-section systematics that were shifting DUNE sensitivities 25–30% (Puhan et al.) are precisely what a nuclear-effect-free reference process pins down. The data-correction scheme is validated by 10,000 fake-data throws showing background predictions stable against ~60% MC variations, with \(k_{\rm cut}\) factors anticorrelated with the model shifts. Caveats are candidly stated: QEH purities are only ~41%/36% and signal efficiencies 3.6%/4.8%, so the analysis leans heavily on control-region-driven background subtraction; FSI weights are derived solely from neutrino-mode inclusive CC data and applied unchanged to antineutrino mode. The flux uncertainty still dominates the cross section itself — the payoff is prospective, once this becomes a flux standard candle.
