The LZ event looks for exits

Yesterday Langhoff exiled the thermal higgsino to \(10^5\)–\(10^6\) GeV; today the model-builders push back, trying to keep inelastic DM at ordinary scales by dodging the solar-capture bound rather than surrendering to it. Seven of the day’s papers touch the 248 keV recoil, and the useful ones are no longer fits but evasion strategies — light mediators, cosmic-ray boosting, two-component spectra. Meanwhile Nguyen et al. arXiv:2609.11833 reconfirm the Pospelov–Ramani bound in full generality, so the escape hatches have to work hard. Away from xenon, an axion-cosmology cluster and a clutch of collider spin/EFT papers carry the real methodological weight.

Themes

LZ: escape routes and a reconfirmed bound

The pattern is now evasion. Qi et al. arXiv:2609.10636 build a \(U(1)_{B-3L_\tau}\) scalar whose \(f_n/f_p\) suppresses solar iron capture, arguing the electroweak solar bound doesn’t transfer to light-mediator realizations; Heikinheimo et al. arXiv:2609.11600 drop the halo tail entirely with cosmic-ray-boosted DM and momentum-dependent operators peaking at 248 keV; Fan et al. arXiv:2609.10491 use two-component exothermic scattering to cut the required cross section and relax IceCube. Frolovsky et al. arXiv:2609.11241 tie a 1 TeV higgsino to Starobinsky supergravity, and Li et al. arXiv:2609.10662 chase inelastic capture in primordial dark stars. Against all this, Nguyen et al. arXiv:2609.11833 confirm Pospelov and show the solar-neutrino non-excess constrains the whole inelastic class — the counter-pressure the memory has tracked for two weeks, now generalized.

The strong-CP/axion thread running since August keeps widening past the lattice program into cosmology and detection.

Casalderrey-Solana et al. arXiv:2609.10679 present an “Efficient Wake” procedure for jet-medium response — tens of thousands of times faster than full hydro, aimed at Monte Carlo jet analyses.

Deep dives

arXiv:2609.11151 — Qubit-Qutrit Quantum Tomography of hadronic Λφ and ΛK*⁰ systems (Veronica Kanachova et al.)

Extending collider quantum tomography to unequal-spin final states, Kanachova et al. formalize the spin-½⊗1 density matrix for Λφ and ΛK*⁰ pairs and confront its inescapable incompleteness: the parity-violating Λ→pπ⁻ is fully self-analyzing, but the parity-conserving V→PP decay carries no rank-1 (dipole) analyzer, so only 23 of the 35 spin parameters are measurable — the meson vector polarization Pᵢ (3) and the dipole–dipole correlations Cᵢⱼ (9) drop out identically. The key result is that entanglement can still be certified exactly from this censored data. Zeroing the 12 missing parameters is equivalent to a “partial time reversal” on the meson (𝒰_B∘T_B), and the reconstruction ρ_meas satisfies ρ_meas^{T_B}=𝒰_B(ρ_meas), so the Peres–Horodecki test collapses to a single diagonalization: a negative eigenvalue of ρ_meas certifies entanglement, with no false positives, and no analysis of the same 23 observables can do better. Pure entangled states retain exactly half their negativity; mixed states have quantifiable “blind windows” (e.g. isotropic-noise dilution entangled for p>1/4 but uncertifiable until p>2/5).

Why it matters: this opens a new hadronization probe. Because local dressing cannot create entanglement, surviving correlations lower-bound the quark-pair correlation at production, and comparing degradation in Λφ/ΛK*⁰ (meson channel) against ΛΛ̄ (baryon channel) as a function of ΔR isolates p_meson vs p_baryon — a coherence-transport quantity never measured. Estimators are simple angular moments extractable from existing datasets. Caveat: the theorems’ validity relies on the specific 12-parameter censoring; other 12-subsets give false positives, and much of the numerical verification was AI-assisted (Claude Opus 5) with a stated but unproven pure-state conjecture.

arXiv:2609.11026 — Positive- and negative-parity charm-strange and bottom-strange mesons from lattice QCD (Forrest Guyton et al.)

Guyton and Meinel deliver a full RBC/UKQCD determination of decay constants, binding energies, and Weinberg compositeness for the lightest scalar, pseudoscalar, vector, and axial-vector Dₛ and Bₛ mesons, using domain-wall light/strange quarks and anisotropic clover charm/bottom on seven ensembles (mπ = 139–431 MeV, a = 0.073–0.114 fm) with combined chiral-continuum extrapolation. For negative-parity states they obtain f_{Dₛ}=251.4(2.1)(0.4)(2.5) MeV, f_{Dₛ}=272.5(4.3)(1.0)(2.7), f_{Bₛ}=228.4(5.8)(0.5)(2.3), f_{Bₛ}=229.2(4.4)(0.8)(2.3) MeV, plus ratios f_{Dₛ}/f_{Dₛ}=1.086(14)(11) and f_{Bₛ}/f_{Bₛ}=1.003(22)(10), consistent with FLAG. The positive-parity sector uses a GEVP with q̄q, derivative, and meson-meson-scattering operators (the latter at both source and sink), Lüscher’s method, and effective-range expansions to extract infinite-volume bound-state masses: m_{Dₛ0}−m_D−m_K=−48(14)(4) MeV, m_{Dₛ1}−m_{D}−m_K=−61(15)(2), m_{Bₛ0}−m_B−m_K=−69(13)(4), m_{Bₛ1}−m_{B}−m_K=−77(10)(5). The charm results agree with experiment (−45.0(6), −44.7(6) MeV). Positive-parity decay constants — including the first-ever lattice f_{Bₛ0*}=207(12)(8)(2) and f_{Bₛ1}=196(16)(11)(2) MeV — are reported.

Why it matters: the below-threshold binding, small positive-parity decay constants, and scattering lengths consistently support a predominantly D⁽⁾K/B⁽⁾K molecular interpretation of the Dₛ0*(2317)-like states, and the negative-parity constants feed dispersive bounds on b→s form factors relevant to the b→sℓℓ anomalies. Caveats acknowledged: two-pion left-hand cuts (which extend above the ground-state energies at low mπ) are neglected, invalidating strict Lüscher/ERE there, and weak-binding corrections to Weinberg’s criterion are expected to be large in some channels.

arXiv:2609.11641 — Distinguishing a pseudoscalar from a vector t t̄ resonance with top-quark spin correlations at the HL-LHC (Ahmed Bellagroudi et al.)

Bellagroudi and Fassi ask a post-discovery question: given a t t̄ resonance, can dilepton spin correlations separate a CP-odd 2HDM pseudoscalar A (0⁻, spin-singlet ¹S₀, iγ₅ vertex) from a leptophobic topcolor Z′ (1⁻, chiral vector)? Using MadGraph+Pythia+Delphes with matrix-element spin-preserving decays and neutrino-weighting reconstruction, they find a parton-level helicity-correlation gap Δc_hel≃0.43 (A: +0.33, Z′: −0.08), nearly mass-independent, surviving reconstruction with dilution ≈0.66. Normalizing A to CMS HIG-22-013 coupling limits and rate-matching Z′ so the test probes spin structure, the LO t t̄-only baseline gives 2.27σ (400 GeV) and 2.74σ (800 GeV) at 3000 fb⁻¹, using a 4×4 (c_hel, c_han) template likelihood. Reverse non-nested direction agrees; equal-yield tests give 1.86 and 2.70σ (confirming 800 GeV is shape-driven); profiling a Gaussian-constrained SM c_hel nuisance yields 2.07/2.63σ (optimistic) down to 1.67/2.26σ. A g⁴-mapped scan places 5σ at couplings ~22%/16% above reference.

Why it matters: this makes the toponium-vs-scalar interpretation debate quantitative, showing spin correlations can in principle assign resonance spin/parity where a rate excess cannot. The 800 GeV point is the cleanest demonstration; 400 GeV is intrinsically fragile because the near-threshold SM continuum is itself spin-singlet/pseudoscalar-like (the same physics driving threshold entanglement enhancement), and 1500 GeV is unreachable (~12 events). Honest caveats abound: LO normalization, fixed on-shell widths, no non-t t̄ backgrounds, and — critically — omitted signal–continuum interference, plus a single-mode “bracket” nuisance rather than a proper mass-dependent sideband fit.

Citation radar

This week’s radar is a single-topic feeding frenzy. Every paper below was posted within days of the LUX-ZEPLIN Collaboration’s late-August preprint reporting one nuclear-recoil-like event at \(E_R = 248\pm23\pm23\) keV in a 2.84 tonne-year exposure (3.4σ local, 2.6σ global). The community has converged with remarkable speed on a single reading — endothermic/inelastic dark matter, whose threshold kinematics naturally selects a high, localized recoil energy that conventional elastic WIMP spectra cannot reproduce — and is now racing to claim the most predictive particle realization. The result is a tight cluster of cross-citing papers (they reference each other heavily), which explains the near-identical citation counts. This is what a fresh anomaly looks like when it hits a theory community primed with a ready-made mechanism.

arXiv:2609.02608 — Dark Matter at the Kinematic Edge: Interpreting the 248 keV LZ Nuclear-Recoil Candidate (Mattia Di Mauro)

Di Mauro provides the most thorough single-author phenomenological recast of the LZ event. Using the published efficiency curves and operator significances, the paper argues that elastic spin-independent scattering fails quantitatively — normalizing to one event near 248 keV would demand ~\(1.4\times10^3\) accompanying low-energy recoils — while endothermic inelastic scattering shifts the spectrum into the observed window. Two concrete realizations are worked out: a thermal pseudo-Dirac fermion with off-diagonal vector coupling (\(m_\chi\simeq1\) TeV, \(\delta\simeq297\) keV, \(\sigma_N\simeq6.5\times10^{-43}\) cm²) that ties the direct-detection rate to relic-abundance coannihilation, and a more predictive thermal Higgsino (\(1.1\) TeV, \(\delta\simeq377\) keV) testable via a gamma-ray line.

At ~41 citations in roughly a month, this reads as the community’s reference recast: it carefully catalogs which NREFT operators LZ actually tested, stresses that the event selects “spectral hardness” rather than a unique Lorentz structure, and quantifies halo-tail sensitivity. Its detailed efficiency digitization and explicit relic–direct-detection relation make it a natural citation anchor for the follow-up papers below.

arXiv:2609.01475 — Inelastic Dark Matter Signature at High Recoil Energy in LUX-ZEPLIN and CRESST (Liangliang Su et al.)

Su, Yang, and Yang deliver a compact Letter interpreting the event as endothermic inelastic DM, adding a distinctive ingredient: terrestrial propagation. They compute both direct scattering of surviving ground-state halo DM and the excited-state component produced by upscattering inside the Earth, concluding that for \(m_\chi\gtrsim500\) GeV and \(\delta=\mathcal{O}(300)\) keV the Earth-produced component is kinematically forbidden — leaving direct endothermic scattering on xenon as the signal and eliminating daily modulation. An illustrative two-bin likelihood gives a best-fit \((m_\chi,\delta)\simeq(1.105\,\mathrm{TeV},350\,\mathrm{keV})\) for \(\bar\sigma_n=10^{-37}\) cm².

The headline forward-looking claim — testability via the planned CRESST upgrade, whose heavier tungsten target reaches the same transition at lower recoil energy with ~1.5 tonne·day exposure — gives experimentalists a concrete target and likely drives some of its ~41 citations. As one of the earliest posted (Sept 1) interpretations, it also functions as a template others cite when framing the inelastic reading and the CRESST complementarity.

arXiv:2609.02807 — A Peccei-Quinn Origin for Inelastic Electroweak Dark Matter after LUX-ZEPLIN (Luca Visinelli)

Visinelli embeds the inelastic electroweak-doublet interpretation in a Peccei–Quinn completion: spontaneous PQ breaking leaves a residual parity that stabilizes the lighter neutral state and generates the Majorana splitting via a heavy PQ-charged singlet, while a KSVZ colored pair supplies the QCD anomaly. Integrating out the singlet yields the dimension-five operator that splits a vectorlike doublet, giving a Higgsino-like off-diagonal \(Z\) coupling. The construction realizes a mixed axion–WIMP cosmology: for benchmark doublet masses 287–509 GeV the EW component supplies only 7–21% of the relic density, with the QCD axion providing the rest through misalignment (\(v_S\simeq4.4\times10^{11}\) GeV).

The paper is notable for its careful complementarity discussion — solar-capture/IceCube limits, excited-state radiative decay (~20 km decay length, leaving LZ a clean single recoil but enabling luminous scintillator signatures), and collider disappearing-track bounds. Its ~39 citations likely reflect both the appeal of unifying the two leading DM paradigms and its extensive, honest cross-referencing of the competing LZ interpretations, which positions it centrally in the emerging literature web.

arXiv:2609.02868 — Inelastic Dark Photon Dark Matter for the LUX-ZEPLIN High-Recoil Event and the Galactic Halo Gamma-Ray Excess (Kimiko Yamashita)

Yamashita attempts a two-anomaly fit: a \(U(1)_X\) dark-photon DM at \(m_X=420\) GeV that both explains Totani’s halo-like Fermi-LAT gamma-ray excess (via a \(p\)-wave-suppressed, Sommerfeld-enhanced \(W^+W^-\) channel) and the LZ event (via inelastic \(X+N\to V+N\) with \(\delta\simeq316\) keV, saturating the kinematic ceiling for xenon). A second light scalar \(\phi'\) mediates the inelastic transition while a dark parity forbids the elastic amplitude that would otherwise violate SI limits.

The ambition — one dark sector, two independent anomalies, same 420 GeV mass — is the clear draw, and at ~35 citations it is well-cited despite requiring a fairly elaborate scalar sector (the paper candidly defers “a full symmetry construction” and a profile-likelihood significance to future work). It leans on the same detector-resolution smearing (\(\sigma_E=32.5\) keV) argument as the others. The transparent AI-assistance disclosure is a sign of the times. Its citation count is somewhat inflated by membership in the tightly cross-citing LZ cluster.

arXiv:2609.01892 — A PQ-Symmetric High-Scale SUSY Interpretation of the LZ High-Energy Recoil (Wen Yin)

Yin reads the event as near-threshold upscattering of 1.08-TeV thermal Higgsino dark matter, with a neutral-Higgsino splitting of 0.28–0.35 MeV fixing electroweak gauginos at \(\mathcal{O}(10^7)\) GeV via the standard integrating-out relation \(\delta_0\simeq m_Z^2(s_W^2/M_1+c_W^2/M_2)\). The paper then builds out the full high-scale SUSY picture: gravity-mediated sfermions at \(\sim10^7\) GeV, a Kim–Nilles PQ mechanism setting \(\mu=1.08\) TeV plus a QCD axion, HSSUSY-checked Higgs mass at \(\tan\beta\simeq1.9\)–2.2, gauge unification with suppressed proton decay, and avoidance of the gravitino/moduli problems.

The distinctive prediction — a “super-invisible” axion with accidentally cancelled photon coupling (\(E/N=2\) from the Higgsino contribution) — plus potential proton-decay reach make this a smoking-gun-oriented scenario. At ~32 citations it is the most model-building-heavy entry, cited as the SUSY-embedded counterpart to the more agnostic Higgsino recasts. The framing throughout is appropriately cautious, treating the single non-blind event as a “working hypothesis.”
