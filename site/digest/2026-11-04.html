BaBar’s ππ, measured twice

BaBar arXiv:2611.04596 re-measures \(e^+e^-\to\pi^+\pi^-(\gamma)\) with the full 460 fb⁻¹ ISR sample. This time it separates pions from muons by their angular distributions rather than by particle ID. The new analysis alone gives \(a_\mu^{\pi\pi}=525.9\pm3.5\pm3.8\) (×10⁻¹⁰, 0.3–1.4 GeV). Combined with the 2009 measurement it gives 517.8 ± 2.4 ± 3.3, so the earlier BaBar result must sit well below the new one. How the paper quantifies that compatibility is the section to read. It lands the day after another slice of the τ-route isospin budget for HVP was tightened (11-03). Elsewhere, toponium gets its first NNLO cross section, and FASER closes part of the X17 parameter space.

Themes

NRQCD at higher order

Klein et al. arXiv:2611.03782 compute the NNLO short-distance coefficient for pseudoscalar toponium and get 4.87 pb at 13 TeV. Scale uncertainties shrink roughly fourfold relative to NLO, but the Coulomb-scale dependence of the matrix elements now dominates the error. That makes the remaining uncertainty a bound-state problem, the same treatment Buccioni’s genus-two threshold expansions (10-26) still lacked. It is also the piece the NRQCD-vs-CP-odd-Higgs reading of the CMS excess turns on. Smaller NRQCD entries: Li et al. arXiv:2611.04910 find NLO more than doubles the \(T_{4c}\) radiative widths, and Gong et al. arXiv:2611.04981 argue that slow convergence in \(J/\psi+\eta_c\) is intrinsic to the process.

Old signals, closed windows

  • X17. FASER arXiv:2611.03904 recasts its dark-photon and ALP nulls as a protophobic X17 and excludes \(6\times10^{-5}<\epsilon_e<1.2\times10^{-4}\). A high-coupling window remains, which matters given September’s finding that cosmic muons reproduce the Atomki opening-angle peaks.
  • DAMA. Heikinheimo et al. arXiv:2611.04890 add inelastic scattering, quenching variations and halo rescaling to the full NREFT. DAMA still cannot coexist with LZ, PICO-60 and CDMSlite.
  • Squarks. Agashe et al. arXiv:2611.03794 go the other way: the simplified model undercounts production, and the realistic LHC limit is 2.6–3.0 TeV.

Light ions, ordered by density

Neeraj et al. arXiv:2611.04865 show that the CMS O–O \(\Lambda/K^0_S\) shape is not set by multiplicity. Matching p–Pb at equal transverse density cuts the mismatch by ~60%, and the tension disappears only with sub-nucleonic structure. Ambrus et al. arXiv:2611.05239 find Ne/O \(v_2\) ratios are robust across hydro and kinetic theory, while \(v_3\) ratios are not. Both extend last week’s push past oxygen toward Ne–Ne.

Weak mixing at low \(Q\)

Huang et al. arXiv:2611.03751 propagate PREX-II/CREX to neutron radii below 1% uncertainty and extract \(\sin^2\theta_W=0.231^{+0.016}_{-0.015}\) from COHERENT. That sits between CONUS+’s 0.225 and the CEνNS+ν–e combination at 0.233. Serao et al. arXiv:2611.04600 warn that Ge and Xe targets leave nearly parallel blind directions for light mediators.

Worth a look

  • arXiv:2611.04596: A new pion/muon separation and an internal BaBar spread of ~8×10⁻¹⁰. (Also posted as arXiv:2611.04621.)
  • arXiv:2611.03782: The cross section any toponium-vs-scalar fit now needs, with the error budget located in the Coulomb scale.
  • arXiv:2611.03904: A collider null that bites into the X17 parameter space directly, rather than a projection.
  • arXiv:2611.04865: Uses only published data, and gives a falsifiable Ne–Ne prediction (within 3–5% of O–O).
  • arXiv:2611.03794: Moves quoted squark limits by ~1 TeV. HL-LHC and FCC-hh reach estimates inherit the correction.

Also noted

  • arXiv:2611.04531 A New Bound on Light-by-Light Scattering in the Low-Energy Regime — Aimé Matheron et al. (hep-ex). Colliding an XFEL with a high-intensity laser improves the low-energy bound by ten orders of magnitude.
  • arXiv:2611.03783 GUT Color-Triplet Around the Corner… — Gia Dvali et al. (hep-ph). Splits Yukawas instead of masses, giving a TeV-scale long-lived triplet. It is the opposite bet to the IR-fixed \(M_{H_c}\) behind Allwicher’s SU(5) flip (11-02).
  • arXiv:2611.04267 Semiparametric Estimation of Physics Parameters and Parton Distribution Functions — Huanbiao Zhu et al. (hep-ph). An influence-function debiasing removes the need for joint PDF+SMEFT refits, extending last week’s fit-machinery thread.
  • arXiv:2611.03813 Presupernovae Strongly Constrain Electrophilic MeV Bosons — Yu Cheng et al. (hep-ph). Progenitor-phase 511 keV injection beats supernova bounds by up to 100× at 1–3 MeV.
  • arXiv:2611.04014 Branching Fractions and CP-Violating Asymmetries in D => PP Decays — Franco Buccella et al. (hep-ph). Charged-mode tension persists. The paper gives testable \(K^0_SK^0_S\) and \(\pi^0\pi^0\) asymmetry predictions for the still-open charm-CPV question.

Deep dives

arXiv:2611.04596 — A New Precise Measurement of the \(e^+e^-\to\pi^+\pi^-(\gamma)\) Cross Section with the ISR Method at BaBar (BaBar Collaboration)

The new analysis drops particle ID and separates \(\pi\pi\gamma\) from \(\mu\mu\gamma\) with template fits to \(|\cos\theta^*|\) in bins of two-track mass. Pions follow \(\sin^2\theta^*\); muons go from flat to \(1+\cos^2\theta^*\). This allows a \(p_T>0.1\) GeV track cut instead of the 2009 analysis’s 1 GeV/\(c\), which retains the asymmetric topologies at large \(|\cos\theta^*|\) where the two shapes differ most. The fit uses 7.6M events from the full 460 fb⁻¹. As a validation, the muon spectrum is compared absolutely to QED and gives \(R_{\mu\mu}=0.9920\) with 0.69% total precision; the analysis was unblinded only after this check.

The result is \(a_\mu^{\pi\pi}(0.3\)–\(1.4\,\mathrm{GeV})=525.9(3.5)(3.8)\), against 513.3(2.2)(3.1) for 2009. The gap is 12.6×10⁻¹⁰, and the new cross section sits ~2% higher across the ρ peak. Compatibility is assessed in two ways: - Local scan: in 50 MeV intervals the differences are mostly <2σ in the central region, and conservative systematic-correlation scenarios move these by ≤0.2σ. - Integrated window: over 0.6–0.8832 GeV the 2009 result is −1.4σ from 2026.

The 517.8(2.4)(3.3) combination inflates uncertainties wherever local χ²>1. In the same window, 2026 is +0.9σ from CMD-3, −4.2σ from KLOE and −3.0σ from BESIII. BaBar calls it consistent with τ-based estimates and recent lattice results.

Caveats: - Below 0.5 GeV and above 1 GeV the \(\mu\mu\) background overwhelms the signal, and the new result is not competitive with 2009. The 0.3–0.5 GeV piece reads 60.7 vs 57.6. - The combination therefore mixes two methods whose relative weight depends on energy.

arXiv:2611.03782 — Toponium hadroproduction at next-to-next-to-leading order in NRQCD (Sven Yannick Klein et al.)

The new ingredient is the NNLO QCD short-distance coefficient for \(gg\to\eta_t\). It uses nested soft-collinear subtraction, two-loop amplitudes and a soft-improved matching scheme. The cross section also includes: - NLO EW corrections, - \(\Theta_t\) channels, - \(v^2\) and P-wave terms, - associated production, - NNLO+RG-improved perturbative LDMEs summed over radial states.

At 13 TeV the result is \(\sigma=4.87^{+0.63}_{-0.65}\) pb. NLO raises LO by 28% and NNLO lowers NLO by 5%. The NLO EW term is a flat −7%, driven by \(y_t^2\) Higgs exchange. All other pieces are each below about 1%; only \(q\bar q\to\gamma^*/Z^*\to\Theta_t\) reaches ~1% below 10 TeV. The seven-point scale band narrows about fourfold relative to NLO, to \(^{+0.5\%}_{-3.0\%}\). The paper warns that the small NNLO shift at \(\mu=m_t\) is unrepresentative, since the correction grows above \(2m_t\).

The error budget is now dominated by non-hard sources: - Coulomb scale of the LDMEs: ±12%, nearly independent of order and \(\sqrt{s}\). - \(\alpha_s(m_Z)\): ±5%, also mainly through the LDMEs. - \(m_t\): ±0.8%.

The authors point to N³LO S-wave LDMEs as the remedy; these already exist for the spin-triplet state.

Caveat: this is a zero-width result, counting only \(M_{t\bar t}<2m_t\). It is not yet the threshold spectrum ATLAS/CMS measure; that comparison awaits the promised finite-width Green’s-function extension.

arXiv:2611.03904 — Constraints on a Long-Lived X17 Vector Boson with FASER (FASER Collaboration et al.)

FASER statistically combines two existing searches: - Dark-photon search: 27 fb⁻¹, 0 events observed on a background of 2.3×10⁻³. - ALP search: 57.7 fb⁻¹, 1 event on 0.44±0.39. It uses the timing scintillator as a veto, so it covers decays after the decay volume and is orthogonal to the dark-photon search.

Together they use the full magnetized region as decay volume. The reinterpretation is for a strictly protophobic vector with \(\epsilon_n=10^{-2}\).

Production is dominated by proton bremsstrahlung, which is not suppressed despite \(\epsilon_p=0\). The form factors are evaluated at \(t=m_X^2\), where both \(F_1\) and \(F_2\) are nonzero even though \(F_1(0)=0\). \(\eta\to X\gamma\) and neutron bremsstrahlung at the TAN are subleading.

At 17 MeV the 90% CL CLs exclusion is \(2\times10^{-8}<\epsilon_e<3\times10^{-4}\). That fully covers the low-coupling window \(6\times10^{-5}\)–\(1.2\times10^{-4}\), which had only recently reopened after an E141 re-analysis. The high-coupling window (\(6\times10^{-4}\)–\(1.5\times10^{-3}\)) survives because the X decays before reaching FASER. Under an alternative model with \(\epsilon_n=\epsilon_e\), the excluded range is \(2\times10^{-5}<\epsilon<1.5\times10^{-4}\).

Caveats: - The bremsstrahlung off-shell damping factor alone carries ~40% signal uncertainty. - The model is simplified: the small proton and axial couplings required by pion and kaon decay bounds are neglected. - Results are stated to be insensitive to \(\epsilon_n\) within the Be-favoured range.
