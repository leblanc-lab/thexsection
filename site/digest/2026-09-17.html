The 17 MeV boson meets cosmic muons

The day’s sharpest single paper is a debunking: Benmansour et al. arXiv:2609.18383 show that Geant4 simulations of cosmic-ray muon coincidences in the Atomki five- and six-arm spectrometers reproduce the very opening-angle peaks — near 140° in the ⁸Be window, 120° in the ⁴He window — long read as evidence for a 17 MeV boson, with a cosmic-to-signal ratio above unity. It doesn’t close the case, but it puts the burden squarely back on the beam-off studies. Around it the weight sits where it did yesterday, in precision QCD and nucleon structure, plus a genuine f0(980) convergence and a quiet continuation of the LZ story — now down to three papers, all cosmological escape routes rather than fits.

Themes

Precision QCD and structure

A dense fixed-order/structure day. Gauld et al. arXiv:2609.17812 deliver bottom-flavoured jets through \(b\bar b\) production at NNLO+PS with full mass effects, confronting ATLAS/CMS/LHCb and pinning the jet-algorithm differences to \(g\to b\bar b\) splittings. Chen et al. arXiv:2609.18828 complete the NLO electroweak corrections to \(gg\to\gamma\gamma\) — sub-percent inclusive but −10% in the tails, with a dip-bump at the Higgs threshold. On the formal side, Skands arXiv:2609.18102 proposes “Cat’s Cradle” diagrams as an intermediate power counting for resolved jet substructure, Rainaldi arXiv:2609.18627 recasts DGLAP scheme transformations as a gauge group, and Prygarin et al. arXiv:2609.18269 give a Lévy-process reading of the forward BFKL kernel. This extends yesterday’s four-loop/splitting-function center of gravity.

The f0(980) under a microscope

Three independent angles on the same scalar, a day after BESIII’s \(D_s\to f_0(980)\) favored an \(s\bar s\)-dominant picture. Kumano arXiv:2609.17974 argues from Belle fragmentation functions that f0(980) looks like \(q\bar q\) (\(s\bar s\)) at high energy even if it is \(K\bar K\)-molecular at low energy — structure that depends on the probing scale. Lacey arXiv:2609.17765 reaches a compatible conclusion via anisotropy-response scaling in p+Pb, finding a single-meson-like response distinct from the molecular benchmark. Zheng et al. arXiv:2609.18890 work the heavier f0(1500) in rare \(B_s\) decays. A rare case of low- and high-energy methods triangulating one state.

LZ evasion goes cosmological

The recoil traffic is a trickle, and the surviving papers all target the IceCube/solar-capture bound. Nagata et al. arXiv:2609.18564 set the relic abundance by a primordial asymmetry rather than freeze-out, so there is no antiparticle population to annihilate in the Sun — the cleanest structural escape yet. Xing arXiv:2609.17935 produces the excited state by galactic self-scattering and detects it exothermically; Ge et al. arXiv:2609.16529 supply relativistic inelastic Xe response functions that could shift any of these fits. The falsifiability pivot the memory tracked has fully given way to model cosmology.

ML in event reconstruction

The tooling wave keeps colonizing reconstruction. Mao et al. arXiv:2609.18928 introduce VyPER, a hypergraph-plus-diffusion framework doing jet assignment and neutrino kinematics jointly; Lazarovits et al. arXiv:2609.16276 offer PSICHE, probabilistic clustering with learned variable jet sizes in space and time. Two Tsoi et al. papers push self-supervised pre-training — VICReg-based mass regression arXiv:2609.17726 and augmentation-free EMD pairing arXiv:2609.17738 — and Benne et al. arXiv:2609.17659 use domain adaptation to kill background sculpting in weakly-supervised anomaly searches.

Title: Structure of \(\Lambda(1405)\) and \(f_0(980)\) shown in fragmentation functions

Abstract

The internal quark-gluon configuration of the exotic hadron candidates Λ ​ ( 1405 ) \Lambda(1405) and f 0 ​ ( 980 ) f_{0}(980) is investigated by using their fragmentation functions. Λ ​ ( 1405 ) \Lambda(1405) is one of the most probable candidates for an exotic hadron with an internal structure of a meson-baryon molecule or a five-quark state, and f 0 ​ ( 980 ) f_{0}(980) could be a tetraquark, a K ​ K ¯ K\bar{K} molecule, or an ordinary quark-antiquark meson. There were experimental indications that the second moment of the Λ ​ ( 1405 ) \Lambda(1405) fragmentation function has an anomalously small value in comparison with the ordinary hadrons from the electron-positron annihilation measurements. We explain this fact by the meson-baryon molecular configuration or the compact five-quark state, and this proposal opened a field of exotic-hadron studies by the fragmentation functions. The details of these fragmentation-function studies are explained in this proceedings for Λ ​ ( 1405 ) \Lambda(1405) , together with the studies of f 0 ​ ( 980 ) f_{0}(980) . These fragmentation functions can be measured at various facilities in the near future including the LHC, Belle II, EIC, and EicC, so that the internal structure of these particles will be clarified.

1 Introduction

Hadron mass is one of the fundamental quantities in physics; however, it has not been understood yet at the level of elementary particle physics [ 1 , 2 ] . There are two mass systems in our universe. One is the Higgs mass system for quarks and leptons, and the other is the hadron mass system for protons, neutrons, and other hadrons. The Higgs mechanism explains only a few percent of the nucleon mass, and the remaining part comes from the strong interaction. Currently, the origin of hadron masses is understood by the trace anomaly of the energy-momentum tensor and the quark condensate [ 1 , 2 ] . However, this explanation is not complete, so that the origins of hadron masses and hadron spins are the topics of current investigations both theoretically and experimentally [ 3 ] . In particular, the internal structure of exotic hadron candidates, such as Λ ​ ( 1405 ) \Lambda(1405) and f 0 ​ ( 980 ) f_{0}(980) , is one of the most interesting topics because their structure cannot be explained by the ordinary quark model.

The fragmentation function indicates a probability of finding a hadron from a parton (quark or gluon), and it is expressed by the variable z z , which is the energy fraction of the hadron from the parton. The fragmentation functions are used generally for explaining hadron-production cross sections in high-energy reactions, and they are also valuable for finding the internal structure of exotic hadron candidates. In this proceedings, the internal structure of Λ ​ ( 1405 ) \Lambda(1405) and f 0 ​ ( 980 ) f_{0}(980) is investigated by using their fragmentation functions.

2 Determination of fragmentation functions

The fragmentation functions are determined from the experimental data of hadron-production cross sections, mainly by the electron-positron annihilation processes e + ​ e − → h ​ X e^{+}e^{-}\to hX . The fragmentation functions are then obtained by the global analysis of these experimental data, in the same way used for determining the parton distribution functions.

The fragmentation function is expressed by two terms, one is the singlet fragmentation function D S D_{S} and the other is the non-singlet fragmentation function D N ​ S D_{NS} , in terms of the flavor-singlet and non-singlet distributions. Here, the singlet and non-singlet fragmentation functions are given by the quark and antiquark fragmentation functions.

The z z dependence of the fragmentation function is generally parametrized by the following form at the initial scale Q 0 2 Q_{0}^{2} :

(1)

where N i N_{i} is the normalization constant, and α i \alpha_{i} and β i \beta_{i} are the parameters to be determined by the experimental data. The subscript i i indicates the quark flavor or gluon. The fragmentation functions are evolved from the initial scale Q 0 2 Q_{0}^{2} to the experimental scale Q 2 Q^{2} by the DGLAP evolution equations [ 4 ] .

This quantity indicates the average energy fraction of the hadron from the parton. The second moment is a useful quantity for investigating the internal structure of hadrons.

3 Λ ​ ( 1405 ) \Lambda(1405) and f 0 ​ ( 980 ) f_{0}(980)

The Λ ​ ( 1405 ) \Lambda(1405) is a hyperon with spin-parity J P = 1 / 2 − J^{P}=1/2^{-} , and it is one of the most probable candidates for an exotic hadron. The Λ ​ ( 1405 ) \Lambda(1405) is considered to be a meson-baryon molecular state of N ​ K ¯ N\bar{K} or a compact five-quark state. The internal structure of Λ ​ ( 1405 ) \Lambda(1405) is investigated by using the fragmentation functions.

The fragmentation function of Λ ​ ( 1405 ) \Lambda(1405) was determined from the electron-positron annihilation data. There were experimental indications that the second moment of the Λ ​ ( 1405 ) \Lambda(1405) fragmentation function has an anomalously small value in comparison with the ordinary hadrons [ 5 ] . This fact is explained by the meson-baryon molecular configuration or the compact five-quark state.

The f 0 ​ ( 980 ) f_{0}(980) is a scalar meson with spin-parity J P = 0 + J^{P}=0^{+} . The internal structure of f 0 ​ ( 980 ) f_{0}(980) is not well understood, and it is considered to be a tetraquark, a K ​ K ¯ K\bar{K} molecule, or an ordinary quark-antiquark meson. The internal structure of f 0 ​ ( 980 ) f_{0}(980) is investigated by using the fragmentation functions.

The fragmentation functions of f 0 ​ ( 980 ) f_{0}(980) were determined from the Belle experimental data [ 6 ] . The second moment of the f 0 ​ ( 980 ) f_{0}(980) fragmentation function was obtained, and the internal structure of f 0 ​ ( 980 ) f_{0}(980) is discussed.

4 Summary

The internal structure of exotic hadron candidates Λ ​ ( 1405 ) \Lambda(1405) and f 0 ​ ( 980 ) f_{0}(980) was investigated by using their fragmentation functions. The second moment of the Λ ​ ( 1405 ) \Lambda(1405) fragmentation function has an anomalously small value in comparison with the ordinary hadrons, and this fact is explained by the meson-baryon molecular configuration or the compact five-quark state. The internal structure of f 0 ​ ( 980 ) f_{0}(980) is also investigated by using the fragmentation functions.

These fragmentation functions can be measured at various facilities in the near future including the LHC, Belle II, EIC, and EicC, so that the internal structure of these particles will be clarified.
