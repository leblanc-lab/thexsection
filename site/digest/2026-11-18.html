ATLAS sees instanton-like isotropy

ATLAS Collaboration arXiv:2611.22092 reports the first LHC search for QCD instanton-induced events, and it finds an excess of low-mass, isotropic events at 3.5σ. The data are 1.6 nb⁻¹ of very-low-luminosity 13 TeV collisions. The background is minimum-bias, predicted from data plus reweighted Monte Carlo, so soft-QCD modeling is where the scrutiny starts. The abstract doesn’t say whether the 3.5σ is local. The excess joins the 13.6 GeV diphoton and 146 GeV \(e\mu\) on the list of unclosed LHC excesses, a list that has already prompted a look-elsewhere reckoning. The rest of the day: two more thermal LZ models, an ALP window closed with old LHC data, and several papers on what quoted errors mean.

Themes

LZ, thermal and composite

Buttazzo et al. arXiv:2611.21737 build inelastic dark matter from stable composite-Higgs resonances. Their mixed regime gives a few-TeV mass, ~300 keV splittings and ~10° mixing. The abstract claims consistency with solar capture and with the LZ high-energy sideband, the sideband search being one of the two decisive tests flagged in September.

Leng et al. arXiv:2611.22890 use a radion portal to land at 1 TeV with δ=340 keV. Their solar-neutrino check is only a proxy, with rates “comparable in magnitude” to IceCube’s reconstructed scales. That leaves the constraint every LZ model has designed around since September untested here, and Lin et al.’s 11-13 recomputation had just tightened it.

Windows closed, not loosened

  • The pion chimney. Blinov et al. arXiv:2611.21728 close the ALP mass window around the \(\pi^0\), which no search had covered, using the ATLAS monojet and inclusive-photon results. The key point is that mixing changes the two eigenstates’ lifetimes without equalizing them.
  • \(B\to Ka\). Banerjee et al. arXiv:2611.21764 add flavor-conserving ALP couplings, with the flavor change supplied by SM weak loops.
  • Neutrino dipoles. Fiorillo et al. arXiv:2611.21729 set \(\mu_{\nu_e}\lesssim4.8\times10^{-13}\mu_B\) from SN 1987A, superseding all existing bounds.

Last week’s audits mostly loosened limits; today’s go the other way.

What the error bars mean

  • HERA PDFs. Canonero et al. arXiv:2611.22891 treat quoted HERA systematics as themselves uncertain. That cuts \(\chi^2\) from 1363 to 1306 for 1131 dof, mostly at low \(x\) and \(Q^2\), and widens the PDF uncertainties without shifting the central PDFs.
  • \(gg\to ZH\). Zhang et al. arXiv:2611.23020 find top-mass-scheme variations of 32–50% in boosted STXS bins, and show an inclusive K-factor cannot fix LO+PS samples.
  • Lensing \(H_0\). Brady et al. arXiv:2611.21821 find the decreasing-\(H_0\) trend with lens redshift drops to ~1σ once mass-sheet uncertainties are propagated.

This continues the week’s self-audit habit, now applied to inputs rather than to limits.

Worth a look

  • arXiv:2611.22092 (ATLAS): A first search that comes back with 3.5σ. Read the minimum-bias background construction before reading the significance.
  • arXiv:2611.21728 (Blinov et al.): A clean kinematic argument that closes a window everyone had stepped around. It adds a separate bound from the \(\pi^\pm\)–\(\pi^0\) mass splitting.
  • arXiv:2611.21737 (Buttazzo et al.): The first LZ model in this run to check off solar capture, the sideband and gamma rays in a single abstract.
  • arXiv:2611.22891 (Canonero et al.): A substantial part of HERA’s notorious data–theory tension turns out to be error modeling, not physics.

Deep dives

arXiv:2611.22092 — Search for the low-mass isotropic signature of QCD instantons in \(pp\) collisions at \(\sqrt{s}=13\) TeV using the ATLAS detector (ATLAS Collaboration)

The result. ATLAS fits an excess of soft, isotropic events in 1.6 nb⁻¹ of 2015 data at ⟨μ⟩=0.028. The observed significance is 3.5σ against an expected 1.5σ. The fitted signal strength is μ=2.3±0.6, corresponding to σ_vis=2.4±0.7 μb. The post-fit signal-region yields are ~3.9k signal on ~28.8k background, against 32,789 events observed.

The method. - Selection. Events are restricted to 10<S_T<20 GeV. Acceptance × efficiency is 7.4% for signal and 27% for background. - Classifier. An ABCDisCo DNN is trained on five track variables (AUCs 0.82 and 0.85). It defines a data-driven ABCD plane. - Non-closure correction. Residual correlations are corrected bin by bin using the mean of EPOS, Pythia 8 and Sherpa 3.

Caveats. As the digest warned, the background is the crux. - EPOS non-closure reaches 20% in the signal region before the fit. - The fit pulls the Sherpa 3 and EPOS-ABCD non-closure nuisance parameters by ~0.8σ. - The physics validation region sits systematically above the model. - Using an EPOS-only closure target gives μ=1.9±0.8 at 3.0σ. - The authors state plainly that “no background-only model can fully describe the data.” - The paper does not discuss look-elsewhere effects. - The theory cross-section varies by factors of ~20–24 under scale choices, so μ≠1 says little either way. - Control fits at 20–40 GeV in S_T return μ compatible with zero.

Why it matters. The text names what would distinguish an instanton from mismodeled soft QCD: the charm content predicted by the ‘t Hooft vertex, and the mass spectrum. Both remain unchecked.

arXiv:2611.21728 — Plugging the Pion Chimney (Nikita Blinov et al.)

The result. The authors diagonalize the a–π⁰ system exactly. At the chimney floor (c_GG/f=3×10⁻³ GeV⁻¹) the eigenstates are split by ~12.6 keV, far above the 8 eV π⁰ width. The two states share production but have different lifetimes, which leads to two complementary LHC constraints: - ATLAS monojet. The 10⁻³ GeV⁻¹ bound is rescaled by the survival probability to an effective radius of 12.5 m. This excludes everything except a window around m_π⁰, a few MeV wide at 3×10⁻³ GeV⁻¹ and 30 MeV wide at 3×10⁻². - ATLAS inclusive photon (139 fb⁻¹). In that window the boosted state decays early, and its two photons merge into a single isolated photon with a harder spectrum than the background. This closes the gap.

Together the two cover the chimney up to the EFT cap, c_GG/f≈0.15 GeV⁻¹ for Λ=1 TeV.

Above the cap. In KSVZ completions, colored states above ~1 TeV imply c_GG/f<1.4×10⁻³ (N·y_Q/2) GeV⁻¹, or at most ~10⁻² for the most generous allowed choices.

Other findings. - The K⁺-width bound disappears for m_a≈120–135 MeV, because an ALP passes KLOE’s π⁰ selection. - A purely kinematic bound from the π±–π⁰ splitting excludes 134.1–135.4 MeV. It lies entirely inside the photon exclusion.

Caveats. - The large-coupling region rests on a model-dependent UV argument; a DFSZ-type completion evades it. - The simulation is leading order with a single effective detector radius. - The authors could not reproduce stronger claims in arXiv:2609.21991.

Why it matters. The authors expect analogous chimneys near the η and η′.

arXiv:2611.21737 — Thermal Composite Dark Matter at LZ (Dario Buttazzo et al.)

The result. Stable fermionic resonances of the minimal composite Higgs model give three thermal inelastic fits to LZ230616 (E_R=248 keV in 2.84 t·yr): - (2,2)₀, composite Higgsino. It needs δ≳370 keV and M≳20 TeV (g_ψ≳3). The IceCube solar recast reaches ~20 TeV but misses the best-fit region. - Pure Y=0 triplet of (3,2)₁/₂. Strong dynamics alone supply a cross section suppressed by ξ²/16, which allows δ~150 keV at a few TeV. However, its recoil spectrum peaks low and disfavors a 248 keV event. H.E.S.S. line searches exclude M≲4 TeV and a narrow interval near 9 TeV. - Mixed triplets. A top-induced benchmark gives M~6 TeV, δ~300 keV and |θ|~10°. IceCube excludes masses below ~5 TeV.

The sideband argument. The sideband constraint is quantitative. At 5 TeV, |θ|=10° predicts N_HSB≈0.2, while 40° predicts N_HSB≈3.5. Larger mixing is therefore penalized by the empty sideband.

Caveats. - Sideband acceptance is taken as unity with no smearing, because the real acceptance isn’t public. - Results assume the SHM and negligible backgrounds. - Swapping the Vietze response for a Helm form factor shifts the required δ by 18–24 keV. - The relic calculations are deferred to an “in preparation” paper. - The 10° preference comes partly from a prior-dependent Bayesian posterior.
