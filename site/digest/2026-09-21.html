R(D*) gets more precise, and a new baryon

The day’s weight sits with the flavor experiments. Belle II delivers its most precise hadronic-tag \(R(D^{(*)})\) arXiv:2609.21947 — \(R(D^*)=0.242\pm0.019\pm0.016\), \(R(D)=0.439\pm0.055\pm0.046\) — landing consistent with both the SM and prior measurements, which neither sharpens nor kills the LFU tension the memory has flagged all year as the flavor follow-up to watch. Alongside it, LHCb reports first observation of the doubly charmed \(\Omega_{cc}^+\) at \(8.7\sigma\) arXiv:2609.21921, a genuine discovery. The LZ recoil, meanwhile, is still generating theory but has shifted almost entirely into constraint-and-complementarity mode.

Deep dives

arXiv:2609.21947 — Test of lepton flavor universality with \(\bar B\to D^{(*)}\tau\bar\nu_\tau\) and \(\bar B\to D^{(*)}\ell\bar\nu_\ell\) at Belle II (Belle II Collaboration et al.)

Using 387×10⁶ Υ(4S) decays with hadronic tagging, Belle II performs a simultaneous 2D fit in missing-mass-squared and residual calorimeter energy across five exclusive \(D^{(*)}\) samples, extracting \(R(D^*)=0.242\pm0.019\text{(stat)}\pm0.016\text{(syst)}\) and \(R(D)=0.439\pm0.055\pm0.046\) (correlations −0.40 stat, −0.20 syst). This supersedes the earlier hadronic-tag \(R(D^*)\) with twice the precision and delivers the first hadronic-tag \(R(D)\) at Belle II — the most precise hadronic-tagged determination to date, matching the best simultaneous measurements. Systematics are dominated by simulated-sample size (4.8%/8.4%) and, for \(R(D)\), \(B\)-tagging and low-momentum \(\pi^0/\gamma\) efficiencies.

\(R(D^*)\) sits within \(0.5\sigma\) of the SM; \(R(D)\) is \(2.0\sigma\) high, but the combination agrees with the SM at \(1.5\sigma\) and with the world average at \(1.3\sigma\). The \(R(D^*)\) central value landing below SM is the notable point for the LFU-anomaly narrative: this measurement neither sharpens nor kills the tension, easing rather than reinforcing it. Honest caveats are visible: the poorly-known gap-mode background is floated and found 42–82% smaller in data than simulation (validated against a \(\bar B\to D^*\pi^0\ell\nu\) control sample), and the hadronic-\(B\) normalization in the \(D^{*0}_{[D^0\pi^0]}\) sample sits \(3.1\sigma\) below prediction in the signal region — both absorbed into systematics rather than driving new uncertainty. The combined signal-plus-\(q^2\)-sideband fit shifts the central values by only ~2%.

arXiv:2609.21921 — Observation of the doubly charmed baryon \(\Omega_{cc}^+\) (LHCb collaboration et al.)

LHCb reports first observation of \(\Omega_{cc}^+\) (scc) in \(\Omega_c^0\pi^+\), with \(\Omega_c^0\to pK^-K^-\pi^+\), using 6.3 fb⁻¹ of 13.6 TeV data from the upgraded detector. A BDT-driven selection (32% signal efficiency, background retention \(2.4\times10^{-4}\)) yields a peak at \(8.7\sigma\) global significance (9.0σ local; 184±24 signal events), validated against the \(\Xi_{cc}^{++}\to\Xi_c^+\pi^+\) control channel (2770±110 events). The mass is \(3725.9\pm1.0\text{(stat)}\pm0.2\text{(syst)}\pm0.4\text{(lifetime)}\pm0.6\text{(ext)}\) MeV/\(c^2\), extracted via the mass difference \(M(\Omega_{cc}^+)-M(\Xi_{cc}^{++})=104.3\) MeV/\(c^2\) to cancel momentum-scale and companion-pion biases. The structure persists above 9σ with a >170 fs decay-time cut, consistent with a weakly decaying state and disfavoring a strong resonance.

This completes the SU(4)\(_f\) spin-½ 20-plet of ground-state baryons — every predicted member now established — and lands squarely in the 3660–3780 MeV/\(c^2\) theory window, adding another rung to the doubly-charmed spectrum after this year’s \(\Xi_{cc}^+\) observation. The main caveat is intrinsic: the \(\Omega_{cc}^+\) lifetime is unmeasured, so the selection-induced mass bias (parameterized linearly over 90–190 fs, correction 0.30 MeV/\(c^2\) at the 140 fs midpoint) carries a dedicated 0.4 MeV/\(c^2\) uncertainty. Lifetime and additional decay channels await the larger Run 3 dataset already in hand.

arXiv:2609.21823 — LZ’s Stairway to Heaνen: limits on elastic DM scatters from solar capture (Debajit Bose et al.)

Bose et al. take the elastic NREFT operators that LZ reported as ≥3σ fits to the 248 keV LZ230616 recoil and reverse-engineer their best-fit Wilson coefficients. Since LZ published favored regions only for \(\mathcal{L}_{10}^s\) (magnetic-dipole spin-spin) and the inelastic \(\mathcal{O}_1^s\), they build an approximate energy-dependent likelihood calibrated to reproduce LZ’s published local significances, validating against LZ’s own \(\mathcal{L}_{10}^s\)/\(\mathcal{O}_1^s\) curves (agreement to a factor of a few). They then compute solar capture rates (WimPyC) for each operator, assume equilibrium annihilation to \(\{W^+W^-, b\bar b, \tau^+\tau^-, \nu\bar\nu\}\), and confront the resulting neutrino fluxes with IceCube (2016/2025 solar-direction \(\nu_\mu\), 2014 atmospheric \(\nu_e\)) and Super-K atmospheric data.

Large weak-scale mass ranges fitting LZ230616 are excluded, with the direct \(\nu\bar\nu\) channel giving the strongest and \(b\bar b\) the weakest limits; for \(\mathcal{L}_6^v\), \(\mathcal{L}_8^s\), \(\mathcal{L}_8^v\) the LZ-favored regions are almost entirely ruled out. This extends the “constraint-and-complementarity” mode the digest flags, mirroring the inelastic-operator treatment of Nguyen et al. Two honest caveats: the \(\mathcal{L}_4\) operators survive even the \(\nu\bar\nu\) channel, and for several operators the excluded couplings lie where the EFT cutoff falls below the ~GeV solar-core momentum transfer — i.e., the NREFT description itself is invalid. Standard-halo velocities are assumed throughout, and LMC boosts or substructure could shift both the fits and the capture bounds.
