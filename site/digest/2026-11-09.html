A perturbative answer at the \(t\bar t\) threshold

Re arXiv:2611.09299 argues that threshold-sensitive top observables, meaning spin correlations and \(m_{t\bar t}\) near \(2m_t\), can be predicted reliably by keeping the first few powers of \(\alpha_s/v\). Reweighting nominal Monte Carlo this way agrees with data. That is the QCD side of the toponium-vs-CP-odd-Higgs question behind CMS’s threshold excess. Last week’s NNLO toponium cross section and the 2HDM interference tool were built to settle the same question. The other clean result is a negative: realistic nuclear structure does not rescue a proposed fix for the gallium anomaly. The rest of the 43 papers are mostly theory and detector design.

Themes

Top threshold, three angles

Alongside Re, Aushev et al. arXiv:2611.09797 use CMS Open Data to reach 30 GeV \(m_{t\bar t}\) resolution near threshold in dileptons. ML beats analytic reconstruction by ~30%, and the authors flag the \(\nu\bar\nu\) approximation as the limiting factor. Djouadi et al. arXiv:2611.09666 show that signal–background interference raises low-\(\tan\beta\) MSSM \(H/A\) rates by up to tens of percent. Current limits barely move, but HL-LHC mass ranges will. Taken together: the scalar reading needs interference handled properly, and the bound-state reading may need less new machinery than assumed.

Neutrino anomalies and relations

Castillo et al. arXiv:2611.09788 test a recent non-factorization proposal for the gallium deficit with shell-model transition densities. The cross section still comes out 20% above measurement. Xing arXiv:2611.09478 conjectures \(\theta_{23}=(\theta_{12}+\theta_{13})/2+\pi/8\), which predicts \(43.46^{\circ}\) from JUNO and Daya Bay inputs. It is numerology, but cheap to falsify. Choi et al. arXiv:2611.09037 find that strong flavor-dependent ν–DM potentials in the TXS 0506+056 spike relax IceCube flavor limits by up to four orders of magnitude.

Quantum resources, audited

Shen et al. arXiv:2611.08586 show that “minimal magic” predictions depend on which magic measure is used. That is the quantity behind the year’s “quantum magic in EW scattering” thread. Bleau et al. arXiv:2611.08595 confirm that mean-field neutrino oscillations match full multiparticle simulation at large \(N\). That undercuts the case for quantum advantage except in the special configurations they identify. Both fit last month’s warning that standard tomography may fail at NLO: quantum-information claims in HEP are now being checked by their own community.

Nuclear structure in the initial state

Zakharov arXiv:2611.09404 adds collective quadrupole and octupole vibrations to \(^{208}\)Pb and claims this resolves the ultracentral \(v_2\)-to-\(v_3\) puzzle. This extends last week’s light-ion finding that flow eccentricities respond to nuclear forces, now applied to the heaviest system. Barata et al. arXiv:2611.08579 separate broadening, radiation and medium response in the back-to-back dijet TEEC.

Worth a look

  • arXiv:2611.09299 (Re): Perturbative threshold predictions that can be applied event by event. Read before attributing the excess to anything elementary.
  • arXiv:2611.09788 (Castillo et al.): Fixing gallium would need fine-tuned densities with percent-level cancellations, orders of magnitude larger than the shell model gives.
  • arXiv:2611.08586 (Shen et al.): Two measures give \(\sin^2\theta_W=1/4\) at all energies, while the min-relative entropy runs with energy. The physics conclusion depends on the measure.
  • arXiv:2611.09404 (Zakharov): \(\epsilon_2\{2\}/\epsilon_3\{2\}\approx0.68\)–0.79 once Pb vibrations are included. A simple test of a long-standing puzzle.
  • arXiv:2611.09150 (Ding et al.): The lattice topological susceptibility goes from linear to quadratic in \(m_l\) through the crossover, extracted from single ensembles. The axion-mass relevance is my inference, not the authors’ claim.

Deep dives

arXiv:2611.09299 — Spin Correlations in \(t\bar t\) Production and Decay at the LHC in Perturbative QCD (Emanuele Re)

Bound-state effects near \(2m_t\) can be computed in ordinary perturbative QCD, provided the observable integrates over \(M_{t\bar t}\) up to a cut at least ~5 GeV above threshold. The argument rests on the integrated spectral density. Bound-state poles enter at relative \(\mathcal{O}(a_1^3)\), i.e. N³LO, and combine with a continuum term into an analytic result. The consequence is concrete: once the full expansion is kept, the naive toponium contribution is cut by a factor of two. Adding an \(\eta_t\) term on top of a perturbative continuum therefore double-counts. Re keeps the first few \(\alpha_s/v\) terms of the Sommerfeld expansion, with a Coulomb scale of several tens of GeV set by the cut rather than by \(v\to0\), and matches them to NLO+PS and NNLO+PS. This pushes NLO+PS predictions for the spin observable \(D\) toward the ATLAS/CMS data. The shift is smaller on top of NNLO, which already contains the leading enhanced terms. For CMS’s 2018 \(d\sigma/dM_{t\bar t}\), the \(\mathcal{O}(\alpha_s^3)\) terms are “useful, but not crucial” once NNLO is included.

These are proceedings. The companion paper builds three POWHEG generators: hvq‑thr1 (narrow width), hvq‑thr2 (off-shell), and an upgraded bb4l. Their spread gives an estimate of the narrow-width-approximation uncertainty. In the thr2 comparison, the threshold increment is 1.62 pb against 2.73±0.61 pb from Garzelli et al. at \(M_{\rm cut}=345\) GeV. The two converge by about 350 GeV. Caveats: the claim fails for measurements fine enough to resolve the peak, and the author flags the newer, more exclusive CMS/ATLAS \(M_{t\bar t}\) analyses as possibly changing the conclusion.

arXiv:2611.09788 — Possible Solution to the Gallium Anomaly? Nuclear Transition Densities (D. Castillo et al.)

A recent PRL claimed that dropping lepton–nuclear factorization resolves the gallium anomaly. This paper finds that the claim does not survive realistic nuclear structure. The authors run nine shell-model calculations of the Gamow–Teller radial transition density for \(^{71}\)Ga↔\(^{71}\)Ge. These span four \(pf g_{9/2}\) Hamiltonians, bare and effective GT operators, and an LNPS space extended to \(0f_{7/2}\). All are normalized to the \(^{71}\)Ge EC half-life, \(11.465\pm0.003\) d, and convolved with Dirac–Hartree–Fock–Slater electron wavefunctions. The densities form a narrow band. The unfactorized cross sections are \(5.26\pm0.01\) (\(^{51}\)Cr) and \(6.30\pm0.01\) (\(^{37}\)Ar) ×10⁻⁴⁵ cm², against measured values of \(4.44\pm0.33\) and \(5.21\pm0.72\). That gives experiment/theory ≈ 0.83, and the factorized and unfactorized results agree to ~1%. This is expected, since the Fermi function varies by only ~3% across the nucleus.

The PRL’s fitted densities (mDG, mTG) have overall scales 100× and 1000× the shell-model ones. Their nodes cancel so strongly that the net integral is only 2.5% and 0.25% of the integrated absolute value. Refitting with a shell-model radial ansatz requires coefficients ~3000× the calculated ones, again with percent-level cancellation. Caveats: a harmonic-oscillator radial basis is used, and \(q_{\rm EC}\) absorbs two-body currents, weak magnetism and radiative corrections. The authors argue these are subleading but call for ab initio (VS-IMSRG) cross-checks. The result is consistent with the solar-source analysis, which shows no deficit.

arXiv:2611.08586 — Magic Is in the Eye of the Measure (Zhi-Heng Shen et al.)

For two qubits, “maximal magic” depends on the measure, and so does the minimal-magic determination of \(\sin^2\theta_W\). The paper proves global maxima exactly for four measures: - \(M_2\): \(\log_2(16/7)\) on 480 states, certified by a sum-of-squares argument. - \(\mathfrak{D}_{\min}\) and \(\mathfrak{D}_{\max}\): a shared maximum \(\log_2[6/(2+\sqrt3)]\approx0.685\) on 640 states, certified by branch-and-bound over ~4.4×10⁶ boxes. - LR: \(\log_2[(1+\sqrt5)/2]\) on a continuum of 192 planes, certified by LP vertex enumeration over 626,626 vertices.

The three maximizer sets are mutually disjoint.

In tree-level Møller scattering, \(M_2\) is minimized at \(s_W^2=0.2317\) at \(m_Z\) (measured value 0.23122) and tracks the \(\overline{\rm MS}\) running to ~1 TeV. \(\mathfrak{D}_{\min}\) has a shallow minimum 7% low at \(m_Z\). \(\mathfrak{D}_{\max}\) and LR instead give a cusp at exactly \(1/4\) at every energy. The cause is structural. These two measures are maxima over witnesses, so their kinks point upward, and they grow linearly in \(|g_V|\) near stabilizer states. At \(g_V=0\) the singlet scatters into itself, which pins the minimum there. One of the authors (Yin) co-authored the original Møller minimal-magic paper, so this is an internal audit. The authors stress that both the \(1/4\) pinning and the \(\mathfrak{D}_{\min}\) kinks rest on exact tree-level properties and may not survive higher orders.
