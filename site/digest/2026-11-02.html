Quad lenses lean toward core collapse

Gilman et al. arXiv:2610.40766 analyze 29 quadruply imaged quasars and find halo densities consistent with gravothermal core collapse. They interpret this as elastic Yukawa SIDM and apply cluster and satellite-evaporation limits. The favored region is 20–80 GeV dark matter with MeV mediators and \(\sigma/m\sim10^2\)–\(10^4\) cm²/g at 10 km/s. Mediator bounds then push them toward an invisibly decaying neutrinophilic scalar. This is the third result in two weeks pointing away from CDM-density subhalos. Zhang et al. found none of 5000+ CDM subhalos dense enough, and Tekverk et al. found SIDM matches three known perturbers far more often. The new paper is a population statement, not a per-perturber one, though the authors still say “hints.” Elsewhere, an extra loop order excludes a minimal SUSY GUT, and phase-transition GW predictions shed three idealizations. The 248 keV LZ event drew no papers.

Themes

Phase transitions, less idealized

Three papers each relax a standard assumption: - Cutrona et al. arXiv:2610.40775 find that gravity, through non-minimal couplings, can enlarge or shrink the parameter space where supercooled radiative-symmetry-breaking transitions complete. - Finetti et al. arXiv:2610.40781 let dark-sector sound waves leak into the visible fluid. The total GW signal is generally suppressed and its spectral shape changes. - Blum et al. arXiv:2610.41504 show nucleated bubbles are not spherical, with \(\delta R/R\sim(\hbar/S_b)^{1/2}\approx0.1\) for typical cosmological transitions.

This is in the spirit of last week’s error-budget thread, now applied to GW forecasts.

Loop order decides

Allwicher et al. arXiv:2610.40821 find that Tera-\(Z\) gauge-coupling precision becomes sensitive to higher-loop running all the way up. In minimal SU(5) with proton-decay limits, two loops point to PeV-scale SUSY, while three loops appear to exclude the model.

Adelaja et al. arXiv:2610.40823 propose a retained signature-changing soft-gluon term as the source of the unexplained non-planar \(\zeta_2\) violations at small \(x\). Those violations appear in the three- and four-loop non-singlet splitting functions, which were September’s headline. The proposal reproduces both with no tuned coefficient and predicts five-loop terms.

Bera et al. arXiv:2610.42062 complete the leading-colour two-loop integrals for \(t\bar t\gamma\), including elliptic entries. That follows last week’s genus-two \(t\bar t\) amplitudes.

Structure over size

Alison et al.’s CoReNet arXiv:2610.40749 builds the decay chain into the architecture. With 1508 parameters it matches a transformer more than 100× larger in \(HH\to4b\), extending last week’s tagger-compression run. Hart et al.’s REM arXiv:2610.41889 goes from hits to particles to resonances in full simulation, beating ML particle flow. Chung arXiv:2610.41894 asks the information-theoretic version of the question for calorimeters.

Neutrino generators, rebuilt

Graham-Jones et al. arXiv:2610.40866 put a consistent chiral EFT with \(\Delta(1232)\) pion production into Sherpa, going through FeynRules and UFO. Prasad et al. arXiv:2610.40912 decompose a DNN’s inclusive electron–carbon prediction into exclusive generator channels by reweighting.

Worth a look

  • arXiv:2610.40766: A 29-lens population pointing at core collapse, with a concrete particle model attached. It is the strongest entry yet in the SIDM-vs-CDM lensing thread.
  • arXiv:2610.40821: The two-loop vs three-loop flip on minimal SUSY SU(5) is a sharp result. The “IR determination” of the colour-triplet mass explains why.
  • arXiv:2610.41894: Treats hadronic resolution as an information ladder. On the same Geant4 events, the full record reaches 1.29% vs 2.14% at 20 GeV, and each readout dimension gets a measured value.
  • arXiv:2610.40749: Already used in several CMS searches. It recovers truth jet pairings without ever being trained on them.
  • arXiv:2610.42143: Kats makes a first strange-quark polarimetry measurement via Λ in \(W\to c\bar s\), on CMS Open Data. The result is consistent with zero for now; the method and projections are the point.

Deep dives

arXiv:2610.40766 — Hints of self-interacting dark matter from strong gravitational lensing (Daniel Gilman et al.)

What they did. This is a recast, not new data. It takes the G26 likelihood on median core-collapse times in two halo-mass bins, \(10^6\)–\(10^8\) and \(10^8\)–\(10^{10.7}\,M_\odot\), from 29 JWST quads. G26 disfavors CDM with Bayes factors of 8:1 to 24:1. The authors map that likelihood onto elastic Yukawa SIDM using the Gurian et al. gravothermal \(t_c\), which is valid across Knudsen regimes. They then multiply in cluster (\(<0.1\) cm²/g at 1500 km/s), satellite-evaporation (\(<10\) cm²/g at 250 km/s) and relic-density likelihoods.

The likelihood peaks at \(\sigma_{10}\approx1000\) or \(200\) cm²/g, depending on the subhalo-modeling weights. Both peaks sit at \(\alpha_\chi\sim10^{-3.5}\)–\(10^{-3}\) and \(m_\chi/m_\phi>10^{3.5}\). At \(\alpha_\chi=10^{-3}\) the viable region is \(m_\chi\sim20\)–80 GeV with \(m_\phi<20\) MeV, much of it near the \(s\)-wave resonances. To survive mediator bounds, they adopt Dirac DM with a complex scalar dilepton that decays to \(\nu_R\nu_R\); lepton charge forbids tree-level Higgs mixing. Kinetic decoupling at \(\sim0.14\) MeV clears the \(\gtrsim7\) keV requirement. \(\Delta N_{\rm eff}\) can stay below 0.107, but only with extra entropy release or a tuned Higgs-portal history.

Why it matters. The paper turns the lensing thread into a concrete particle-physics target with \(N_{\rm eff}\) and direct-detection handles. It also shows that elastic scattering cannot collapse standard field halos faster than 2.0 Gyr and 2.9 Gyr in the two mass bins. Shorter collapse times would point to inelastic or dissipative physics instead.

Caveats. CDM is not formally excluded. The result depends on the assumed tidal acceleration (3–5×), subhalo abundance, and collapsed-profile slope. The relic relation carries a factor-of-5 allowance because the bound-state-formation calculation does not yet exist. If mergers delay collapse, the inferred long-mean-free-path cross sections are only lower bounds.

arXiv:2610.40821 — The Yottascale at Tera-\(Z\): Petascale SUSY? (Lukas Allwicher et al.)

What they did. Gauge couplings are run from \(m_Z\) to the GUT scale at successive loop orders and compared against current and FCC-ee input errors; \(\delta\alpha_s\) drops from \(9\times10^{-4}\) to \(10^{-4}\). In the SM, 2-loop running is already only marginally adequate, and FCC-ee precision would resolve 4-loop effects. In the CMSSM, the 2-loop SUSY threshold shift to \(\alpha_3\) makes unification worse at three loops: \(\bar\xi\) goes from 0.1–5% to about 2.4%.

In minimal SUSY SU(5), the 1-loop GUT matching fixes \(M_{H_c}\) independently of \(\alpha_5\). The reason is group-theoretic: \(X\) and \(\Sigma\) together fill a complete \(\mathbf{24}\). The same holds in Georgi–Glashow, but fails for multi-field breaking sectors.

This makes \(M_{H_c}\) purely IR-determined and loop-order sensitive. With \(\tau/\tau_{\rm SK}\simeq1.55\times10^{-4}(M_{H_c}/{\rm YeV})^2(m_S/{\rm PeV})^2\), the two loop orders give opposite answers: - Two loops: \(M_{H_c}\) grows with \(m_S\), so the Super-K \(K^+\bar\nu\) bound is evaded near PeV. - Three loops: \(M_{H_c}\) falls with \(m_S\) and stays excluded at every SUSY scale, including against Hyper-K projections.

Why it matters. It is a clean example of perturbative order, rather than data, flipping a model’s viability. It also gives a concrete theory requirement for Tera-\(Z\).

Caveats. The 2-loop GUT thresholds are unknown, and the authors admit they could cancel the effect if comparable in size. Results above \(m_S\sim100\) TeV are extrapolated with analytic degenerate-spectrum thresholds. The analysis does not cover split SUSY or gravity-induced operators.

arXiv:2610.41894 — Calorimetry as inference: the information content of particle showers (Wonyong Chung)

What they did. The paper casts calorimetry as Fisher/Bayes estimation on a lattice of records. It proves monotonicity under refinement, exact information scaling under Poisson thinning, and a \(1/(1+{\rm cv}^2)\) clustering cost. It also shows that the \(a/\sqrt E\oplus b\) budget is the Gaussian-equality case of a two-sided bound.

It then measures a resolution ladder with trained estimators (validated to \(\le0.34\%\) against exact posteriors on solvable cascades). The data are Geant4 11.4.2 FTFP_BERT \(\pi^-\) events in a large Cu block, with the neutron tracking cut removed. At 20 GeV the ladder reads:

Record \(\sigma/E\)
Sum 4.07%
+ \(S,C\) 2.16%
+ longitudinal shape 1.60%
+ time 1.32%
+ counts 1.29%

The full record is about \(5.5\%/\sqrt E\oplus0.3\%\) over 20–100 GeV. That is a factor 1.7–1.9 below the Lee–Livan–Wigmans one-functional construction evaluated on the same events (2.14%; 2.7% as published).

What the readout dimensions are worth: - Shape: radial information is worth only 2–3%; depth sampling saturates at about \(1\,\lambda_I\). - Time: a 10 ns gate costs about a third in resolution. - Floor: in-volume neutrinos from stopped-\(\mu\) chains leave an estimated 0.99% floor, barely reduced from their 1.12% marginal. This argues for muon-stop tagging.

Caveats. All records are truth-level, single-particle and pileup-free. The count-based record elements have no hardware counterpart, so 1.60% is the hardware-relevant number. Cross-run spreads reach 0.19% at 20 GeV. Model dependence is real: - Transferring FTFP_BERT-trained estimators to QGSP_BERT events shifts the full record by +19% (no retraining was done for QGSP_BERT). - Retraining with high-precision neutron transport leaves the top value nearly unchanged (1.30%) but cuts the time increment from 0.28% to 0.17%.
