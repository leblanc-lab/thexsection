Two groups close the g⁶ pressure

Kajantie et al. arXiv:2610.23675 and Chala et al. arXiv:2610.23676 both report the complete \(\mathcal O(g^6)\) pressure of hot Yang-Mills, a gap open for over two decades, and both land on the lattice side. Kajantie et al. get quantitative agreement with 4D lattice data over a broad temperature range. Chala et al. agree within errors with the older lattice-fitted coefficient. This follows last week’s N³LO cold-QED pressure (10-12), and the thermal loop-tree-duality method that did the work there reappears in the first paper. Unlike the other recent “next order” results, which mostly exposed the true error budget, these two are a direct pass of the dimensional-reduction EFT against data. Elsewhere, Crivellin et al. close off a \(|V_{cb}|\)-based escape from \(C_9\), and NA61/SHINE reports an isospin effect it cannot explain.

Themes

Hot QCD pressure and N³LO building blocks

The two \(g^6\) papers, from different groups and presumably independent calculations, agree with each other and with the lattice. Devoto et al. arXiv:2610.22533 supply the three-loop rapidity beam-function kernels and so the full singular structure of color-singlet rapidity distributions at N³LO, which is the ingredient fully differential Higgs/DY work needs. Peng et al. arXiv:2610.22477 propose soft-hadron energy correlators as a flow probe, from p+p to Pb+Pb.

b→sℓℓ: the \(|V_{cb}|\) escape route closes

Crivellin et al. arXiv:2610.22490 test whether CKM unitarity violation, via NP contamination of \(|V_{cb}|\) and hence a smaller \(V_{ts}\), can produce the lepton-flavor-universal \(b\to s\ell\ell\) deficit. They find a no-go: RG running breaks the custodial protection, and vector-like quarks fail EWPO and \(K\)/\(B_s\) mixing bounds. This bears on both the \(C_9\) question and the inclusive/exclusive \(|V_{cb}|\) puzzle. LHCb’s first observation of \(\Xi_b^-\to\Xi^-\mu^+\mu^-\) arXiv:2610.22452 gives \(\mathcal B=(118\pm24\pm7\pm38)\times10^{-8}\). The normalisation error is dominated by SU(3) breaking, the same symmetry that strained in the \(B\to DP\) fit on 10-15.

Anomalies meeting their inputs, again

Abazajian et al. arXiv:2610.23713 find the 20 GeV halo excess depends strongly on foreground modelling, so it does not support a smooth NFW halo or a shared origin with the LZ event. That fits Cholis’s Fermi-bubble substructure argument from 10-09. No LZ model-building papers appeared today. Barčot et al. arXiv:2610.23474 review the neutrino anomalies and say recent results weakened several tensions without leaving an uncontested explanation, which matches last week’s gallium and IceCube-decoherence nulls. The NA61/SHINE result below is the day’s counter-current.

Exotics versus threshold dynamics

Garcia Tecocoatzi et al. arXiv:2610.22914 predict detectable \(4P\) all-charm tetraquarks with \(J^{PC}=3^{--},2^{--}\). That sits against Swanson’s no-exotics di-\(J/\psi\) fit from 10-14. Damen et al. arXiv:2610.23240 give a sum-rule molecular reading of \(\chi_{c1}(4010)\).

Worth a look

  • arXiv:2610.23675 Kajantie et al. — the nine nonfactorizing four-loop sum-integrals completing the hard contribution for general \(SU(N_c)\), and the explicit IR cancellation across scales. Read it for how the lattice comparison is made.
  • arXiv:2610.23676 Chala et al. — the same quantity from a separate calculation, and the first direct comparison with the lattice-fitted \(g^6\) coefficient. Read next to the paper above.
  • arXiv:2610.22490 Crivellin et al. — a clean statement of why a modified \(W\)-\(c\)-\(b\) vertex cannot fake \(C_9\) without fine-tuning. A useful reference whenever someone proposes it.
  • arXiv:2610.23498 SHINE Collaboration — a charged-over-neutral kaon excess in central Ar+Sc at 8.8 and 11.9 GeV. They say it is not explained by known mechanisms, and they find a consistent excess from 2.6 to 200 GeV. Uncertainties are sizeable, so the question is whether other experiments confirm it.
  • arXiv:2610.23713 Abazajian et al. — a systematic foreground scan (281 diffuse configurations plus bubble templates) showing simulated mismodelling can produce NFW-like emission of the observed amplitude.

Also noted

  • arXiv:2610.22418 Long-Lived Charge Trap Characterization for Rare-Event Searches with Skipper-CCDs — Ansh Desai et al. (physics.ins-det). 1787 traps characterized, with no significant effect on SENSEI’s single-electron rate. A hidden population is not excluded.

Deep dives

arXiv:2610.23675 — Complete \(g^6\) pressure of hot Yang–Mills theory (Keijo Kajantie et al.)

The missing nonlogarithmic hard coefficient is \(\tilde\beta_{\rm E1}=6236\pm40\). That converts to \(q_c(N_c{=}3,N_f{=}0)=-3591.2\pm16.9_{\rm pert}\pm250_{\rm mag}\), consistent with the old lattice-inferred \(\tilde\beta_{\rm E1}\simeq6000\pm600\).

Method. - The 65 four-loop vacuum graphs give about \(10^5\) sum-integrals. These reduce to 21 canonical structures, and \(\xi\)-dependence cancels at that stage. - Nine nonfactorizing sum-integrals were unknown. The authors evaluated them with thermal loop-tree duality. Matsubara sums are done analytically, and new local IR counterterms for zero-mode soft regions are added to BPHZ-style UV subtractions. The finite remainders were Monte Carlo integrated, at roughly \(10^6\) CPU-hours. - The pole coefficients come out analytically. They confirm the previously inferred hard-scale IR poles and their explicit cancellation against EQCD/MQCD. - The method independently reproduces the known \(I_6\).

Lattice comparison. The comparison uses Borsanyi et al. data in an EQCD-resummed scheme, with scale set by FAC and varied by a factor of 2. The band agrees with the data down to a few \(T_c\). In the same scheme, \(g^3\) and \(g^4\) overshoot Stefan–Boltzmann and \(g^5\) undershoots. The authors read this as support for the “Laine conjecture”.

Caveats. - The magnetic constant \(B_G\) now dominates the error, by a factor of about 15 over the new perturbative piece. - The full result holds only for SU(3), because the four-loop MQCD matching is incomplete for general \(N_c\). - The “only at \(g^6\)” statement is tied to this resummation prescription. The authors note that other schemes behave better at lower orders.

arXiv:2610.23676 — The pressure of the gluon plasma at \(\mathcal O(g_s^6)\), perturbatively (Mikael Chala et al.)

This is an independent route to the same quantity, giving \(q_c(0)=-3846\pm515\). That is about 0.5σ from the value \(-3526(4)(55)(30)\) obtained by fitting the \(g^6\) formula to Borsanyi et al. trace-anomaly data over \(10\)–\(1000\,\Lambda_{\overline{\rm MS}}\).

Method. - Background-field Feynman gauge, with a different propagator basis. - Reduction via SIRENA using unimodular loop-momentum changes and no genuine four-loop IBP. This yields 10 connected and 6 factorizable sum-integrals. - The connected integrals are evaluated numerically: Schwinger parameters, sector/subsector decomposition, partial Poisson resummation, and Sobol QMC with 4 Matsubara modes. - Poles are cross-checked by doing the hard-to-soft matching in the full bosonic SM 3D EFT. This needs a four-loop EFT vacuum counterterm that they compute for the first time. The resulting 22 equations have rank 14 and fix 7 pole combinations.

Lattice comparison. The strict expansion at \(\bar\mu=2\pi T\) matches the lattice within 3% above \(2.5\,\Lambda_{\overline{\rm MS}}\), and within 1.5% from \(3\) to \(1000\,\Lambda_{\overline{\rm MS}}\).

Caveats. - The error is about twice the Helsinki total. The two \(\beta_G\) errors alone account for only about \(\pm170\) of it, so the numerical sum-integral allowances dominate. - Both groups compare against the same lattice dataset, so the lattice check is not independent across the two papers. - The cross-check between the groups covers one shared master integral and the final \(q_c\); the two central values differ by about 255.

arXiv:2610.22490 — Explaining \(b\to s\ell^+\ell^-\) with CKM Unitarity Violation? A No-Go Theorem (Andreas Crivellin et al.)

Updated fit. The authors refit LFU \(b\to s\ell\ell\) data with new LHCb/CMS inputs. The best fit is \(C_9=-0.87\), \(C_{10}=0.24\), at 5.4σ (\(\Delta\chi^2\approx33\)). This excludes the bins nearest the \(J/\psi\).

The proposed escape. Rescaling \(V_{cb}\), and hence \(V_{ts}\), alone can buy about 23 units of \(\chi^2\). The natural operator for this is \(C^{(3)}_{23}\) (for \(C^{(3)}_{22}\), the required couplings are \(\mathcal O(10)\)). But SMEFT/WET running gives \(C_9\approx(33C^{(3)}_{23}+24C^{(1)}_{23})\) and \(C_{10}\approx-20(30C^{(3)}_{23}+27C^{(1)}_{23})\), in units of TeV/\(\Lambda^2\) at \(\Lambda=2\) TeV. So custodial \(C^{(1)}=-C^{(3)}\) at the high scale does not survive.

Why it fails. - Requiring the right-sign \(\delta V_{cb}\) together with \(C_9<0\) forces \(C_{10}\lesssim-22C_9\). - The only good-fit region is fine-tuned: \(C^{(1)}_{23}\approx-1.1C^{(3)}_{23}\), \(C^{(3)}_{23}\approx-1\). There the \(V_{cb}\) shift has the wrong sign and is overcompensated by \(Z\)-\(b\)-\(s\). - A 10% positive shift already raises the \(B_s\)-mixing prediction by about 20%. - Vector-like quarks at 2 TeV, either \(U\) alone or the custodial pairs \(D{+}T_2\) and \(T_1{+}T_2\), cap \(C_9\) below a quarter of the needed size, given \(B_s\) mixing, EWPO and \(\epsilon_K\).

Scope caveats. - Right-handed currents (\(O_{Hud}\)) are excluded. The authors argue these cannot resolve the inclusive/exclusive \(V_{cb}\) split. - The analysis fixes the scale at 2 TeV. - The underlying anomaly still rests on form-factor and nonlocal-hadronic inputs.
