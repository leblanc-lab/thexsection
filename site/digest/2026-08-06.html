Neutrino handles and axion channels

The day’s weight sits in neutrino experiment design and a broad axion spread, with hadron spectroscopy as the numerical ballast underneath. DUNE recasts its oscillation sensitivity in a Bayesian framework and, as a byproduct, produces the first study of its sensitivity to the leptonic Jarlskog invariant (arXiv:2608.04059) — a parametrization-independent CP measure. Alongside it, two genuinely different experimental ideas: turning detector motion into interaction spectroscopy at Hyper-K (arXiv:2608.04091), and using positron-emitting neutrinoless modes to fingerprint the underlying lepton-number-violating operator (arXiv:2608.04187). The muon-collider thread persists in a minor key (a top-partner study, arXiv:2608.04399), and the automation drumbeat continues with an LLM-driven SMEFT tool.

Deep dives

arXiv:2608.04059 — A Bayesian approach to the long-baseline neutrino oscillation sensitivity of DUNE (DUNE Collaboration et al.)

DUNE recasts its established sensitivity inputs (the same flux, GENIE-based cross-section, and detector models as the 2020 TDR) into a Bayesian MCMC framework using MaCh3, with Adaptive Metropolis proposal tuning (~50× fewer steps than manual tuning, further reduced ~15× by 5% downsampling). This is explicitly not an updated sensitivity but a demonstration of what the Bayesian treatment adds. Two payoffs stand out. First, four-dimensional posteriors expose the strong sin²θ₂₃–sin²θ₁₃ correlation, producing two well-separated octant lobes; applying a reactor short-baseline θ₁₃ constraint post hoc via prior reweighting yields “a significant increase” in θ₂₃-octant sensitivity — the reactor prior breaks the appearance-channel degeneracy that otherwise smears the octant. Second, and the headline, this is the first study of DUNE’s sensitivity to the Jarlskog invariant J, a parametrization-independent CP measure derived trivially as a posterior of the sampled parameters. Studies span NuFIT 6.0 (NO, lower octant) and T2K-NOvA (IO, upper octant) Asimov points, δ_CP = 0, −π/4, −π/2, at 200 and 1000 kt-MW-yr.

Why it matters: it establishes the machinery for parametrization-independent CP inference and flexible constraint incorporation as DUNE moves toward real data, where robust systematic propagation and derived-quantity posteriors become essential. Caveats are honestly flagged: results are Asimov-based, mass-ordering sensitivity is not evaluated (assumed >5σ), θ₁₂ and Δm²₂₁ are fixed by Gaussian priors (no solar samples yet), and the ND is found to be systematics-limited with the current CC-inclusive binning.

arXiv:2608.04091 — Moving a Detector to Probe New Neutrino Interactions: IWCD at Hyper-Kamiokande (Thomas Schwemberger et al.)

Schwemberger and Takhistov reframe the movable IWCD not as a systematics-control tool but as an interaction spectrometer: sliding the same 300-ton water Cherenkov detector through the J-PARC off-axis beam (1°, 2.5°, 4°, peaking at ~1.0, 0.6, 0.4 GeV) exposes one target/apparatus to distinct incident spectra while keeping detector and interaction uncertainties correlated. Using the NCQE/CCQE event-rate ratio R^θ — in which common normalization largely cancels — they show that combining positions breaks vector-axial and normalization degeneracies that a single spectrum cannot, because the vector-axial interference term B carries a distinct energy dependence. Combining three positions with a 5% correlated normalization benchmark, they project 95% CL NSI sensitivities of −0.07 ≲ ε^{uA}{μμ} ≲ 0.06, −0.10 ≲ ε^{uV}{μμ} ≲ 0.12, and −0.05 ≲ ε^{udL}_{μμ} ≲ 0.05. Axial NC NSI, which do not contribute to the coherent matter potential, are the key complementary target to oscillation and high-energy scattering probes; the left-handed CC coefficient, which merely rescales CCQE, benefits little from multiple positions.

Why it matters: it gives movable/multi-position detectors a genuinely new physics use — spectroscopy of interaction structure — extensible to single-pion channels and other beams. Caveats are substantial and acknowledged: the analysis retains only the dominant ν_μ component, treats oxygen as an incoherent free-nucleon sum with a hard Pauli-blocking cutoff (yielding NCQE rates above NEUT), adopts an 80% flat NCQE efficiency and a schematic 5–10% systematic, and for light mediators the low-Q² cutoff removes most position-dependent discriminating power.

arXiv:2608.05150 — The QCD Axion and Neutrino Masses (Hridoy Debnath et al.)

Debnath, Fileviez Pérez, and Miller perform a unified analysis identifying the PQ-breaking scale with the neutrino-mass-generation scale (M_seesaw ~ v_S ~ f_a) across both DFSZ and KSVZ, for six mechanisms: Type-I/II/III seesaw, the Zee model, and colored seesaw I/II. For each they compute the E/N anomaly ratio and hence g_aγγ. Within DFSZ, Type-I, Type-II and Zee leave E/N unchanged from the minimal model (new fields are singlets or scalars), whereas Type-III’s fermionic electroweak triplets shift E/N (8/3→11/3 for DFSZ-I, 2/3→5/3 for DFSZ-II) — a measurable g_aγγ signature. In KSVZ, colored seesaw I and II predict E/N = 0 and 16/9, giving g_aγγ^I/g_aγγ^II ~ 14. A robust generic prediction across all frameworks is a derivative axion-neutrino coupling ∝ m_ν/f_a. If the axion is dark matter, a→νν decays produce a monochromatic flux peaked at E_ν = m_a/2 lying above both the CNB and solar thermal fluxes, with branching ratios and lifetimes sensitive to the neutrino mass ordering.

Why it matters: it ties the strong-CP solution, neutrino mass, and dark matter to one scale and yields correlated, testable handles — future haloscopes (ADMX, JWST bounds already excise parameter space), a distinguishing g_aγγ per mechanism, and a PTOLEMY-adjacent neutrino signal. Caveats: the axion-neutrino couplings are enormously f_a-suppressed, the neutrino-flux detectability is “very challenging,” and the seesaw–PQ identification is an assumed upper bound on f_a rather than a forced relation.
