A lattice measure for chiral gauge theories

Craig arXiv:2610.09093 constructs a gauge-invariant measure for overlap fermions nonperturbatively, in a class of nonabelian chiral gauge theories that includes the SM. He calls the result “a candidate nonperturbative lattice definition of the Standard Model.” The construction holds on admissible gauge fields and goes through Lüscher’s reconstruction, so it falls short of a simulation recipe. Still, it is the most consequential claim of the day. Elsewhere, LZ-2024 closes a door that has nothing to do with the 248 keV event. Lattice phase-transition papers also cluster, which is relevant to Pisarski’s open question on chiral transition order.

Themes

LZ, a null and two fits

Bao et al. arXiv:2610.10219 find that LZ-2024 excludes sub-100 GeV Bino dark matter via \(h\)- and \(Z\)-resonances at >5.9σ and >4.1σ. This holds even with adverse 3σ FLAG shifts, partial dark-matter fractions and no LHC input. On the recoil, Lian et al. arXiv:2610.09753 argue that Higgsino-pair inelastic fits overproduce solar neutrinos, while singlino ground states survive. That turns the solar bound from veto into composition probe. Sheikh et al. arXiv:2610.09516 get an excited fraction near ½ from thermal quasi-pNGBs, which is the exothermic regime Oliveira flagged on 09-29.

Deep dives

arXiv:2610.09093 — A gauge-invariant measure for lattice chiral fermions (Nathaniel Craig)

Craig claims a nonperturbative, smooth, exponentially local, gauge-invariant overlap-fermion measure for \(SU(5)\) with \(\mathbf{10}\oplus\bar{\mathbf{5}}\). He extends it to all four global forms \([SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)]/\Gamma_{\rm SM}\), \(\Gamma_{\rm SM}=1,\mathbb{Z}_2,\mathbb{Z}_3,\mathbb{Z}_6\), by embedding SM links into \(SU(5)\).

The key move replaces Lüscher’s abelian interpolation of the gauge potential to zero, which can exit the admissible domain for nonabelian fields. Instead:

  • A smooth connection is reconstructed from \(U\) and sampled at auxiliary spacings \(h_n=3^{-n}\), with fine plaquettes obeying \(\|1-(F_hU)(p)\|\le C\varepsilon h^2\).
  • Hermitian Wilson operators at successive spacings are interpolated linearly. The intermediate operators are not link fields, and a uniform gap \(g\) is proved throughout.
  • Each step contributes a curvature-strip term \(B_n\), and the measure term is \(\mathfrak{L}=\sum_n B_n\).
  • Return phases telescope. An exact colour-trace identity for \(\mathbf{10}\oplus\bar{\mathbf{5}}\) cancels the leading terms, which makes the sum converge.
  • Global integrability on noncontractible loops uses an auxiliary \(SU(3)\) connection under which the fermions pair conjugately.

The general sufficient conditions are cubic-anomaly cancellation plus even multiplicity of half-integer-isospin representations under an \(SU(2)\subset SU(N_c)\). The author says the latter is an artefact of the method.

Where “admissible” bites: - The construction requires a sufficiently small plaquette bound \(\varepsilon\). It is volume-independent but not quantified in the extracted text. - The measure is fixed only up to one constant phase per connected component of admissible fields. The current’s transformation laws do not guarantee that every choice of these relative phases respects discrete symmetries.

The convergence, locality and gap estimates sit in appendices not included in the extract. Lean verification is relegated to supplementary material. As the digest notes, this is an existence construction, not a simulation algorithm.

arXiv:2610.10219 — Is the Light Neutralino Dark Matter Still Viable in the MSSM After the LZ-2024 Results? (Zhiyang Bao et al.)

LZ-2024 excludes the sub-100 GeV Bino \(h\)-funnel at \(Z>5.9\) (\(p=1.8\times10^{-9}\)) and the \(Z\)-funnel at \(Z>4.1\) (\(p=2.1\times10^{-5}\)). The result holds for \(\Omega h^2=0.12\) and for \(0.06\) (half the dark matter), without imposing the LHC bound \(\mu\gtrsim760\) GeV.

The argument rests on one coupling (\(C_h\) or \(C_Z\)) setting both annihilation and scattering. Finite mediator width and thermal smearing cap \(\langle\sigma v\rangle\), so the relic density imposes a floor on \(\sigma^{\rm SI}\) or \(\sigma^{\rm SD}_n\).

The analysis is deliberately conservative: - Loop corrections: full one-loop vertex corrections (904 diagrams) give \(K^{\rm SI}\lesssim1.5\) and \(K^{\rm SD}_n\lesssim1.3\). Scale variation changes these corrections by at most 5%. - Hadronic inputs: FLAG-2024 values (\(\sigma_{\pi N}=42.2\pm2.4\) MeV) are used, with worst-case \(3\sigma\) rescalings of 0.78 (SI) and 0.62 (SD) applied at every point. The authors note that the Roy–Steiner value \(\sigma_{\pi N}\simeq58\)–59 MeV would only raise \(\sigma^{\rm SI}\).

The resulting cross-section floors sit nearly an order of magnitude above the LZ-2024 limits. LZ-2022 gave only \(Z<2\).

Caveats: - The LZ-2024 recast is micrOMEGAs’ background-free Poisson model. It is calibrated near 90% CL and then extrapolated far into the tail. - DDCalc implements only LZ-2022. There, micrOMEGAs underestimates \(Z\), which supports the claim that the micrOMEGAs numbers are conservative. - Near-degenerate Higgsino–Wino points (splitting \(<3\) GeV) were cut for counterterm instability.

What remains for a thermal Bino is coannihilation, heavy-Higgs resonance or well-tempering, which together point to \(|m_{\tilde\chi}|\gtrsim200\) GeV.

arXiv:2610.09753 — Can Nuclear Recoils and Solar Neutrinos Resolve the Composition of Singlino-Higgsino Dark Matter? (Jingwei Lian et al.)

Abstract only. The paper’s point is a degeneracy: swapping ground and excited states can leave an inelastic recoil spectrum unchanged while shifting ground-state annihilation by orders of magnitude. Solar-neutrino signals from captured dark matter then distinguish compositions that recoils cannot.

Applied in the NMSSM to fits of the high-energy LZ candidate, the analysis finds: - Disfavoured: Higgsino-pair inelastic fits overproduce solar signals. - Solar-compatible: singlino ground states, and Higgsino ground states with weak inelastic transitions. - Escape route: an elastic Higgsino alternative evades the solar bound by closing the inelastic transition that drives capture.

The abstract gives no quantitative limits. The authors acknowledge residual degeneracies tied to annihilation, halo and detector assumptions.
