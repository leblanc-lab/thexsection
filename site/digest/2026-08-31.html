Two firsts from CMS and LHCb

The day’s two anchors are both experimental firsts, and both null-or-clean rather than anomalous. CMS runs the first search for TeV-scale leptoquarks produced through muon–quark scattering (arXiv:2608.28377), exploiting the proton’s charged-lepton content to reach a production mode nobody had used before. LHCb reports the first observation of the Cabibbo-suppressed \(\Xi_c^0\to pK^-\) with its decay asymmetry (arXiv:2608.27894). Around them the weight sits in an unusually coherent axion cluster — detection, cosmology, and strong-CP theory in one day — plus the recurring quantum-information-at-colliders thread and a heavy-ion strangeness bloc.

Two experimental firsts

CMS arXiv:2608.28377 turns quantum fluctuations inside the proton into a resonant single-LQ channel, excluding new regions for masses 1.5–3.6 TeV in the \(\mathrm{LQ}(u\mu)\) scenario — a genuinely new handle rather than another pair-production limit. LHCb arXiv:2608.27894 observes \(\Xi_c^0\to pK^-\) at \(\mathcal{B}=(4.5\pm0.5\pm0.2\pm0.9)\times10^{-5}\) and pins its asymmetry parameter, adding a charm-baryon CPV probe.

Axions, from fibers to the domain-wall problem

Three angles converge. Dvali et al. arXiv:2608.27551 propose coiled optical fibers in a magnetic field, with the signal a photon phase shift rather than conversion loss — a route to high ALP mass. Gorghetto et al. arXiv:2608.27553 show that PQ-violating operators can destroy \(N>1\) axion strings before the QCD potential matters, evading the domain-wall problem for \(f_a\lesssim10^{10}\) GeV and predicting an nEDM within two orders of the current limit. Di Vecchia et al. arXiv:2608.28253 revisit the \(U(1)\)-vs-strong-CP tension from a 1PI-action view. Together they extend the strong-CP thread that ran through last week’s lattice pair — now on the cosmology and detection sides.

Quantum observables at colliders

The magic/entanglement program keeps advancing. Chang et al. arXiv:2608.27559 compute NLO electroweak corrections to entanglement entropy and stabilizer Rényi entropy (magic) in \(\ell^+\ell^-\to\tau\tau, ZZ\), showing radiative effects coherently shift the quantum structure — a precision handle at future lepton colliders, following the season’s magic work. Alongside, Bashore et al. arXiv:2608.28057 build Drell-Yan amplitudes and their interference into quantum circuits, and Mahmood et al. arXiv:2608.28084 benchmark quantum vs classical ML regression on CMS open data.

Heavy-ion strangeness and suppression

A substantial nuclear bloc. Yang et al. arXiv:2608.28277 argue thermal decay, not color screening, drives \(\Upsilon\) suppression at RHIC. Coc et al. arXiv:2608.28456 add finite-width \(\rho/\omega\) spectral functions to resonance decays, easing but not resolving the soft-pion puzzle.

Deep dives

arXiv:2608.28377 — Search for scalar leptoquarks produced via muon-quark scattering in pp collisions at √s = 13 TeV (CMS Collaboration)

The first LHC search exploiting the proton’s charged-lepton content to produce a single TeV-scale scalar leptoquark resonantly through muon–quark fusion, using the full Run 2 dataset (138 fb⁻¹). The lepton comes from a photon fluctuation inside one proton (LUXlep PDFs), fusing with a quark from the other; the signature is a high-pₜ muon plus a light- or b-quark jet, with a soft second muon often lost down the beampipe. The observable is the μ-jet invariant mass, fit for a bump on a smooth background across eight categories (one/two muon × BDT tight/loose × b-tag). Data agree with background; 95% CL limits on σ·B exclude new territory in the (M_LQ, λ) plane: 1.5–3.6 TeV at couplings 0.2–0.6 for LQ(uμ), and M_LQ > 1.8 TeV at λ > 1 for LQ(bμ).

Why it matters: unlike pair production (QCD-driven, nearly λ-independent, giving a vertical exclusion) or nonresonant t-channel, this resonant mode scales as λ² and gains reach precisely where pair production dies from phase space — large λ and high mass. It’s a genuinely new handle motivated by the (now-fading) B-meson flavor anomalies, and complements existing modes rather than merely tightening them. Caveat: limits assume only lepton-induced production, neglecting gluon-induced single production; the authors flag this as conservative (weakening limits by ~10–15%) and verify via injection tests that the bias stays below half a standard deviation. The dominant systematic is the freely-floating background fit, i.e. statistics.

arXiv:2608.27894 — Observation of the Ξ_c⁰ → pK⁻ decay and measurement of its decay asymmetry (LHCb collaboration et al.)

First observation of the singly Cabibbo-suppressed, purely W-exchange decay Ξ_c⁰ → pK⁻, reconstructed through the clean cascade Ξ_b⁻ → Ξ_c⁰(→pK⁻)π⁻ in 5.4 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV pp data. A signal yield of 134±14 is extracted from a simultaneous fit with the normalization channel Ξ_c⁰ → pK⁻K⁻π⁺ (6930±90 events), giving a relative rate R = (9.26±0.94±0.43)×10⁻³ and, using the known normalization branching fraction, B(Ξ_c⁰ → pK⁻) = (4.5±0.5±0.2±0.9)×10⁻⁵. Exploiting the cascade, an angular fit of the Ξ_c⁰ helicity angle (assuming α_{Ξ_b} = −1, validated by the analogous Λ_b measurement) yields the decay asymmetry α = 0.32±0.15±0.01, compatible with zero.

Why it matters: pure W-exchange charm-baryon decays are notoriously beyond reliable computation, and BESIII’s recent Λ_c⁺ → Ξ⁰K⁺ analysis already showed deviations from theory. This measurement supplies both a branching fraction and an asymmetry for a channel never before seen. The measured B sits significantly below most predictions (IRA/TDA ≈ 3.1×10⁻⁴, i.e. the observed value is far lower once the normalization uncertainty is accounted for), and the asymmetry sharply discriminates models — consistent with IRA/TDA/SU(3) predictions near zero but excluding current-algebra (α = 0.99) and one IRA (−0.81) result. The asymmetry consistent with zero indicates negligible S–P interference. Caveats: the dominant uncertainty is the 20% normalization branching fraction; the α measurement is statistics-limited and assumes unpolarized Ξ_b⁻ (a 5% polarization systematic is assigned). CP-violation sensitivity awaits the upgraded-LHCb dataset.

arXiv:2608.27553 — Imperfect axions with no domain wall problem (Marco Gorghetto et al.)

A new resolution of the domain-wall problem for post-inflationary axions with domain-wall number N > 1. The standard cure — a small PQ-violating operator that biases the N degenerate QCD minima — is tightly squeezed between the nEDM bound (θ_eff ≲ 10⁻¹⁰) and dark-matter overproduction from late wall decay. The authors point out that because the QCD axion mass is strongly temperature-suppressed (m_a²(T) ∝ T⁻⁸), a temperature-independent PQ-violating potential can dominate before the QCD potential switches on at T⋆. In that regime the PQ term (which has trivial wall number) forms unstable walls at H = m_PQ that destroy the string network before the stable N > 1 QCD walls ever form. Analytics plus N = 2 lattice simulations (3072³, “fat-string” system) confirm the network decays for m_PQ/m_a(T⋆) ≳ 0.32; accounting for the logarithmic string-tension enhancement extrapolated to the physical log ≈ 60 raises this critical value to ≈ 1.8.

Why it matters: this opens viable N > 1 post-inflationary parameter space (including DFSZ-motivated N = 6) requiring f_a ≲ (2/N)·5×10⁹ GeV, i.e. m_a ≳ (N/2) meV, in the 10⁻³–10⁻² eV window. Crucially, the needed PQ violation predicts an nEDM within two orders of magnitude of current limits — squarely in reach of the planned factor-100 improvement, tying the domain-wall solution to a testable signal. Caveats the authors stress: simulations reach only log ≈ 4.5–6 versus the physical ~60, forcing extrapolation of c_PQ; and the large relativistic-axion energy density radiated during scaling may obstruct wall formation entirely (App. C), which would shrink the early-decay region below the purple-dashed line. Dedicated simulations near the physical point are needed.
