Chiral lattices and Tera-Z reach

Witten arXiv:2611.05051 describes a variant of Craig’s algorithm for 4D gauge theories with chiral fermions, a variant Hsu also proposed recently. The lattice gauge field is interpolated over the continuum and coupled to continuum chiral fermions. It works in any dimension and for any anomaly-free representation, but it cannot run as Monte Carlo, because the chiral fermion path integral is not positive. Most of the rest of the day is projections and readout hardware rather than data. The physics cluster is FCC-ee reach, picking up last week’s finding that Tera-Z precision decides minimal SU(5) at three loops. The hard numbers are older or limits: ATLAS’s 8 TeV diphoton+jets measurement and Auger’s sterile-neutrino bounds.

Themes

Chiral fermions, lattice and ladder

Witten’s note pairs with Chi et al. arXiv:2610.42103. They run DMRG on a four-leg ladder realization of the 3-4-5-0 chiral model and find quantized charge pumping in all three boundary phases, including a fully gapped symmetric-mass-generation edge. Their analogy to topological leptogenesis via \(B-L\) is suggestive rather than derived.

FCC-ee as the decisive test

Greljo et al. arXiv:2611.05464 put vector-like “flavour chain” messengers at the TeV scale. They then show the same Higgs couplings shift \(Z\)/\(W\) couplings at tree level: LEP/SLD already require \(M_F/|y_F|\gtrsim3\) TeV, and FCC-ee would reach 100 TeV. Vignaroli arXiv:2611.05501 projects \(Z\)-pole reach for heavy-neutral-lepton dipoles. The reach matches HL-LHC at 1 TeV, though local UV operators can mimic all four coupling shifts. Lahiri et al. arXiv:2611.05685 target the CP phase of the 95 GeV scalar via \(\tau\tau\) asymmetries at Higgs factories.

Haloscopes, readout first

Wu et al. arXiv:2611.05444 claim a 5–10× scan-rate gain over quantum-limited homodyne detection from electro-optic photon counting at 5–12 GHz. Their optimum uses an overcoupled cavity. Wiedemann et al. arXiv:2611.06761 cover the other end with a 300–700 MHz KI-TWPA at 2.5–3.5 quanta. Both continue the cavity-side work that followed last month’s off-haloscope proposals.

What the data actually pin down

Serao et al. arXiv:2611.06129 find that combined spectroscopy plus \(g-2\) leaves formally unbounded X17 nucleon-coupling directions. That matters because yesterday’s FASER result left only the high-coupling window open. Serao also wrote yesterday’s Ge/Xe blind-direction paper, so identifiability is becoming a habit. Abbas et al. arXiv:2611.06824 show the four known Adler coefficients resolve \(c_{5,1}\) only to about ±24, and that the often-quoted 283 is a linear extrapolation.

Deep dives

arXiv:2611.05051 — A Note On Lattice Definition of Gauge Theories With Chiral Fermions (Edward Witten)

Witten defines a lattice chiral gauge theory without lattice fermions. The link variables are restricted to “admissible” fields, meaning every plaquette satisfies \(|U_P-1|<\epsilon\). Each admissible configuration is interpolated to a continuum gauge field, and continuum chiral fermions are coupled to that field. The fermion weight is \(|\det\not{D}_W^+|\exp(-i\pi\eta(\mathcal{D}_Y))\), where \(\eta\) is the APS eta-invariant on a five-manifold \(Y\) bounding spacetime. By the Dai–Freed gluing law, this weight is independent of the choice of \(Y\) exactly when the representation is free of both perturbative and global anomalies. Instanton sectors are handled with fiducial cobordism generators. Their phases are the renormalized theta-angles. Infinite-order generators give continuous thetas, and torsion generators give discrete ones.

The construction works for any compact gauge group, any anomaly-free representation, any dimension, and any compact spin manifold, not just \(T^4\). Witten justifies the general-manifold case by the requirement that chiral theories couple consistently to gravity. He notes that on \(T^4\) the cluster-decomposition shortcut fails for discrete thetas and for fractional instanton number. He also argues that numerically evaluating the continuum determinant does not reintroduce doubling.

The caveats are explicit: - Sign problem. The chiral fermion weight is not positive, so the scheme cannot be run as Monte Carlo. - Admissibility. Irrelevance of the admissibility constraint in the continuum limit rests on the asymptotic-freedom expectation, not a proof. - Novelty. Witten describes the main novelty as “possibly” a sharper treatment of the fermion path integral and its anomalies. The same variant appears in Hsu, and earlier proposals ran along similar lines.

arXiv:2611.05464 — Flavour Chains at the TeV Edge (Admir Greljo et al.)

Vector-like-fermion chains generate Yukawas as \(y_F z^N\) with \(z\simeq0.02\), and the Minimal Flavour Protection group \(SU(2)_q\times U(1)_X\) protects them. The same Higgs link generates \(\mathcal{O}_{H\ell,Hq}^{(1,3)}\) at tree level with \(C\sim|y_F|^2/M_F^2\) and no spurion suppression.

Current bounds - A six-class fit to LEP/SLD data, cross-checked with three public codes, gives \(M_F/|y_F|\gtrsim3\)–7 TeV. The indicative tuning is \(1/\Delta_{\rm total}\simeq0.3\%\). - \(B_s\to\mu\mu\) is currently stronger for the down-type classes, at about 9 TeV. Projected flavour reach stays at about 9 TeV or below.

FCC-ee projections - The reach is about 100 TeV unprofiled and about 50 TeV profiled. - The profiled tuning reaches \(\mathcal{O}(10^{-3})\) in scenario S1 and \(\mathcal{O}(10^{-4})\) in S2. - In the experiment-only scenario, the profiled bounds collapse to a nearly universal 52–57 TeV. This reflects an approximate flat direction from the \(R_f\) ratios, which better \(\Gamma_Z\) precision could lift.

Why rearranging the chain doesn’t escape. Moving the Higgs link shifts the effect between left- and right-handed currents but fixes their product. The floor becomes \(M_F\gtrsim(|y_F|\,y\,\Lambda_L\Lambda_R)^{1/2}\), which puts the \(b\) and \(\tau\) chains above 15 and 10 TeV at Tera-\(Z\). That configuration is about 100× less tuned than the minimal chain but still heavily tuned. This estimate assumes \(\Lambda_R\approx\Lambda_L\), which the authors did not compute.

Caveats - The tuning scales as \(|y_F|^4\), and \(|y_F|\sim0.2\) removes today’s tuning entirely. - The Higgs-criticality motivation needs \(|y_F|\gtrsim0.7\). With 18 Higgs-coupled states, it lowers \(h_{\rm crit}\) from \(3\times10^{11}\) GeV to 7.3 TeV at the 5 TeV benchmark. - That mechanism still needs stabilizing bosons below about 100 TeV and leaves a \(3\times10^{-3}\) residual hierarchy.

arXiv:2611.05432 — Parton wave functions, density matrices and entropy in DGLAP evolution (S. Bondarenko et al.)

Entropy from densities. The one-parton matrix built from Lipatov’s wave functions supports two distinct entropies: - Sampled-parton entropy: \(S_1\), computed from the trace-normalized matrix. - Mode entropy: \(S_{\rm mode}=\sum_\lambda g_B(n_\lambda)\), which bounds any state with the same one-body data, \(S_{\rm vN}\le S_{\rm mode}\). Equality holds only for the thermal Gaussian state.

The pure hadron has zero entropy regardless of its occupations. The Kharzeev–Levin geometric entropy is \(g_B(\bar n-1)\), the one-mode special case. Its large-\(\bar n\) form \(1+\ln\bar n\) is 47% high at \(\bar n=1.5\).

Rapidity cells. Summing isolated unit-rapidity cell entropies, rather than merging them, overcounts by \(\Delta_Y=\tfrac12\omega Y^2\). At \(\omega=0.4\) this is 11.6 at \(Y=8\), a factor of 3.8. The gap depends on the chosen resolution.

Evolution. Averaging over unrecorded emission histories, assumed incoherent across scales, gives a Lindblad equation in \(\ln Q^2\) with \(\hat\Sigma=\hat{\mathcal R}^\dagger\hat{\mathcal R}/2\). Its occupations reproduce LO DGLAP, including virtual terms, at leading-log accuracy. This holds for strong \(k_\perp\) ordering in light-cone gauge with dilute occupations.

Caveats - The thermal-Gaussian closure supplies Bose/Pauli factors, but its own error estimate is unfavourable. Its initial rate derivative deviates from the exact one by a relative \(4n\) and has the wrong sign for \(1/6<n<1/2\). - The Lamb-shift-like term is set to zero by assumption. - No recombination is derived. - The proposed HERA/LHC test compares mode numbers inferred from hadron factorial moments with transverse mode counts. The extracted text cuts off before the test is specified.
