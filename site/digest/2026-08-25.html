Deep dives

arXiv:2608.23009 — Search for the lepton-flavor-violating decay τ→μγ at Belle II (Belle II Collaboration et al.)

Belle II sets \(\mathcal{B}(\tau^\pm\to\mu^\pm\gamma)<9.5\,(12.2)\times10^{-8}\) at 90% (95%) CL on 428 fb⁻¹ (393×10⁶ \(\tau\tau\) pairs), from an unbinned 2D extended maximum-likelihood fit in \(M_{\rm bc}\) and \(\Delta E/\sqrt{s}\) after a GBDT selection (final signal efficiency 5.97%, τ⁺τ⁻ rejection 99.8%). Eight events are observed in the signal region against a sideband-extrapolated background of \(5.7\pm1.2\); the fitted branching fraction \((2.8^{+4.4}_{-2.6})\times10^{-8}\) is consistent with zero. The observed limit is slightly weaker than the expected \(7.6\,(9.2)\times10^{-8}\), attributed to a statistical upward fluctuation. Systematics are dominated by the 5.7% GBDT data/MC efficiency uncertainty (total 6.0% on the efficiency×luminosity×cross-section product); the dominant physical background is \(\tau\to\mu\nu\bar\nu\) plus an ISR/beam photon.

Why it matters: this is the flagship charged-LFV channel, where SM rates are \(\sim10^{-54}\) and many BSM scenarios (SUSY, seesaw, Littlest Higgs) predict \(10^{-10}\)–\(10^{-8}\). The result does not yet beat Belle’s \(4.2\times10^{-8}\) (988 fb⁻¹) or BaBar’s \(4.4\times10^{-8}\), but on less than half the luminosity it achieves 41% higher signal efficiency and 81% lower background than the Belle analysis, and introduces a unified likelihood with profiled nuisance parameters — establishing the machinery for the far larger datasets to come. The honest caveat is scale: 428 fb⁻¹ is a fraction of Belle II’s design target, so this is a foundation-laying benchmark rather than a world-best number.

arXiv:2608.22587 — Calculation of neutron electric dipole moment from Lattice QCD (Thomas Blum et al.)

Blum et al. extract the \(\bar\theta\)-induced nEDM via a new Feynman–Hellmann route: rather than the noisy forward limit of the \(F_3\) form factor, they compute the matrix element of the local (single-time-slice) topological charge density between nucleon ground states spatially polarized by a uniform background electric field. Because the dipole interaction is anti-Hermitian, these states have mixed parity and are constructed variationally through a non-Hermitian GEVP over a four-operator basis. On three 2+1-flavor domain-wall ensembles (\(a\approx0.11\) fm, \(m_\pi=340,420,576\) MeV, electro-quenched), they obtain a statistically significant signal and extrapolate to \(d_n=-0.0050(4)(8)\,\bar\theta\) e·fm. Comparison with the experimental bound gives \(|\bar\theta|\lesssim10^{-11}\), confirming the strong-CP problem.

Why it matters: previous \(F_3\)-based calculations at the physical point (Alexandrou et al., Bhattacharya et al.) yield no significant signal because the global topological-charge summation is too noisy; truncated-sum methods (Dragos et al., Liang et al.) introduce their own systematics. Sampling the topological charge locally sidesteps both global summation and the \(Q^2\to0\) extrapolation, and the authors show the conventional positive-parity projection suffers large excited-state contamination that the GEVP removes — plausibly explaining a real discrepancy with the lighter-mass points of Liang et al. The method extends directly to Weinberg three-gluon and four-quark CP-odd operators. Caveats are explicit: single lattice spacing, electro-quenched approximation, and a physical-point extrapolation anchored at \(m_\pi=340\) MeV — finite-volume and discretization effects remain to be quantified.

arXiv:2608.21510 — Electroweak and Single Top-Quark Conspiracy in Current LHC Data (Christoph Englert et al.)

Englert, Kogler, and Spannowsky ask whether recent above-SM central values in \(tWZ\) (\(\mu=1.77\pm0.32\)) and \(tq\gamma\) (\(1.34^{+0.19}_{-0.17}\)), plus a large ATLAS \(tHq\) (\(7.2^{+4.6}_{-4.0}\)) alongside below-SM \(t\bar tH\) (\(0.59^{+0.22}_{-0.20}\)), form a coherent SMEFT pattern while VBS/triboson channels stay SM-like. A linearized (\(\mathcal{O}(\Lambda^{-2})\)) five-coefficient Warsaw-basis fit to eight LHC rates plus five \(Z\)-pole observables gives \(\chi^2_{\min}=11.2\) (8 d.o.f., \(p=0.19\)), improving on the SM by \(\Delta\chi^2=13.4\) (local \(\sim2.1\sigma\)). The largest pulls are a triple-gauge \(C_W\) (\(\sim1.9\sigma\)) and the weak top dipole \(C_{tW}\) (\(\sim1.7\sigma\)), with positive \(C_{Hq}^{(3)}\); \(C_{HW}\) is poorly constrained. Fixing \(C_W=0\) still fits (\(\chi^2=14.9\), 9 d.o.f.). Notably, the fit absorbs the \(A_{\rm FB}^{0,b}\) anomaly — but only by shifting \(A_e\) ~4σ below LEP/SLD, a subset artifact flagged by the authors.

Why it matters: this is the EFT-as-diagnostic thread — using SMEFT not for limits but to test UV consistency. Under weakly-coupled renormalizable matching, no single scalar, vector, or vector-like-quark multiplet reproduces the pattern: currents need a charge-2/3 singlet, the dipole’s opposite chirality needs a hypercharge-1/6 doublet, and positive \(C_{Hq}^{(3)}\) demands triplet custodial partners protecting \(Zb_L\bar b_L\). A benchmark multi-threshold VLQ sector (1.6–2.0 TeV) reproduces the current alignment with \(\kappa_t\simeq0.91\), but the fit-preferred dipole must come from unspecified loop dynamics. Caveats: linearized truncation with no EFT uncertainty, no experimental covariances, and a preselected measurement set — the “\(2\sigma\)” is explicitly local and not a global SM improvement.
