Three atmospheric detectors, one ordering

Jin et al. arXiv:2610.12208 fit public Super-K (pre-Gd), IceCube-DeepCore and six-unit KM3NeT/ORCA data jointly, with shared systematics correlated. They find \(\Delta\chi^2=11.0\) for normal ordering, and pseudo-experiments calibrate that to inverted ordering excluded at 3.3σ. Nine days ago JUNO reached 2.1σ only by leaning on long-baseline \(\Delta m^2_{31}\), so this is an independent route. It is a combination of public samples, though, not a collaboration result. Elsewhere, ATLAS reports s-channel single top at 5.1σ, phase transitions get firmer perturbative control, and the X(2370) glueball candidate faces a reanalysis that does not establish it.

Charged LFV, ahead of Mu3e and conversion

This is new as a cluster. Di Lecce et al. arXiv:2610.10718 show that an accidental \(U(3)\times\)CP in the composite sector aligns dipoles with Yukawas. That brings compositeness from ~100 TeV down to a few TeV, with \(\mu\to3e\) and \(\mu N\to eN\) as the probes. Ali et al. arXiv:2610.12373 find that \(\mu\to e\) limits on τ-sector operator pairs beat direct τ bounds by 10–100s. That holds only if dimension-6 running and dimension-8 mixing are both kept. Deep dives

arXiv:2610.12208 — Evidence for Normal Neutrino Ordering with Combined Atmospheric Neutrino Experiments (Miaochen Jin et al.)

Inverted ordering is excluded at 3.3σ using atmospheric data alone. Only 2 of 4397 IO pseudoexperiments exceed the observed \(\chi^2_{\rm IO}-\chi^2_{\rm NO}=11.0\). The Clopper–Pearson 68% interval on that tail fraction gives \(Z\in[3.08,3.60]\sigma\), and the more conservative \(CL_s\) gives 2.55σ. The ingredients are: - Super-K I–V (pure water), with an independent high-statistics GENIE Monte Carlo and a bug-fixed SK release confirmed by the collaboration. - IceCube-DeepCore, 8 years. - ORCA6.

Flux and cross-section nuisances (39 of 143) are shared across experiments. The individual preferences (\(\Delta\chi^2=6.2\), 1.6, 0.5) add coherently via mantle/core MSW and parametric resonances at 2–10 GeV. Relative to a trivial \(\chi^2\) sum, the correlated treatment tightens \(\Delta m^2_{31}\) by 19% and \(\sin^2\theta_{23}\) by 9%. Results: \(\Delta m^2_{31}=2.44^{+0.06}_{-0.04}\times10^{-3}\) eV² (2.2%), \(\sin^2\theta_{23}=0.52\) with \(\Delta\chi^2=0.3\) between octants, and \(\delta_{CP}=1.30^{+0.24}_{-0.28}\pi\) (≈0.3π disfavoured at >2.7σ). The atmospheric contours sit closer to T2K than to NOvA. The authors estimate that adding JUNO’s \(\Delta m^2_{31}\) gives \(\Delta\chi^2\simeq16\), near 4σ, but that figure still needs joint pseudoexperiments.

Caveats. - The overall goodness of fit is \(p=0.011\), driven by ORCA6; it rises to 0.12 once finite Monte Carlo statistics are included. - The parameter goodness-of-fit test shows 2.2σ inter-experiment tension. IceCube and ORCA6 worsen by 13.7 and 9.6 in \(\chi^2\) at the combined best fit. - The shared CCQE normalization is pulled by −2.5σ. - \(\theta_{13}\) is fixed, and the ORCA energy-scale nuisance is absent. - The \(\delta_{CP}\) constraint draws more on data residuals than on the Asimov expectation. - This is a combination of public data, not a collaboration result.

arXiv:2610.11937 — Reassessing the X(2370) in \(J/\psi\to\gamma K_S^0K_S^0\pi^0\) (Xiang-Kun Dong et al.)

A coherent coupled-channel fit does not require an X(2370) in \(K_S^0K_S^0\pi^0\). The fit uses a 12-channel K-matrix with dispersive loops, applied to digitized BESIII data: - two-dimensional mass maps (6264 \(KK\) bins and 6006 \(K\pi\) bins); - the parent-mass spectrum.

Adding an eighth bare state near 2.37 GeV lowers the deviance by only 1.26% (7926.4→7826.6, with 16 extra parameters), and the projected line shapes are nearly identical. The extra pole sits at \(2424-i\,149\) MeV, twice as broad as the PDG reference \(2377-i\,74\). Its identification with X(2370) is unclear.

The physics consequence is for the flavor-singlet argument. If the true X(2370)→\(K\bar K\pi\) yield is smaller than the 1D fits imply, the denominator of BESIII’s \(\mathcal R<0.081\) shrinks. The small \(K^*\bar K\) yield would then no longer demonstrate selective suppression, which weakens the glueball and \(\eta_c\)-analogy readings without excluding them.

Caveats, stated by the authors. - Bin contents are reconstructed from a colour palette, with no inter-distribution correlations, efficiency, or resolution. - No significance or pole uncertainties are quoted. - Only mesonic channels are modelled, so the \(\Sigma\bar\Sigma\) interpretation is not tested. - \(K_0^*(1430)\) parameters were tuned within their uncertainties. - The \(J/\psi\to K^{*0}\bar K^{*0}\) background is not modelled.

arXiv:2610.11465 — Observation of s-channel single top-quark production at 13 TeV with ATLAS (ATLAS Collaboration)

(Abstract only.) ATLAS reports the first single-experiment observation of s-channel single top, at 5.1σ observed (5.2σ expected). The dataset is 140 fb⁻¹, with events selected for exactly one lepton, missing \(E_T\), and exactly two b-jets. A GNN separates the signal from \(t\bar t\), W+jets and t-channel backgrounds, and a likelihood fit to its output gives \(\sigma_{t\bar b+\bar tb}=10.8\pm1.5\,(\text{stat})\pm1.6\,(\text{syst})\) pb, consistent with the SM. The comparable statistical and systematic errors suggest further gains will need systematic reductions as well as more data. The abstract does not quote the SM prediction or a \(|V_{tb}|\) interpretation.

Citation radar

The radar this week is dominated by one hook: the single LZ nuclear recoil near 248 keV. That one event is generating its own literature, which tells you the community treats a 2.6σ, background-free curiosity as worth building models around. The papers collecting citations are the ones that give everyone a concrete scenario to point at.

arXiv:2609.07811 — Higgsino Dark Matter Interpretation of the LZ High-Recoil Event in the GNMSSM with TeV-Scale Gauginos (Subhadip Bisal et al.)

Bisal, Cao and Li interpret the LZ event as endothermic, Z-mediated Higgsino up-scattering, χ̃₁⁰N→χ̃₂⁰N. In the MSSM, a ~350 keV splitting needs |M₁,₂|~10⁶–10⁷ GeV or a Bino–Wino cancellation tuned to ~10⁻⁴. In the GNMSSM, Higgsino–Singlino mixing adds an independent contribution ∝λ²v²/(dμ_tot) that cancels the gaugino term.

The paper presents six benchmarks from the authors’ earlier scan, with these properties: - m_χ ≈ 660–1110 GeV and δ = 333–350 keV. - |M₁,₂| = 2–5 TeV, with both signs of M₁M₂ represented. - Ωh² within a few percent of the measured value. - Δχ² < 1 under the Standard Halo Model in every case, best 0.03.

The authors are candid on two limits. The tuning is relocated, not removed: Δ_FT exceeds 5×10³ at every point. The IceCube bound of δ ≳ 566 keV remains open, and the provided text cuts off before their singlet-resonance evasion is worked out.

The paper has drawn 49 citations since its 7 September posting. That likely reflects the LZ-event wave rather than a decisive result; the introduction alone cites ~40 interpretation papers. Its value as a citation is practical: explicit benchmarks with neutralino masses quoted to six significant digits make it the convenient reference for “the singlet-extended Higgsino option.”
