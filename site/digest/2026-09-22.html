Dark QCD claims the recoil

The LZ event is back at roughly a tenth of the listings — seven interpretation papers — but the register has shifted again: where September ran from fits to bounds to complementarity, today’s crop converges on composite readings, with confining dark sectors doing the work of the inelastic splitting. The day’s firmest single results sit elsewhere, though. Lin et al. arXiv:2609.22698 deliver a first-principles lattice value for the QCD contribution to the axion-photon coupling, resolving a standing ChPT discrepancy, and ATLAS arXiv:2609.24950 posts a null top-philic \(Z'\) search that speaks directly to the year’s toponium-vs-scalar question. Underneath, the precision-QCD multiloop program from last week keeps rolling.

Themes

The LZ recoil, now a confining-sector story

The model zoo has a new organizing principle. Sannino et al. arXiv:2609.24988 read the 248 keV recoil as composite inelastic DM — a dimension-ten operator splitting the lightest baryon of an \(SU(3)_{\rm D}\) theory, with a leptophobic baryon-number vector as mediator — while Sheng et al. arXiv:2609.23477 recast the inelasticity as a hyperfine transition within a single dark hadron in PQ-augmented dark QCD. Alongside: KK dark-photon mediation (Ahmed et al. arXiv:2609.22739), a SUSY-\(SU(6)\) pseudo-Dirac Higgsino arXiv:2609.23691, scalar-portal quark-Yukawa deviations (De arXiv:2609.23096), and cosmic-ray boosting (Chauhan et al. arXiv:2609.24982). The useful counterweight is Zurowski et al. arXiv:2609.24764, quantifying how shell-model form-factor uncertainties propagate into Si/Ge exclusion limits — the nuclear-structure caveat under all of this.

Precision QCD: form factors and DIS moments

The multiloop center of gravity from last week continues. Blümlein et al. arXiv:2609.24279 lay out the holonomic/large-moment machinery behind their gluonic three-loop massive form factors (companion to yesterday’s arXiv:2609.22034), Kidonakis arXiv:2609.23247 pushes the massive cusp anomalous dimension to four loops, and Pozzoli arXiv:2609.24910 reports the first leading-colour two-loop \(t\bar tW\) amplitude. On the DIS side, Klann et al. arXiv:2609.23452 compute NLO heavy-quark coefficient Mellin moments and flag up to 20% errors in the standard quark-channel parametrisations.

Deep dives

arXiv:2609.22698 — First-Principles Determination of the QCD Contribution to the Axion-Photon Coupling Using Domain-Wall Fermions (Tian Lin et al.)

Using two near-physical \(N_f=2+1\) domain-wall ensembles (24D and 32Dfine, both \(M_\pi\approx143\) MeV), Lin et al. compute \(\mathcal{C}_{\rm QCD}\) — the model-independent QCD piece of \(g_{a\gamma\gamma}\) — via anomalous Ward identities rather than direct topological-charge insertion. The trick is to split \(\mathcal{C}_{\rm QCD}\) into an exact anomaly term (\(5/3-\xi\), fixed by charge assignments and \(z=m_u/m_d\)) plus a light-quark-mass-suppressed correction extracted from the isoscalar \(P^0\)-VV three-point function, itself only a few percent of the total. Continuum extrapolation gives \(\mathcal{C}_{\rm QCD}^{\rm IS}=1.619(30)\), \(\mathcal{C}_{\rm QCD}^{\rm IB}=0.347(22)\), and \(\mathcal{C}_{\rm QCD}=1.965(35)\), with the IB shift dominated by the explicit \(-\xi\) term and a tiny pion-pole correction (\(-0.0059\), 0.3%).

Why it matters: this is an independent lattice benchmark for the quantity entering every axion-photon search, in a regime where \(E/N\) and \(\mathcal{C}_{\rm QCD}\) partially cancel. Crucially, the authors resolve the standing NLO ChPT spread (1.63–2.05): the SU(3) result had a sign error (since corrected by its authors), and the U(3) IB discrepancy traces to an effective \(m_u/m_d\) ~15% too large. With common inputs, ChPT and lattice reconcile. Caveat: chiral fermions keep discretization mild, but only two lattice spacings enter the continuum limit; the extrapolated correction carries ~60% statistical uncertainty, and systematics are explicitly “not yet complete.” Their IS value sits \(2.2\sigma\) from the competing gluonic background-field determination (\(\mathcal{C}_{\rm QCD}^{\rm IS}=1.47(6)\)), underscoring the value of independent systematics.

arXiv:2609.24950 — Search for a top-philic heavy resonance in association with top quarks (ATLAS Collaboration)

ATLAS searches for a colour-singlet top-philic \(Z'\) decaying to \(t\bar t\) and produced via \(t\bar t Z'\) or \(tZ'\), using same-sign-dilepton and \(\geq3\)-lepton final states in 140 fb\(^{-1}\) (13 TeV) plus 56 fb\(^{-1}\) (13.6 TeV). A mass-parameterised transformer classifier (Salt framework), fed event- and object-level features including the \(Z'\) mass hypothesis, discriminates signal from the dominant \(t\bar t t\bar t\) and \(t\bar t W/Z/H\) backgrounds; \(t\bar t W\) and non-prompt backgrounds are constrained in dedicated control regions. No excess is seen (largest local significance \(2.4\sigma\) at \(m_{Z'}=400\) GeV, \(c_t=3\)). For \(c_t=1\), \(\theta=0\), masses below 1150 GeV are excluded (1250 expected); for \(c_t=3\), below 2000 GeV (\(\theta=0\)). Cross-section limits improve ~15× over the previous ATLAS single-lepton \(t\bar t Z'\) search, which could not exclude \(c_t=1\) at any mass.

Why it matters: this is the vector-resonance handle the toponium-vs-scalar debate around the low-mass \(t\bar t\) excess has lacked — a direct top-philic \(Z'\) constraint, now reaching \(c_t=1\). The \((c_t^2,\theta)\) scans exploit the interplay of three-top (\(tZ'\), \(\propto\cos^2\theta\)) and four-top (\(t\bar t Z'\)) modes. Caveats: the result is statistics-dominated, with leading systematics from \(t\bar t t\bar t\) modelling; the SM four-top cross-section itself sits high versus theory (\(22.5^{+6.6}_{-5.5}\) vs \(12.0\pm2.4\) fb), and an alternative +70% \(t\bar t t\bar t\) fit leaves the limit unchanged only through competing normalisation/uncertainty effects.

arXiv:2609.24988 — Interpreting the LZ 248 keV Event using Dark QCD (Francesco Sannino et al.)

Sannino and Turner read LZ’s \(248\pm23_{\rm stat}\pm23_{\rm sys}\) keV recoil candidate as composite inelastic dark matter. A confining \(SU(3)_{\rm D}\) theory with SM-singlet dark quarks produces a Dirac baryon \(B\sim UUD\); a dimension-ten, dark-number-violating operator involving a \(U(1)_B\)-breaking scalar \(S\) splits it into two Majorana states \(\chi_{1,2}\) with \(\delta=m_2-m_1\). Endothermic \(\chi_1 N\to\chi_2 N\) scattering, mediated by a leptophobic baryon-number vector \(V\), suppresses low-energy recoils. Their benchmark — \(m_1=1\) TeV, \(\delta=300\) keV, \(M_V=1.44\) TeV, \(g_B=0.146\) (\(M_V/g_B\simeq9.9\) TeV) — gives \(\sigma_p^0=6.5\times10^{-43}\) cm², inside LZ’s two-sided 90% interval, yielding ~0.26 accepted events in 2.84 tonne-years. A 300 keV splitting maps to a matching scale \(\Lambda_*\simeq12\) TeV for order-one coefficients. The construction embeds anomaly-cancelling spectators, a stabilising \(\mathbb{Z}_{18}\) (forbidding proton decay), and pion/pseudoscalar decay channels.

Why it matters: it is today’s cleanest confining-sector reading of the LZ event, tying the inelastic splitting to explicit dark-QCD dynamics rather than a bolted-on mass gap, and it survives solar-neutrino (\(\sigma_p^0<9.9\times10^{-43}\) cm² for \(b\bar b\)) and ATLAS dijet (\(g_q^{95}\simeq0.084>0.05\)) bounds. Caveats are frank: the benchmark probes the high-speed halo tail near threshold (\(E_R^\star=267\) keV), so rates depend sensitively on the assumed velocity distribution and nuclear response near a diffraction minimum; the event is only \(2.6\sigma\) post-look-elsewhere; the excited fraction must be \(f_2<10^{-3}\); and relic abundance is treated as input, not derived.
