New instruments for old dark-matter questions

The day’s weight sits on detection methodology, not results — two leading limits arrive from unconventional apparatus. POLONAISE (arXiv:2608.20464) runs the first ultraheavy-DM search with a magnetically levitated milligram ferromagnet, pushing seven orders of magnitude in mass past optical levitation; and a HFIR cold-neutron-beam search (arXiv:2608.21120) improves the \(n\to n'\) transformation limit by an order of magnitude — the same mirror-neutron channel that surfaced on 08-13. Flavor stayed quiet and consistent: LHCb’s first \(\Lambda_b^0\to\Lambda(1520)\mu\mu\) angular analysis (arXiv:2608.21082) lands on the SM, and Pich et al. reaffirm the data-driven approach can’t reach the observed charm CPV. A tooling-heavy day rounds it out.

Themes

Dark matter: new detection handles

POLONAISE arXiv:2608.20464 resolves TeV-scale impulses with a levitated ferromagnet, setting leading composite-DM cross-section limits across \(10^6\)–\(10^{15}\) GeV. On the wave-like end, Moroi’s pedagogical review arXiv:2608.20745 lays out qubit-based DM sensing, and Liang et al. arXiv:2608.20715 finally compute the transverse dielectric response for light-DM–electron scattering in silicon — 4–6 orders below the longitudinal, giving the first rigorous justification for the standard approximation. With the HFIR \(n\to n'\) result arXiv:2608.21120, four distinct hardware routes into hidden sectors in one day.

Flavor nulls hold

LHCb’s \(\Lambda_b^0\to\Lambda(1520)\mu\mu\) angular analysis arXiv:2608.21082 — \(A_{\rm FB}\) and \(S_{1cc}\) over 0.1–12.5 GeV² — agrees with the SM, a baryonic complement to the mesonic \(b\to s\ell\ell\) picture that has been consolidating rather than tensioning since the mid-month \(C_9\) flare. Pich et al. arXiv:2608.21212 tighten the two-channel rescattering treatment of charm CPV with a single well-determined strong-scattering input and conclude the Standard Model still falls short — the anomaly stays unexplained, not resolved.

Tooling: GPU, quantum simulation, RL

Bothmann et al. arXiv:2608.21338 present the first end-to-end GPU-resident event generator with normalizing-flow proposals feeding Pepper, turning billion-event high-multiplicity samples from multi-week to sub-day jobs. Davoudi et al. arXiv:2608.21258 strip a factor of ~\(10^{14}\) from product-formula QCD quantum-simulation cost estimates — a reminder the field’s headline gate counts are still algorithm-limited. And Jha et al.’s CDRL arXiv:2608.20686 uses symbolic certificates to steer reinforcement learning through a \(>10^{26}\) neutrino-flavor-model space, extracting reusable rules along the way.

Precision for lepton colliders

Frixione et al. arXiv:2608.21181 compute the electron PDFs at NNLO in QED, and show the \(\Delta\) scheme kills the \(\overline{\rm MS}\) soft-log enhancement — arguing NNLO is needed for the \(10^{-4}\) precision targets of hundred-GeV lepton colliders. This feeds directly into the season’s running FCC-ee precision thread.

Deep dives

arXiv:2608.20464 — First Search for Ultraheavy Dark Matter Using a Magnetically Levitated Particle (Dennis G. Uitenbroek et al.)

(abstract only) POLONAISE uses a milligram-scale ferromagnet levitated in a superconducting trap as an impulse sensor, reaching a force sensitivity of 0.07 fN Hz⁻¹ᐟ² and resolving momentum kicks as small as 1 TeV/c. Treating every candidate impulse as a possible DM scatter, the collaboration sets optimum-interval upper limits on the neutron coupling for DM interacting via a light mediator: for masses 10⁶–10¹⁵ GeV/c² and mediators below 30 meV/c², they exclude couplings down to α_n = 3.2×10⁻⁹ at 95% CL, giving leading constraints on the DM–neutron cross section for composite DM.

The headline is the apparatus, not a signal. Magnetic levitation extends the mass reach of levitated-sensor DM searches seven orders of magnitude beyond optical levitation, opening the ultraheavy frontier where composite/dark-nugget candidates deposit resolvable single-scatter impulses. This is the mechanical-sensor counterpart to the day’s other hidden-sector hardware routes (qubit sensing, the HFIR mirror-neutron beam). With only the abstract available, the exposure, background model, and impulse-candidate treatment can’t be assessed here — but the quoted sensitivity and mass span are the concrete takeaways.

arXiv:2608.21082 — Angular analysis of the decay Λ_b⁰ → Λ(1520)μ⁺μ⁻ (LHCb collaboration et al.)

First angular analysis of this baryonic b→sℓℓ mode, using LHCb’s full Run 1+2 9 fb⁻¹ sample. The leptonic forward-backward asymmetry A^ℓ_FB,3/2 and the CP-averaged S_1cc are extracted in four narrow q² bins (0.1–12.5 GeV²/c⁴) plus the wide [1.1,6.0] bin, with the Λ(1520) reconstructed in pK⁻ and spin-1/2 Λ* contributions plus their interferences modelled explicitly. All measured values agree with SM predictions built on lattice-QCD and dispersive form factors — e.g. A^ℓ_FB,3/2 = 0.16±0.15±0.06 and S_1cc = 0.19±0.30±0.18 in [1.1,6.0]. The single mild outlier is S_1cc = 1.35±0.54±0.17 in [6.0,8.0], but statistics dominate throughout.

This is the baryonic complement to the mesonic b→sℓℓ program, probing the same FCNC transition with different hadronic structure and spin content — a useful cross-check as the mesonic picture has consolidated rather than sharpened since the mid-month C₉ discussion. Caveats are candid: the measurement is statistics-limited, the fit sits near physical boundaries (biases up to ~18% of the statistical error, corrected via pseudoexperiments), and systematics are dominated by combinatorial and Λ_c⁺-feeddown backgrounds. Discriminating among form-factor predictions awaits larger samples.

arXiv:2608.21120 — New Limits on n→n′ Transformation from HFIR Cold Neutron Beam (James M. Rogers et al.)

A neutron-regeneration search at HFIR’s CG-2/GP-SANS cold beam pushes the mirror-neutron transformation limit an order of magnitude below the prior SNS results. A 3.5 mm cadmium wafer at the center of a 6.6 T split-pair magnet absorbs all beam neutrons (to <10⁻²⁰); any n→n′ conversion in the first magnet half lets sterile n′ pass the cadmium and regenerate to detectable neutrons in the second half, caught by the low-background ³He detector 20 m downstream. No signal appears above background: 95% CL regeneration-probability limits reach ~1.25×10⁻¹¹ per neutron (B=0) up to a few×10⁻¹¹ at nonzero field, translating to an order-of-magnitude improvement in 2θ₀² across Δm = 0.1–1000 neV.

The gains come from HFIR’s ~400× higher average intensity, colder/wider spectrum, and the facility ³He detector. This is the same mirror-neutron channel flagged on 08-13, and one of four hidden-sector hardware routes in the day’s digest. Honest caveats: the 2021 run (~40 h) suffered detector activation of ⁵⁵Mn→⁵⁶Mn, raising and time-varying the background, so it carries reduced weight; the 2024 run (~90 h) is cleaner but background still crept up ~8% over the run. Beam-intensity normalization carries ~4–6% systematics reconciled across multiple monitors. Further reach demands higher intensity, longer beam time, and hard background reduction.
