A muon limit amid flavor numerology

The cleanest standalone result is a number: the Fermilab g-2 collaboration sets a new direct muon EDM limit, \(|d_\mu|<1.1\times10^{-19}\,e\,\)cm (arXiv:2608.11124), consistent with zero. Around it the day’s bulk sits in two familiar places — neutrino/flavor model-building (a second Barger commensurate-mixing paper in as many days, plus two seesaw constructions) and QCD matter (a first Bayesian extraction of baryon susceptibilities, small-system scaling, the ultra-central flow puzzle). The collider-QI thread the memory has tracked all season reappears in a concrete tool for tau polarimetry, and a small cluster of AQN/warp-drive papers supplies the exotica.

Themes

Flavor structure, from seesaw to numerology

Barger arXiv:2608.10312 follows up his \(\pi/12\) model (08-11) with a broader claim: quark and lepton mixing both collapse onto powers of a single parameter \(\varepsilon=|V_{ub}|^{3/10}\) and a \(\pi/24\)-quantized “digital clock” unitarity triangle, again pinning \(\delta_{\rm CP}\simeq296^\circ\) (or \(244^\circ\)) for DUNE/Hyper-K to test. He is explicit that this is parametrization-level, no dynamics. On the seesaw side, Han et al. arXiv:2608.10062 build texture-zero structures where arbitrarily large lepton-number violation coexists with sub-eV masses, predicting accessible HNLs, while Akita et al. arXiv:2608.10123 show a lepton-asymmetry mechanism that lets HNLs evade the BBN bound and enter SHiP’s reach.

QCD matter and small systems

Pihan et al. arXiv:2608.10238 report the first Bayesian extraction of baryon-number susceptibilities from RHIC BES proton cumulants, with \(\chi_2^B\) tracking lattice up to \(\mu_B\sim300\) MeV and a hint of enhancement beyond — notably, the nonmonotonic \(\hat C_3/\hat C_1\) needs no irreducible multi-baryon correlations. Alongside, EPOS4 scaling from Pb to O (arXiv:2608.10578), ALICE’s ultra-central \(v_3/v_2\) puzzle arXiv:2608.11199, and vorticity effects on HRG yields arXiv:2608.10561 keep the small-system/transport program that ran through last week’s OO results ticking.

Collider precision and quantum information

Beyond the muon EDM, CMS posts the first dedicated four-top resonance search in the dilepton channel arXiv:2608.10148 (SM-consistent, vector mediators excluded to 850 GeV). Haisch et al. arXiv:2608.10064 push SMEFT electroweak precision observables to NLL with the full 210-coefficient dependence — the reference basis for future \(e^+e^-\) global fits, continuing the SMEFT-precision thread (Celada’s FCC-ee accounting, 08-07). And Winterbottom et al.’s TauPolaris arXiv:2608.10961 uses conditional normalizing flows to reconstruct tau polarimetric vectors, distinguishing entanglement in \(H\to\tau\tau\) at \(4.3\sigma\) and improving CP sensitivity 18% — the collider-QI program moving from top tomography into a deployable analysis tool.

Deep dives

arXiv:2608.11124 — An improved direct limit on the muon electric dipole moment (2 Collaboration et al.)

The Fermilab Muon g−2 collaboration sets \(d_{\mu^+}=(-0.35\pm0.19_{\rm stat}\pm0.34_{\rm sys})\times10^{-19}\,e\,\)cm from its 2019–20 straw-tracker data (\(2.34\times10^9\) positrons across Run-2, Run-3a, Run-3b), yielding \(|d_\mu|<1.10\times10^{-19}\,e\,\)cm at 95% CL — a factor 1.5 improvement on the BNL E821 limit. The measurement uses the traceback method: a muon EDM tilts the spin-precession plane, producing an oscillation in the average vertical decay angle \(\langle\theta_y\rangle\) that is \(\pi/2\) out of phase with the anomalous precession. Data are fit in 8 momentum bins × 2 stations × 3 runs (48 measurements, average reduced \(\chi^2=1.08\)), combined via BLUE. Combining with BNL tightens the bound to \(|d_\mu|<0.98(0.86)\times10^{-19}\,e\,\)cm at 95(90)% CL.

Why it matters: the result stays ~19 orders of magnitude above the SM prediction (\(\sim10^{-38}\,e\,\)cm), so it functions as a clean BSM null test on a CP-violating source independent of the anomalous-moment program, complementing the model-dependent indirect bound (\(1.9\times10^{-20}\)).

Caveat, honestly flagged: this is systematics-limited, dominated (by far) by the vertical-alignment false-signal correction (\(3.12\times10^{-20}\), vs. \(1.87\) statistical). Notably, the analysis failed the initial soft-unblinding \(3\sigma\) check, triggering the internal review that uncovered the vertical-misalignment and longitudinal-field systematics; a data-driven correction was then developed while still blinded. The alignment uncertainty is itself statistics-limited and should shrink with Runs 4–6 (2.5× the present dataset).

arXiv:2608.10961 — TauPolaris: reconstructing tau lepton polarimetric vectors with conditional normalizing flows (Daniel Winterbottom et al.)

TauPolaris estimates the undetected neutrino momenta in \(\tau\) decays with a conditional normalizing flow (nine spline-coupling blocks, transformer conditioning network), modeling the full joint density \(p(\bm\nu\mid\mathbf{c})\) rather than a point regression. This preserves the correlations between momentum components — crucially reconstructing the \(\tau\) mass at the physical 1.78 GeV, where an MSE-trained transformer biases low and pushes the neutrino collinear with the visible \(\tau\). A gradient-ascent MAP estimate in the flow’s latent space gives per-event neutrino configurations with ~40% better angular resolution than the transformer and ~20% better than sampling. On simulated LHC \(pp\) collisions with Delphes+CMS-HPS detector modeling, three applications are demonstrated: distinguishing quantum entanglement from a classically-correlated null in \(H\to\tau\tau\) at \(>4.3\sigma\) (and Bell witness \(m_{12}\) at \(4.0\sigma\)) with 3 ab⁻¹; improving CP-violation sensitivity via \(\phi_{CP}\) by 18% overall (up to 88% in \(3\pi^\pm0\pi^0\)–\(3\pi^\pm0\pi^0\), and enabling \(3\pi^\pm1\pi^0\) reconstruction for the first time); and new polarimetric variables to suppress \(Z\to\tau\tau\).

Why it matters: this moves the collider-QI program from top-quark tomography toward a deployable analysis tool, extending the optimal polarimetric-vector method to every \(\tau\) decay topology (previously restricted to 3-prong modes with secondary vertices).

Caveats: entirely simulation-based, with hand-added track smearing to patch Delphes; the entanglement \(4.3\sigma\) is an upper limit set by finite pseudoexperiments, and yields are extrapolated from a single BDT window assuming preserved S/B. The \(\mathcal{C}\)/\(m_{12}\) estimators are biased on finite data, requiring pseudoexperiment calibration.

arXiv:2608.10064 — Electroweak precision observables at NLL order in the SMEFT (Ulrich A. Haisch et al.)

Haisch and Stefanek upgrade SMEFT predictions for electroweak precision observables to next-to-leading-logarithmic accuracy by combining one-loop EW matching with two- and one-loop RG evolution from \(\Lambda\) to \(m_Z\), leveraging the newly available two-loop SMEFT beta functions. A key methodological point, proven via the \(T\)-parameter contribution of the right-handed four-top operator \(Q_{uu}^{3333}\): two-loop matching is not needed for NLL — higher-order matching cannot generate \(\ln(\Lambda/m_Z)\) logarithms, so one-loop matching plus NLL running suffices. The headline deliverable is numerical NLL expressions encoding the full dependence on all 210 independent Wilson coefficients (excluding only \(Q_H\), which enters at NNLL), released as ancillary files, with explicit results tabulated for five benchmark operators.

Why it matters: whereas tree-level matching reaches only 23 operators, NLL running opens EWPO sensitivity to the entire operator set — including operators contributing only at two-loop order (\(Q_G\), \(Q_{HG}\), \(Q_{uH}^{33}\)), which have no one-loop EWPO effect at all. For the top chromomagnetic dipole \(Q_{uG}^{33}\), the genuine two-loop \(L\) (green) terms actually dominate the \(L^2\)/\(L\) (red) terms, driven by a large \(y_t^3\) beta function mixing into \(Q_{HD}\). This is positioned as the reference basis for FCC-ee/CEPC global fits at sub-permille precision, continuing the SMEFT-precision thread.

Caveats: the framework is illustrated on current LEP/SLC fits and projections rather than delivering a full global result here; couplings are fixed at \(m_Z\) (differences are NNLL). The authors note complementary concurrent work (Mantani et al.; Born et al.), and stress that a complete analysis must retain both oblique and non-oblique observables.
