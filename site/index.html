Look-elsewhere gets a manual

Grosso et al. arXiv:2611.34888 review the look-elsewhere effect from Gross–Vitells through to ML-based searches. The timing is good. Last week the unclosed-excess list gained ATLAS’s 3.5σ instanton-like entry, and that paper never discusses look-elsewhere. The review flags two unsettled points:

  • What counts as “elsewhere.” It can mean one distribution or an entire programme.
  • ML searches. They carry an implicit post-selection bias with no analytic correction.

Both are where this month’s significance arguments live, from Keshet’s line triads (2.8σ blind, 6.1σ combined) to the ATLAS coverage audit. The heavier physics today is precision CKM-side inputs. Two more LZ models and two more agents fill out the listing.

Themes

Precision inputs, CKM-side

Cao et al. arXiv:2611.34804 compute two-loop QED corrections to β decay with full electron-mass dependence. For the neutron they get \(\delta_R=(29.199\pm0.001)\times10^{-3}\), about 70× more precise than the \(m_e\to0\) result. For superallowed decays, the Z-independent \(\alpha^2\) terms are (0.1–0.5)×10⁻⁴, roughly the expected size. The \(V_{ud}\) floor therefore stays nuclear: it is still Novario’s ~6×10⁻⁴ contact-term limit from 11-17.

D’Ambrosio et al. arXiv:2611.34856 show that the large two-photon piece of the paramagnetic EDM conserves CP. That leaves EDMs a clean probe of Im(\(V_{ts}^*V_{td}\)), and puts them on a B-free kaon CKM plane alongside \(K_S\to\mu\mu\) and \(K_L\to\pi^0\nu\bar\nu\).

Tao et al. arXiv:2611.34263 complete NNLO NRQCD for all 34 \(B_c^{(*)}\) form factors. Their \(R(J/\psi)=0.2836\) falls between lattice and LHCb, a small new data point for the LFU tension that Belle II’s \(R(D^*)\) eased in September.

LZ: two more, one mentions the Sun

Li et al. arXiv:2611.33787 place a 1.1 TeV higgsino in deflected AMSB and use messenger decays to fix the abundance. They list solar capture as a test. They also offer an elastic reading of the same event, with a higgsino asymmetry \(|X_A|\simeq10^{-2}\).

Biondini et al. arXiv:2611.34438 get endothermic scattering at dimension five in a leptophilic sector; elastic scattering only enters at dimension six. Their abstract never mentions solar capture, the bound that killed September’s consensus higgsino.

Partons from Hamiltonians

Araz et al. arXiv:2611.33280 compute flavor-resolved pion-like PDFs in 1+1D NJL with tensor networks. That makes three Minkowski-space tensor-network PDF papers in two days, after yesterday’s Schwinger-model pair. Čuić et al. arXiv:2611.34631 use finite elements to turn the non-invertible shadow-GPD problem into a merely ill-conditioned one. Experimental errors then set a minimum resolution in momentum fraction, which matters for the GPD program revived in September.

Agents graded against references

Devoto et al. arXiv:2611.33279 have LLMs implement NNLO nested soft-collinear subtraction for VBF Higgs and compare the output with human-written code. That is a verifiable task in Alimena’s sense, but the abstract gives no verdict. Zhou et al.’s NuSrcAgent arXiv:2611.33324 runs IceCube public-data likelihood analyses and reproduces published results.

Worth a look

  • arXiv:2611.34888 (Grosso et al.): Concrete reporting recommendations for both classical and ML searches. The ML section links trials corrections to selective inference.
  • arXiv:2611.34804 (Cao et al.): A 70× cut in the neutron radiative-correction uncertainty, and evidence that \(\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2)\) QED is not where the \(V_{ud}\) budget hides.
  • arXiv:2611.34856 (D’Ambrosio et al.): A CKM consistency test across electrons, muons and neutrinos. Lepton-specific new physics would split the determinations.
  • arXiv:2611.33292 (Arcangeli et al.): The Δ(1232) channel roughly doubles supernova axion luminosity and triples emission above 200 MeV. Water-Cherenkov counts rise about 10×. It can also mimic a hotter core in γ-ray fits.
  • arXiv:2611.34886 (Yu): Time-weighted energy correlators are IR unsafe at two loops, through a soft parton pair rather than a single soft gluon. Read this before building timing into correlator measurements.

Also noted

  • arXiv:2611.34808 Probing Ultralight Dark Matter at the Electroweak Scale — Martin Bauer et al. (hep-ph). Finds CP-odd \(Z\to\tau\tau\) dipole observables beat atomic clocks.
  • arXiv:2611.34892 Massive Triangle in Cosmological Correlator beyond Conformal Coupling — Taushif Ahmed et al. (hep-th). The latest in the run of closed-form cosmological correlators, here weight-three and weight-four polylogarithms.

Deep dives

arXiv:2611.34888 — The Look-Elsewhere Effect in Searches for New Physics: from Classical Methods to Machine Learning (Gaia Grosso et al.)

The review’s core demand is procedural. Any global p-value must state which “elsewhere” it corrects for: a single distribution, a set of analyses, or the whole programme. Without that, the reader cannot match the correction to their own scepticism. The authors also argue that 5σ should be applied to the global significance. Today the threshold is applied locally and absorbs analysis-level trial factors of several hundred. In ATLAS’s Higgs discovery, 5.9σ local became 5.1σ global, a trial factor of about 100.

The classical material includes several points that are often missed: - For s=1, the trial factor grows linearly with Z_local. - It is set by the width of the signal template, not the bin width. - Searches that are partly continuous and partly discrete, such as LLP scans over a few benchmark cτ values, have no clean treatment.

For ML, searches sit on a continuum. A supervised classifier fixed before unblinding adds no post-selection bias. Weakly supervised and goodness-of-fit methods carry an implicit LEE set by an emergent “resolution” that cannot be tuned directly. The standard post-selection remedies map onto ML practice: - Conditioning corresponds to toys with full retraining. - Splitting corresponds to train/test splits, whose sensitivity cost “has not been established.” - Constraining corresponds to regularisation.

Anomaly detection is recast as screening, suitable for FDR control, followed by a targeted search on independent data. E-values are offered for the “look-elsewhen” problem only.

This bears directly on last week’s ATLAS 3.5σ entry, which never states its scope. Caveat: the extracted text ends mid-Section 6, so the recommendations section was not seen.

arXiv:2611.34804 — QED corrections to neutron and nuclear β-decay at order α² (Zehua Cao et al.)

The full two-loop correction with exact m_e/E dependence, including real-virtual and double-real emission, gives δ_R,static = (29.199 ± 0.001)×10⁻³. The previous value was (29.18 ± 0.07 ± 0.01 ± 0.02)×10⁻³, whose dominant error came from neglected power corrections.

The form factors are derived two ways, by direct timelike evaluation and by a subtracted dispersion relation, and the two agree. The work also yields the first direct two-loop QED computation of the zero-recoil HQET matching coefficient E(m). Its Zα² kernel agrees with Crosas–Mereghetti.

For superallowed decays, the Z-independent ⟨δ^(2,0)⟩ ranges from about +0.013×10⁻³ (¹⁰C) to +0.044×10⁻³ (⁷⁴Rb). These values come from virtual, real-virtual and real-real pieces that each exceed 10⁻⁴ but largely cancel. Standard analyses include these terms only via leading logs, so the result is an additive shift to δ′_R at roughly the size of current uncertainties.

The paper says plainly that the neutron error is now dominated by g_A/g_V and short-distance corrections. A complete O(α²) nuclear analysis still lacks the finite Z²α³ low-energy matrix element. This supports the digest’s reading: the V_ud floor is nuclear, not QED.

arXiv:2611.34856 — Probing the CKM Origin of CP Violation with Paramagnetic EDMs and Rare Kaon Decays (Giancarlo D’Ambrosio et al.)

The two-photon K_L→e⁺e⁻ vertex is large, |g_L^{e,γγ}(0)| = 4.0×10⁻¹⁴, about 35× the electroweak K_S vertex. Because it conserves CP, it must pair with the CP-odd Q₆-penguin part of the kaon–nucleon coupling, for which the authors estimate h_L/h_S = 3.2×10⁻⁴ for Th. The resulting shift to C_S is 1.1% of the LO electroweak term, or 0.76% of the Ema–Gao–Pospelov value once the K_S pole is included. CP-odd π⁰/η couplings and charged-pion loops add about 2.4%, making them the largest EM term. All of this is well below the 30% weak-nucleon normalisation uncertainty, so the paramagnetic EDM tracks Im(V_ts*V_td).

The EDM then joins the CP-odd S-wave of K_S→μμ and K_L→π⁰νν̄ on a kaon CKM plane, (A²(1−ρ̂), A²η̂), with no B input. Lepton-specific new physics would split the three determinations: - Electron-coupled Z′ (5 TeV, g_q = 0.5, g_e = 1): shifts the EDM determination by 12%, and by up to 27% within LEP contact limits. - Muon leptoquark with a new phase: enhances the K_S S-wave about 36×. This is consistent with LHCb’s preliminary bound B(K_S→μμ) < 2.7×10⁻¹¹.

Caveats: - The CP-odd baryon matrix elements rest on factorisation, which the authors call a model assumption; a 10% change in m_s moves them about 20%. - The overall sign of the weak couplings is unfixed. - θ̄ contamination stays below 10% only if |θ̄| ≲ (2–4)×10⁻¹⁵. - A 20% measurement needs sensitivity of a few ×10⁻³⁶ e cm, far beyond the current HfF⁺ limit of 4.1×10⁻³⁰ e cm.
