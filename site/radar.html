Citation radar

A weekly sweep of INSPIRE-HEP for recent papers the community is citing heavily but the daily deep dives missed.

Week of Friday, September 25, 2026

The radar this week is a single story. All four papers are responses to LZ230616, the lone ~248 keV nuclear recoil LZ reported at 2.6σ global (3.4σ local) significance, and all four were posted within about 48 hours of one another. Their bibliographies overlap almost completely, and much of each count probably reflects the reflexive “see also Refs. [x–y]” citing typical of a fast anomaly literature. Still, the sequence is instructive. The first wave asked whether a TeV electroweak multiplet with a few-hundred-keV splitting fits the event. This batch shifts to whether anything like that survives the Sun.

arXiv:2609.07807 — Not so good νs for Higgsino dark matter as LZ excess: stringent limits from Super-Kamiokande and IceCube (Debajit Bose et al.)

The authors compute solar capture of Higgsino DM through inelastic scattering on heavy solar nuclei, followed by annihilation to WW/ZZ. They confront the resulting neutrino flux with angular-cut atmospheric ν_e and ν_μ data from Super-K and IceCube. For the 1.08 TeV thermal Higgsino, splittings δ ≲ 557 keV are excluded, which covers the LZ-preferred 340–360 keV band. The strongest and weakest datasets differ by only ~20%. They also map limits for non-thermal Higgsino masses, where Super-K overtakes IceCube near 100 GeV.

The paper has drawn ~39 citations since 7 September. The most likely reason is that it is an independent cross-check of Pospelov–Ramani’s solar bound using different datasets and arriving at a comparable limit. That makes it the second half of the standard “solar capture disfavors Higgsinos” citation pair. The authors are candid that the thermal Higgsino survives if the local velocity distribution is non-standard, for example through LMC motion or streams. They also flag that the Helm form factor oscillates rapidly near 250 keV in xenon. An abstract-level note that one author thinks the event is background, sitting too close to the upper energy cut, may make the paper more memorable, but that is speculation.

arXiv:2609.06571 — The Inert Doublet Model of Dark Matter and the LUX-ZEPLIN High-Recoil Event (Lei Wang et al.)

This paper realizes the endothermic transition H N → A N through the off-diagonal Z coupling of the inert doublet. Because that coupling is fixed by electroweak strength, the scattering normalization is not a free parameter. The authors impose relic density, perturbativity, oblique parameters, elastic LZ limits and dwarf-galaxy limits. A profile likelihood on the event then gives a best fit at m_H = 1080 GeV and δ = 369 keV, with a GeV-scale charged splitting and λ_L ≈ −2×10⁻⁴. They stress that the event constrains δ far more tightly than the mass.

The ~35 citations likely come from its role as the canonical scalar counterpart to the Higgsino fit, with the full IDM constraint stack applied. The paper does not address solar capture, which is the main vulnerability of fixed-coupling interpretations. Lee–Youn (below) note that an inert-doublet fit lies in the same region as the Z₂-doublet case they estimate at roughly 10⁴ times the IceCube limit. The authors also acknowledge that the LZ likelihood cannot be fully reconstructed from public information.

arXiv:2609.07800 — Inelastic Singlet-Doublet Fermion Dark Matter in light of the 248 keV LZ event (Debasish Borah et al.)

Here a singlet-doublet fermion is split into pseudo-Dirac states by a hypercharge-1 scalar triplet, and the same triplet generates neutrino masses via type-II seesaw. The Majorana lightest state has no diagonal Z coupling, so the Z-mediated inelastic rate scales as sin⁴θ and the mixing sets the normalization. The preferred region moves with mixing: - At sinθ = 0.7, the fit prefers δ ~ 310–370 keV and masses of 10⁴–10^5.8 GeV. - At sinθ = 0.0135, it prefers δ ~ 30–180 keV and masses of 10^2.5–10^3.8 GeV.

Only the two small-mixing benchmarks evade elastic LZ bounds.

The ~34 citations likely reflect membership in the standard list of LZ interpretations. It also appears to be a direct precursor to the mixing-suppression mechanism developed in arXiv:2609.09138, which cites it. On substance it is thin: - The fit is a signal-only extended likelihood. - Relic density is taken from earlier work. - There is no solar or sideband analysis.

arXiv:2609.09138 — Mixing-suppressed inelastic dark matter: a minimal model for the LZ 248 keV event (Seung J. Lee et al.)

This is the most thorough of the four. The authors show that a full-strength Z transition at the recoil-inferred splitting predicts ~4000 events. Fixed-coupling models such as the Higgsino must therefore push δ deep into the halo tail, but solar gravitational acceleration undoes that suppression. A Majorana singlet mixed with a vector-like doublet instead suppresses the rate through sin⁴θ without shifting the spectrum. The same mixing drives capture as sin⁴θ and out-of-equilibrium annihilation as sin¹²θ, and weak Higgs cooling keeps the captured population extended.

With relic density and a one-event normalization imposed, they find δ = 302–305 keV conservatively (thermalized population). Under their cooling ansatz this rises to 331–343 keV near a 730–733 GeV endpoint. Falsifiable predictions include: - a tungsten rate ~46 times the xenon rate at the nominal ceiling; - no germanium or argon signal; - June-to-December rate ratios up to ~56.

Its ~34 citations are probably earned by its common-assumption comparison table, which places the Higgsino at ~4×10³ times the IceCube limit. That makes it a natural reference point for later papers.

Week of Friday, September 18, 2026

This week’s radar is dominated by a single experimental spark: LUX-ZEPLIN’s report of one anomalous ~248 keV nuclear-recoil event (LZ230616), with a 3.4σ local / 2.6σ global significance. All five heavily-cited papers below are theory responses posted within days of the LZ preprint, and together they map the interpretive landscape that has formed almost overnight—endothermic vs. exothermic vs. neutrino-upscattering, Higgsino vs. inert-doublet vs. axion-portal. The rapid, cross-citing cluster (these papers reference each other extensively) is itself the story: a genuine community pile-on around a possible first direct-detection signal, with everyone racing to stake a mechanism and stress-test it against solar-capture and halo-tail constraints.

arXiv:2609.04186 — Axion Portal Dark Matter and the LUX-ZEPLIN High-Recoil Event (James Unwin)

Unwin proposes an elastic explanation of the LZ event, in deliberate contrast to the inelastic Higgsino interpretations dominating the early literature. The paper UV-completes the pseudoscalar-pseudoscalar operator ℒ₄ = (χ̄iγ⁵χ)(N̄iγ⁵N)—one of the templates for which LZ reported its largest local significances—via an axion portal: Dirac fermion dark matter (m_χ ≈ 400 GeV) coupled to a GeV-scale pNGB mediator, with a single multi-TeV vectorlike quark completing the up-quark coupling. The hard recoil spectrum arises from momentum dependence (moderated by a pion pole) rather than inelastic kinematics, so it is insensitive to the uncertain high-velocity halo tail; the model reproduces the relic abundance via p-wave freeze-out and brackets the LZ significance at 2.6–3.2σ.

At ~43 citations since early September, its pull likely comes from being the first full elastic UV completion of a favored LZ operator—offering a distinct, halo-robust alternative just as the field worried the Higgsino reading was fragile. The bonus correlation with the first-row CKM unitarity deficit adds cross-sector appeal.

arXiv:2609.04163 — TeV Higgsino Interpretation of the LZ High-Recoil Event with Intermediate-Scale Electroweak Gauginos (Xiaokang Du et al.)

Du and Wang address a specific tension in the popular TeV-Higgsino reading: reproducing a sub-MeV neutral-Higgsino splitting normally pushes electroweak-gaugino masses toward ~10⁷ GeV. They show that with opposite-sign bino and wino masses, the non-universal SU(5) GUT boundary condition M₁ᴳ/M₂ᴳ = −3/5 cancels the leading dimension-five contribution, and homogeneous one-loop running preserves this down to the Higgsino scale. The residual finite-gaugino terms then yield a controlled branch with a wino near 120 TeV for a 350 keV gap, μ = 1.091 TeV, tanβ = 10. They classify the mixed SU(5) representations (1+24, 1+75, 1+200) that realize the ratio, but candidly note the small gap is far more sensitive to bino–wino alignment than to overall scale, and that the inelastic Z coupling remains unsuppressed—so the full-density thermal Higgsino stays in tension with solar-capture bounds.

The ~42 citations reflect its role as the top-down SUSY-model-building companion to the phenomenological Higgsino fits: it supplies a concrete GUT-scale mechanism others can cite when invoking small Higgsino splittings.

arXiv:2609.04185 — Atmospheric neutrino up-scattering explanation of LZ 2026 excess (Sk Jeesun et al.)

Jeesun and Majumdar break from the dark-matter framing entirely: they explain the isolated high-recoil event via atmospheric neutrinos up-scattering into a massive BSM fermion χ (ν𝒩→χ𝒩) through a scalar mediator. The key kinematic feature is that producing a ~GeV-mass χ requires a minimum recoil energy of ~200 keV, naturally cutting off the low-energy tail and mimicking an isolated event exactly where LZ sees one. They identify a closed 90% CL band—m_χ ≈ 0.7–2 GeV, coupling √(y_χy_q)/m_φ ≈ 2×10⁻² GeV⁻¹—bounded below by the low-recoil veto and above by the falling atmospheric flux, and argue collider/beam-dump/supernova limits evade this region.

At ~40 citations, this is the field’s principal non-dark-matter alternative, keeping the interpretation space honest. Its value is providing a distinct testable hypothesis (a sterile-neutrino-like signal) that upcoming low-background experiments could distinguish from the halo-DM readings.

arXiv:2609.02505 — Dark Matter as the Z₂ Partner of the Standard Model Higgs Boson (Yasunori Nomura)

Nomura connects the LZ event to an inert-doublet model previously selected—in prior work—to fit a Fermi-LAT Galactic-halo gamma-ray excess at several-hundred-GeV mass. The conceptual hook is that the inert Z₂ parity is exactly equivalent (by field redefinition) to an interchange symmetry between two Higgs doublets, making dark matter the Z₂-odd partner of the SM Higgs. Phenomenologically, gauge invariance fixes the off-diagonal Z coupling, and the halo-plus-thermal mass window (440–600 GeV) selects a narrow strip of ~350 keV neutral splittings near the kinematic boundary where inelastic Z-exchange can produce the recoil. A sharp prediction follows: because scattering probes the extreme halo tail, future events should show large annual modulation peaking near early summer—and LZ230616 occurred June 16.

The ~32 citations track the paper’s ambition to unify two anomalies (gamma-ray excess + LZ event) under one minimal, technically-natural model. The v2 note honestly concedes the solar-capture (Pospelov–Ramani) constraint may require δ ≳ 500 keV, showing the interpretation under active pressure.

arXiv:2609.05204 — Exothermic Dark Matter at LZ (Carlos Henrique de Lima)

de Lima pursues the exothermic route: a metastable excited state χ_H down-scatters (χ_H𝒩→χ_L𝒩), releasing the mass splitting δ into the recoil so the signal does not require the halo’s high-velocity tail—the chief liability of endothermic Higgsino readings. Realized in a minimal inelastic dark-photon model, the fit favors m_χ ~ 30–200 GeV and δ ~ 0.5–1 MeV (bounded by δ < 2m_e for cosmological stability), with GeV-scale mediators and ε ~ 10⁻⁶. Both secluded freeze-out (yielding a small excited fraction f_H) and low-reheating freeze-in can supply the rate. The standout prediction is target dependence: because xenon is being probed near its diffraction minimum, argon and germanium should see ~3× more events at higher recoil energies (Table 1), giving DEAP-3600 and DarkSide-20k a clean cross-check.

At ~31 citations, its appeal is offering the halo-robust, testable counterpoint to endothermic models—and explicitly evading the solar-capture bound because χ_L is kinematically inert. Its dense cross-referencing of the whole LZ-interpretation cluster makes it a natural hub in this fast-moving conversation.

Week of Friday, September 11, 2026

This week’s radar is a single-topic feeding frenzy. Every paper below was posted within days of the LUX-ZEPLIN Collaboration’s late-August preprint reporting one nuclear-recoil-like event at \(E_R = 248\pm23\pm23\) keV in a 2.84 tonne-year exposure (3.4σ local, 2.6σ global). The community has converged with remarkable speed on a single reading — endothermic/inelastic dark matter, whose threshold kinematics naturally selects a high, localized recoil energy that conventional elastic WIMP spectra cannot reproduce — and is now racing to claim the most predictive particle realization. The result is a tight cluster of cross-citing papers (they reference each other heavily), which explains the near-identical citation counts. This is what a fresh anomaly looks like when it hits a theory community primed with a ready-made mechanism.

arXiv:2609.02608 — Dark Matter at the Kinematic Edge: Interpreting the 248 keV LZ Nuclear-Recoil Candidate (Mattia Di Mauro)

Di Mauro provides the most thorough single-author phenomenological recast of the LZ event. Using the published efficiency curves and operator significances, the paper argues that elastic spin-independent scattering fails quantitatively — normalizing to one event near 248 keV would demand ~\(1.4\times10^3\) accompanying low-energy recoils — while endothermic inelastic scattering shifts the spectrum into the observed window. Two concrete realizations are worked out: a thermal pseudo-Dirac fermion with off-diagonal vector coupling (\(m_\chi\simeq1\) TeV, \(\delta\simeq297\) keV, \(\sigma_N\simeq6.5\times10^{-43}\) cm²) that ties the direct-detection rate to relic-abundance coannihilation, and a more predictive thermal Higgsino (\(1.1\) TeV, \(\delta\simeq377\) keV) testable via a gamma-ray line.

At ~41 citations in roughly a month, this reads as the community’s reference recast: it carefully catalogs which NREFT operators LZ actually tested, stresses that the event selects “spectral hardness” rather than a unique Lorentz structure, and quantifies halo-tail sensitivity. Its detailed efficiency digitization and explicit relic–direct-detection relation make it a natural citation anchor for the follow-up papers below.

arXiv:2609.01475 — Inelastic Dark Matter Signature at High Recoil Energy in LUX-ZEPLIN and CRESST (Liangliang Su et al.)

Su, Yang, and Yang deliver a compact Letter interpreting the event as endothermic inelastic DM, adding a distinctive ingredient: terrestrial propagation. They compute both direct scattering of surviving ground-state halo DM and the excited-state component produced by upscattering inside the Earth, concluding that for \(m_\chi\gtrsim500\) GeV and \(\delta=\mathcal{O}(300)\) keV the Earth-produced component is kinematically forbidden — leaving direct endothermic scattering on xenon as the signal and eliminating daily modulation. An illustrative two-bin likelihood gives a best-fit \((m_\chi,\delta)\simeq(1.105\,\mathrm{TeV},350\,\mathrm{keV})\) for \(\bar\sigma_n=10^{-37}\) cm².

The headline forward-looking claim — testability via the planned CRESST upgrade, whose heavier tungsten target reaches the same transition at lower recoil energy with ~1.5 tonne·day exposure — gives experimentalists a concrete target and likely drives some of its ~41 citations. As one of the earliest posted (Sept 1) interpretations, it also functions as a template others cite when framing the inelastic reading and the CRESST complementarity.

arXiv:2609.02807 — A Peccei-Quinn Origin for Inelastic Electroweak Dark Matter after LUX-ZEPLIN (Luca Visinelli)

Visinelli embeds the inelastic electroweak-doublet interpretation in a Peccei–Quinn completion: spontaneous PQ breaking leaves a residual parity that stabilizes the lighter neutral state and generates the Majorana splitting via a heavy PQ-charged singlet, while a KSVZ colored pair supplies the QCD anomaly. Integrating out the singlet yields the dimension-five operator that splits a vectorlike doublet, giving a Higgsino-like off-diagonal \(Z\) coupling. The construction realizes a mixed axion–WIMP cosmology: for benchmark doublet masses 287–509 GeV the EW component supplies only 7–21% of the relic density, with the QCD axion providing the rest through misalignment (\(v_S\simeq4.4\times10^{11}\) GeV).

The paper is notable for its careful complementarity discussion — solar-capture/IceCube limits, excited-state radiative decay (~20 km decay length, leaving LZ a clean single recoil but enabling luminous scintillator signatures), and collider disappearing-track bounds. Its ~39 citations likely reflect both the appeal of unifying the two leading DM paradigms and its extensive, honest cross-referencing of the competing LZ interpretations, which positions it centrally in the emerging literature web.

arXiv:2609.02868 — Inelastic Dark Photon Dark Matter for the LUX-ZEPLIN High-Recoil Event and the Galactic Halo Gamma-Ray Excess (Kimiko Yamashita)

Yamashita attempts a two-anomaly fit: a \(U(1)_X\) dark-photon DM at \(m_X=420\) GeV that both explains Totani’s halo-like Fermi-LAT gamma-ray excess (via a \(p\)-wave-suppressed, Sommerfeld-enhanced \(W^+W^-\) channel) and the LZ event (via inelastic \(X+N\to V+N\) with \(\delta\simeq316\) keV, saturating the kinematic ceiling for xenon). A second light scalar \(\phi'\) mediates the inelastic transition while a dark parity forbids the elastic amplitude that would otherwise violate SI limits.

The ambition — one dark sector, two independent anomalies, same 420 GeV mass — is the clear draw, and at ~35 citations it is well-cited despite requiring a fairly elaborate scalar sector (the paper candidly defers “a full symmetry construction” and a profile-likelihood significance to future work). It leans on the same detector-resolution smearing (\(\sigma_E=32.5\) keV) argument as the others. The transparent AI-assistance disclosure is a sign of the times. Its citation count is somewhat inflated by membership in the tightly cross-citing LZ cluster.

arXiv:2609.01892 — A PQ-Symmetric High-Scale SUSY Interpretation of the LZ High-Energy Recoil (Wen Yin)

Yin reads the event as near-threshold upscattering of 1.08-TeV thermal Higgsino dark matter, with a neutral-Higgsino splitting of 0.28–0.35 MeV fixing electroweak gauginos at \(\mathcal{O}(10^7)\) GeV via the standard integrating-out relation \(\delta_0\simeq m_Z^2(s_W^2/M_1+c_W^2/M_2)\). The paper then builds out the full high-scale SUSY picture: gravity-mediated sfermions at \(\sim10^7\) GeV, a Kim–Nilles PQ mechanism setting \(\mu=1.08\) TeV plus a QCD axion, HSSUSY-checked Higgs mass at \(\tan\beta\simeq1.9\)–2.2, gauge unification with suppressed proton decay, and avoidance of the gravitino/moduli problems.

The distinctive prediction — a “super-invisible” axion with accidentally cancelled photon coupling (\(E/N=2\) from the Higgsino contribution) — plus potential proton-decay reach make this a smoking-gun-oriented scenario. At ~32 citations it is the most model-building-heavy entry, cited as the SUSY-embedded counterpart to the more agnostic Higgsino recasts. The framing throughout is appropriately cautious, treating the single non-blind event as a “working hypothesis.”

Week of Friday, September 4, 2026

This week’s radar is dominated by a single new experimental anomaly and the theory rush to claim it. Both featured papers, posted the same day (September 1, 2026), interpret the LZ Collaboration’s ~248 keV high-energy nuclear-recoil “event of interest” as inelastic Higgsino dark matter — and both drew ~10 citations almost immediately. Attention is flowing hard toward the oldest, most canonical WIMP candidate suddenly being handed a possible signal, and toward the fact that the TeV Higgsino’s interaction strength is fixed rather than fitted, making it uniquely predictive against the new data.

arXiv:2609.01583 — Higgsino Dark Matter Interpretation of the LUX-ZEPLIN 248 keV Nuclear-Recoil Event (Katherine Freese et al.)

Freese and Theodosopoulos make the minimal, sharp argument: a nearly pure ~1 TeV Higgsino realizes inelastic dark matter through the off-diagonal Z coupling of two nearly degenerate neutral Majorana states split by δ, and this fixes the inelastic Higgsino–nucleon cross section at σ ≃ 1.86×10⁻³⁹ cm², rather than leaving it free. Using the LZ scalar-to-vector conversion (σ_V ≃ 3.2 σ_SI for xenon), they show the fixed Higgsino prediction falls inside LZ’s two-sided 90% interval for δ ~ 350 keV at m = 1 TeV. Honestly, the paper also concedes tension: rescaling to the thermally preferred 1.1 TeV (via an efficiency-weighted rate ratio ≈0.8) pushes the interval below the prediction, though δ slightly above 350 keV — near xenon’s kinematic ceiling — would recover consistency.

The ~10 citations in days reflect first-mover advantage on a fresh anomaly plus the authors’ prominence. The headline draw is the “no free parameter” framing: the LZ event lands where the most economical SUSY WIMP predicts. The caveats are stated plainly — the rescaling is not a proper LZ likelihood reanalysis — but the provocative claim (a classic candidate matching a real excess) is what the community is picking up.

arXiv:2609.01590 — TeV Higgsino Dark Matter from LZ Nuclear Recoil to Fermi-LAT Gamma Rays (Lei Wu et al.)

Wu, Zhang, and Zhu pursue the same inelastic-Higgsino idea far more ambitiously, welding together three signals under one MSSM benchmark. Freeze-out fixes m ≈ 1.09 TeV; a joint likelihood combining relic density, an energy-only LZ recast, and the 14-year Fermi-LAT Galactic-center excess selects δ ≈ 324 keV. They further argue that destructive interference between tree-level Higgs-exchange and electroweak-loop elastic amplitudes can suppress solar capture/thermalization, evading the 10-year IceCube bound, and that the predicted γγ/γZ line plus endpoint sits near current H.E.S.S. sensitivity and within CTAO’s projected reach. The extensive Supplemental Material builds an internally consistent Einasto–Eddington halo, an annulus-by-annulus Fermi-LAT reweighting, and a response-matched CTAO projection.

The ~10 citations track the same anomaly-driven surge, but this paper’s appeal is different: it packages the LZ interpretation into a fully testable multi-messenger program with near-term falsifiability at CTAO. The authors are unusually candid that their LZ proxy is not the official likelihood and that the IceCube cancellation band is only shown to be “parametrically possible,” not located. Its citations likely accrue as the more complete reference framework for anyone extending the Higgsino-LZ story to indirect detection.

Week of Friday, August 28, 2026

This week’s radar leans toward review-and-benchmark literature: two are pedagogical or synthesis pieces (a functional-QCD encyclopedia chapter, a cross-paradigm naturalness audit), and two are technical toolkits that the community will compare against (higher-point energy-correlator jet functions, a consolidated singlet-extension constraint map). The common thread is consolidation — papers that stake out reference values, unify scattered results, or hand collaborations something reusable.

arXiv:2606.03703 — Phase structure of strong interaction matter from Functional QCD (Christian S. Fischer et al.)

This Encyclopedia of Nuclear Physics chapter is a pedagogical survey of Dyson–Schwinger and functional-renormalization-group approaches to the QCD phase diagram. It walks through the technical machinery (the 1PI effective action, quark gap equations, order parameters for chiral and confinement transitions, fluctuation observables), then collects the headline functional-QCD results: agreement with lattice at small μ_B, a chiral critical endpoint clustered at (T,μ_B)≈(90–115, 600–700) MeV across high-quality truncations, and a predicted kurtosis peak at √s_NN≈5 GeV connecting to freeze-out data. It also flags the “onset of new phases” (moat regime, inhomogeneous condensates) beyond μ_B/T≳4.5.

The ~5 citations in a few weeks likely reflect its role as a citable entry point: it argues that functional QCD is “the only framework” for direct high-density computation, provides a curated CEP-location consensus, and supplies a ready reference for anyone connecting theory to RHIC/FAIR/HIAF programs. Review pieces that fix a community consensus number (here, the CEP window) tend to accrue citations fast as the default reference.

arXiv:2606.02714 — Projected Energy Correlators: Two-Loop Jet Functions and NNLL Resummation (Kyle Lee et al.)

The authors extend NNLL collinear resummation of projected N-point energy correlators (ENCs) from N=2,3 up to N=6, in both e⁺e⁻ annihilation and H→gg, matched to NLO. The key deliverable is the two-loop jet function for N=4,5,6, computed semi-analytically via IBP and differential equations, with explicit numerical jet constants cross-checked against Event2 and Eerad3. They add leading non-perturbative corrections governed by two universal soft matrix elements Ω̄_1q, Ω̄_1g, and study α_s sensitivity through ENC/EEC ratios whose log-log slopes track twist-two spin-(N+1) anomalous dimensions.

The paper’s citation traction (~5 in weeks) is best read as a supply-side event: it provides missing perturbative ingredients (the higher-point jet constants) that others need to make higher-N correlators usable for precision phenomenology. With CMS already extracting α_s from the three-point correlator inside jets, and Higgs-factory (FCC-ee/CEPC) H→gg motivation explicit, this is positioned as an enabling toolkit for a live α_s-extraction program with complementary systematics — exactly the kind of reference-computation paper that gets cited by follow-up analyses.

arXiv:2606.12775 — Are Primordial Black Holes a Natural Dark Matter Candidate? (Stefano Profumo)

Profumo applies three fine-tuning measures (Barbieri–Giudice, Strumia–Rattazzi, and an island half-width ε) uniformly across three non-inflationary PBH mechanisms, six inflationary PBH model classes, and seven particle-DM benchmarks, all evaluated on the observed relic contour. The provocative headline: PBH fine-tuning is set by the analytic structure of the abundance map, not by whether DM is a particle or a gravitational relic. Biased-domain-wall PBHs (Δ=9/2) are as natural as off-resonance WIMPs and freeze-in; single-exponential mechanisms cluster at Δ≈ln(T_form/T_eq)≈14–50; only single-field ultra-slow-roll inflation is severely tuned, via a double exponential. It also reconciles the Barbieri–Giudice vs. Iovino–Riotto dispute as answering complementary questions.

The ~5 citations likely stem from the contrarian framing — directly rebutting the common “PBH DM is fine-tuned” dismissal with a quantitative, cross-paradigm protocol. A single-author polemic offering a clean universality-class taxonomy and a memorable structural identity (Δ≈log of a scale ratio) is the kind of claim people cite to endorse, dispute, or position against.

arXiv:2606.12533 — Constraining the real scalar singlet extension of the SM (Moritz Bosse et al.)

This is a comprehensive constraint map of the real singlet extension (xSM), combining a state-of-the-art electroweak phase transition treatment (two-loop dimensionally-reduced 3D EFT validated against lattice) with high-scale vacuum stability (three-loop gauge, two-loop Yukawa/scalar RG running) and collider probes: Higgs signal strength, trilinear κ₃, EWPO, and resonant ZZ/di-Higgs searches. Focusing on a heavy singlet, they find SFOEWPT-viable parameter space up to ~1 TeV, show that self-coupling deviations can exceed hZZ deviations by 𝒪(10), and identify a challenging “funnel region” (θ→0) where a two-step transition and vacuum stabilization coexist with vanishing tree-level Higgs-coupling shifts. They project HL-LHC and FCC reach and release a UFO implementation.

The early ~5 citations probably reflect its value as a unified reference: it overlays cosmological requirements with distinct collider handles rather than doing one in isolation, updates prior HL-LHC/100-TeV studies, and ships reusable MadGraph tooling. For a benchmark model this well-studied, a fresh, internally consistent constraint synthesis becomes the natural thing to cite.

Week of Friday, August 21, 2026

This week’s radar catches two very different flavors of “quietly essential” physics. One is a theory proposal riding the surging quantum-information-at-colliders wave; the other is the kind of infrastructure measurement that underwrites nearly every cross section the LHC produces. Both are accumulating citations fast for reasons that say something about the field’s current priorities: extending quantum-correlation studies to new systems, and pinning down the reference numbers everyone builds on.

arXiv:2606.30759 — Quantum Information of Photon Pairs at Lepton Colliders (Kun Cheng et al.)

Cheng, Han, Li, and Yan extend the collider quantum-information program to photon pairs, a system that pioneered entanglement and Bell tests at low energy but had not been tackled at high energy. Their trick is to exploit near-on-shell photon conversion (γ*→ℓ⁺ℓ⁻): in the small-virtuality limit the conversion factorizes from the hard process and the two transverse photon polarizations form an effective qubit, with linear polarization reconstructed from the dilepton azimuthal distributions. Using existing Belle data they project a 7.4σ CHSH violation, plus measurements of quantum discord (5.6%) and nonstabilizerness/magic (1.6%), and demonstrate consistency with the kinematic approach for virtual photons.

At ~7 citations in about a month, the pace reflects a crowded, fast-moving subfield rather than a singular result—the reference list itself is dominated by 2025–2026 quantum-information-at-colliders papers, many by overlapping author groups. The appeal here is a concrete, data-ready proposal in a new channel (photons) with a clean qubit reduction that the authors note extends to h→γγ and other spin-1 systems.

arXiv:2606.26832 — Precision luminosity measurement in proton-proton collisions at √s = 13 TeV with the CMS detector (Arzunik Gevorgyan et al.)

This is CMS’s legacy Run 2 luminosity measurement, delivering 0.73% precision on the full 2015–2018 pp data set (140.22 ± 1.02 fb⁻¹)—described as the most precise ever at a bunched-beam hadron collider. The paper details the van der Meer calibration machinery and a battery of new refinements: residual orbit-drift corrections from BPM data, a two-step tracker-to-nominal length-scale calibration, an improved luminous-region treatment of transverse nonfactorization, better beam-beam modeling, and combination of four independently calibrated luminometers (HFET, HFOC, PLT, PCC). Long-term stability is validated against the Z→μμ rate, with the extracted σ_fid = 708 ± 12 pb consistent with theory.

The citation logic here is structural, not provocative: luminosity is the normalization for every cross section and, as the paper itself notes, the dominant systematic in several CMS measurements. At ~5 citations in six weeks, it is on track to become the standard reference that Run 2 CMS analyses cite for their luminosity uncertainty—the number everyone downstream simply has to quote. Its sub-percent milestone also sets the baseline going into HL-LHC.

Week of Friday, August 14, 2026

This week’s radar points to the speculative frontier of flavor model-building, where a single-paper octonionic construction is drawing early attention less for a confirmed result than for staking out sharply falsifiable predictions in the neutrino sector.

arXiv:2606.27836 — Leptonic CP Conservation and the Quark CP Phase from Octonionic Flavor Structure (Bishnu Gupta Teli et al.)

Working within the octonionic unification programme — one Standard-Model generation realized on complexified octonions via the Clifford algebra \(\mathcal{C}l(6)\), extended to three generations by \(G_2\) automorphisms and ladder-operator rotors — Teli and Singh prove a localization theorem for where a CP-violating phase can live. arXiv:2606.30354 — Solution of Canonical Differential Equations for Integrals on Arbitrary Geometries (Michał Czakon et al.)

Czakon and Tancredi tackle a real bottleneck: canonical ε-factorized systems beyond the polylogarithmic case have connection matrices containing transcendental functions — elliptic or Calabi-Yau periods — for which closed forms are often unavailable, blocking numerical evaluation. Their key observation is that these functions are not arbitrary: since the original master integrals obey rational differential equations, so do the transcendental building blocks. Adjoining these auxiliary equations yields an enlarged purely-rational system solvable by standard numerical ODE methods, with correct analytic continuation guaranteed automatically. They demonstrate on the 220 two-loop masters for di-jet and γ+jet production through a heavy-quark loop — 16 square roots plus elliptic periods — and ship a lightweight, commercial-software-free C++ package built on Boost’s Bulirsch-Stoer solver, achieving sub-second double-precision evaluations.

~5 citations since late June. The draw is a general, deployable recipe that sidesteps the hardest obstacle to using canonical bases in realistic amplitudes: no special-function implementation needed, universally applicable to any Feynman integral. Given the surge of parallel efforts (Baur–Duhr, Liu–Zhang) it references, this lands in a hot and crowded space, positioned as the production-oriented option.
