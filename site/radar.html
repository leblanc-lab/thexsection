Citation radar

A weekly sweep of INSPIRE-HEP for recent papers the community is citing heavily but the daily deep dives missed.

Week of Friday, August 28, 2026

This week’s radar leans toward review-and-benchmark literature: two are pedagogical or synthesis pieces (a functional-QCD encyclopedia chapter, a cross-paradigm naturalness audit), and two are technical toolkits that the community will compare against (higher-point energy-correlator jet functions, a consolidated singlet-extension constraint map). The common thread is consolidation — papers that stake out reference values, unify scattered results, or hand collaborations something reusable.

arXiv:2606.03703 — Phase structure of strong interaction matter from Functional QCD (Christian S. Fischer et al.)

This Encyclopedia of Nuclear Physics chapter is a pedagogical survey of Dyson–Schwinger and functional-renormalization-group approaches to the QCD phase diagram. It walks through the technical machinery (the 1PI effective action, quark gap equations, order parameters for chiral and confinement transitions, fluctuation observables), then collects the headline functional-QCD results: agreement with lattice at small μ_B, a chiral critical endpoint clustered at (T,μ_B)≈(90–115, 600–700) MeV across high-quality truncations, and a predicted kurtosis peak at √s_NN≈5 GeV connecting to freeze-out data. It also flags the “onset of new phases” (moat regime, inhomogeneous condensates) beyond μ_B/T≳4.5.

The ~5 citations in a few weeks likely reflect its role as a citable entry point: it argues that functional QCD is “the only framework” for direct high-density computation, provides a curated CEP-location consensus, and supplies a ready reference for anyone connecting theory to RHIC/FAIR/HIAF programs. Review pieces that fix a community consensus number (here, the CEP window) tend to accrue citations fast as the default reference.

arXiv:2606.02714 — Projected Energy Correlators: Two-Loop Jet Functions and NNLL Resummation (Kyle Lee et al.)

The authors extend NNLL collinear resummation of projected N-point energy correlators (ENCs) from N=2,3 up to N=6, in both e⁺e⁻ annihilation and H→gg, matched to NLO. The key deliverable is the two-loop jet function for N=4,5,6, computed semi-analytically via IBP and differential equations, with explicit numerical jet constants cross-checked against Event2 and Eerad3. They add leading non-perturbative corrections governed by two universal soft matrix elements Ω̄_1q, Ω̄_1g, and study α_s sensitivity through ENC/EEC ratios whose log-log slopes track twist-two spin-(N+1) anomalous dimensions.

The paper’s citation traction (~5 in weeks) is best read as a supply-side event: it provides missing perturbative ingredients (the higher-point jet constants) that others need to make higher-N correlators usable for precision phenomenology. With CMS already extracting α_s from the three-point correlator inside jets, and Higgs-factory (FCC-ee/CEPC) H→gg motivation explicit, this is positioned as an enabling toolkit for a live α_s-extraction program with complementary systematics — exactly the kind of reference-computation paper that gets cited by follow-up analyses.

arXiv:2606.12775 — Are Primordial Black Holes a Natural Dark Matter Candidate? (Stefano Profumo)

Profumo applies three fine-tuning measures (Barbieri–Giudice, Strumia–Rattazzi, and an island half-width ε) uniformly across three non-inflationary PBH mechanisms, six inflationary PBH model classes, and seven particle-DM benchmarks, all evaluated on the observed relic contour. The provocative headline: PBH fine-tuning is set by the analytic structure of the abundance map, not by whether DM is a particle or a gravitational relic. Biased-domain-wall PBHs (Δ=9/2) are as natural as off-resonance WIMPs and freeze-in; single-exponential mechanisms cluster at Δ≈ln(T_form/T_eq)≈14–50; only single-field ultra-slow-roll inflation is severely tuned, via a double exponential. It also reconciles the Barbieri–Giudice vs. Iovino–Riotto dispute as answering complementary questions.

The ~5 citations likely stem from the contrarian framing — directly rebutting the common “PBH DM is fine-tuned” dismissal with a quantitative, cross-paradigm protocol. A single-author polemic offering a clean universality-class taxonomy and a memorable structural identity (Δ≈log of a scale ratio) is the kind of claim people cite to endorse, dispute, or position against.

arXiv:2606.12533 — Constraining the real scalar singlet extension of the SM (Moritz Bosse et al.)

This is a comprehensive constraint map of the real singlet extension (xSM), combining a state-of-the-art electroweak phase transition treatment (two-loop dimensionally-reduced 3D EFT validated against lattice) with high-scale vacuum stability (three-loop gauge, two-loop Yukawa/scalar RG running) and collider probes: Higgs signal strength, trilinear κ₃, EWPO, and resonant ZZ/di-Higgs searches. Focusing on a heavy singlet, they find SFOEWPT-viable parameter space up to ~1 TeV, show that self-coupling deviations can exceed hZZ deviations by 𝒪(10), and identify a challenging “funnel region” (θ→0) where a two-step transition and vacuum stabilization coexist with vanishing tree-level Higgs-coupling shifts. They project HL-LHC and FCC reach and release a UFO implementation.

The early ~5 citations probably reflect its value as a unified reference: it overlays cosmological requirements with distinct collider handles rather than doing one in isolation, updates prior HL-LHC/100-TeV studies, and ships reusable MadGraph tooling. For a benchmark model this well-studied, a fresh, internally consistent constraint synthesis becomes the natural thing to cite.

Week of Friday, August 21, 2026

This week’s radar catches two very different flavors of “quietly essential” physics. One is a theory proposal riding the surging quantum-information-at-colliders wave; the other is the kind of infrastructure measurement that underwrites nearly every cross section the LHC produces. Both are accumulating citations fast for reasons that say something about the field’s current priorities: extending quantum-correlation studies to new systems, and pinning down the reference numbers everyone builds on.

arXiv:2606.30759 — Quantum Information of Photon Pairs at Lepton Colliders (Kun Cheng et al.)

Cheng, Han, Li, and Yan extend the collider quantum-information program to photon pairs, a system that pioneered entanglement and Bell tests at low energy but had not been tackled at high energy. Their trick is to exploit near-on-shell photon conversion (γ*→ℓ⁺ℓ⁻): in the small-virtuality limit the conversion factorizes from the hard process and the two transverse photon polarizations form an effective qubit, with linear polarization reconstructed from the dilepton azimuthal distributions. Using existing Belle data they project a 7.4σ CHSH violation, plus measurements of quantum discord (5.6%) and nonstabilizerness/magic (1.6%), and demonstrate consistency with the kinematic approach for virtual photons.

At ~7 citations in about a month, the pace reflects a crowded, fast-moving subfield rather than a singular result—the reference list itself is dominated by 2025–2026 quantum-information-at-colliders papers, many by overlapping author groups. The appeal here is a concrete, data-ready proposal in a new channel (photons) with a clean qubit reduction that the authors note extends to h→γγ and other spin-1 systems.

arXiv:2606.26832 — Precision luminosity measurement in proton-proton collisions at √s = 13 TeV with the CMS detector (Arzunik Gevorgyan et al.)

This is CMS’s legacy Run 2 luminosity measurement, delivering 0.73% precision on the full 2015–2018 pp data set (140.22 ± 1.02 fb⁻¹)—described as the most precise ever at a bunched-beam hadron collider. The paper details the van der Meer calibration machinery and a battery of new refinements: residual orbit-drift corrections from BPM data, a two-step tracker-to-nominal length-scale calibration, an improved luminous-region treatment of transverse nonfactorization, better beam-beam modeling, and combination of four independently calibrated luminometers (HFET, HFOC, PLT, PCC). Long-term stability is validated against the Z→μμ rate, with the extracted σ_fid = 708 ± 12 pb consistent with theory.

The citation logic here is structural, not provocative: luminosity is the normalization for every cross section and, as the paper itself notes, the dominant systematic in several CMS measurements. At ~5 citations in six weeks, it is on track to become the standard reference that Run 2 CMS analyses cite for their luminosity uncertainty—the number everyone downstream simply has to quote. Its sub-percent milestone also sets the baseline going into HL-LHC.

Week of Friday, August 14, 2026

This week’s radar points to the speculative frontier of flavor model-building, where a single-paper octonionic construction is drawing early attention less for a confirmed result than for staking out sharply falsifiable predictions in the neutrino sector.

arXiv:2606.27836 — Leptonic CP Conservation and the Quark CP Phase from Octonionic Flavor Structure (Bishnu Gupta Teli et al.)

Working within the octonionic unification programme — one Standard-Model generation realized on complexified octonions via the Clifford algebra \(\mathcal{C}l(6)\), extended to three generations by \(G_2\) automorphisms and ladder-operator rotors — Teli and Singh prove a localization theorem for where a CP-violating phase can live. Their central claims are asymmetric and clean: the quark (1,2) transport is the occupation flip of a single ladder mode, forcing \(A_d = A_u^*\) and the exact one-parameter law \(\phi_{12} = -2\chi\), with the programme’s geometric rotor sitting at the maximal, quadrature-balanced point \(|\phi_{12}|=\pi/2\). For leptons they prove a “reality theorem”: every charged-lepton and neutrino transport amplitude is real for the entire admissible transport class, so leptonic Dirac CP is conserved (\(J_\ell=0\), \(\delta^\ell_{\rm CP}\in\{0,\pi\}\)) unless a Higgs “bridge” mixes the identity line with the lepton-flavor plane — the sole, uncomputed loophole. A minimality assumption on the neutrino Jordan spectrum \((-\delta_\nu,0,+\delta_\nu)\) then selects inverted ordering, \(m_{\beta\beta}\simeq19\) meV and \(\Sigma m_\nu\simeq0.10\) eV.

Notably, the authors themselves quantify “the lean,” conceding that current global fits disfavor their inverted-ordering-plus-conserved-CP package at roughly \(3\sigma\) per axis, with DESI cosmology also in tension. The ~5 citations two months out most plausibly reflect internal traffic within this tightly-knit programme (many references are the authors’ own companion papers), amplified by the paper’s unusually explicit falsification table tied to JUNO, DUNE, Hyper-K, and next-generation \(0\nu\beta\beta\). The reason for broader uptake is not yet visible from the text; the appeal is a concrete, near-term-testable prediction rather than an established measurement. (The acknowledgments also flag heavy reliance on AI tools in the derivations.)

Week of Friday, August 7, 2026

This week’s radar leans toward methodological reckoning rather than new measurements. Two entries are LLM/ML tooling for physics workflows (one autonomous ROOT agent, one scaling-law study for jet generation); two are theory papers that reframe or correct existing formalisms (a control theorem for string inflation, a partonic rethink of bubble-collision particle production); and one proposes a new collider observable for quantum-correlation tests. The common thread: papers offering filters, corrections, and invariants that let the community decide which existing approaches to trust.

arXiv:2605.17318 — RooAgent: An LLM Agent for Root-Based High Energy Physics Analysis (Aman Desai)

RooAgent wraps PyROOT routines (inspection, cutflows, fitting, significance, CLs) as tools an LLM invokes from plain-language prompts, supporting both a LangGraph agent (GPT-4.1 via Copilot, DeepSeek-V3 via Ollama) and an MCP server for Claude. The paper benchmarks it on simulated \(pp\to ZH\) events, a toy statistical search, and ATLAS \(H\to ZZ^*\to 4\ell\) open data, reporting 19/20 single-task successes with runtimes of 7–225 s and consistent numerical results across backends.

With ~6 citations since posting, its pull is timing rather than a headline result: the reference list shows a burst of “agentic AI for HEP analysis” papers in late 2025–2026, and RooAgent is a concrete, PyPI-installable entry in that fast-moving conversation. It is the kind of tool paper that accumulates citations by being cited alongside peers as evidence that LLM-driven analysis is becoming practical. The candid documentation of failure modes (case-sensitive tree lookups, model-dependent binning choices) also makes it a useful honest datapoint.

arXiv:2605.10197 — Controlled Penumbral Inflation from Monodromic Valleys (Pirzada et al.)

The paper derives a covariant “control theorem” for single-field inflation in the penumbra — the near-boundary region of complex-structure moduli space. It shows that a monodromy-preserving odd branch term rotates the light trajectory saxionically and produces a plateau when \(\Delta\equiv p+2\nu-q>0\), with single-clock control requiring \(p<2\) (or \(p=2\) with a large entropy-mass prefactor). The benchmark predicts \(r\sim10^{-3}\) with correlated running \(\alpha_s\simeq-r/2\), a target for LiteBIRD/CMB-S4, and provides an exactly solvable “invariant attractor equation.”

At ~6 citations in a few months, the appeal is that it turns a qualitative regime into a decision principle: a local EFT filter that flags which monodromic valleys deserve expensive global string embedding before that work is attempted. That framing — “filtered search” over the landscape — plus a falsifiable observable corridor gives both string phenomenologists and CMB modelers something to cite. Honestly, with such a specialized construction and dense formalism, some early citations are likely from the immediate collaborators/community around the cited penumbra literature (Lanza–Westphal, Grimm) rather than broad uptake.

arXiv:2605.13250 — Determining the Spin-Analyzing Powers via Invariants of the Spin Correlation Matrices and Probing the Bell Non-Locality at the Lepton Colliders (Dianwei Wang et al.)

For two-fermion production via single-mediator exchange at \(e^+e^-\) colliders, the authors prove that \(\mathrm{Tr}[C]\) of the spin correlation matrix is a rotation-invariant, scattering-angle-independent quantity, equal to \(+1\), \(+1\), \(-3\) for gauge-boson, CP-even scalar, and CP-odd scalar exchange respectively. This lets one fix the product of spin-analyzing powers \(\alpha_{F_a}\alpha_{F_b}\) and reconstruct \(C\) from angular correlations alone — thereby evading the no-go theorem that otherwise blocks Bell tests at colliders. They apply it to \(e^+e^-\to J/\psi\to\Lambda\bar\Lambda\) at BESIII, finding CHSH-Horodecki violation for \(|\cos\theta_\Lambda|<0.44\).

The ~5 citations reflect a hot, crowded subfield (the reference list runs past 130 collider-entanglement papers) where the no-go theorem on measuring spin-analyzing powers is the central obstruction. A claimed clean invariant that circumvents it is exactly the kind of tool the community was waiting for. Given the authors’ own dense self-citation cluster (Pei, Wu, Li et al.), a share of early citations is internal, but the observable’s generality gives it broader traction potential.

arXiv:2605.28940 — Neural Scaling Laws for Jet Generation (Oz Amram et al.)

This is the first systematic study of neural scaling laws for generative jet modeling, training the autoregressive OmniJet-\(\alpha\) on ~180M real jets from the CMS-derived Aspen Open Jets dataset. Clean power-law scaling appears with model size \(N\) (with the SWD physics metric monotonically tracking the next-token-prediction loss), but scaling with dataset size and compute is weak and saturates early. The authors introduce a learnable window diagnostic and show it is dramatically smaller for jet NTP (\(W=3.2\) nats, effective perplexity 1330) than for language (\(W=8.7\), perplexity ~5), attributing the rapid saturation to QCD stochasticity and the mismatch between \(p_T\)-ordering and parton-shower causality.

At ~5 citations, the draw is a genuinely surprising negative-ish result on real detector data: scaling laws — the organizing principle of modern foundation models — behave qualitatively differently for generic QCD jets. That directly informs anyone planning large pretraining runs on collider data, and the “learnable window” concept is a portable, quotable diagnostic. The authorship overlap with the AOJ dataset and OmniJet ecosystem also naturally seeds citations from that active foundation-model community.

arXiv:2607.15279 — Particle production from bubble collisions (Anish Ghoshal et al.)

Ghoshal, Pal, and Strumia argue that the standard “off-shell” Schwinger-like formalism for heavy-particle production in ultra-relativistic bubble collisions is unphysical: the off-shell propagator \(\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi(p^2)\) depends on field-space parameterization (manifesting as gauge dependence for vectors) and can give nonzero rates even in free theories, while the perfectly-elastic collision assumption parametrically overestimates hard production. They propose instead a partonic picture — Lorentz-contracted walls undergo near-free passage, and hard production arises from on-shell scatterings among wall quanta, giving rates suppressed by \(1/\gamma^2\) in the hard regime. They recompute scalar/fermion/vector/graviton cross sections and revisit dark matter, leptogenesis, and gravitational-wave implications, finding substantially reduced (though not negligible) yields.

The ~5 citations in a couple of months signal a provocative correction to a whole line of work (13 papers are grouped as using the flawed approach). Any claim that a widely-used mechanism was overestimated — and that DM/leptogenesis/GW signals must be revised downward — forces responses from exactly those authors, which is a reliable citation driver.

Week of Friday, July 31, 2026

This week’s radar tilts heavily toward methods and machinery: two of the four papers are software releases (an LLM-driven phase-transition pipeline and a spectral Feynman-integral solver), and two probe early-universe or quantum-information observables. The recurring signal is that the community is rapidly citing infrastructure — agentic frameworks, transport codes — and staking positions in the fast-moving debate over primordial-black-hole formation from phase transitions.

arXiv:2606.19425 — LeWRON: Agentic Analysis of Electroweak Phase Transitions (Isaac R. Wang)

LeWRON is an LLM-agent framework that takes a BSM Lagrangian (or a literature target) and orchestrates the full electroweak-phase-transition pipeline: constructing the finite-temperature effective potential, tracking thermal histories, computing nucleation, and predicting gravitational-wave spectra. Its design leans on role-separated model calls (deterministic coding vs. reasoning agents), CAS-assisted symbolic derivations, audited Pydantic artifacts, and human checkpoints. The paper validates in “reproduction mode” (recovering the singlet-scalar nucleation temperatures of Ref. [71] to <1%, and a 2HDM+a potential) and “discovery mode” (ALP-Higgs and 2HDM+S), where it claims to improve on prior renormalization treatments.

The ~5 citations in roughly two months reflect a surge of “hep-ph agent” papers (HEPTAPOD, MadAgents, DarkAgents, Collider-Bench, all cited here) rather than physics results per se. LeWRON is positioned as the first domain-specialized agent for end-to-end EWPT analysis, and it is being cited as part of this emerging cluster establishing what agentic workflows for particle theory can do — a claim to a niche everyone in that subfield now references.

arXiv:2606.11296 — Tripartite Entanglement in \(e^+e^- \to t\bar{t}Z\) (Dorival Gonçalves et al.)

No HTML rendering was available, so this entry rests on title and metadata alone. The paper studies tripartite quantum entanglement in the three-body final state \(t\bar{t}Z\) at a future lepton collider — extending the now-active program of measuring entanglement and Bell-inequality violation in top-quark pairs to a genuinely three-party system.

With ~5 citations shortly after posting, it appears to be riding the broader wave of collider quantum-information studies that took off after entanglement was established as an observable in \(t\bar{t}\). Tripartite entanglement is a natural and less-explored frontier, so an early concrete proposal in the \(e^+e^-\) environment plausibly serves as an anchor reference for follow-up work. Without the text I cannot verify the specific entanglement measures used or the headline sensitivity, and the citation pattern is best read as topical momentum rather than a single decisive result.

arXiv:2606.26691 — CHESS: CHEbyshev pSeudo-Spectral transport for Feynman integral differential equations (Yuanche Liu et al.)

CHESS is a Wolfram Language package for high-precision one-dimensional transport of \(\epsilon\)-factorized differential equations for Feynman master integrals. Given a path-pulled-back connection \(B(t)\) — supplied either as constant-matrix/scalar-one-form data or as a direct numerical evaluator — it uses Chebyshev–Lobatto spectral collocation, solving the triangular \(\epsilon\)-hierarchy sequentially with one factored node matrix acting on many right-hand sides, plus residue-based regularization of spurious endpoint singularities. Benchmarks are striking: a physical-region two-loop six-point double-pentagon reaches agreement with AMFlow at the \(10^{-101}\) level; three-loop five-point families with up to 734 masters complete in under 80 s; and a fixed-protocol comparison shows 8.8–17.9× speedups over DiffExp. An elliptic non-planar-triangle example exercises the direct-matrix mode.

The ~5 citations in about a month signal a needed tool: multi-scale, high-multiplicity integral families (three-loop five-point, two-loop six-point) are exactly the current bottlenecks, and CHESS slots into the DiffExp/SeaSyde/AMFlow ecosystem with demonstrable performance gains. Its deliberately narrow scope — transport only, given a canonical basis and path — makes it easy to adopt as a drop-in propagation layer.

arXiv:2605.11332 — Reviving primordial black hole formation in slow first-order phase transitions (Wen-Yuan Ai et al.)

This paper is a direct rebuttal to recent claims that PBH formation from slow first-order phase transitions is ruled out. The problem: bubble simulations extract the density contrast in the spatially flat gauge, \(\delta^{(F)}\), whereas the collapse threshold is defined in comoving gauge, and \(\delta^{(F)}\simeq 10\,\delta^{(C)}\) at horizon re-entry — apparently killing the mechanism. Using a gauge-invariant treatment that avoids the superhorizon approximation, the authors confirm the suppression, but then argue the mechanism survives: if post-transition reheating is slow (\(\Gamma_\phi < H_*\)), the Universe enters an early matter-dominated era in which even small overdensities grow and collapse. They derive a model-independent condition (\(\beta/H_n \lesssim 18\), EMD duration \(\gtrsim 10^3 H_*^{-1}\)), extend Doroshkevich statistics to non-Gaussian \(P(\delta_k)\), and realize the scenario in a conformal \(U(1)_X\) model yielding asteroid-mass PBHs that can be all of dark matter.

The ~5 citations since May reflect its role in a live, contested debate (Franciolini et al.; Wang et al. are the foils). A “the mechanism isn’t dead after all” paper in an active controversy naturally attracts rapid engagement from both camps.

arXiv:2605.15259 — TransitionListener v2.0: Robust gravitational wave predictions for cosmological phase transitions (Jonas Matuszak et al.)

No rendering was available, so this reads from title and metadata alone. The name signals a public code release: a “v2.0” update to a tool that maps particle-physics models of first-order phase transitions onto predicted stochastic GW spectra, with “robust” suggesting improved treatment of the notoriously uncertain transition parameters (nucleation rate, bubble-wall dynamics) that plague these forecasts. ~5 citations since mid-May. If the accumulation reflects anything, it is the standard pattern for phenomenology tooling: a maintained, ready-to-run pipeline gets cited by every follow-up model study that needs GW predictions without rebuilding the machinery. arXiv:2606.02744 — IterInt: Evaluating iterated integrals via differential equations (Gideon Baur et al.)

Text unavailable; I rely on the title plus a corroborating external reference. Notably, this paper is cited within Czakon–Tancredi (arXiv:2606.30354) below, which describes it as “a package that transforms iterated integrals into a differential-equation system and solves it numerically,” with the actual integration performed in C++ under a Mathematica interface. That framing — iterated integrals as the formal solutions of canonical differential equations — places IterInt squarely in the current push to make ε-factorized systems numerically tractable. ~5 citations since early June. The likely driver is the same one animating the whole subfield right now: a concrete, usable tool for evaluating the transcendental functions that appear beyond the polylogarithmic case, arriving just as multi-loop practitioners need exactly that.

arXiv:2605.21419 — Cosmological Collider Signatures from Right-Handed Neutrino Loop (Jingtao You et al.)

No rendering available. The title places this in the cosmological-collider program, where heavy particles active during inflation imprint oscillatory, mass-dependent features in the primordial non-Gaussianity of curvature perturbations. The novelty signaled is a loop-level computation: right-handed neutrinos contributing at one loop rather than tree level, which is the natural way to probe fermions whose masses might otherwise leave no signal. This connects the collider formalism to leptogenesis-relevant physics, plausibly explaining early interest — it ties a fashionable inflationary observable to the seesaw sector. ~5 citations since May. Without the text I cannot verify the specific amplitude or the predicted signal strength.

arXiv:2604.25726 — Imprint of domain wall annihilation on induced gravitational waves (Rishav Roshan)

Roshan studies a minimal Z₂-symmetric real-scalar extension of the SM in which domain walls, made unstable by a small bias term, annihilate and dump most of their energy into the long-lived scalar. If that scalar dominates before decaying, it drives an early matter-dominated era that dramatically amplifies scalar-induced GWs (working in the non-linear regime via the Fernandez et al. simulation framework), while its eventual decay injects entropy that dilutes the direct domain-wall signal. The headline result is a distinctive double-peaked spectrum — a higher-frequency peak from wall annihilation, a lower-frequency peak from enhanced induced GWs — with both peaks potentially visible to THEIA and μARES, so detection in one band predicts the other.

The paper’s appeal is the correlated multi-band signature from a single symmetry-breaking event, a cleaner falsifiable target than isolated-source studies. ~5 citations in roughly two months, likely from the active domain-wall/induced-GW community (many self-citations to King, Borah, and collaborators are visible), reflecting momentum rather than settled impact.

arXiv:2606.30354 — Solution of Canonical Differential Equations for Integrals on Arbitrary Geometries (Michał Czakon et al.)

Czakon and Tancredi tackle a real bottleneck: canonical ε-factorized systems beyond the polylogarithmic case have connection matrices containing transcendental functions — elliptic or Calabi-Yau periods — for which closed forms are often unavailable, blocking numerical evaluation. Their key observation is that these functions are not arbitrary: since the original master integrals obey rational differential equations, so do the transcendental building blocks. Adjoining these auxiliary equations yields an enlarged purely-rational system solvable by standard numerical ODE methods, with correct analytic continuation guaranteed automatically. They demonstrate on the 220 two-loop masters for di-jet and γ+jet production through a heavy-quark loop — 16 square roots plus elliptic periods — and ship a lightweight, commercial-software-free C++ package built on Boost’s Bulirsch-Stoer solver, achieving sub-second double-precision evaluations.

~5 citations since late June. The draw is a general, deployable recipe that sidesteps the hardest obstacle to using canonical bases in realistic amplitudes: no special-function implementation needed, universally applicable to any Feynman integral. Given the surge of parallel efforts (Baur–Duhr, Liu–Zhang) it references, this lands in a hot and crowded space, positioned as the production-oriented option.

Week of Friday, July 17, 2026

This week’s radar leans toward infrastructure and framing: papers that hand the community a reusable tool, a systematic classification, or a reference review rather than a single headline number. Two automation/formalism papers (finite-temperature matching, multi-axion classification), a major experimental-systematics review, and a collider quantum-information study round out a set where the common thread is enabling other people’s calculations. All four sit at five citations, consistent with their recent posting.

arXiv:2604.21332 — Disentangling new physics with quantum entanglement in \(t\bar{t}\) production at future lepton colliders (Masato Arai et al.)

Arai, Mawatari and Okada compute quantum-information observables—the entanglement marker \(D_{\min}\), the concurrence, and the maximal CHSH parameter \(B_{\max}\)—for \(l^-l^+\to t\bar t\) in the SM and three s-channel extensions: a scalar mediator, a \(U(1)_{B-L}\) \(Z'\), and Randall–Sundrum KK gravitons. Working from helicity amplitudes and the two-qubit density matrix, they find each Lorentz structure reorganizes spin correlations distinctively: the scalar reduces entanglement, \(U(1)_{B-L}\) induces energy-dependent interference distortions, and RS gravitons produce qualitatively new high-energy angular structures with multiple local extrema. Results are cross-checked against MadGraph event-by-event spin-density-matrix simulation.

At ~5 citations since April, the paper rides the fast-growing “entanglement at colliders” wave triggered by the ATLAS/CMS \(t\bar t\) entanglement observations. Its appeal is likely as a concrete worked template for applying these observables as BSM probes at future lepton colliders (ILC, muon collider)—a natural next-step reference for a subfield actively hunting for new discriminating variables. The honest read: citations reflect topical momentum more than a singular result.

arXiv:2605.06787 — The structure of multi-axion solutions to the strong CP problem (Mario Fernández Navarro et al.)

Fernández Navarro and collaborators build a general framework for multi-axion solutions, organizing the theory space by two structural conditions: whether a PQ symmetry is broken only by QCD (“QCD-PQ”) versus more generally (“general-PQ”), and whether the QED and QCD anomaly vectors \(\bm E\) and \(\bm N\) are parallel (universal \(E_i/N_i\)) or not. Relaxing the assumptions behind the known sum rule of Ref. [89], they derive a generalized \(N\)-axion sum rule (via Sherman–Morrison inversion) that admits values above or below one, and show axions can now populate regions to the left of the QCD band—not only the right. They map these regimes onto UV completions, noting e.g. that non-universal anomaly coefficients would rule out simple GUTs.

The ~5 citations in roughly a month likely reflect its value as a classification scheme: it gives the axion-search community a clean language (“where can axions sit relative to the QCD line, and why”) that motivates broadening experimental targets beyond the canonical band. Framework papers with explicit sum rules and a theory-space figure tend to become quick reference points.

arXiv:2605.28671 — CP-violation or Nuclear Excitation: Reviewing the Role of Neutrino Interaction Model Uncertainties on Accelerator-Based Neutrino Oscillation Measurements (Stephen Dolan et al.)

Dolan, Pickering, Stowell, Wilkinson and Wret provide a Reports on Progress in Physics review of how few-GeV neutrino–nucleus interaction modeling limits accelerator oscillation measurements at T2K, NOvA, Hyper-K and DUNE. It walks through neutrino–nucleon cross sections (CCQE, resonant/shallow/deep inelastic, diffractive/coherent pion production, radiative corrections) and nuclear effects (spectral functions, RPA, FSI, 2p2h), then quantifies why near-to-far-detector extrapolation does not trivially cancel systematics—different targets, detector responses, oscillated fluxes, and \(\nu_e/\nu_\mu\) cross-section differences. A recurring device is “model spread” across event generators (NEUT, NuWro, GENIE, GiBUU) as a lower bound on interaction uncertainty.

As a comprehensive, up-to-date review with extensive tables and generator comparisons, it is a natural citation anchor: authors of DUNE/Hyper-K systematics studies can point to it for context and for the argument that interaction uncertainties are “the least clear” path to control. The headline claim is clean and striking: for the s-channel QED process e⁻e⁺→ff̄, 100% transversely polarized beams produce a maximally entangled Bell state across the entire phase space, independent of scattering angle and energy—in sharp contrast to the unpolarized case, where maximal entanglement requires ultra-relativistic central scattering.
