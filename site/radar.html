A weekly sweep of INSPIRE-HEP for recent papers the community is citing heavily but the daily deep dives missed.

Week of Friday, July 17, 2026

This week’s radar leans toward infrastructure and framing: papers that hand the community a reusable tool, a systematic classification, or a reference review rather than a single headline number. Two automation/formalism papers (finite-temperature matching, multi-axion classification), a major experimental-systematics review, and a collider quantum-information study round out a set where the common thread is enabling other people’s calculations. All four sit at five citations, consistent with their recent posting.

arXiv:2604.21332 — Disentangling new physics with quantum entanglement in \(t\bar{t}\) production at future lepton colliders (Masato Arai et al.)

Arai, Mawatari and Okada compute quantum-information observables—the entanglement marker \(D_{\min}\), the concurrence, and the maximal CHSH parameter \(B_{\max}\)—for \(l^-l^+\to t\bar t\) in the SM and three s-channel extensions: a scalar mediator, a \(U(1)_{B-L}\) \(Z'\), and Randall–Sundrum KK gravitons. Working from helicity amplitudes and the two-qubit density matrix, they find each Lorentz structure reorganizes spin correlations distinctively: the scalar reduces entanglement, \(U(1)_{B-L}\) induces energy-dependent interference distortions, and RS gravitons produce qualitatively new high-energy angular structures with multiple local extrema. Results are cross-checked against MadGraph event-by-event spin-density-matrix simulation.

At ~5 citations since April, the paper rides the fast-growing “entanglement at colliders” wave triggered by the ATLAS/CMS \(t\bar t\) entanglement observations. Its appeal is likely as a concrete worked template for applying these observables as BSM probes at future lepton colliders (ILC, muon collider)—a natural next-step reference for a subfield actively hunting for new discriminating variables. The honest read: citations reflect topical momentum more than a singular result.

arXiv:2605.06787 — The structure of multi-axion solutions to the strong CP problem (Mario Fernández Navarro et al.)

Fernández Navarro and collaborators build a general framework for multi-axion solutions, organizing the theory space by two structural conditions: whether a PQ symmetry is broken only by QCD (“QCD-PQ”) versus more generally (“general-PQ”), and whether the QED and QCD anomaly vectors \(\bm E\) and \(\bm N\) are parallel (universal \(E_i/N_i\)) or not. Relaxing the assumptions behind the known sum rule of Ref. [89], they derive a generalized \(N\)-axion sum rule (via Sherman–Morrison inversion) that admits values above or below one, and show axions can now populate regions to the left of the QCD band—not only the right. They map these regimes onto UV completions, noting e.g. that non-universal anomaly coefficients would rule out simple GUTs.

The ~5 citations in roughly a month likely reflect its value as a classification scheme: it gives the axion-search community a clean language (“where can axions sit relative to the QCD line, and why”) that motivates broadening experimental targets beyond the canonical band. Framework papers with explicit sum rules and a theory-space figure tend to become quick reference points.

arXiv:2605.28671 — CP-violation or Nuclear Excitation: Reviewing the Role of Neutrino Interaction Model Uncertainties on Accelerator-Based Neutrino Oscillation Measurements (Stephen Dolan et al.)

Dolan, Pickering, Stowell, Wilkinson and Wret provide a Reports on Progress in Physics review of how few-GeV neutrino–nucleus interaction modeling limits accelerator oscillation measurements at T2K, NOvA, Hyper-K and DUNE. It walks through neutrino–nucleon cross sections (CCQE, resonant/shallow/deep inelastic, diffractive/coherent pion production, radiative corrections) and nuclear effects (spectral functions, RPA, FSI, 2p2h), then quantifies why near-to-far-detector extrapolation does not trivially cancel systematics—different targets, detector responses, oscillated fluxes, and \(\nu_e/\nu_\mu\) cross-section differences. A recurring device is “model spread” across event generators (NEUT, NuWro, GENIE, GiBUU) as a lower bound on interaction uncertainty.

As a comprehensive, up-to-date review with extensive tables and generator comparisons, it is a natural citation anchor: authors of DUNE/Hyper-K systematics studies can point to it for context and for the argument that interaction uncertainties are “the least clear” path to control. The ~5 citations so soon after a May posting reflect the standard trajectory of an authoritative review that consolidates a sprawling literature into one referenceable document.

arXiv:2604.21972 — Matchotter: An Automated Tool for Dimensional Reduction at Finite Temperature (Javier Fuentes-Martín et al.)

Fuentes-Martín, López Miras and Moreno-Sánchez present Matchotter, a finite-temperature module inside the Matchete package that automates one-loop dimensional reduction—integrating out heavy Matsubara modes to build the purely spatial 3D EFT used for cosmological phase-transition computations—for generic Lagrangians including SMEFT. They adapt functional matching techniques to the thermal path integral (identifying the EFT with the “hard region” of the effective action), handle Lorentz-breaking sum-integrals, and additionally automate supersoft matching (integrating out Debye-massive temporal gauge bosons). The tool is validated against SU(N) Yang–Mills, the Abelian Higgs model, a Yukawa toy model, and the electroweak SMEFT, with benchmark timings from seconds to ~30 minutes.

The headline selling point is systematic inclusion of higher-dimensional operators, filling a gap left by the widely used DRalgo (restricted mostly to renormalizable interactions). Given rising interest in higher-precision phase-transition predictions for gravitational-wave observables, a public, automated code addressing a “tedious and highly error-prone” bottleneck is exactly the kind of deliverable that accrues citations quickly—~5 already—as groups adopt it for BSM phase-transition studies.

Week of Sunday, July 12, 2026

This week’s radar leans overwhelmingly toward the electron-positron and precision frontier. Two clusters dominate: quantum-information observables (entanglement, spin correlations, and “magic”) reconstructed from collider final states, and the τ lepton’s electromagnetic properties as a precision probe of new physics at future machines. The common thread is that both programs are being sharpened in anticipation of FCC-ee, a muon collider, and a polarized Belle II/SuperKEKB—and both are converging on the same reactions (e⁺e⁻→ττ, DIS dijets, ee→ff̄), examined through complementary lenses.

arXiv:2604.11887 — Spin Correlation and Quantum Entanglement of Fermion Pairs in Transversely Polarized e⁻e⁺ Collisions (Yi-Jing Fang et al.)

The headline claim is clean and striking: for the s-channel QED process e⁻e⁺→ff̄, 100% transversely polarized beams produce a maximally entangled Bell state across the entire phase space, independent of scattering angle and energy—in sharp contrast to the unpolarized case, where maximal entanglement requires ultra-relativistic central scattering. The authors trace this to the coherent |RL⟩+|LR⟩ superposition selected by the vector current, verified both via the explicit spin density matrix and a “diagonal basis” argument at amplitude level. For electroweak processes (tt̄, ττ, bb̄) the picture is modulated by the axial/vector coupling ratio, with the ττ case remaining nearly maximally entangled thanks to the accidental (1−4s²_W)≈0 cancellation, while bb̄ is diluted.

With ~11 citations shortly after posting, the appeal is a concrete, actionable proposal: transverse beam polarization as a tunable knob for engineering entanglement at colliders. It slots directly into the fast-growing collider-quantum-information literature (the introduction alone cites dozens of recent entries), offering both a provocative “always maximally entangled” result and a practical experimental handle for CEPC/FCC-ee.

arXiv:2604.14281 — Probing τ lepton dipole moments at future Lepton Colliders (Dario Buttazzo et al.)

This paper maps the sensitivity of FCC-ee and a multi-TeV muon collider to the τ anomalous magnetic moment a_τ and electric dipole moment d_τ, working in SMEFT with the dipole operators C_τB, C_τW. It surveys many channels—e⁺e⁻→ττ, γγ→ττ, H→ττγ, associated μ⁺μ⁻→ττH, and vector-boson scattering—and finds them highly complementary. Headline numbers: FCC-ee’s γγ→ττ gives |Δa_τ|≲3×10⁻⁵ (linear NP effects dominant, driven by the Tera-Z luminosity), e⁺e⁻→ττ yields the best |d_τ|≲2.5×10⁻¹⁸ e·cm, while a 14 TeV muon collider improves d_τ by two further orders of magnitude via the energy-growing ττH channel.

The ~8 citations reflect its role as a reference sensitivity study feeding the European Strategy discussion: it quantifies, channel by channel, how future facilities could extend the weakly constrained τ dipole moments by orders of magnitude over LHC/Belle II. Its multi-channel, multi-facility scope makes it a natural point of comparison for anyone assessing the physics case for these machines—including the companion Belle II asymmetry work below.

arXiv:2604.11697 — Quantum entanglement in electron-nucleus collisions: Role of the linearly polarized gluon distribution (Michael Fucilla et al.)

This work computes the spin density matrix of a back-to-back qq̄ pair inclusively produced in electron-nucleus scattering, within kT-factorization/CGC, incorporating gluon saturation and the linearly polarized Weizsäcker-Williams gluon distribution G₂. A notable structural result: after azimuthal averaging in the correlation limit, the density matrix reduces exactly to the one-gluon-exchange collinear result, so multiple gluon exchange does not modify it—demonstrating a robustness/universality of the matrix. Staying differential in the azimuthal angle φ_{P,q}, however, exposes sensitivity to G₂: for bottom pairs at q⊥ of a few GeV, entanglement and magic (stabilizer Rényi entropy) become anisotropic, peaking at φ_{P,q}=π/2 where P⊥ and q⊥ are orthogonal.

With ~6 citations, the draw is its bridge between two active communities—gluon saturation/EIC physics and collider quantum information. It cross-validates the earlier one-gluon-exchange result while showing that intrinsic transverse-momentum effects, unique to the small-x framework, provide a new handle on quantum resources. The EIC-oriented framing (heavy-quark spin transfer to baryons as a measurement channel) gives it a concrete experimental hook.

arXiv:2604.16596 — New Physics Reach through Precision at Future Colliders: a Multi-Pronged Approach (Tommaso Armadillo et al.)

This is a large, tool-oriented projection study interpreting FCC-ee, LEP3, and the Linear Collider Facility in three complementary frameworks: Higgs κ-modifiers, effective Higgs/electroweak couplings, and a global SMEFT fit (61 operators, RGE evolution, linear and quadratic terms, NLO where available). It also matches EFT onto UV-complete benchmarks (composite Higgs, single-particle extensions) and releases an updated open-source SMEFiT framework so the results are reproducible and extensible. Findings include the individual-vs-marginalized fit hierarchy (FCC-ee dominates individual bounds via Tera-Z precision; LCF wins marginalized fits by breaking correlations with higher-energy runs), and a careful treatment of theory-uncertainty scenarios.

The ~6 citations likely reflect two features: its timing alongside the ESPPU 2026 process—it explicitly extends and critiques the Physics Briefing Book methodology—and the public, customizable SMEFiT release, which invites the community to reproduce and re-run collider comparisons. This is the kind of comprehensive, infrastructure-bearing reference that accumulates citations as a standard comparison point for future-collider new-physics reach.

arXiv:2604.16598 — Four-fermion operators, Z-boson exchange, and τ lepton dipole moments (Joël Gogniat et al.)

A focused, careful study of the subleading contaminations to asymmetry-based determinations of a_τ and d_τ in e⁺e⁻→ττ at B-factory energies, relevant to a polarized SuperKEKB upgrade. The authors show tree-level Z-boson exchange enters the transverse-longitudinal asymmetry at ≃3×10⁻⁶ (relevant once precision approaches 10⁻⁶), while four-fermion operator effects are at most ≃10⁻⁵ C v²/Λ². More interesting are two loop-level observations: four-fermion insertions on a photon line generate imaginary parts probing otherwise-inaccessible coefficients (including a novel bound on 4τ operators), and the dipole operator itself generates Im F₂ at one loop—opening a route to a_τ via the normal asymmetry A_N without a polarized electron beam, testable at ≲10⁻⁵ to reach the Schwinger term.

At ~5 citations, this is a companion/precision-theory piece to the τ-dipole program (it cites the FCC/muon-collider study above), valued for cleanly delineating which subleading effects matter at the target precision and for the concrete, near-term A_N strategy realizable already at Belle II—an intermediate goal that gives experimentalists something to aim at now.
