## ATLAS on tZq, and a tooling tide

The one full-dataset collaboration result is ATLAS's $tZq$ measurement ([arXiv:2608.25476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25476)), SM-consistent inclusive and differential cross sections with a top spin asymmetry and dimension-6 limits. Around it the day is diffuse and method-heavy: two clusters that both extend recent threads — Higgs self-coupling phenomenology (the quartic push from 08-19) and amplitude-analysis machinery (yesterday's SINNs). Add a spread of neutrino-detection proposals and a jet-quenching/saturation pairing, and the weight sits on infrastructure more than on any single number.

## Themes

### Top and Higgs, measured and modeled
ATLAS's $tZq$ result [arXiv:2608.25476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25476) measures the top/antitop split, the spin asymmetry ($A=0.34\pm0.15$), and full differential spectra, all SM-consistent, then folds into SMEFT limits — the EFT-as-default reading again on top-sector data. On the self-coupling side, Braathen et al. [arXiv:2608.25705](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25705) identify a model-independent class of higher-order contributions to the trilinear and quartic couplings that shifts the inferred $\kappa_3$ bound from 6.1 to ~4.9, with a public tool — a genuine correction to how self-coupling limits should be read. Liu et al. [arXiv:2608.25504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25504) work $gg\to hh$ in the $U(1)_X$SSM. Together this is the self-coupling program from 08-19 continuing to sharpen.

### Amplitude analysis gets industrialized
A dense methods cluster from hadron spectroscopy. Xiang et al. present CTPWA [arXiv:2608.25963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25963), a GPU covariant-tensor PWA framework two orders of magnitude faster than autograd-based tools, plus a companion phase-space weighting scheme for detector mass-resolution effects [arXiv:2608.25969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25969). Alongside: a two-channel Flatté fit to the LHCb pentaquarks [arXiv:2608.25106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25106) and Ceci et al.'s elastic-unitarity rule linking threshold, pole, and peak [arXiv:2608.25802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25802). Read with yesterday's neural $\pi\pi$ amplitudes, the amplitude-analysis toolchain is clearly in an upgrade cycle.

### Neutrino detection, new handles
Akhmedov et al. [arXiv:2608.25001](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25001) propose resonant induced orbital electron capture for low-energy $\bar\nu_e$ — reaching below the 1.8 MeV IBD threshold, with an atomic-nuclear coincidence tag, aimed at reactor/geo/solar neutrinos. Jesús-Valls' ADoNIS [arXiv:2608.25107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25107) is a fully differentiable neutrino-interaction generator, the autodiff wave reaching event generation once more. NUCLEUS reports state-of-the-art cryogenic CaWO$_4$ detectors ([arXiv:2608.25595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25595), 2.16 eV baseline). Model-building fills in around them (ALP dephasing [arXiv:2608.25069](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25069), democratic-mixing perturbations [arXiv:2608.25430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25430)).

### Jet quenching meets saturation
Caucal et al.'s paired papers [arXiv:2608.25983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25983), [arXiv:2608.25984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25984) recast jet fragmentation in a medium via BMS evolution, with the coherence angle playing the role of the saturation scale and the large-$N_c$ limit mapping onto Balitsky-Kovchegov — a clean conceptual bridge between jet quenching and high-energy QCD. It sits alongside a low-$x$/DIS cluster (Beneke et al. NLP endpoint factorization [arXiv:2608.25502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25502), Bertone et al. absorptive PDFs [arXiv:2608.25724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25724)).

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2608.25476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25476) — Measurement of $tZq$ inclusive and differential cross-sections in $pp$ collisions at $\sqrt{s}=13$ TeV with ATLAS (ATLAS Collaboration)

*Abstract only.* Working in the trilepton channel with the full 140 fb$^{-1}$ Run 2 dataset (three light leptons, 2–4 jets, 1–2 $b$-tags), ATLAS measures the $pp\to t\ell^+\ell^-q$ cross-section for $m_{\ell\ell}>30$ GeV — including resonant and non-resonant dilepton production — as $95.2\,^{+8.5}_{-7.9}\text{(stat.)}\,^{+5.5}_{-5.3}\text{(syst.)}$ fb, consistent with the most advanced SM predictions. Separate top-quark and top-antiquark cross-sections and their ratio are extracted, and absolute and normalised differential spectra are unfolded at both particle and parton level across many kinematic variables. The top-quark spin asymmetry, from the unfolded polarisation angle, is $A=0.34\pm0.14\text{(stat.)}\pm0.06\text{(syst.)}$, again SM-consistent. The differential results are interpreted in SMEFT, setting limits on dimension-6 operators involving the top quark.

**Why it matters:** This is the day's one full-dataset collaboration result and extends the "EFT-as-default" reading of top-sector data — every observable is SM-consistent, so the payoff is the constraint on anomalous top couplings via the spin asymmetry and differential shapes rather than any deviation. The caveat is inherent: with everything SM-compatible the sensitivity is entirely in the operator limits, and the polarisation measurement remains statistics-limited (statistical uncertainty on $A$ more than double the systematic).

### [arXiv:2608.25705](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25705) — Impact of model-independent higher-order contributions to the Higgs-boson self-couplings (Johannes Braathen et al.)

Braathen, Naskar and Weiglein identify a closed, model-independent class of higher-order contributions to $\lambda_{hhh}$ and $\lambda_{hhhh}$: SM-like diagrams containing only the observed Higgs $h$ in the loop but with vertices dressed by loop-corrected coupling modifiers $\bar\kappa_\lambda$, $\bar\kappa_4$. Using an effective-potential setup inspired by leading-order HEFT, they compute these up to two loops in an on-shell tadpole/mass scheme (cross-checked against a FeynArts/FormCalc one-loop diagrammatic calculation), yielding analytic $\kappa_\lambda(\bar\kappa_\lambda,\bar\kappa_4)$ and $\kappa_4(\bar\kappa_\lambda,\bar\kappa_4)$. Confronting these with the current experimental $\kappa_\lambda\in[-0.71,6.1]$, perturbative unitarity, and $\kappa_4$ bounds turns the one-dimensional interval into a non-trivial two-dimensional allowed region. For $\bar\kappa_4=1$ the inferred upper bound tightens from the experimental 6.1 to $\bar\kappa_\lambda<4.9$; blind directions open up at large positive $\bar\kappa_4$ (reaching $\bar\kappa_\lambda\gtrsim8$ before unitarity caps it near 7). A public code, `check_kappas`, implements the mapping.

**Why it matters:** This is a genuine correction to how self-coupling limits should be translated into BSM parameter space, continuing the 08-19 self-coupling thread. The effect is largest precisely where current bounds sit — far from the SM — and would shrink if future measurements land near $\kappa_\lambda\simeq1$. Honest caveats: the results carry residual $\overline{\text{MS}}$ renormalisation-scale dependence (bounds shift noticeably between $Q=m_h$ and $Q=v$, especially at large $|\bar\kappa_4|$), and the extracted $\bar\kappa$'s are themselves scale-dependent quantities requiring careful matching to a UV model.

### [arXiv:2608.25963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25963) — A High Performance Partial Wave Analysis Framework for Hadron Spectroscopy (Benhou Xiang et al.)

Xiang, Fang and Liu present CTPWA, a covariant-tensor PWA framework that exploits the factorisation of the amplitude: kinematic vertex tensors depend only on event four-momenta and decay topology, not fit parameters, so they are precomputed once and cached in GPU memory. The intensity becomes a per-event quadratic form $I=\boldsymbol\theta_a^\dagger\mathbf{B}\boldsymbol\theta_a$, letting the negative log-likelihood, its analytic gradient, and full analytic Hessian be evaluated as batched dense linear algebra entirely on-device, with L-BFGS minimisation also on the GPU — eliminating graph re-tracing, host-device transfers, and per-iteration allocation. Benchmarks (10 channels, 3 polarisations) show per-iteration cost flat at 0.4–2.8 ms versus linear growth to ~400 ms for conventional pipelines; at $10^6$ events NLL+gradient takes ~2 ms (vs 23 ms GPU autograd, 2.3 s CPU autograd) and the analytic Hessian ~6 ms (vs 0.75 s and 75 s), while conventional pipelines exhaust device memory. A realistic $J/\psi\to K^+K^-\eta$ fit with 16 resonances, 33 couplings and $10^6$ events converges in ~7 s (fixed resonance parameters) or ~100 s (floating), after a one-time ~94 s precompute.

**Why it matters:** This is exactly the infrastructure high-statistics BESIII/LHCb analyses need, part of the day's amplitude-analysis upgrade cycle alongside the neural $\pi\pi$ work. The analytic Hessian makes uncertainty estimation practical at high statistics. Caveat: gains depend on the likelihood being quadratic in couplings; floating resonance parameters break the phase-space pre-averaging and raise per-iteration cost roughly sevenfold, and memory reaches ~32 GB in that mode.
