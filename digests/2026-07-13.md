## The vibe today

After a fortnight of tool-building and precision-machinery papers, today reads like the phenomenologists reclaiming the floor. It's a broad, model-builder's-and-spectroscopist's day — no single collaboration bombshell, but a wide field of theory: mass matrices and majorons, reheating histories, a dozen exotic hadrons, and neutrinos pressed into service as probes of everything from the neutron-star equation of state to the Earth's deep interior. The through-line is *neutrinos as instruments*. They turn up as terrestrial gauges of in-medium hyperon potentials, as scanners of the planet's density profile, and as messengers filtered through the dark-matter spikes around active galactic nuclei. The community is increasingly comfortable treating the neutrino not as the object of study but as the flashlight.

The other half of the room is doing hadron spectroscopy and dark-sector model-building, both in a consolidating mood. Exotic states — molecular, compact, tetra- and pentaquark — get computed across half a dozen frameworks, while the dark sector keeps its now-habitual reliance on discrete symmetries and non-standard reheating to fix relic abundances. And two threads the memory has been tracking resurface cleanly: the $b\to s\ell\ell$ long-distance-versus-$C_9$ debate gets a fresh data-driven entry, and the Belle II GNN calorimeter trigger — whose radiation-reliability was stress-tested back on July 3 — is now reported fully commissioned and running at low latency.

A cerebral, diffuse day, then: lots of frameworks, several genuinely useful reinterpretations, and one nice instance of a bound being quietly deflated.

## Themes

### Neutrinos as terrestrial probes

The day's most distinctive cluster is a trilogy by Nowak ([arXiv:2607.09255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09255), [arXiv:2607.09273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09273), [arXiv:2607.09280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09280)) arguing that charged-current accelerator-neutrino interactions on argon produce $\Lambda$ and $\Sigma$ hyperons *inside* the nucleus, turning hyperon final-state interactions at SBND and DUNE into a laboratory handle on the in-medium potentials that govern the neutron-star "hyperon puzzle." The forecasts are sobering but honest: a naive Fisher analysis promises sub-MeV precision on $U_\Lambda$, but marginalizing over the low-density slope degrades that to several MeV, and $YN$ cross-section systematics dominate $U_\Sigma$. The third paper links this all the way to quarkyonic hyperon onset and the maximum mass. Alongside, Zhuridov et al. ([arXiv:2607.09591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09591)) push neutrino oscillation tomography of the Earth to second-order perturbation theory with a multi-shell scheme that handles density jumps between layers — again casting GeV atmospheric neutrinos at KM3NeT/DUNE/Hyper-K as geophysical instruments.

### AGN neutrino–dark matter scattering

A small but pointed exchange. Abbaslu et al. ([arXiv:2607.08833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08833)) build a model in which high-energy AGN neutrinos scatter off the dark-matter spike around supermassive black holes, distorting the flavor ratio that IceCube-Gen2 will soon measure precisely. On the very same day, Farzan ([arXiv:2607.08904](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08904)) revisits the flux-attenuation bounds derived from exactly this physics and shows they are far more model-dependent than advertised: for a light vector mediator well below the collision energy, the neutrino energy loss — and hence the attenuation — becomes negligible, relaxing the limits substantially. It's a healthy reminder that "spike constraints" on neutrino-DM scattering carry hidden microphysics assumptions.

### Dark relics from discrete symmetries and reheating

The memory's running theme — discrete symmetries doing double duty and reheating histories setting abundances — is well represented. Fujisawa et al. ([arXiv:2607.09131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09131)) build minimal majoron dark matter from an exact $Z_N\subset U(1)_{B-L}$ gauge symmetry, with Planck-suppressed operators generating the mass; $Z_5$ is excluded by neutrino decays but $Z_7$ predicts a 1–10 MeV majoron probeable by COSI. Fernández Navarro ([arXiv:2607.08814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08814)) identifies a "minimal high-quality QCD axion" as a 5D Wilson-line phase with exponential quality protection and no bulk QCD. On the reheating side, Mambrini's "seesaw reheating" ([arXiv:2607.08828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08828)) ties the reheating temperature to lepton-number breaking and sterile-neutrino dark matter, while Barman et al. ([arXiv:2607.09282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09282)) study leptogenesis with sub-sphaleron reheating temperatures — a direct sequel to yesterday's low-reheating leptogenesis result. Borah et al.'s "Dark Phoenix" ([arXiv:2607.08838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08838)) is the most inventive: dark matter that thermally overproduces is rescued by a transient post-freeze-out decay window opened by finite-temperature mass effects and shut by a first-order phase transition.

### Flavor windows in B decays

Bordone et al. ([arXiv:2607.09458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09458)) give an updated data-driven dispersive analysis of $B\to K^*\mu^+\mu^-$ using LHCb's full angular distribution, finding $C_9$ below its SM value but significantly closer once long-distance contributions are constrained by data — tension no more than $2\sigma$. This is the natural continuation of Ciuchini et al.'s July 3 argument that hadronic effects, not a short-distance shift, drive the anomalies. Complementing it, Colangelo et al. ([arXiv:2607.09216](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09216)) fit Belle II $b\to u$ data in a SMEFT-consistent generalized Hamiltonian, and Zhang et al. ([arXiv:2607.09573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09573)) deliver the first NLO SCET sum-rule form factors for $B\to D_1(2420)/D_1'(2430)$.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2607.08904](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08904) — Revisiting bounds on neutrino dark matter interaction at spikes (Yasaman Farzan)

**The finding:** The much-quoted "spike" bounds on neutrino–DM scattering — derived from the attenuation of AGN neutrino fluxes traversing the dense DM cusp around supermassive black holes — collapse for light vector mediators. Farzan's point is kinematic: the cascade equation contains both a loss term ($-\sigma\Phi$) and a regeneration term (the down-scattered flux re-populating each energy bin). Previous analyses (Cline et al., Cline–Puel, Dixit–Mohlabeng–Razzaque) kept only the loss term, valid only if each collision removes a neutrino from the beam. But when $m_{Z'}^2/(2m_{DM})\ll E_\nu$, the differential cross section is sharply peaked at $E'_\nu\simeq E_\nu$ — scattering is nearly forward, energy loss negligible, and the two terms cancel. Defining an effective suppression factor $f$, for $\Phi\propto E_\nu^{-3.2}$ and $m_{Z'}^2/m_{DM}=2$ GeV, Farzan finds $2\times10^{-3}<f<10^{-2}$ across the 1.5–15 TeV NGC 1068 window — relaxing the "most stringent" bound by up to ~1000×.

**Why it matters:** Landing the same day as Abbaslu et al.'s paper *deriving* AGN flavor signals from this same scattering, it's a clean cautionary counterpoint: "spike constraints" on $\sigma_{\nu DM}$ carry hidden microphysics (mediator mass, symmetric vs. asymmetric DM, flavor structure). Farzan further notes symmetric DM can annihilate to $\nu\bar\nu$, and flavor-selective couplings can suppress attenuation while still distorting flavor ratios — leaving room for IceCube-Gen2/KM3NeT to *discover* spike effects rather than merely exclude them. **Caveat:** the companion analysis establishing the flavor phenomenology is "work in progress," so the positive-signal claims rest partly on unpublished results.

### [arXiv:2607.09273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09273) — Neutrino-induced hyperon final-state interactions as constraints on the in-medium hyperon potential (Jaroslaw Nowak)

**What they did:** Nowak proposes that charged-current accelerator (anti)neutrinos on $^{40}$Ar create $\Lambda,\Sigma$ hyperons *inside* the nucleus, so their final-state interactions become a terrestrial probe of the in-medium potentials $U_Y(\rho)$ that drive the neutron-star "hyperon puzzle." Using the internal StrangeMC generator, three observables respond monotonically to the potentials: the trapped-$\Lambda$ fraction and escaping-$\Lambda$ momentum (for $U_\Lambda$), the escaping-$\Sigma$ momentum, and a kaon-vetoed FSI-$\Sigma^+$ tag (a final state the weak vertex cannot make) for $U_\Sigma$. A detector-level Fisher forecast gives $\delta U_\Lambda\approx0.3$ MeV and $\delta U_\Sigma\approx3$–4 MeV *at fixed* low-density slope $\gamma$. Fed through a GM1 relativistic mean field anchored to established depths, the same potentials yield $M_{\max}=1.94\,M_\odot$ and $\Lambda_{1.4}=1034$.

**The honesty:** The headline sub-MeV number is heavily caveated. Because hyperons are born below saturation, $U_\Lambda(\rho_0)$ and $\gamma$ are 99.8% anti-correlated; marginalizing over $\gamma$ (which the data alone fix only to $\delta\gamma\approx0.8$) degrades $\delta U_\Lambda$ to 5.6 MeV. Two further systematics — the transport prescription (−6 MeV) and $YN$ cross sections (−5/+2 MeV) — push the realistic $U_\Lambda$ budget to ~10 MeV. Worse, the $YN$ uncertainty biases $U_\Sigma$ by $\mathcal{O}(150)$ MeV, not an artifact of the $\Sigma^+$ tag but because the $\Lambda$ spectrum itself is $YN$-sensitive. The propagated $M_{\max}$ is explicitly an *inference* dominated by the external high-density prior, not a neutrino measurement; the ~$0.3\,M_\odot$ EOS-model systematic dwarfs the statistical reach. StrangeMC is uncommissioned against community generators — a stated next step.

### [arXiv:2607.09539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09539) — Top-Antitop Production and Decay at Threshold at the LHC in QCD Perturbation Theory (Paolo Nason et al.)

**What they did:** Nason, Pelliccioli, Re and Rottoli deliver three NLO+PS generators (`thr1`, `thr2`, `bb4l`) incorporating threshold-enhanced (Coulomb/Sommerfeld) corrections in $t\bar t$ production, extending their earlier NRR work up to $(\alpha_s/v)^6$ and, crucially, with finite-width treatment (`thr2` modifies the phase space for off-shell tops; `bb4l` uses the full `b_bbar_4l` off-shell code). In the "ATLAS bin" ($m_{t\bar t}<350$ GeV, $p^\star<50$ GeV) they find total cross sections near 10 pb with a threshold *excess* over baseline NLO of ~4 pb — compatible with the Garzelli et al. NRQCD result and with the 8.5–9.3 pb "$\eta_t$" numbers CMS/ATLAS extract. The perturbative series converges: truncating at $\alpha_s^3$ already captures the full result to better than 1 pb, so all-orders GFRW resummation is unnecessary at LHC resolution.

**Why it matters:** This directly addresses the recent near-threshold "toponium" observation, and sharpens the interpretation. The authors argue forcefully that (i) the pseudoscalar dominance follows from the Landau–Yang theorem for gluon fusion regardless of bound-state formation; (ii) toponium is *part of* the perturbative QCD cross section, so claiming an "excess above QCD consistent with QCD-predicted toponium" is contradictory; and (iii) the genuine bound-state contribution is small (~2.6–4.5 pb depending on generator/scale) and cannot be cleanly separated from continuum given the top width smearing. A new physical effect: imposing double-top virtuality cuts suppresses the sub-threshold cross section via phase-space reduction, cutting the bound-state yield by ~1 pb — captured by `thr2`/`bb4l` but not `thr1`. **Caveat:** the exact finite-width resummation formula (Appendix A) is derived but not implemented; single-top contamination near threshold requires a full experimental signal/background study the authors defer.
