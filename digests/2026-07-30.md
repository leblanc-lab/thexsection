## A seven-sigma flavor claim and a first for FASER

The day's sharpest result is a theory fit, not a measurement: Bhattacharya et al. [arXiv:2607.27202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27202) extend last year's charmless $B\to PP$ SU(3)$_F$ puzzle to $B\to VV$ and find the discrepancy with the SM SU(3)$_F$ limit growing to above $7\sigma$. On the experimental side, FASER posts the first LHC search for quirks [arXiv:2607.26195](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26195) and ATLAS updates $HH\to b\bar b\tau\tau$ with a mild upward pull [arXiv:2607.26879](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26879). Underneath, a strong lattice-gauge-theory pair and a heavy-ion methods cluster give the day real depth, and the early-universe defect thread the memory has tracked all month resurfaces with GUT-scale symmetry breaking.

## Themes

### Flavor SU(3) under strain
Bhattacharya et al. [arXiv:2607.27202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27202) fit $B\to\rho K^*$ under isospin (acceptable), then $\rho/K^*$ under SU(3)$_F$ ($5.2\sigma$), then the full $\rho,K^*,\phi,\omega$ set ($>7\sigma$). The theoretical input is deliberately minimal — group theory in the exact SU(3)$_F$ limit — so the number is hard to wave away, though whether ~30% SU(3)-breaking rescues it is the open question they flag. This is a different corner from the $b\to s\ell\ell$/$\nu\bar\nu$ modes the week has circled: a hadronic-amplitude anomaly, not a rare FCNC. Lin's geometric CKM reparameterization [arXiv:2607.26681](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26681) sits nearby but is far more speculative.

### Non-Abelian lattice with dynamical fermions
Rouxinol et al. deliver a variational Monte Carlo treatment of the untruncated 2+1D SU(2) theory coupled to staggered fermions — a methods paper [arXiv:2607.26131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26131) and its phase-diagram application [arXiv:2607.26132](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26132), mapping a magnetic-flux transition and a gauge-matter delocalization crossover along the physical coupling line. A neural-network gauge wavefunction plus a Gaussian fermionic correction gives a sign-problem-free route to continuous non-Abelian groups with matter. Ding et al.'s quenched study of static potentials under imaginary rotation [arXiv:2607.26939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26939) rounds out the lattice end.

### Jets as spectroscopy of the medium
Two papers push jet-medium coupling toward genuine observables. Kurkela et al. [arXiv:2607.26851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26851) argue the angular moments of the jet wake isolate non-hydrodynamic modes — higher moments encode the collision operator's relaxation spectrum, giving the first observable claimed to be exclusively sensitive to the non-hydro sector. Soudi et al. [arXiv:2607.26143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26143) derive an equilibrating parton shower directly from QCD effective kinetic theory that reproduces the full linearized Boltzmann equation, handling hard energy loss and thermal equilibration on equal footing.

### GUT-scale defects and gravitational waves
Hemming et al. [arXiv:2607.27158](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27158) simulate GUT symmetry breaking, finding biased domain walls plus monopoles with novel interactions — monopole absorption on walls, magnetically charged black holes from collapsing walls, and a biased-wall GW background. Brunelli et al. [arXiv:2607.26124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26124) get a multi-peaked high-frequency signal from multiple cosmic superstrings, and Jinno et al. [arXiv:2607.26569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26569) show rippled bubble walls resonantly enhance production of particles far heavier than the transition scale. A continuation of the defect/phase-transition GW drumbeat.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2607.27202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27202) — Anomalies in Hadronic $B\to VV$ Decays (Bhubanjyoti Bhattacharya et al.)

Bhattacharya et al. extend last year's charmless $B\to PP$ SU(3)$_F$ puzzle to the vector-vector modes, fitting HFLAV/PDG data with reduced matrix elements (equivalently topological diagrams) in the exact SU(3)$_F$ limit, the only theoretical simplification being neglect of the small Wilson coefficients $c_7,c_8$ (a ~10% effect). Three fits are staged. The isospin-only $B\to\rho K^*$ fit is acceptable ($\chi^2/\text{d.o.f.}=3.6/3$, $p=0.31$) — but forces $|\widetilde{P_{uc}}/\widetilde{P_{tc}}|\sim 200$, some 300× above the expected $\lesssim0.5$; imposing the physical constraint $\leq1$ collapses it to $4.4\sigma$. The combined SU(3)$_F$ fit over $V\in\{\rho,K^*\}$ gives $39.2/5$, a $5.2\sigma$ discrepancy; adding $\phi,\omega$ pushes it to $130/20$, $p<10^{-15}$, i.e. $>7\sigma$. The jump is driven partly by $\mathcal{B}(B^0\to\phi K^{*0})$ and $\mathcal{B}(B^0\to\omega K^{*0})$ (each contributing >20 to $\chi^2$) and by the failure of $f_L\gg f_T$ in the new modes sharpening tension with $\Delta S=1$ polarization data.

If it holds, this is a hadronic-amplitude anomaly distinct from the $b\to s\ell\ell/\nu\bar\nu$ FCNC anomalies — a breakdown of the SM's flavor-symmetry structure in tree/penguin dominated decays. The result's strength is its minimal theory input; its acknowledged vulnerability is that only exact SU(3)$_F$ was tested. The authors concede that whether realistic ~30% SU(3)$_F$-breaking can absorb a $7\sigma$ gap "must be checked," though they judge it unlikely. Robustness was verified with three independent codes and tens of thousands of seeds.

### [arXiv:2607.26195](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26195) — First Search for Quirks at the LHC with FASER (FASER Collaboration)

FASER delivers the first LHC quirk search, using 186 fb$^{-1}$ of 13.6 TeV $pp$ data (2022–2024). Quirks — pairs charged under a QCD-like infracolor SU($N_\text{IC}=2$) with right-handed-lepton SM charges — are Drell-Yan produced forward, remain bound by an unbreakable IC string, and arrive at FASER (480 m downstream) slow and out-of-time. The signature is a pair depositing ~2 MIPs of charge in all eight scintillators with reconstructed speed $0.6<\beta<1$ and arrival time $t_0$ offset from the nominal bunch. Backgrounds (through-going muons via an ABCD estimate, muon-induced "kinked" showers) total well below one event; the signal region contains **zero observed events**. Using a CL$_s$ fit, masses up to 179 GeV are excluded at $\Lambda=10$ keV, and for the first time weak-scale quirk masses are excluded across the broad range $300\,\text{eV}<\Lambda<100\,\text{keV}$.

This opens a genuinely new frontier: non-Abelian hidden sectors (motivated by neutral naturalness and IC-glueball dark matter) had no prior dedicated LHC search, only the DØ Tevatron limit. FASER's forward, low-background geometry turns the quirks' slowness into a clean discriminant. Caveats are honest and intrinsic: only ~0.03–0.04% of produced quirks reach the fiducial region, with reconstruction/selection efficiency ~1% (10%) at $m_\mathcal{Q}=50$ (175) GeV, and the signal normalization carries a ~25% ISR/FSR modeling uncertainty (up to 28% simulation-statistics uncertainty at small $\Lambda$). This is half of Run 3, so reach will grow.

### [arXiv:2607.26879](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26879) — Improved $HH\to b\bar b\tau^+\tau^-$ Analysis with 196 fb$^{-1}$ (ATLAS) (ATLAS Collaboration)

*(Abstract only.)* ATLAS updates its non-resonant di-Higgs search in $b\bar b\tau\tau$, combining 140 fb$^{-1}$ at 13 TeV with 56 fb$^{-1}$ at 13.6 TeV plus reconstruction and analysis improvements. The signal strength is $\mu_{HH}=2.6^{+1.4}_{-1.0}$, an observed (expected) significance of 2.6 (1.2)$\sigma$ over background-only, and 1.65$\sigma$ above the SM prediction — a mild upward pull consistent with the SM. The self-coupling modifier is constrained to $\kappa_\lambda\in[-3.4,1.6]\cup[5.5,10.1]$ observed ($[-1.7,8.5]$ expected) at 95% CL. As validation, ZH and ZZ in the same final state are measured at 3.5 (2.4)$\sigma$ and 1.8 (2.7)$\sigma$ respectively.

The result advances the $HH$ program incrementally: the tightened $\kappa_\lambda$ interval sharpens the constraint on the Higgs potential, while the ~2.5× SM central value continues a pattern of slight excesses in this channel worth tracking as Run 3 statistics accumulate. The excess is modest — the SM lies comfortably within 1.65$\sigma$ — so no anomaly is claimed. The validation channels demonstrate the method's control, though the observed ZH pull sits above expectation and ZZ below, indicating the measurements remain statistics-limited.
