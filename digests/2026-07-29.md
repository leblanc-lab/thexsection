## Galactic neutrinos and electroweak photons

The day's weight sits on two clean observations at opposite scales. IceCube establishes high-energy neutrino emission from the Milky Way's plane at 5.7σ using all three flavor morphologies over 12 years ([arXiv:2607.25966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25966)) — the Galactic diffuse signal, now robustly in hand and feeding the multi-messenger machinery the week's UHE-neutrino tension papers have been circling. At the LHC, ATLAS observes electroweak $\gamma\gamma jj$ production at 6.2σ ([arXiv:2607.25869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25869)), extending the electroweak-boson program that ran through last week's $WZjj$ polarization and $ZZ$ work, and casting the result in dimension-8 aQGC limits. Around these, a cluster of early-universe simulation-code releases and a scattering of flavor/CP methods.

## Themes

### Electroweak boson production and quartic couplings
ATLAS's $\gamma\gamma jj$ observation [arXiv:2607.25869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25869) lands slightly below the SM cross section (13.8 vs 17.1 fb) and sets aQGC bounds — the same dim-8 EFT language the memory has flagged as the default for VBS-adjacent searches. Yilmaz et al. [arXiv:2607.25935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25935) project $ZZ\gamma$ aQGC reach at FCC-hh an order of magnitude past current LHC limits, and ATLAS's Run 2 large-radius jet calibration [arXiv:2607.25893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25893) (1% JES/JMS to 1 TeV) is the enabling infrastructure for the boosted end.

### Galactic neutrino astronomy
Beyond the 5.7σ plane detection, IceCube [arXiv:2607.25966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25966) finds the inner Galaxy a prominent source — 217 shower events above 5 TeV against 154 expected. This is the constructive counterpart to the week's UHE-neutrino anomaly (the 7.5σ ANITA/KM3NeT-vs-IceCube tension): the diffuse Galactic component is exactly what those joint fits assume as background.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2607.25966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25966) — High-energy neutrino emission from the Milky Way (R. Abbasi et al.)

Abstract only. Combining all three neutrino-flavor event morphologies with updated ice modelling, calibration and reconstruction over 12 years of IceCube data, a predefined global analysis establishes high-energy neutrino emission from the Galactic plane at **5.7σ**. A follow-up study identifies the inner Galaxy as a prominent source: **217 shower events above 5 TeV visible energy against an expected background of 154.4 ± 4.1**. The multi-flavor approach is what pushes this over the discovery threshold — earlier IceCube plane hints leaned on cascades alone.

Why it matters: this is the constructive counterpart to the week's UHE-neutrino tension thread. The diffuse Galactic component is precisely the background assumed in the ANITA/KM3NeT-vs-IceCube joint fits, so having it in hand at >5σ tightens those interpretations while opening Galactic multi-messenger astronomy — cosmic-ray propagation and neutrino properties probed over kiloparsec baselines. As an abstract-only entry, the systematic treatment of the diffuse-emission modelling (which sets whether the inner-Galaxy excess is source-like or propagation-driven) can't be assessed here.

### [arXiv:2607.25869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25869) — Observation of electroweak $\gamma\gamma jj$ production at 13 TeV in 140 fb⁻¹ with ATLAS (ATLAS Collaboration)

Abstract only. ATLAS observes the electroweak production of two isolated photons plus two jets at **6.2σ**, with a fiducial cross section of **$13.8^{+3.0}_{-2.6}$ fb**, sitting below but consistent with the SM prediction of **$17.1 \pm 2.4$ fb**. Fiducial and differential cross sections are measured for both the EW-only and combined QCD+EW $\gamma\gamma jj$ processes, and the data are interpreted in EFT, setting limits on several dimension-8 Wilson coefficients parameterising anomalous quartic gauge couplings.

Why it matters: this extends the ATLAS electroweak-boson program — following recent $WZjj$ polarization and $ZZ$ work — into a new VBS-adjacent channel, using the same dim-8 aQGC language that is now the default for these searches (and the framework Yilmaz et al. project forward to FCC-hh). The measured central value ~20% below SM is within the ~2 fb uncertainties, so no tension, but the modest downward pull is the kind of thing differential distributions and more data will need to pin down.

### [arXiv:2607.24930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24930) — Gravitational waves decay in vacuum: a low-energy effect of quantized gravitation (Diego Blas et al.)

Blas and Oller argue that GWs, treated as coherent graviton states rather than classical fields, decay into photon pairs via graviton fusion ($gg\to\gamma\gamma$, Skobelev amplitude) — a process forbidden classically and semiclassically, vanishing as $\hbar\to0$, and enhanced by the graviton number squared $N^2$. The key technical point (Supplement SA): the coherent-state mode cannot propagate in the internal graviton line because momentum conservation forces it on-shell at $s=0$, where the derivative coupling vanishes. The radiated power scales as $W_\gamma \propto G^2 E_{\rm rel}^2\omega_s^2\sigma_\omega^2$, giving a compact form $W_\gamma=\tfrac{3}{50\pi}(r_m/\ell)^2\hbar\omega^2$. They derive first constraints on recurrent GW populations from CMB $\mu$/$y$ distortions (COBE/FIRAS: $|\mu|<9\times10^{-5}$, $|y|<1.5\times10^{-5}$), UV/X-ray and $\gamma$-ray backgrounds, and D/⁴He photofission, all cast as bounds on $\sqrt{\Gamma}\,\epsilon E_{\rm rel}/M_\odot$ vs $\omega_s$.

Why it matters: a genuinely new, purely quantum-gravitational handle on GW sources, whose detection would evidence quantized gravity. The honest caveat is front and center: for astrophysical binaries the photon luminosity is minuscule (comparable to Hawking radiation of the equivalent mass, number luminosity $\sim10^6\,{\rm s}^{-1}$ at merger), and SGWB depletion is negligible ($\sim10^{-110}\,{\rm m}^{-3}{\rm s}^{-1}$; $|\Phi|\approx10^{-25}\,{\rm Hz}^{-1}$ to the GUT scale). Only in ultralight-dark-matter scenarios, where huge occupation numbers boost $gg\leftrightarrow\phi\phi$, do rates reach $\sim10^{54}\,{\rm s}^{-1}$ — and that requires the exotic light-scalar sector to exist.
