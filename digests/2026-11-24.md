## Analysis agents, scored blind

Novák et al. [arXiv:2611.28194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28194) give the AI-agent thread its first scorecard for experimental analysis. Each agent builds a single workflow on CMS open data, and that workflow is then run on sealed datasets with injected signals and shifted detector settings. Averaged over four tasks, Gemini 4 Argon reaches 60% of the expert reference score, GPT 6 Astra 38% and Claude Opus 5.5 31%. The last is the same model that delivered verified Feynman-integral bases yesterday. Theory output can be checked exactly; analysis output has to be checked by injection, and this is the first benchmark to do that, putting into practice Alimena's evidence-over-code standard from 11-19. Also today: three trigger papers working at tens to hundreds of nanoseconds, a list of DM assumptions that matter more than expected, and colour that refuses to stay subleading.

## Themes

### Triggers in nanoseconds
This is new; nothing in recent memory covers it.
- **Learned event embedding.** Rozanov et al. [arXiv:2611.28355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28355) distill an offline transformer into a 7512-parameter quantised Level-1 encoder running in 147 ns. It rejects 99% of background while keeping 96% of held-out SM processes and 88% of 52 BSM benchmarks.
- **Symbolic anomaly detector.** Bollhalder et al. [arXiv:2611.30896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30896) distill a normalising-flow anomaly detector into an explicit 577-operation formula that runs in 42.8 ns.
- **CMS Phase-2 muons.** Estrada et al. [arXiv:2611.30394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30394) detect muon showers from Drift Tube hits in about 20 ns.

Signature-agnostic selection now fits inside the L1 latency budget.

### Dark matter's quieter assumptions
- **Halo shape.** Shao et al. [arXiv:2611.29197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29197) find that halo anisotropy moves LZ elastic limits by ±30% above 40 GeV but by up to 10× below 15 GeV.
- **Resonant freeze-out.** Arcadi et al. [arXiv:2611.28230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28230) drop the kinetic-equilibrium assumption at the Higgs resonance. The coupling needed for the relic abundance shifts by up to 10× for pseudoscalar fermion DM, which strengthens direct-detection constraints.
- **Single-electron backgrounds.** Santos-Olmsted et al. [arXiv:2611.28211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28211) propose using xenon detectors' full dark-electron rate in an annual-modulation search. Sensitivity then scales as $S\lesssim\sqrt{B}$ rather than $S\lesssim B$, with no new hardware.

This continues the week's habit of auditing error bars, now applied to dark matter.

### Plumbing for EFT fits
- **SMEFT plus PDFs.** Cole et al. [arXiv:2611.28216](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28216) fit 15 Wilson coefficients and the proton PDFs simultaneously. They add parity violation via LEFT matching and include charged-current high-mass Drell–Yan for the first time. The PDFs barely move.
- **EFT-safe acceptance.** ATLAS [arXiv:2611.30272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30272) recasts $H\to4\ell$ STXS into a Lorentz-invariant fiducial region with $A^{LIF}_{SM}=0.8546$, production-independent to 0.07%. EFT reinterpretations then need no EFT-dependent efficiency corrections.

EFT is the year's standard collider language, and these papers are infrastructure for it.

## Worth a look
- **[arXiv:2611.28194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28194)** (Novák et al.): The model rankings will date fast. The sealed-world design, with injection tests applied to a single fixed workflow, is the lasting contribution.
- **[arXiv:2611.29072](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29072)** (Chargeishvili): Exact full-colour soft evolution up to 15 gluons, an 8×10⁸-dimensional colour space handled on one node. For gap-vetoed jet rates, leading colour overestimates the full result by 1.3×, 1.8× and 6.6× at 8, 10 and 14 partons.
- **[arXiv:2611.29197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29197)** (Shao et al.): For the 248 keV LZ event, some anisotropic halos make splittings kinematically accessible where solar-neutrino bounds on the higgsino lose force. That is the constraint that has excluded the higgsino reading since Pospelov. The authors stress that kinematic access does not establish viability.
- **[arXiv:2611.29717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29717)** (Chen et al.): Magic production vanishes for single gluon, photon, W, Higgs and graviton exchange, and for leptonic Z exchange at $\sin^2\theta_W=1/4$. The result is computed in the chirality basis, so the basis-dependence of magic measures reported by Altomonte et al. on 11-16 applies here too.
- **[arXiv:2611.30877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30877)** (Reboud et al.): Subtract one simple pole per narrow resonance, then convolve the smooth remainder on a grid set by the detector resolution. This is a structural answer to the normalisation problems from sharply localized terms that Zhu et al. flagged on 11-17.

McMule now covers $e^+e^-\to K\bar K$ at NNLO in initial-state QED, with very large corrections at CMD-3 energies. Relevant to HVP and the radiative-return generator comparison from 11-23.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2611.28194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28194) — HEP-Ex-Bench: Evaluating Agentic Performance in High Energy Physics Experiment Data Analyses with Sealed Worlds (Andrzej Nov\'ak et al.)

Each agent gets one session of at most 7.5 h on a public world built from 2016 CMS open simulation (MiniAOD, full detector simulation). It must deliver a single pipeline, which is then frozen and rerun on 10–40 sealed worlds per task. Every world has a hidden truth ($m_H$, $\mu$, $\Gamma_H/\Gamma_H^{SM}$, or lepton and jet corrections) and one of 13 detector profiles that distort scale, resolution and efficiencies. The score is a strictly proper log score relative to an expert reference run on the same worlds. A shared-bias term growing like $\sqrt N$ is added, along with weighted reviewer checks: goodness of fit, truthfulness against an evaluator refit, and Z-peak closure. With $\gamma=2$, the ratio reads as an effective fraction of the data.

Across 99 sessions per task and eleven models:
- Gemini 4 Argon averages 60±8% of the reference, GPT 6 Astra 38%, Claude Opus 5.5 31%.
- No open-weight model exceeds 10%.
- Sessions undercover far more often than they overcover.
- Run-to-run spread exceeds the differences between prompt tiers. Opus 5.5 on HiggsLepMass reaches 89% in its best session against a 49% mean.
- The P2 expert plan adds nothing over P1.

The harness is validated adversarially. A memorised 125 GeV scores <1% and understated uncertainties score 34%. With ten worlds, every flawed archetype falls below the reference in ≥96% of draws.

Caveats the authors flag:
- Worlds vary detector response and signal, but not generators. The six JetCalib sessions that beat the reference gain mainly because the reference's modelling-dominated JES uncertainties exceed the injected variations.
- Scores depend on the chosen reference and weights.
- Only one experiment is covered.

### [arXiv:2611.29072](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29072) — A billion colour states: exact soft-gluon evolution beyond leading colour for many-jet QCD (Bakar Chargeishvili)

Leading colour degrades steadily with multiplicity. Take a soft veto outside the jets at $t=0.1$ ($Q/Q_0\approx14$), inside the $t\approx0.05$–0.12 range the author maps to LHC dijet vetoes. There, LC overestimates the full-colour no-emission factor by median factors of 1.27, 1.83 and 6.6 at 8, 10 and 14 partons. The gap fraction is off by 2.2 at $n=14$. Pairs among the three hardest jets form a colour singlet 3–9% of the time; LC sets this to zero.

The method builds an orthogonal multiplet basis by coupling partons one leg at a time, with each step fixed by a Casimir eigenvalue (Jucys–Murphy elements). Norms come in closed form, and dipoles act only on the label segment between the two partons, via small recoupling tables. $\Gamma$ is therefore never stored: $\Gamma x$ is a block "sweep" and $e^{-t\Gamma}$ a Krylov iteration. Fourteen gluons take about an hour on one A100. Fifteen gluons (827,888,726 states) take 3–7 h per $t$ value on a CPU node with up to 857 GB.

For showers, the formal $1/N_c^2$ suppression is outweighed by the growing number of unconnected pairs. Caveats:
- The calculation uses one-loop $\Gamma$ at fixed coupling, with colour-diagonal factors dropped.
- Events are flat in phase space, and real emission is included only up to five hard partons.
- Independent cross-checks stop at $n\le7$. Beyond that, validation rests on internal consistency.
- The $n=15$ points show feasibility and do not enter the LC comparison.

### [arXiv:2611.29197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29197) — Quantifying the Impact of Galactic Dark Matter Halo Models on LZ Results (Jialiang Shao et al.)

The authors rescale LZ limits by the ratio of predicted yields across 22 self-consistent BCFS halos, truncated at a common $v_{esc}=544$ km/s.

Elastic results:
- Above about 40 GeV, the SI envelope is $R\approx0.8$–1.3 relative to the SHM.
- At 9 GeV it opens to 0.17–1.63 (class B).
- Extending the class-D cutoff to 1000 km/s drives $R$ to about 0.04.
- At 9 GeV, the $v_{esc}$ scan (factor ~2 for classes A/B) and the efficiency treatment (factor 2–3) matter about as much as the halo.
- In the SD channel at 40 GeV, nuclear-structure uncertainty (factors 3.6 and 13 for neutron and proton) still exceeds the roughly 2× halo spread.

For the 248 keV event (LZ: 3.4σ local, 2.6σ global), they use a background-free single-event likelihood at 1.1 TeV. The tail sets the inelastic ceiling: $\delta_{max}\approx384$ keV for the SHM and 556 keV for an LMC-like tail. The higgsino cross section (~7×10⁻³⁹ cm²) crosses these two contours at δ≈360 and 455 keV, both below the 566 keV solar-neutrino threshold. Some anisotropic contours reach 700–775 keV, but only at cross sections well below the higgsino value (D1/D3 near 3×10⁻⁴⁰ cm²).

Caveats:
- Limits are rescaled by yield ratio rather than refit with the LZ likelihood.
- The suite is not a posterior, and it mixes profile, rotation and anisotropy.
- The 1000 km/s cutoffs are sensitivity tests, not measurements.
- The single-event fit assumes unit efficiency, and its coverage is uncalibrated.
