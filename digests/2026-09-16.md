## Four loops and a structure day

After a month in which one xenon recoil owned the front page, today's weight sits squarely in QCD. The headline is Gehrmann et al. [arXiv:2609.17267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17267) completing the non-singlet splitting functions at four loops — a two-line abstract behind a landmark calculation. Around it clusters a dense DIS/structure program, much of it EIC- and FCC-facing, and a genuine charm-CPV result [arXiv:2609.16219](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16219) that nudges an open question from the August flavor flare. LZ, by contrast, is down to three model-building follow-ups, and the interpretation has migrated from halo kinematics to early-universe cosmology.

## Themes

### Precision QCD and DIS machinery
The four-loop non-singlet splitting functions (Gehrmann et al. [arXiv:2609.17267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17267)) anchor the day. Alongside, a wave of DIS tooling: Helenius et al. add multiplicative NLO matching for DIS in Pythia 8 [arXiv:2609.17015](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17015) and push dimuon production — the strange-PDF handle — to NNLO via SIDIS coefficients [arXiv:2609.17120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17120), finding a small-$x$ suppression that may ease the dimuon-vs-LHC strangeness tension. Chithirasreemadam et al. deliver VALO1.0, new real-photon PDFs with Monte Carlo uncertainties [arXiv:2609.17163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17163). Gonzalez et al. [arXiv:2609.16114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16114) broaden the energy-correlator program to "generalized detectors" measuring charged-hadron energy — IRC-unsafe observables brought under factorization control.

### Nucleon structure and small-x
A deep structure stack, mostly EIC-oriented. Caucal et al. derive the small-$x$ quark fracture function in the CGC and find hadronization largely washes out saturation signatures [arXiv:2609.16953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16953); Alvaro et al. give one-loop gluon-TMD matching relations [arXiv:2609.16945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16945); Vertesi introduces hadron-anchored energy correlators for charm fragmentation [arXiv:2609.16753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16753). Add COMPASS unpolarized azimuthal asymmetries [arXiv:2609.17222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17222), NLO BFKL eigenfunction phenomenology (Polizzi et al. [arXiv:2609.17259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17259)), a small-$x$ proton-spin fit (Tawabutr et al. [arXiv:2609.16654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16654)), and Fornetti et al.'s double-parton "double-EMC" ratio [arXiv:2609.16892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16892).

### The LZ event, now a cosmology problem
The recoil traffic has thinned to a trickle, and the surviving papers no longer fit halo kinematics — they track dark-sector cosmology. An et al. [arXiv:2609.17412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17412) show that whether the LZ signal is endothermic or exothermic is *dynamically selected* by early-universe $\chi_2\chi_2\leftrightarrow\chi_1\chi_1$ conversion — scalar couplings favor endo, pseudoscalar preserve $f_2\simeq1/2$ and give exo. Arcadi et al. fit it in the 2HD+a model with a light pseudoscalar [arXiv:2609.17196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17196), and Ge et al. [arXiv:2609.16529](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16529) supply relativistic nuclear response functions for inelastic Xe scattering that could shift interpretations. The falsifiability pivot the memory tracked for three weeks has run its course.

### Dark sectors against the cosmological tensions
Two dark-sector papers take aim at the standard-cosmology anomalies. Buckley et al. [arXiv:2609.16120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16120) introduce an MeV-scale dark QCD whose confining pion "sticky" radiation eases the Hubble tension, landing $H_0=71.60\pm0.63$ with a residual $3\sigma$. Clini et al. [arXiv:2609.17423](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17423) revive an equivalence-principle violation in the dark sector to fit DESI, finding a ~0.5%-of-gravity dark force at up to $3\sigma$ with DR2. Both are speculative but concrete; note also the CMB spectral-distortion review [arXiv:2609.16194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16194) and CMB-HD foreground study [arXiv:2609.16128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16128).

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2609.17267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17267) — Non-singlet splitting functions at four loops (Thomas Gehrmann et al.)

The full non-singlet splitting functions $P_{\rm ns}^{+}$, $P_{\rm ns}^{-}$, and $P_{\rm ns}^{s}=P_{\rm ns}^{\rm v}-P_{\rm ns}^{-}$ are now known analytically at four loops for arbitrary Mellin moment $n$ — the reference the field will cite for N³LO evolution. The method computes off-shell quark operator matrix elements for twist-two spin-$n$ operators, using generating functions to keep $n$ arbitrary while retaining standard IBP identities (via Qgraf, Form, Reduze2, and the finite-field Finred code). Rather than fight the elliptic geometries appearing in one color sector, the differential equations in the tracing parameter $t$ are Taylor-expanded about $t=0$, where the integrals reduce to known four-loop propagator masters; moments up to $n\sim4000$ are then generated and the all-$n$ result reconstructed from an ansatz of harmonic sums up to weight 7. Results confirm all known color coefficients and reproduce independent fixed-moment checks, the four-loop cusp coefficient $A_q^{(4)}$, and yield fully analytic virtual anomalous dimensions $B_q^{(4)}$ and $B_g^{(4)}$ plus the eikonal soft anomalous dimension $f_r^{(4)}$.

Why it matters: consistent N³LO LHC cross sections require exactly these functions, previously available only as numerical approximations or partial analytics — anchoring today's precision-QCD theme. Agreement with the Moch–Ruijl–Ueda–Vermaseren–Vogt numerics is good, though $P_{\rm ns}^{(3),+}$ sits slightly above their error band at small $x$. One honest wrinkle: the $\ln^4 x$ small-$x$ terms disagree with the resummation prediction of Davies et al., by a piece subleading in $N_c$ and proportional to $\zeta_2$ — possibly an $(i\pi)^2$ phase effect the authors flag for investigation. The singlet case remains, requiring gauge-variant operator renormalization.

### [arXiv:2609.16219](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16219) — Enhanced Standard Model bound on charm CP violation from QCD penguins (Ali Mohamed et al.)

Using light-cone sum rules with an auxiliary momentum inserted at the weak vertex (following the $B\to\pi\pi$ methodology of Khodjamirian) to avoid parasitic cuts, Mohamed and Piscopo compute for the first time the QCD-penguin operator matrix elements for $D^0\to K^+K^-$ and $D^0\to\pi^+\pi^-$ at leading order in $\alpha_s$. The scalar penguins $Q_5,Q_6$ are strongly enhanced: $-i\langle Q_6\rangle_{\pi\pi}=2.09$ GeV³ versus $0.228$ for the current-current $Q_1^d$ — roughly a factor of ten. Two sources drive this: quark-condensate contributions arising at tree level (enhanced by $16\pi^2$ over the loop-induced terms) and an annihilation-type contribution from the $\bar u u$ component entering already at twist three. Recomputed current-current matrix elements agree with the earlier Lenz et al. determination to within 5–10%, and the resulting branching ratios ($\mathcal{B}(\pi\pi)=1.22\times10^{-3}$, $\mathcal{B}(KK)=2.44\times10^{-3}$) match HFLAV within their ~100% uncertainties.

Why it matters: the 2019 LHCb $\Delta A_{\rm CP}\sim10^{-3}$ needs penguin-to-tree ratios of order unity, an order of magnitude above naive estimates, and whether this fits the SM hinges on hadronic matrix elements. Enhanced penguins narrow the SM-vs-experiment gap — direct progress on the standing charm-CPV puzzle flagged in August. Caveat, stated plainly: the strong phases remain unconstrained at LO $\alpha_s$, so no quantitative CP-asymmetry prediction follows; the bound assumes maximal phases. The annihilation approximation carries an expected $\geq30\%$ error because $s_0/m_D^2\sim20$–30% (versus ~3% for $B$ decays).

### [arXiv:2609.16120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16120) — Dark Radiation Sticks Together: Dark QCD and the Hubble Tension (Matthew R. Buckley et al.)

Buckley, Fernandez, and Putney propose a confining $SU(N_c)$ dark sector with heavy stable quarks (the dark matter) and light quarks whose confinement at $\Lambda_{\rm D}\sim$ MeV turns the dark radiation into light dark pions. Crucially, residual strong interactions make this radiation a *viscous imperfect fluid* rather than free-streaming — modeled in a modified CLASS with drag ($\Gamma_{\rm drag}$) and relaxation-time equations. The viscosity suppresses high-$\ell$ CMB oscillations relative to free-streaming $\Delta N_{\rm eff}$, letting the model raise $H_0$ without spoiling the high-$\ell$ multipoles or $S_8$. A full MCMC over Planck, ACT, DESI DR2, Pantheon+, SH0ES, and BBN yields $H_0=71.60\pm0.63$ km/s/Mpc with a residual tension of $\sim3\sigma$ ($Q_{\rm DMAP}=3.0\sigma$ vs $6.0\sigma$ for $\Lambda$CDM), competitive with Hubble World Cup "finalists." It also fits DESI BAO better than Planck-only $\Lambda$CDM.

Why it matters: this is a genuinely different mechanism for the Hubble tension — not just extra $N_{\rm eff}$, but self-interacting radiation whose fluid behavior evades the high-$\ell$ constraints that cap free-streaming models near $4\sigma$. The large late-time self-interaction cross section ($\sim\Lambda_{\rm D}^{-2}$) evades Bullet Cluster bounds because hard momentum transfer requires perturbative heavy-quark scattering. Honest caveats: the best-fit $\Lambda_{\rm D}\sim1$ MeV produces dark acoustic oscillations in tension with Lyman-$\alpha$, though $\Lambda_{\rm D}\gtrsim3$–5 MeV evades this with negligible fit degradation; and the CLASS modifications were partly LLM-generated.
