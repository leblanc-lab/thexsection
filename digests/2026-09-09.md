## The LZ event turns testable

Week three of the LZ recoil, and roughly a quarter of today's 127 papers are still interpreting one event. The model-building has saturated — inert doublets, singlet-doublets, $B{-}L$ scalars, GUT embeddings, dark dimensions, warped extra dimensions all fit the same 248 keV — so the useful papers are no longer the fits but the tests. Two things changed the register today: the bounds keep arriving (Bose et al. [arXiv:2609.07807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07807) and Di Mauro et al. [arXiv:2609.06760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06760) both re-execute the solar-capture argument that killed the thermal Higgsino), and the interpretations are finally reaching out to colliders, argon, and directional signals. Away from xenon, the genuine physics is a jet-quenching color-decoherence pair and a clutch of real axion and neutrino results.

## Themes

### The LZ zoo, and the pivot to falsifiability
The endothermic Higgsino is now excluded from every direction — Bose et al. [arXiv:2609.07807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07807) push the Super-K/IceCube splitting bound to $\gtrsim557$ keV, Di Mauro et al. [arXiv:2609.06760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06760) confirm the thermal Higgsino dies while generic endothermic survives. So the field is escaping in three directions: away from the halo tail via boosted dark matter (Kannike et al. [arXiv:2609.07742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07742), Alhazmi et al. [arXiv:2609.06890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06890), Liang et al. [arXiv:2609.06756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06756)) and exothermic scattering (Baer et al. [arXiv:2609.06153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06153), with a clean argon prediction); toward exotic mechanisms like neutron disappearance (Aghaie et al. [arXiv:2609.09037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09037)); and, most usefully, toward collider tests — Cheung et al. [arXiv:2609.08712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08712) predict sub-cm disappearing tracks from the Higgsino chargino, Kotlarski et al. [arXiv:2609.06750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06750) tie the splitting to a 10s-of-TeV $Z'$ at FCC-hh. Asadi et al. [arXiv:2609.09107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09107) stand out for a distinctive signature rather than another fit.

### Jet quenching gets its colors
Bossi et al. [arXiv:2609.05609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05609) combine inclusive jet $R_{AA}$ with energy-energy correlators to extract quark and gluon quenching factors, finding coherent energy loss incompatible with Casimir scaling — until color decoherence is added, which restores it. Mehtar-Tani [arXiv:2609.05614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05614) supplies the generating-functional machinery for exactly this coherent-to-incoherent EEC evolution. A clean data-plus-theory pairing, and a genuinely new thread against the LZ noise.

### Axions, lab to starlight
Two real experimental limits landed: Lee et al. [arXiv:2609.08144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08144) reach 1.3×KSVZ at 15.6 μeV with a broadly tunable HTS cavity, and Winter et al. [arXiv:2609.07908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07908) bound the axion-EDM coupling via $^{207}$Pb solid-state NMR. Levasseur et al. [arXiv:2609.08504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08504) deliver a distance-independent RGB-star bound on the axion-electron coupling, and Witte et al. [arXiv:2609.08840](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08840) work out axion-hair pulsar electrodynamics. The strong-CP thread the memory has tracked since August now has a live detection wing.

### Neutrino experiments clear the ledger
NOvA's dual-baseline sterile search [arXiv:2609.08900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08900) finds nothing and excludes most of IceCube's allowed region — its first analysis to fit neutrino and antineutrino modes jointly. Alongside: NOvA's neutron-response remodeling [arXiv:2609.06956](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06956), and Lunardini et al.'s [arXiv:2609.08753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08753) two-parameter DSNB template as detectors approach discovery.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2609.08712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08712) — From LUX-ZEPLIN to Colliders: Probing Higgsino Dark Matter (Kingman Cheung et al.)

Taking the 248 keV LZ recoil at face value as 1.1 TeV thermal Higgsino inelastic DM with neutral splitting $\delta m_0\simeq350$ keV, Cheung, Kang, and Kumar work out the necessary collider corollary: the charged Higgsino sits $\Delta m_+\sim(330$–$350)$ MeV above the neutral state (electroweak loops dominating over a $\sim-9$ MeV finite-gaugino tree contribution), giving proper lifetime $\tau\approx0.025$ ns, $c\tau\simeq(6$–$8)$ mm, and decays to a soft $\sim300$ MeV pion. This is a sub-centimeter disappearing tracklet. Current ATLAS disappearing-track ($m_{\chi^\pm}\lesssim225$ GeV) and CMS soft-track ($\lesssim185$ GeV) limits sit far below the 1.1 TeV target because the survival probability through three pixel hits ($R\sim9$ cm) is $\sim3\times10^{-6}$ at $\beta_T\gamma=1$; the 0.57 fb production cross section compounds the problem. HL-LHC needs better short-track reconstruction, not luminosity; dedicated studies find $5\sigma$ reach at a 100 TeV collider ($30$ ab$^{-1}$) and a 10 TeV muon collider ($10$ ab$^{-1}$).

Why it matters: this is the digest's cleanest LZ-to-collider bridge, converting a direct-detection splitting into a one-parameter trajectory in the $(m_{\chi^\pm},c\tau)$ plane — a genuinely falsifiable prediction rather than another fit. Caveats are stated honestly: the authors flag the unresolved high-energy $S1c$ sideband tension (acceptance not public), note the charged splitting is not a precision pole-mass calculation, and stress the collider reaches are conditional projections tied to assumed detector geometry and beam-induced backgrounds.

### [arXiv:2609.09107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09107) — For Whom the Xenon Recoils: Magnetic Inelastic Dark Baryons (Pouya Asadi et al.)

Asadi et al. build a strongly-coupled dark-baryon model where a transition magnetic dipole operator ($g_M\simeq-2\sqrt3$, roughly twice the neutron moment) drives inelastic scattering while an accidental "$\mathcal{H}$-parity" forbids all diagonal one-photon moments, suppressing elastic scattering. In the benchmark $N_c=N_f=3$ theory with dark quarks in an SU(2)$_L$ triplet, the neutral 3-plet/5-plet baryons carry opposite $\mathcal{H}$-parity; hyperfine and $W$-exchange contributions to their splitting produce $\delta\sim\mathcal{O}(100)$ keV near dark-quark mass $\sim800$ GeV ($m_\chi\sim2.4$ TeV). Fitting the LZ event (spin-dependent nuclear response) gives best-fit $(m_\chi,\delta)=(6.8\text{ TeV},300\text{ keV})$ for SHM and $(14\text{ TeV},280\text{ keV})$ for a MW+LMC profile, with viable ranges $1$–$50$ TeV and $100$–$500$ keV.

The distinctive handle: the excited state de-excites via a photon of energy $\delta$, with decay length $\sim4.7$ m — comparable to detector size — so a fraction of recoils carry a coincident $100$–$500$ keV photon, offering directional information (the excited state travels with the heavy DM), a discriminant no other iDM reading provides. It also predicts weaker solar-capture constraints (spin-dependent capture on rare odd-mass isotopes; annihilation dominantly to dark mesons rather than neutrinos), evading the bounds killing the thermal Higgsino. Caveats: mass-splitting predictions carry $\mathcal{O}(10^7)$ dynamic-range uncertainty from the variational quark-model estimate (only lattice could sharpen them), the single-event fit neglects backgrounds, and halo-profile choice dominates the preferred region.

### [arXiv:2609.05609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05609) — The Colors of Jet Quenching (Hannah Bossi et al.)

Bossi, Larose, and Mehtar-Tani perform a data-driven extraction of quark and gluon quenching factors by jointly fitting ATLAS inclusive-jet $R_{AA}$ and CMS energy-energy correlators (EECs) for $R=0.4$ jets, restricted to the small-angle region ($R_L<0.07$) where hard-collinear dynamics dominate and soft medium response is subleading. The two observables have complementary flavor sensitivity — gluon EECs peak at $R_L=0.025$ versus $0.012$ for quarks — breaking the identifiability problem that plagues $R_{AA}$-only extractions. Under the coherent-energy-loss null hypothesis they extract $S_q\simeq0.8$, $S_g\simeq0.3$, incompatible with Casimir scaling ($S_g=S_q^{9/4}\simeq0.54$–0.58) at 8–11$\sigma$. Adding color decoherence via the antenna/dipole quenching factor $S_{\rm dip}(\theta)$ — which interpolates from coherent ($\theta\ll\theta_c$) to independent loss with a slow $\theta^{-2/3}$ power-law tail — improves the fit ($\chi^2$/dof $0.93\to0.13$) and restores Casimir scaling ($S_q\simeq0.65$, $S_g\simeq0.40$, within $1.5\sigma$). The medium resolution angle is tightly constrained: $\theta_c=0.057\pm0.005\,(\text{fit})^{+0.014}_{-0.008}\,(\text{syst})$, $p_T$-independent as expected.

Why it matters: this is direct evidence, drawn from data rather than tuned Monte Carlo, that the QGP partially resolves internal jet substructure down to $\theta_c\simeq0.05$–0.07 — the color-decoherence transition made quantitative, paired with Mehtar-Tani's companion generating-functional derivation. Caveats are stated plainly: absolute $\chi^2$ values are not meaningful because published uncertainties are added in quadrature without correlations, so only relative fit quality is claimed; it is a proof-of-concept, not a global analysis, with vacuum reference and flavor fractions held fixed and higher-order/medium-response effects deferred.
