## ATLAS limits look too well-behaved

Şahin et al. [arXiv:2611.24065](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24065) read about 400 exclusion plots from 2020–25 ATLAS and CMS searches straight from the vector graphics.

- **CMS** matches the nominal coverage: 66.1% of observed limits fall inside the 1σ band and 95.3% inside 2σ.
- **ATLAS** puts 99.2±0.4% inside its 2σ band, which is too many.

Their pseudo-experiments allow three explanations: conservative uncertainties, low-count searches, or expected limits computed after a fit to data. The last is the default in common statistical tools, and the plots don't say which method was used. This matters for the list of unclosed LHC excesses, which grew by a 3.5σ instanton-like entry yesterday.

The day's boldest claim, Keshet's 70/74.5 GeV WIMP line triads in 23,000 stacked clusters [arXiv:2611.21595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21595), shows why the method question matters. The blind scan alone gives 2.8σ trial-corrected. The quoted 6.1σ appears only after combining with the author's own earlier samples.

## Themes

### Priors decide the GW verdict
Bringmann et al. [arXiv:2611.23371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23371) replace the power-law SMBHB background with a realistic population model. The PTA preference for a phase transition then drops from 2.8σ to 1.5σ. AbdusSalam et al. [arXiv:2611.24625](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24625) find that LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA phase-transition constraints from O1–O4a depend strongly on the prior. Last week's GW papers worried that predicted transition signals depend on poorly known hydrodynamics. These two show the same fragility on the data side.

### Superradiance clouds, smaller again
Bottaro et al. [arXiv:2611.23384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23384) show that self-interactions let the 211 cloud feed the ground state faster than that state falls into the black hole. This caps the cloud mass, and below about 4×10⁶ M☉ the cap is lower than assumed. That is the same conclusion as Foster's 11-17 gravitational Auger effect, reached by a different mechanism: black-hole spin bounds on axions need rechecking. On the lab side, Benabou et al. [arXiv:2611.23374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23374) cost-optimize detectors for the GUT-scale QCD axion. A 6 T toroid about 14 m across (~120 MCHF) beats the 16 T DMRadio-GUT design at roughly half its cost.

### $V_{ud}$: the three-body piece
Chambers-Wall et al. [arXiv:2611.24825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24825) compute three-nucleon δ_NS matrix elements with quantum Monte Carlo. The $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2)$ terms are large enough to move $V_{ud}$ at the 10⁻⁴ level. They also extract $V_{ud}$ from ¹⁰C, the transition where Novario's coupled-cluster calculation (11-17) came in 30% below QMC. The abstract gives no value, so the comparison has to wait for the paper.

### Spectroscopy, with brakes on
Peng et al. [arXiv:2611.24372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24372) reproduce the $K_SK_S\eta'$ dip and the 2.4 GeV enhancement through interference among known pseudoscalars, without BESIII's extra X(2800). That undercuts the glueball reading of X(2370), as did Kang's 11-10 sum-rule result that the X(2370) band can be empty. Danilkin et al. [arXiv:2611.24694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24694) extract the f₂(1270) and f₂′(1525) poles from radiative $J/\psi$ multipoles without PDG input.

## Worth a look
- **[arXiv:2611.24065](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24065)** (Şahin et al.): Concrete evidence about how expected limits are being produced. Its recommendation, that publications state how expected limits are computed, is cheap to adopt.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2611.24065](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24065) — An Updated Meta-Analysis of LHC Exotic Physics Results (Ece \c{S}ahin et al.)

ATLAS expected-limit bands are miscalibrated, while CMS bands look fine. Şahin and Özcan extracted observed limits and ±1σ/±2σ bands directly from the SVG geometry of 168 ATLAS and 205 CMS exclusion plots. These come from 55 exotics publications (2020–25), with uncertainties from a bootstrap over publications. In normalized averaging, CMS sits at 66.1±4.4% (1σ) and 95.3±1.9% (2σ). ATLAS has 73.3±7.6% inside 1σ, compatible with nominal, and 99.2±0.4% inside 2σ, well above nominal. The raw-averaged ATLAS 1σ fraction is 81.8±5.9%, which is +2.3σ.

The 2σ excess survives several checks:
- It persists in columns where the band edges are well separated (99.5±0.3%).
- It holds in both publication-year halves and on a citation-independent subset.
- The plot reading matches HEPData for 1522 of 1523 cross-checked points.

In the 2019 study, both experiments matched nominal coverage.

The pyhf toys separate the candidate explanations by which side of the band the observed limits avoid:
- **Low counts:** with one expected event, 92% of limits land inside 1σ and none below. This mainly empties the lower side.
- **A-posteriori bands:** reach about 99% inside 2σ only when the signal region itself constrains an uncertainty of half the background or more.

ATLAS has almost no points beyond *either* 2σ edge. The authors therefore favour bands that are too wide, from conservative uncertainties or a-posteriori computation, over low counts alone. Because most analyses fix backgrounds with control regions, they doubt a-posteriori bands explain the whole effect.

Caveats:
- The scope is a topic-restricted exotics subset.
- The authors cannot tell common practice from a few analyses. Single publications move ATLAS by up to about 1σ.
- Özcan is an ATLAS author.
- No paper in the sample states whether its bands are a priori or a posteriori.

The authors' four-item disclosure list (tool and version, limit type, asymptotic vs. toys, convention) is the actionable output.

### [arXiv:2611.24270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24270) — A fully differential charged-particle phase-space measurement of Z+jets production with ATLAS (ATLAS Collaboration)

ATLAS has published Z→μμ+jets (140 fb⁻¹, $p_T^{\mu\mu}>200$ GeV) as an unbinned unfold of the full charged-particle final state. Each event averages about 50 tracks and exceeds 250 in some, so the unfold is effectively 150–750 dimensional. The release is particle-level MG5+Py8 events with OmniFold weights in fb, plus 162 weight variations (27 systematic, 135 bootstrap).

The method changes against the earlier 24-observable MultiFold analysis:
- Particle Transformers replace the earlier MLPs.
- Networks are pretrained on about 14M MG5+Py8-vs-Sherpa events. The authors call this essential because only about 247k data events pass selection.
- Unfolding runs seven iterations.
- Compute cost was roughly 400k A100-hours.

Showcase results:
- **Charged multiplicity:** MG5+Py8 overshoots and Sherpa undershoots $n_{ch}$, by up to about 50%.
- **Energy correlator:** the first all-charged-particle EEC in pp, at ≤5% precision, with clear perturbative, free-hadron and wide-angle regimes. Both generators miss the smallest-z region.
- **Jet shapes:** C/A, $k_t$ and anti-$k_t$ jet shapes come from the same sample without recalibration.
- **Intrinsic dimension:** the first measured jet intrinsic dimension, below 10 at all EMD scales.

Caveats:
- Only charged particles are measured.
- Hadron-species fractions and process labels are not constrained; they come from MG5+Py8.
- The unfolding uncertainty is among the dominant terms and grows in low-statistics regions.
- Users must validate each new observable on the released pseudodata, with at least 5000 effective events per bin. This is not a formality: finer $n_{ch}$ binning drops to p=0.041, and $m_{j1}$ already sits at p=0.016.
- No goodness-of-fit procedure is yet recommended for genuinely unbinned downstream use.

### [arXiv:2611.23371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23371) — Seeing through the fog: astrophysical priors and the evidence for new physics in PTAs (Torsten Bringmann et al.)

binaryfog emulates holodeck SMBHB population spectra, including per-bin median, width and skew of the realisation scatter, in about 0.2 ms instead of about 3 min. That speed makes it feasible to profile over all six astrophysical parameters in new-physics fits to the 2026 re-run NANOGrav 15-yr CURN free spectrum.

Under NANOGrav's own priors, replacing the $(A,\gamma)$ power law with the population model lowers every new-physics preference:

| Model | Power law | Population model |
|---|---|---|
| Phase transition | 2.8σ | 1.5σ |
| Cosmic superstrings | 2.9σ | 1.5σ |
| Inflation | 2.8σ | 1.5σ |
| Domain walls | 2.1σ | 0.7σ |

The driver is that binary lifetime τ_f and inner hardening index can bend the binary spectrum at low frequency, which a power law cannot.

The result is prior-dominated:
- **Flat priors:** 0.2σ.
- **Tomczak–Kormendy priors** (the narrower widths of the underlying fits): 3.3σ, because those priors predict too faint a background.
- **Survey-average priors** (the authors' recommendation): 1.7σ.
- **HD+MP+DP+CURN spectrum:** 0.6σ.
- **Lowering the spectrum by 0.1–0.2 dex**, as pulsar red-noise priors may require: down to 1.0σ.

The binaries-alone fit pins τ_f near the 0.1 Gyr edge of its prior. Under survey-average priors, requiring τ_f > 2 Gyr pushes the phase-transition preference above 2σ, and τ_f > 6.5 Gyr pushes it above 3σ. The verdict is therefore tied to the final-parsec problem.

Caveats:
- Significances are nominal. The χ² conversion ignores parameters that are non-identifiable under the null.
- Orbits are assumed circular.
- Frequency bins are treated as independent.
- The per-realisation Gaussian likelihood is justified only in the three lowest bins.
- The best-fit phase transitions sit at α≫1, beyond where the sound-wave template was simulated (α up to about 0.1). A strongly supercooled template gives 1.9σ, against 3.2σ with the power law.
