## Themes

### Calorimeter Evolution: Software Meets Hardware

Earlier this week, we saw the ATLAS Collaboration showcase and calibrate its transformer-based GN2 algorithm for jet flavor tagging [arXiv:2607.05322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05322), [arXiv:2607.06199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06199). Today, the transformer wave officially crashes into electromagnetic calorimetry. Matos et al. [arXiv:2607.08175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08175) push past traditional high-level shower shape variables to process raw, cell-level calorimeter signals. By applying a Transformer architecture to the fine-grained segmentation of modern detectors, they achieve a massive performance leap in identifying highly collimated diphotons (typical of light axion-like particles) from single-photon backgrounds. Crucially, the model allows for direct invariant mass regression from the calorimeter cells themselves, effectively turning the calorimeter into a high-precision spectrometer for light resonances.

This software revolution is being matched step-for-step by hardware innovations designed to recover "invisible" energy. Giomataris et al. [arXiv:2607.08587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08587) present a novel sampling hadronic calorimeter that uses alternating lead absorbers and gadolinium-loaded liquid scintillator (LAB-Gd). This design goes beyond capturing the prompt hadronic shower; it directly measures delayed energy from neutron moderation and capture. Utilizing Geant4 simulations of 10 GeV protons, they show that registering this delayed neutron component allows for an event-by-event correction that slashes the hadronic energy resolution from 21.8% to 13.3%. It is a stunning reminder that resolving the fluctuations in invisible hadronic energy remains one of the most fertile areas of detector R&D.

### Dark Sector Excesses and Wave Probes

The dark matter landscape today is dominated by a major pixel-level re-evaluation of a classic signal and new ways to search for ultralight candidates. 

Stenhouse et al. [arXiv:2607.08552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08552) revisit the recently reported 20 GeV spherically symmetric halo-like excess in 15 years of Fermi-LAT data. Transitioning from coarse cell-aggregated fits to a rigorous, pixel-level likelihood analysis with full PSF forward folding, they confirm the existence of this high-latitude excess. The spectrum is remarkably consistent with sub-TeV WIMP annihilation ($m_\chi \approx 0.55\text{ TeV}$ for $W^+W^-$, $0.72\text{ TeV}$ for $b\bar{b}$). While a naive interpretation is in $4\text{--}5\times$ tension with dwarf spheroidal galaxy limits, the authors show that standard J-factor and foreground uncertainties can accommodate the signal, pointing to low-velocity-enhanced (Sommerfeld or Breit-Wigner) annihilation as the most natural way to close the remaining gap.

For those looking at the opposite end of the mass scale, the search for quadratically coupled ultralight dark matter is moving to space. Gan et al. [arXiv:2607.08248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08248) demonstrate that LISA can probe quadratic couplings of ultralight scalar fields to Standard Model particles. Unlike linear couplings, quadratic interactions produce a signature at twice the dark matter mass, as well as a low-frequency band below the dark matter kinetic energy. They find that LISA can bypass the severe screening effects that limit terrestrial experiments, carving out new parameter space in the low-mass regime. This analysis is supported by a foundational paper from Schmitz et al. [arXiv:2607.08445](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08445), which derives the first generalized signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) contour lines for LISA's auto-correlation measurements that remain valid for strong stochastic backgrounds, correcting the standard weak-signal approximations often found in the literature.

### Neutrino Blueprints and Mass Structures

Following yesterday's focus on modular flavor symmetries and the mathematical limits of neutrino mixing, today’s papers continue the systematic push to map the PMNS matrix and refine our experimental backgrounds. 

On the structural side, Ricardo Romero [arXiv:2607.08739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08739) introduces a beautiful mathematical framework based on an internal $SU(2)$ pseudospin subalgebra within $SU(4)$ to classify massive neutral-fermion states. This algebraic approach organizes the lepton-number-violating ($\Delta L = 2$) sector: helicity-preserving directions map onto pseudo-Dirac active-sterile mixing, while helicity-flipping directions carry the active-active Majorana mass factor. This allows for a clean classification of neutrino-antineutrino coherence and Majorana projections without assuming a priori that neutrinos are Majorana particles. Meanwhile, Kitagawa et al. [arXiv:2607.08384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08384) use machine learning (specifically flow matching) to revisit one-zero and two-zero mass textures. They show that while cosmological CMB+BAO bounds have cornered two-zero textures into the $A$-series, one-zero textures remain highly viable and offer distinct, testable correlations between the CP-violating phase $\delta_{CP}$ and the sum of neutrino masses.

On the experimental background front, JUNO, DUNE, and Hyper-K have a new gold standard baseline. Cheng et al. [arXiv:2607.08310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08310) present a major update to the 3D atmospheric neutrino flux based on the Honda model. For the first time, they explicitly simulate muon propagation, decay, and nuclear capture inside the Earth. This addition yields a globally significant, site-independent absolute increment in the neutrino flux below 100 MeV—a critical energy window for relic supernova neutrino searches and the "neutrino fog" that threatens direct dark matter detection.

### High-Precision Mathematical Engines

Underpinning all of these phenomenological advances is the silent, ongoing work of upgrading our calculating machinery. 

Following the community's general concern with computing bottlenecks and "Watts per event" from Monday [arXiv:2607.05018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05018), Doherty et al. [arXiv:2607.08723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08723) tackle the long-standing problem of negative and pathologically large weights in NLO Monte Carlo event generation. They deploy a cell-resampling algorithm utilizing the Energy Mover's Distance (EMD) and a spectral variant derived from Optimal Transport. Because EMD is insensitive to soft and collinear radiation, this method allows for stable local weight redistribution at any stage of event generation, significantly reducing the systematic bias introduced by older, binned resampling techniques.

On the multi-loop amplitude side, Huang et al. [arXiv:2607.08477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08477) release `AMFlow 2.0`. This major release introduces an alternative recursion mode, a high-performance differential equation solver, and support for state-of-the-art integration-by-parts (IBP) reducers. Benchmarked on a challenging three-loop, five-point topology, the new version achieves order-of-magnitude improvements in both symbolic and numerical evaluation speeds, providing a critical tool for the sub-percent precision demands of the HL-LHC.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2607.08175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08175) — Transformer-based machine learning using low-level calorimeter signals for collimated photon identification at collider experiments

*Only the abstract was available for this paper.* By utilizing low-level, cell-segmented calorimeter signals directly, this work introduces a deep learning framework designed to distinguish highly collimated diphoton signatures—characteristic of light axion-like particle (ALP) decays—from isolated single-photon showers. Six architectures were systematically evaluated, with a Transformer model demonstrating the highest classification performance, while an MLP Mixer is proposed as a resource-constrained alternative for real-time trigger-level deployment. Critically, the Transformer enables direct invariant mass regression of the collimated system directly from raw calorimeter cells, enhancing the rejection of $\pi^0$ and $\eta$ fake-photon backgrounds.

This shifts the reconstruction paradigm away from traditional hand-engineered, high-level shower shape variables that discard fine-grained spatial features. By extracting sub-shower structure directly from raw cell deposits, this approach essentially repurposes electromagnetic calorimeters as high-precision spectrometers for sub-GeV resonances. A key caveat of this abstract-only analysis is the lack of explicit performance metrics, simulated detector geometries, or systematic uncertainty evaluations in the provided text.

### [arXiv:2607.08552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08552) — The 20 GeV Galactic Halo Excess: Pixel-Level Confirmation and Consistency with Sub-TeV WIMP Annihilation

This work independently reproduces the high-latitude 20 GeV Galactic halo excess in 15 years of *Fermi*-LAT data and extends the analysis to a pixel-level likelihood fit on native $0.125^\circ \times 0.125^\circ$ maps (ROI: $|l| \leq 60^\circ$, $10^\circ \leq |b| \leq 60^\circ$). Incorporating energy-dependent PSF forward folding and bright-source masking, the pixel-level fit confirms the excess and places its normalization $\sim 20\%$ higher than the original cell-aggregated analysis for an NFW $\rho^2$ profile. The halo template is preferred at the level of $2\sum_k \Delta \ln \mathcal{L}_k \approx 1.7 \times 10^3$ ($930$ in the 10–40 GeV band). Fitting the spectrum to prompt $s$-wave WIMP annihilation yields best-fit masses of $m_\chi \approx 0.55\text{ TeV}$ (for $W^+W^-$) and $0.72\text{ TeV}$ (for $b\bar{b}$), with a cross-section of $\langle \sigma v \rangle \approx 1.0 \times 10^{-24}\text{ cm}^3\,\text{s}^{-1}$. 

This pixel-level validation confirms that the 20 GeV excess is a robust, high-latitude feature spectrally and spatially distinct from the low-energy Galactic Center excess (GCE), and its steep angular falloff rules out a flat isotropic background. However, the best-fit cross-section is in $4\text{--}5\times$ tension with dwarf spheroidal (dSph) limits. Folding in diffuse foreground modelling and $J$-factor uncertainties widens this tension to $\mathcal{R} \approx 1.6\text{--}9.3$. Standard velocity-dependent ($p$- or $d$-wave) annihilation models over-resolve the dSph tension but are robustly excluded because their freeze-out couplings under-predict the present-day halo rate by $\sim 7$ orders of magnitude. Reconciling the signal requires a non-minimal dark sector featuring low-velocity-enhanced (resonant Sommerfeld or Breit-Wigner) annihilation, where a sub-GeV mediator sets a saturation velocity between the dwarf and Galactic scales. Furthermore, the absolute fit is dominated by systematic diffuse foreground residuals ($\chi^2/\nu \approx 8$), particularly the GALPROP-versus-GIEM model choice.

### [arXiv:2607.08587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08587) — A Novel Hadronic Calorimeter With A Direct Neutron Readout

This work presents a Geant4 simulation of a sampling hadronic calorimeter consisting of alternating 10 cm lead absorbers and 5 cm active layers of gadolinium-loaded liquid scintillator (LAB-Gd). Testing a 6-layer (3.66 $\lambda_I$) prototype with 10 GeV protons, the authors show that loading the scintillator with 0.5% (2%) Gd drastically reduces the median neutron-capture time from 134.8 $\mu\text{s}$ in pure LAB to 8.9 $\mu\text{s}$ (4.7 $\mu\text{s}$), enabling delayed neutron signal collection within realistic calorimeter acquisition gates. The captured neutron multiplicity scales linearly with the delayed energy deposition, providing a precise 7.97 MeV/capture calibration. Exploiting the anti-correlation between the prompt scintillation signal and the delayed energy (which tracks the "invisible" hadronic energy of secondary neutrons), they apply an event-by-event, branch-wise interpolation correction that slashes the prompt hadronic energy resolution from 21.8% to 13.3% without any event rejection.

This hardware-software hybrid approach directly addresses the stochastic fluctuations of invisible energy, which are the fundamental limit of hadronic calorimeter resolution. It reveals that measuring neutron production can turn an otherwise mediocre HCAL into a highly precise instrument, with intrinsic resolution for fixed-neutron-multiplicity events reaching $\sim 6\%$. A notable caveat is that this study evaluates a preliminary 1D-layered geometry with a simplified 10 GeV proton source, neglecting full 3D shower leakage, detailed optical photon transport, photosensor (APD) noise, and front-end electronic pile-up.
