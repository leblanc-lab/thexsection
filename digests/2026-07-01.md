## The vibe today

Today’s digest is dominated by a sense of "infrastructure exhaustion"—not in a negative sense, but in the realization that the next decade of discovery depends entirely on our ability to compute the "imcomputable." Whether it is reaching NLO electroweak corrections for complex multi-leg vector-boson scattering ([arXiv:2607.01174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01174)), or finally pushing lattice QCD into the regime of inclusive semi-leptonic decays ([arXiv:2607.01116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01116)), the community is systematically narrowing the gap between theoretical prediction and experimental precision. 

The mood is pragmatic and deeply technical. We are seeing a shift away from "discovery" as a singular, heroic event and toward a vision of physics as a high-precision, AI-augmented, and algorithmically optimized stack. Even the "new" physics papers today, like those targeting dark sector self-scattering or axion-superradiance, are built on the back of rigorous, multi-loop matching frameworks and lattice-calibrated non-perturbative methods. It is an era of tool-builders, and the tools they are forging are increasingly sophisticated.

## Themes

### Higher-Order Precision Tools
The push for sub-percent precision is no longer an aspiration; it is the current standard. Denner et al. [arXiv:2607.01174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01174) set a new bar for the LHC by providing the first NLO electroweak corrections for doubly-polarized $ZZ$ scattering. This isn't just a technical exercise; by isolating polarization states at the amplitude level, they provide the sensitivity required for high-luminosity LHC runs to distinguish subtle BSM effects from standard gauge-sector dynamics. Complementing this, Beneke et al. [arXiv:2607.01413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01413) tackle the power-suppressed region ($Q^2/E^2$) of QCD currents, resolving endpoint singularities in a way that finally makes multi-loop event-shape calculations tractable at higher orders.

### Small-$x$ Gluon Dynamics
The EIC era is effectively already here in the theoretical domain. Wang ([arXiv:2607.00439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00439)) makes a significant move, proving that the gluon D-term—crucial for spatial tomography—cannot be extracted from simple leading-eikonal saturation models. By demonstrating that the D-term is fundamentally a sub-eikonal stress probe, Wang moves the field toward a more sophisticated, "stress-decorated" understanding of the gluon field that matches the precision demands of next-gen collider physics.

### Quantum Information Lenses
Quantum information theory continues to bleed into conventional phenomenology. Pan et al. [arXiv:2607.00971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00971) propose using the tracker material itself as a polarimeter to probe the quantum entanglement of diphoton systems. While the significance at the HL-LHC is modest, the methodology represents a creative pivot: using experimental constraints—often seen as sources of noise—as active instruments to perform foundational tests of quantum mechanics at the high-energy frontier.

### Lattice Renormalization Schemes
The lattice community is moving toward more robust, non-perturbative renormalization strategies. Black et al. [arXiv:2607.00493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00493) propose Gradient Flow (GF) schemes that bypass the cumbersome "backward-flow" constructions, allowing for a cleaner path to $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ matching. This is paired with De Santis et al. [arXiv:2607.01116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01116), who demonstrate a concrete application of lattice methods to inclusive $B_s$ decays—showing that lattice data can be reliably extrapolated to match OPE results, a vital step for testing the flavor sector.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2607.01174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01174) — The cleanest of them all: NLO electroweak corrections to vector-boson scattering into doubly polarised ZZ pairs at the LHC (Ansgar Denner et al.)

Denner et al. report the first NLO electroweak (EW) corrections to the fully leptonic $ZZ$ vector-boson scattering (VBS) channel. Using the double-pole approximation to preserve gauge invariance, the authors separate polarization states at the amplitude level and include factorisable EW corrections to both production and decay. They demonstrate that these corrections are substantial, reducing integrated cross sections by approximately 18% (–14% for longitudinal-longitudinal pairs, –18% for transverse-transverse).

This work is critical for the High-Luminosity LHC era, where VBS observables are expected to test electroweak symmetry breaking with high precision. By providing NLO EW control over polarization-sensitive observables—specifically transverse-momentum and angular distributions—the authors enable experimentalists to move beyond simple unpolarized cross-section measurements. The analysis reveals that while EW corrections are significant, they are relatively flat in many angular observables, reinforcing the utility of polarization fractions as a BSM-sensitive probe. A notable caveat is that this precision currently applies to the four-lepton channel; given the low cross-section, meaningful differential measurement will require merging multiple decay channels to overcome statistical limitations.

### [arXiv:2607.00439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00439) — Sub-eikonal stress and model dependence of the small-$x$ gluon D-term (Lei Wang)

Wang demonstrates that the gluon D-term cannot be extracted from leading-eikonal saturation models (like the standard dipole $S_x$ or saturation profiles $Q_s$). The headline finding is a "no-go" result: the D-term is fundamentally a sub-eikonal stress probe—projected by the symmetric-traceless transverse stress $T_g^{ij}$—and therefore cannot be determined by the leading-eikonal momentum form factor $A_g(t)$. 

This provides a necessary theoretical correction for the EIC era, effectively de-coupling spatial tomography from simple eikonal assumptions. By constructing a gauge-invariant "stress-decorated" Wilson line operator, the paper establishes that the sign and magnitude of the gluon D-term depend on sub-eikonal target fields (e.g., $F^{i-}$). While the authors show that a specific "anti-aligned" response model leads to a stable negative forward D-term, the broader conclusion is that the D-term’s model dependence is a feature, not a bug, of higher-order gluon dynamics. The reliance on this specific response model remains a theoretical assumption, highlighting that current saturation models remain incomplete for full 3D gluon imaging.

### [arXiv:2607.01116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01116) — Inclusive $\bar B_s \mapsto X_{\bar sc} \ell \bar \nu$ decays from lattice QCD: computational strategy and a first physical result (Alessandro De Santis et al.)

De Santis et al. introduce a first-principles lattice QCD framework to calculate the inclusive decay rate of $\bar B_s \to X_{\bar sc} \ell \bar \nu$. The methodology bridges the gap between lattice data (computed at unphysical, lighter heavy-meson masses up to 4.3 GeV) and the Operator Product Expansion (OPE), which is valid in the heavy-quark limit. By employing a new technique for four-point correlator computation, they achieve a 7% total error on the inclusive decay rate using $n_f=2+1+1$ ETMC ensembles.

This result is a milestone for flavor physics, as it brings inclusive $B$-decays into the domain of non-perturbative lattice calculations, offering a path to resolve long-standing tensions in the flavor sector that have previously relied heavily on phenomenological inputs. The 7% error is dominated by the limited size of the current configuration set, though the methodology is explicitly designed for systematic reduction as larger datasets become available. The primary caveat is the extrapolation to the physical heavy-quark mass limit, which relies on the OPE remaining robust in the region where lattice data can currently be reliably produced.
