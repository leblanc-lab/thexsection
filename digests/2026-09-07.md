## The LZ event flips to exothermic

For a week the LZ recoil was read as an endothermic higgsino, and for a week that reading got shot down — Pospelov's solar bound, Rodd's empty sideband, McCabe's runaway modulation. Today the interpretation switches sign: three papers move to *exothermic* inelastic dark matter, where the ambient state down-scatters and dumps its splitting as recoil, sidestepping the high-velocity-tail dependence that made the endothermic case fragile. Away from xenon, the day's real breadth is a cluster of long-lived-particle proposals that repurpose existing detectors, plus two clean CMS measurements.

## Themes

### The LZ event turns exothermic
Dent et al. [arXiv:2609.04673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04673), de Lima [arXiv:2609.05204](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05204), and Gu et al. [arXiv:2609.05291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05291) all pivot to exothermic scattering, which naturally produces a recoil peak at ~250 keV with no lower-energy activity and is "largely insensitive to halo uncertainties" — precisely the vulnerability that killed the endothermic reading. Dent uses the empty high-energy sideband to prefer sharper (lighter, larger-δ) peaks and predicts ~4.5 events in LZ's 1000-day run; de Lima lands at $m_\chi\sim30$–200 GeV, $\delta\sim0.5$–1 MeV in a dark-photon model with testable argon/germanium signals; Gu works the inelastic-xenon excitation companion channel. The kinematics still fix the physics, but exothermic buys back the halo robustness the field spent last week losing.

### Long-lived particles, borrowed detectors
Four papers turn detectors built for something else into LLP traps. Bolton et al. probe $\nu$SMEFT HNLs via displaced dilepton vertices at Belle II [arXiv:2609.04332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04332); Min et al. show LHC-produced long-lived particles decaying inside KM3NeT/ORCA could set among the strongest limits on kaon-to-LLP decays [arXiv:2609.04386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04386); and a second Bolton et al. paper reads displaced Majoron decays inside $0\nu\beta\beta$ experiments as a two-orders-of-magnitude sensitivity gain [arXiv:2609.05352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05352). The sharpest is Wang [arXiv:2609.04761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04761): under a shared-coupling condition the flavor ratio of displaced leptons is fixed by PMNS data alone, giving $N_e/N_\mu=0.14$ (normal) vs $2.11$ (inverted) — a δ_CP-free collider handle on mass ordering. This extends the GRENDEL-style repurposing thread from earlier in the week.

### CMS: two clean measurements
CMS observes a negative azimuthal asymmetry $A_2=-0.194$ at >5σ for coherently photoproduced J/ψ in PbPb [arXiv:2609.04376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04376) — first direct evidence the meson inherits the incident photon's linear polarization, and a possible new reaction-plane handle. Separately, the rare loop-induced H→Zγ decay comes in at $\mu=1.10^{+0.52}_{-0.61}$, 1.9σ observed [arXiv:2609.04402](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04402) — SM-consistent, still statistics-limited.

### Precision from EFT and lattice
Cherukuri et al. pin the deuteron magnetic polarizability to 1.3% [arXiv:2609.04615](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04615), with the two expansion schemes converging from 12% disagreement at NLO to 0.17% at NNLO — the same pionless-EFT/on-shell program that produced Tait's BBN rate. Allwicher et al. lay out $10^{-4}$-level kaon/pion leptonic ratios as a lepton-universality probe now that lattice matches [arXiv:2609.05231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05231), and Romero-López et al. derive the finite-volume formalism to reach the Wess-Zumino-Witten term on the lattice via $K\bar K\to3\pi$ [arXiv:2609.05006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05006).

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2609.04376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04376) — Spin-dependent azimuthal asymmetry of coherent J/ψ photoproduction in hadronic PbPb collisions (CMS Collaboration)

**What they did:** CMS measured the azimuthal distribution of decay muons from coherently photoproduced J/ψ (reconstructed at $p_T<0.2$ GeV, $1.6<|y|<2.4$) relative to the second-order event plane $\Psi_2$ in 50–80% central PbPb collisions at 5.36 TeV, using 1.26 nb⁻¹. The $\cos(2\Delta\phi)$ modulation amplitude is $A_2=-0.194\pm0.022\,\text{(stat)}\pm0.018\,\text{(syst)}$ — negative, and different from zero by more than 5σ, the first such observation for spin-1/2 muons. $A_2$ shows no significant dependence on rapidity or centrality (7–18% dominant systematic from signal extraction; 3–9% hadronic/incoherent contamination subtracted via a high-$p_T$ fit extrapolation).

**Why it matters:** Combined with the positive modulation previously seen for spin-0 pions from ρ⁰ decays, this establishes spin-state dependence of azimuthal asymmetries in coherently photoproduced vector-meson decays — direct confirmation that the J/ψ inherits the incident photon's linear polarization. Crucially, using $\Psi_2$ rather than the meson-$p_T$ interference proxy makes the polarization handle work across broad rapidity and different collision systems, opening a novel reaction-plane estimator that is less sensitive to nonflow and fluctuations, potentially valuable for small systems like pA. **Caveat:** the Wu et al. model describes the data well, but the saturation-based Shao et al. calculation overshoots the amplitude by roughly a factor of two, so the theory picture is not settled.

### [arXiv:2609.04673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04673) — Exothermic and Endothermic Inelastic Dark Matter Interpretations at LZ (James B. Dent et al.)

**What they did:** Dent and Newstead reinterpret LZ's single ~248 keV nuclear-recoil event (LZ230616, background $0.0106\pm0.0008$) as *exothermic* inelastic DM, where the ambient heavier state $\chi^*$ down-scatters and dumps its splitting $|\delta|$ as recoil, producing a peak at $E_0=\mu_{\chi N}|\delta|/m_N$. An unbinned shape likelihood fixes the peak to the event energy with $\sigma_p f_*\simeq(0.5$–$3)\times10^{-45}$ cm². The empty high-energy sideband (350–680 keV, zero events) disfavors broad heavy-mass peaks — expected sideband yields per observed event are 0.02 (10 GeV), 19 (42 GeV), 73 (100 GeV) — bounding $m_\chi\lesssim90$ GeV at 95% CL. Excited-state stability then splits solutions into a ~40–90 GeV state with sub-MeV splitting and dark-photon mediator, or a sub-40 GeV state with ~1–10 MeV splitting requiring a leptophobic mediator (to close $\chi^*\to\chi e^+e^-$).

**Why it matters:** Exothermic scattering peaks above the elastic endpoint and is largely halo-tail-independent, sidestepping precisely the high-velocity-tail fragility that undermined the endothermic higgsino readings last week. It predicts ≃4.5 events in LZ's projected 1000-day (~13 tonne-yr) run, and existing XENONnT/PandaX-4T exposures (1.1 and 0.5 expected events) could already test it in extended windows; the $\mu_{\chi N}/m_N$ peak scaling gives a distinct argon/germanium signature. **Caveat:** the sideband recast relies on unpublished efficiency/energy-scale extrapolations beyond calibration, and the Helm form factor gives an $\mathcal{O}(10^{-4})$ suppression with large uncertainty at this $q$ — the authors stress this is no substitute for a collaboration analysis.

### [arXiv:2609.04761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04761) — Flavor Tomography of Long-Lived Neutrino-Mass Mediators (Renjie Wang)

**What they did:** Wang shows that if the long-lived charged particle producing displaced-lepton signals at the HL-LHC is *also* the mediator generating Majorana neutrino masses (the "shared-coupling condition"), the Casas–Ibarra parametrization fixes the displaced-lepton flavor ratio $N_e/N_\mu=\sum_i|U_{ei}|^2 m_i/\sum_i|U_{\mu i}|^2 m_i$ entirely in terms of PMNS data. At NuFit 6.1 best-fit with $m_\text{lightest}=0$: $N_e/N_\mu=0.140$ (normal) vs $2.11$ (inverted), a discrimination ratio $\mathcal{R}=15.1$. The prediction holds identically across Scotogenic, Type-III seesaw, inverse/linear seesaw, and lepton-portal DM models (given quasi-degenerate mediators and near-real $R$). Detector efficiencies cancel in the ratio; $\delta_{CP}$ dependence is $<3\%$. An exact Poisson test gives 3σ ordering discrimination with $\mathcal{O}(25)$ timing-tagged ($\Delta t>200$ ps) displaced leptons; Scotogenic yields for $M_\text{LLP}=300$–600 GeV are $N_\text{disp}\approx64$–2856.

**Why it matters:** This is a $\delta_{CP}$-free collider handle on the mass ordering, structurally immune to the degeneracy that limits T2K/NOvA and complementary to JUNO — because the observable depends on $|U_{\alpha i}|^2$, not $U_{\alpha i}$. It sharpens exactly where prior two-HNL flavor-counting proposals (capped at ≲90% classification) are broad. It extends the week's detector-repurposing theme, exploiting the CMS MTD / ATLAS HGTD timing layers already under construction. **Caveats:** requires the shared-coupling class (real $R$, degenerate mediators); complex-$R$ and non-degenerate corrections reach $\mathcal{O}(10$–$30\%)$; the worst-case $3\sigma$ scan gives $\mathcal{R}=8.85$, dropping to $\mathcal{R}\geq2.2$ for larger $m_\text{lightest}$ within the Planck-allowed range; and it presupposes a positive LLP discovery plus Majorana neutrinos.
