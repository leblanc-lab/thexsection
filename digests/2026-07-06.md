## Themes

### Electroweak Symmetry Breaking and New Physics Limits

The search for the Higgs self-coupling and the broader scalar potential remains the definitive battleground of the LHC. Today, the CMS Collaboration pushes this search to its current limit with a search for nonresonant triple Higgs ($HHH$) production in the highly challenging six-bottom-quark final state [arXiv:2607.05145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05145). While the observed limit of 44 fb is still roughly 580 times the Standard Model cross-section, this work represents a major milestone: for the first time, experimental data is directly ruling out regions of the $(\kappa_3, \kappa_4)$ coupling modifier space that were previously only constrained by theoretical perturbative unitarity bounds. 

To achieve this level of precision, the experiments are heavily relying on advanced flavor tagging. The ATLAS Collaboration showcases this transition with a performance study of the GN2 algorithm [arXiv:2607.05322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05322), a transformer-based model for jet flavor tagging evaluated on $\sqrt{s} = 13.6$ TeV Run 3 data. GN2 achieves up to a factor of three improvement in charm rejection over the older DL1d tagger, demonstrating how deep learning is directly translating to better sensitivity in multi-jet final states.

These experimental advancements are systematically squeezing traditional BSM scenarios. Baer et al. [arXiv:2607.05648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05648) deliver a tough verdict for natural supersymmetry, showing that recent LZ direct-detection limits have essentially cornered stable light higgsino dark matter. Even under the assumption that higgsinos form only a fraction of the total dark matter, the surviving "blind spots" in parameter space require fine-tuned, unnatural gaugino masses. This leaves us with a stark choice: either accept unstable higgsinos, or look for highly specific cosmological histories. Meanwhile, Ashanujjaman et al. [arXiv:2607.05499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05499) explore a clever, overlooked alternative for light charged Higgses below the top mass: the off-shell $H^\pm \to t^*b$ decay mode. By reinterpreting ATLAS dileptonic $t\bar{t}$ data, they show this channel provides powerful constraints in top-philic and aligned two-Higgs-doublet models that conventional searches miss.

### Relic Echoes and Dark Sectors

Looking beyond colliders, the hunt is on for relic signatures of the early universe, but today's papers show that both the theory and the detection strategies require careful handling of dynamical assumptions. 

Nowhere is this clearer than in the search for the Cosmic Neutrino Background (CNB). Long et al. [arXiv:2607.05221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05221) analyze the feasibility of detecting the "relic neutrino wind"—the dipolar anisotropy in the CNB flux due to the Earth's movement. They calculate that detecting this anisotropy with a tritium $\beta$-decay experiment demands an exposure at least $10^5$ times larger than that needed to simply observe the isotropic flux. Furthermore, they highlight a major physical hurdle: for Majorana neutrinos, the wind signal is suppressed relative to the Dirac case by a factor of $(m_\nu/T_\nu)^2$.

In the dark matter sector, theorists are dissecting the initial conditions of vector dark matter. Khan et al. [arXiv:2607.05309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05309) show that the standard stochastic inflationary scenario for generating a dark Higgs condensate fails to provide the large initial displacement needed to seed parametric resonance for vector dark matter. Instead, they identify a robust, classically sourced branch driven by a negative Hubble-induced mass, establishing that vector dark matter relic abundance is a sensitive diagnostic of the inflationary epoch. 

Similarly, Nakano et al. [arXiv:2607.05517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05517) challenge a long-held cosmological assumption regarding global monopole networks. They demonstrate that these networks do not follow a constant scaling law; instead, they exhibit a logarithmic-like density evolution. This result has immediate consequences for the projected gravitational-wave signals and dark matter yields from these topological defects. 

### Amplitude Methods and Hadron Structure

We are also seeing a fascinating cross-pollination of theoretical techniques. Modern on-shell amplitude methods, traditionally the domain of multi-loop perturbative QCD, are being brought to bear on low-energy nuclear physics. Tim M. P. Tait [arXiv:2607.05514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05514) uses on-shell techniques to construct an effective field theory for the $d(p,\gamma)^3\mathrm{He}$ fusion reaction, a critical component of Big Bang nucleosynthesis (BBN). By bypassing the traditional Lagrangian formulation, this approach fits the discrepant LUNA data and traces the systematic shift to a single, natural-sized next-to-leading-order contact term, offering a cleaner path to high-precision nuclear cross sections.

On the nonperturbative QCD front, Chang et al. [arXiv:2607.04871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04871) report the first functional QCD calculation of the pion distribution amplitude using the functional renormalization group and large-momentum effective theory (LaMET). By reaching pion momenta of $4.5\text{ GeV}$, they eliminate extrapolation errors to the light-cone limit, obtaining a second-order moment ($\langle \xi^2 \rangle_\pi = 0.267$) that is significantly smaller than current lattice determinations. Additionally, Deur [arXiv:2607.05144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05144) connects the physical, running QCD effective charge to the analytic structure of partonic propagators, arguing that conjugate singularities in the long-distance limit naturally explain confinement as a spatial suppression of QCD Green’s functions.

### Software and Detector Optimization

As we approach the High-Luminosity LHC and next-generation intensity frontier experiments, computational and structural scaling limits are becoming central engineering challenges.

In the software domain, Molnár et al. [arXiv:2607.05018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05018) introduce the "Watts per event" metric, benchmarking the HIJING++ heavy-ion event generator to optimize multithreading and CPU architecture choices. This systematic look at the energy footprint of event generation represents a necessary step toward sustainable computing in the HL-LHC era. On the reconstruction side, Varni et al. [arXiv:2607.04723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04723) propose a hybrid track-reconstruction algorithm that feeds a Hough transform's parameter space directly into a convolutional neural network. This pipeline successfully suppresses the massive flood of fake track candidates typical of HL-LHC pileup without resorting to expensive coordinate transformations.

For detector R&D, Soleti et al. [arXiv:2607.04944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04944) demonstrate that tetraphenyl butadiene (TPB)-coated wavelength-shifting fibers can reliably detect vacuum-ultraviolet scintillation light in high-pressure xenon and argon. This provides a crucial proof-of-concept for the light collection systems in the NEXT experiment's planned high-pressure time projection chambers.
