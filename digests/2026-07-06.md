## The vibe today

A broad, foundational sort of day — the kind where no single bombshell dominates but several sturdy pillars go up at once. Two results that the week's memory already flags as turning points actually land here: CMS's first direct assault on the triple-Higgs coupling space ([arXiv:2607.05145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05145)) and ATLAS's calibration of the transformer-based GN2 tagger on Run 3 data ([arXiv:2607.05322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05322)). Both feel less like surprises than like the field methodically converting years of instrumentation and theory into hard numbers on the coupling landscape.

Underneath the LHC headlines, the mood is unmistakably QCD-flavored. A cluster of papers reaches into the nonperturbative interior of hadrons — pion distribution amplitudes, proton gravitational form factors, the analytic structure of the effective charge and its link to confinement — while dense-matter QCD shows up through lattice two-color simulations and anomalous Wess-Zumino-Witten terms. This is not new physics; it is the field tending its foundations, and doing so with real ambition.

The other half of the room is chasing dark matter, and the tone there is one of pressure and pivoting. Under the shadow of LZ limits, higgsino WIMPs get their blind-spot escape routes closed off, while others turn to vector dark matter, self-interacting halos, cosmic-ray-DM collisions, and laboratory photon experiments. It's a scattered, exploratory energy — the sound of a community probing every remaining seam.

## Themes

### Nonperturbative QCD and the guts of hadrons

Today's densest thread. Lei Chang and collaborators deliver the first functional-QCD calculation of the pion distribution amplitude via LaMET within the fRG ([arXiv:2607.04871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04871)), reporting a second moment notably *smaller* than lattice-LaMET determinations — a tension worth watching. Kim et al. present the flavor-decomposed gravitational form factors and mechanical structure of the proton, isolating the twist-4 $\bar c$ term and finding a sizable strange-quark contribution to the $D$-term ([arXiv:2607.05079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05079)). Deur offers an unusually intuitive picture, tracing confinement to two conjugate singularities in the effective charge $\alpha_{g_1}$ and reading off exponentially suppressed parton propagators at long distance ([arXiv:2607.05144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05144)). Rounding it out: two-color lattice QCD finding a superfluid sound speed above the conformal bound ([arXiv:2607.04624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04624)), and WZW terms for neutral mesons under magnetic field and rotation ([arXiv:2607.04929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04929)). Collectively, a strong statement that hadron structure and dense-matter QCD remain fertile, first-principles ground.

### Dark sector under pressure — and pivoting

Baer et al. ask whether direct-detection blind spots can save light higgsino dark matter and answer, bluntly, no: the surviving cancellations all sit in the unnatural regime, pushing natural SUSY toward *unstable* higgsinos ([arXiv:2607.05648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05648)) — a direct continuation of the LZ-cornered-WIMP storyline the memory has been tracking. Elsewhere the response is to look somewhere new: Higgsed vector dark matter via parametric resonance, reframed as a probe of inflationary initial conditions ([arXiv:2607.05309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05309)); a fresh Monte-Carlo SIDM implementation in AREPO-2 for gravothermal core collapse ([arXiv:2607.05504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05504)); keV-scale DM limits from Galactic cosmic-ray–DM scattering seen in ANTARES neutrinos ([arXiv:2607.05335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05335)); and two laboratory photon-based searches — an X-ray light-shining-through-walls proposal at HEPS for dark photons ([arXiv:2607.05288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05288)) and a Breit-Wheeler pair-production channel for MeV-scale ALPs with a Fano-type interference signature ([arXiv:2607.05572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05572)). The WIMP is cornered; the search is diversifying.

### The Higgs self-coupling, made experimental

CMS's nonresonant $HHH\to 6b$ search sets the most stringent limit yet on triple-Higgs production and, crucially, excludes for the first time a slice of $(\kappa_3,\kappa_4)$ space previously bounded only by perturbative unitarity ([arXiv:2607.05145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05145)). Complementing the scalar-sector push from below, Ashanujjaman et al. reinterpret ATLAS $t\bar t b\bar b$ measurements to constrain a light charged Higgs through the near-threshold $H^\pm\to t^*b$ channel ([arXiv:2607.05499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05499)) — a genuinely underexplored window, and yet another EFT-style reinterpretation of existing fiducial data, echoing a habit the memory has noted becoming standard.

### Reconstruction infrastructure keeps maturing

The GN2 flavor-tagger calibration in dileptonic $t\bar t$ ([arXiv:2607.05322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05322)) reports up to 2–3× better light/charm rejection over DL1d, with ~1% uncertainty at high $p_T$ — the transformer wave the memory has been following, now benchmarked. On the tracking side, a hybrid Hough-transform-plus-CNN scheme suppresses spurious HL-LHC track candidates by learning directly on the parameter-space image ([arXiv:2607.04723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04723)). Detector hardware fills in around it: WLS fibers for VUV scintillation in high-pressure noble TPCs ([arXiv:2607.04944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04944)), MOSFET diagnostics for p-stop isolation in silicon sensors ([arXiv:2607.05528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05528)), and the fully-digital PUEO beamformer trigger ([arXiv:2607.05285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05285)).

### Neutrinos and light-element inputs

The precision-inputs mindset extends to neutrinos. Ishidoshiro pushes $0\nu\beta\beta$ beyond half-life limits toward stability of the *operator-level* interpretation across isotopes and NME methods, finding light-Majorana exchange the most robust ([arXiv:2607.04932](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04932)). Long et al.'s relic-neutrino-wind exposure penalty ([arXiv:2607.05221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05221)) — a sobering $10^5$ factor — also appears here. And in a methodological standout, Tait recasts $d(p,\gamma)^3$He capture as an on-shell-amplitude EFT, tracing the LUNA–vs–ab initio offset in BBN to a single natural next-to-leading contact term ([arXiv:2607.05514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05514)).

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2607.05145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05145) — Search for nonresonant triple Higgs boson production in the six-b-quark final state at √s = 13 TeV

Using the full Run 2 dataset (138 fb⁻¹), CMS performs the first nonresonant $HHH\to6b$ search built around an inclusive transformer/SPANet reconstruction that spans all resolved-to-merged topologies (three $H$ from small-radius jet pairs, from large-radius jets, or mixtures). A two-stage SPANet classifier plus a data-driven QCD-multijet model (95% of background) with $b/bb$-tag score resampling yields no excess. The observed (expected) 95% CL upper limit is 44 (43) fb, or 588 (572)× the SM $\sigma^{HHH}_{\rm ggF}=0.08$ fb — the most stringent triple-Higgs bound to date. In the coupling framework, $-7.4<\kappa_3<12.4$ (for $\kappa_4=1$) and $-177<\kappa_4<185$ (for $\kappa_3=1$), improving on ATLAS's earlier $6b$ result ($-11<\kappa_3<17$; $-230<\kappa_4<240$).

Why it matters: the two-parameter $(\kappa_3,\kappa_4)$ scan, for the first time in a direct $HHH$ search, excludes a slice of parameter space previously bounded only by perturbative unitarity — pushing the quartic-coupling frontier from theory into data, as the digest flags. Sensitivity is limited by data statistics (~52% of the expected limit), with the QCD-multijet shape the leading systematic. Honest caveats: the coupling scans assume SM signal topology at every coupling value (cross-section-only scaling), and $\kappa_4$-dependent kinematic/shape effects in $HH$ are deferred; including realistic kinematics degrades the expected $\kappa_3$ bound by 12–15%. The $HH$ contribution is fixed to $\mu_{HH}=1$ with profiled uncertainty. HL-LHC projections (S3 scenario) are provided but the current constraint remains ~600× SM.

### [arXiv:2607.05322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05322) — b-jet identification efficiency for GN2 in dileptonic tt̄ at √s = 13.6 TeV

(Abstract only.) ATLAS calibrates the transformer-based GN2 flavour tagger on 56 fb⁻¹ of 2022–2023 Run 3 data, extracting the $b$-jet efficiency and jet-flavour composition simultaneously from a $t\bar t$-enriched dileptonic sample, binned in jet $p_T$ (20–400 GeV) across six cumulative-efficiency working points. GN2 delivers up to 2× (3×) higher light-flavour (charm) rejection than its predecessor DL1d at fixed $b$-efficiency. Data/simulation correction factors fall in 0.9–1.3, with total uncertainty around 1% for $p_T>60$ GeV in the loosest ([65%,0%]) interval.

Why it matters: this converts GN2 from demonstrator to calibrated production tool on Run 3 data, backing the digest's "transformer wave" theme with benchmarked numbers — the same tagging performance that underpins searches like the $HHH\to6b$ analysis above. The ~1% high-$p_T$ uncertainty sets the achievable precision for the many Run 3 analyses that will now adopt GN2.

### [arXiv:2607.05514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05514) — Deuterium-Proton Fusion in an EFT Constructed from On-Shell Amplitudes

Tait builds $d(p,\gamma)^3$He radiative capture as a nuclear-state EFT in which deuteron, proton, and $^3$He are point-like degrees of freedom, and constructs the amplitude entirely from on-shell methods — massive spinor-helicity three-point vertices, BCFW recursion, and an enumerated tower of transverse boundary (two-body-current) operators — bypassing any Lagrangian. The expansion parameter is $p_{\rm rel}/p_*$ with $p_*\approx53$ MeV set by deuteron breakup, giving a contact-operator scale $\Lambda=\sqrt{4\pi}\,p_*\approx187$ MeV. A global Bayesian fit to LUNA/Türkat capture data plus $^3$He ANC and NDA priors returns $S(0)=0.209\pm0.008$ eV b (between Solar Fusion III's 0.203 and Marcucci's ab initio 0.216), with $\chi^2/{\rm dof}=0.83$.

The headline physics result: the persistent offset between the ab initio benchmark and the sub-percent LUNA data is traced to a single natural-sized next-to-leading $E1$ contact term, $t_{E1}\simeq-0.146\pm0.021$ (~15% amplitude, ~30% rate deficit), degenerate with a ~15%-lower effective $^3$He asymptotic normalization. This recasts the PRIMAT–PArthENoPE split (a ~2σ $D/H$ discrepancy) from a choice between curves into a measurable low-energy constant. Caveats stated plainly: the angle-integrated $S$-factor cannot separate the contact term from the ANC (a precise $p$–$d$ doublet elastic measurement is proposed), Türkat data sit ~2.3σ high and are floated, quadrupole contacts are prior-limited, and the analytic work was AI-assisted (Claude), human-validated.
