## LZ's background gets audited

For seven weeks the 248 keV literature has treated LZ's background estimate as fixed and argued only about which dark-matter model survives solar capture. Bandyopadhyay et al. [arXiv:2610.27105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27105) question that estimate. Rebuilding the electron-recoil continuum from LZ's released simulation configuration, they find the published calibration does not constrain the response tail at the probabilities that matter. Tails that match the executable model to 0.47% in the $N_e$ quantiles make the ER contribution comparable to the whole quoted background in the comparison region. This is not a claim that the tail is wrong, and they list the measurements that would settle it. Still, it is the first paper to put the event's significance itself on the table. Elsewhere, birefringence attracts two new sources and τ physics gets a set of future-collider projections.

## Themes

### Birefringence finds new fields
Minotti et al. [arXiv:2610.27082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27082) source the signal from a subdominant stiff ($w=1$) pseudoscalar. BBN and cluster-quasar conversion squeeze $g_{\phi\gamma}$ into $8.9\times10^{-14}$–$5.0\times10^{-13}$ GeV⁻¹, a window astrophysical axion searches can close. Bagherian et al. [arXiv:2610.27059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27059) get birefringence from axion–monopole dynamics, in the same parameter space that fits DESI's phantom-like dark energy. Both join Kasai's lepton-asymmetry route (10-09) as alternatives to an axion explanation of the year's 3.5σ post-dust signal.

### τ at future colliders
Allwicher et al. [arXiv:2610.27057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27057) find that FCC-ee's $2\times10^{11}$ τ pairs improve $\tau\to3\ell$ and $\ell\gamma$ limits by 30–800×. That beats $Z\to\tau\ell$ on reach and on operator coverage, though $h\to\tau\ell$ wins for Yukawa-like operators. Senol et al. [arXiv:2610.28147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28147) project $|a_\tau|\lesssim3.4\times10^{-4}$ at FCC-hh, more than ten times looser than Buttazzo's FCC-ee threshold projection from 10-12. Liu et al. [arXiv:2610.28400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28400) connect electron EDMs to timelike τ dipoles.

### Taggers under budgets
Cox et al.'s LEAF [arXiv:2610.27078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27078) runs a LUT-aware linformer at 35 ns and 28k LUTs for trigger-level jet tagging. Baruah et al.'s SParTA [arXiv:2610.27106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27106) matches ParT on quark–gluon tagging with one-seventh the parameters, by giving full attention only to the hard core of the jet.

### DGLAP as a curve, twice
Kou et al. [arXiv:2610.27886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27886) and Bondarenko et al. [arXiv:2610.28115](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28115) independently put LO DGLAP on the Fisher–Rao manifold on the same day. The first finds a near-small-circle trajectory; the second derives a closed-form lower bound on the evolution speed. Neither changes a fit, but the coincidence is notable.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2610.27105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27105): The Electron-Recoil Response Tail and the LZ High-Energy Event Background

The published LZ response does not fix the ER leakage that the 248 keV event's background estimate depends on. The authors rebuild the ER continuum from NEST's `EFT2024` configuration and get a per-decay leakage of $P\simeq1.3\times10^{-7}$ at a 4.1σ displacement in $N_e$. That yields $N^{\rm ER}_{\rm leak}=(1.9\pm0.1\pm0.4)\times10^{-4}$ counts, against LZ's 0.0106 total in the $500<\mathrm{S1c}<600$ phd region. They then construct three families of distributions (generalised-normal, skew-t, Johnson $S_U$) that match the executable model's mean, variance and skewness exactly. The families agree within a factor of ~2 at matched quantile deviation, yet the tail probability spans more than five orders of magnitude.

The thresholds are close to the model:

- **0.14% quantile agreement:** ER reaches 10% of the quoted background.
- **0.47%:** ER equals the whole quoted background.
- **1.10%:** ER reaches 0.1 counts.

LZ's counter-argument, that no events appear between the event and the ER band, does not discriminate between these tails. Even the heaviest family predicts only 0.69 events in that gap. Two further points:

- **Printed vs. implemented response.** The printed Supplement Eq. 4 gives a fluctuation width 1.475× the implemented one. Taken literally, it implies 0.60 ER counts, far above LZ's quoted total for all backgrounds.
- **Calibration reach.** The tritium and ¹⁴C calibrations stop at S1c = 500 phd, while the event sits at 540 phd.

**Caveats.** The authors explicitly do not claim the tail is wrong, and they do not propagate the result to LZ's 2.6σ global (3.4σ local) significance. The link between the NEST commit and the Letter is circumstantial. The ER selection efficiency is unpublished. The ¹²⁴Xe/¹²⁵I lines are excluded.

**What would settle it.** A tail measurement at the $10^{-5}$ level near $N_q\sim5\times10^3$, or a shape fit using ~$3\times10^3$ events per slice (rising to $3\times10^4$ for the mildest problematic tail).

### [arXiv:2610.28506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28506): Isospin breaking in ρππ couplings

The missing isospin-breaking (IB) ρππ coupling correction comes out small and cuts the corresponding τ-route uncertainty to roughly a third of the White Paper's assumed ±4.7×10⁻¹⁰. The method is a symmetry-preserving Dyson–Schwinger/Bethe–Salpeter contact-interaction model. IB enters dynamically through flavour-dependent beyond-rainbow-ladder couplings $\xi_{u,d}$, tuned to $\Delta m_\rho$, and explicitly through $m_d/m_u\in[1,2]$. Results:

- **Coupling ratio:** $g_{\rho^0\pi\pi}/g_{\rho^+\pi\pi}=1.0024(17)(1)$.
- **Width difference:** $\Delta\Gamma_\rho=+0.39(51)$ MeV, compatible with but tighter than PDG (+0.31 ± 1.3) and a data-based extraction (−0.58 ± 1.01).
- **Effect on $a_\mu$:** the ρππ term is $\Delta a_\mu=-2.32(1.64)\times10^{-10}$, about one-third of the White Paper's error.

The −2.32 is not the net shift. It nearly cancels against the radiative-correction term (−2.22) and the pion-mass phase-space term (+4.09). The total form-factor-ratio IB is $-0.25(1.64)\times10^{-10}$, compared with the older $-1.62(65)\times10^{-10}$.

**Caveats.**
- The contact interaction is a crude kernel; the symmetric coupling comes out at 6.29 against 5.94 from the PDG width.
- The error is driven almost entirely by the poorly known input $\Delta m_\rho=0.7(0.8)$ MeV.
- The τ-side numbers rely on a Gounaris–Sakurai parametrisation.
- The same framework predicts $R_\phi=1.484\pm0.029$, 2.2σ below BESIII.

### [arXiv:2610.28283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28283): Automation of amplitude-level QED resummation with ACRAIC

*The extracted text cuts off before the validation section, so the headline numbers here come from the abstract.*

ACRAIC brings coherent (CEEX-style) amplitude-level YFS exponentiation to general processes inside Sherpa. Previously this existed only for hand-coded fermion-pair production in KKMC. The generalisation works in three steps:

- **Photon assignment.** The initial/final assignment of photons becomes an assignment to emission stages. Gauge invariance forces these stages to be partitions of the charged legs into charge-neutral sets, including resonance stages for $Z/\gamma^*$ and $W$ pairs.
- **Remainders.** The infrared-finite remainders are tree-level helicity amplitudes with $N+2$ and $N+3$ legs and partition-dependent propagator shifts, computed by Comix's Berends–Giele recursion.
- **Virtual correction.** The one-loop electroweak virtual enters as a per-helicity factor from OpenLoops or Recola.

Validation is against KKMC for $e^+e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-$ at photonic $\mathcal O(\alpha^2)$. Comparison is also made with the squared-level $\text{YFS}_{\rm NLO_{EW}}$, which shares the resummation and differs only in matching. At $\mathcal O(\alpha^2)$ the two matchings agree on the size of the missing higher orders to ~$10^{-3}$, and the perturbative uncertainty drops about tenfold relative to $\mathcal O(\alpha)$. The author reports that squared-level matching can go unstable in four-lepton production while the amplitude-level version stays stable. Example applications are the FCC-ee Higgs recoil mass and Z-pole distributions.

**Caveats.**
- "Photonic $\mathcal O(\alpha^2)$" omits two-loop $\gamma Z$ boxes and weak corrections.
- The paper is described as mostly technical, with phenomenology left to dedicated studies.
