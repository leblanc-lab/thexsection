## Dark matter spreads out, one clean rate

A diffuse day with no single collaboration headline — ATLAS's photon-jet resonance search ([arXiv:2608.26935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26935)) is the one full-dataset result and it is a null. The real breadth is in dark matter, where six papers span paleo-detector sensitivity, IceCube annihilation limits, direct/indirect complementarity, and two fresh takes on the baryon–DM coincidence. The cleanest standalone result is Tait's [arXiv:2608.23699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23699) BBN calculation, which retires $n+p\to d\gamma$ from the primordial D/H error budget. Underneath both runs the same tooling tide the memory has tracked all month — differentiable likelihoods, GPU photon transport, and phase-space samplers.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2608.23699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23699) — Deuterium Production in an Effective Field Theory Constructed from On-Shell Amplitudes (Tim M. P. Tait)

Tait computes $n+p\to d\gamma$ in a pionless EFT whose degrees of freedom are the nuclear states themselves: the amplitude is glued from on-shell three-point vertices (the $d$–$n$–$p$ vertex normalized to the measured ANC $A_S=0.8846\,\mathrm{fm}^{-1/2}$), with $S_0^1$ rescattering resummed dispersively, leaving just two short-distance constants — an isovector magnetic ($c_{M1}$) and an electric dipole ($c_{E1}$) contact. A joint Bayesian fit to two thermal-capture measurements and the 22-point SLEGS photodisintegration set returns $c_{M1}=+0.162\pm0.015$ and $c_{E1}=-0.88\pm0.21$, both of natural size, with a comfortable fit ($\chi^2=20.3$ for 22 dof). The thermonuclear rate is pinned to $0.22$–$0.24\%$ across $T_9=0.01$–$3$, rising to $0.25$–$0.27\%$ once truncation (bounded at $0.12\%$) is folded in. Propagated through PRyMordial, this shifts predicted primordial D/H by $-0.06\%$ and cuts this reaction's D/H uncertainty contribution from $0.089\%$ to $0.050\%$ — effectively retiring $np\to d\gamma$ from the error budget, where the $d+d$ burning reactions now dominate.

Why it matters: it delivers the sub-0.3% production anchor needed to keep pace with improving burning-rate data, and demonstrates the on-shell-amplitude program (previously applied to $d(p,\gamma)^3$He) generalizes to a second capture, with the shared $d$–$n$–$p$ vertex promising correlated control over all four primary reactions. Honest caveats: the fitted electric contact pulls $3.5\sigma$ against Rupak's pre-2000 determination ($L_{E1}=+14.9$ vs $-5.3\,\mathrm{fm}^3$), which Tait attributes to a genuine data–data tension between the near-threshold older set and SLEGS's higher reach rather than a calculational disagreement. The two thermal measurements themselves differ by $1.9\sigma$, requiring PDG error scaling. Notably, the paper states all analytic and numerical work was primarily derived using LLMs (Claude, GPT-5.6), human-validated.

### [arXiv:2608.26935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26935) — Search for high-mass resonances in photon-jet final states using 140 fb⁻¹ at √s = 13 TeV with ATLAS (ATLAS Collaboration)

ATLAS scans the $\gamma$–jet invariant-mass spectrum over the full Run 2 dataset for localized excesses, finding none: BumpHunter yields no significant deviation across four regions (inclusive SR plus flavor-tagged SRL/SRC/SRB, the latter using the GN2 transformer tagger). The key advance over the 36.7 fb⁻¹ predecessor is heavy-flavor tagging of the leading jet, enabling separate excited-quark interpretations. For coupling $f=1$, masses are excluded up to 6.0 TeV ($q_L^*$), 3.5 TeV ($c^*$, via a simultaneous SRC+SRB+SRL fit), and 2.6 TeV ($b^*$) — the $c^*$ result being the first direct constraint in this channel at the LHC. Quantum-black-hole thresholds are excluded to 5.3 TeV (Randall-Sundrum, $n=1$) and 7.5 TeV (ADD, $n=6$). Model-independent Gaussian-resonance limits are provided for widths 2%/7%/15%.

Why it matters: it is today's one full-dataset collaboration result, extending compositeness and extra-dimension reach and closing the $c^*$ gap in $\gamma$–jet. Backgrounds are modeled analytically with smoothly-falling functions, validated via spurious-signal tests (bias required $<50\%$ of statistical uncertainty). Caveat: the ADD QBH exclusion (7.5 TeV) is actually slightly weaker than the previous 7.1 TeV in absolute terms only if misread — here it improves — but note downward data fluctuations at high mass, and minor $\sim2\sigma$ local excesses near 1.8 and 2.6 TeV that stay within the expected-limit band. No theoretical uncertainties are assigned to the QBH benchmarks, following prior convention.

### [arXiv:2608.26376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26376) — Efficient binned profile likelihood minimization for precision measurements with RABBIT (David Walter et al.)

Walter and Long present RABBIT, a TensorFlow-2 framework for binned profile-likelihood fits that uses automatic differentiation for exact gradients and Hessian-vector products, feeding a Krylov-subspace trust-region minimizer that solves the trust-region subproblem from HVPs rather than full Hessians. JIT compilation gives near-C++ speed with automatic CPU/GPU dispatch. The statistical model supports Poisson or $\chi^2$ observation terms, log-normal or additive systematics, and Barlow–Beeston (full or lite) statistical uncertainties profiled analytically where possible; when all systematics are symmetrized and additive with a $\chi^2$ model, the likelihood is purely quadratic and the MLE follows deterministically in one step. Physical observables (including unfolded cross sections) are obtained via differentiable "mappings" and "masked channels" with full post-fit uncertainty propagation.

Why it matters: this is the tooling-wave companion the digest flags — precision measurements already hit 3000+ bins and 4000+ nuisance parameters (CMS W-mass), and HL-LHC will push further. Benchmarks on synthetic models show decisive scaling advantages: at 1000 bins / 1024 systematics RABBIT finishes under 10 s where Combine and pyhf exceed the 10-minute limit, and at 100,000 bins only RABBIT succeeds. It was validated against Combine (NLL values and scans agree exactly up to constants) and already used in the CMS W-mass result. Honest caveats: GPU runs are memory-limited (largest fit had 2048 systematics on a V100); the tool complements rather than replaces established frameworks, and for simple models Combine remains fastest.

## Citation radar

This week's radar leans toward *review-and-benchmark* literature: two are pedagogical or synthesis pieces (a functional-QCD encyclopedia chapter, a cross-paradigm naturalness audit), and two are technical toolkits that the community will compare against (higher-point energy-correlator jet functions, a consolidated singlet-extension constraint map). The common thread is consolidation — papers that stake out reference values, unify scattered results, or hand collaborations something reusable.

### [arXiv:2606.03703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03703) — Phase structure of strong interaction matter from Functional QCD (Christian S. Fischer et al.)

This Encyclopedia of Nuclear Physics chapter is a pedagogical survey of Dyson–Schwinger and functional-renormalization-group approaches to the QCD phase diagram. It walks through the technical machinery (the 1PI effective action, quark gap equations, order parameters for chiral and confinement transitions, fluctuation observables), then collects the headline functional-QCD results: agreement with lattice at small μ_B, a chiral critical endpoint clustered at (T,μ_B)≈(90–115, 600–700) MeV across high-quality truncations, and a predicted kurtosis peak at √s_NN≈5 GeV connecting to freeze-out data. It also flags the "onset of new phases" (moat regime, inhomogeneous condensates) beyond μ_B/T≳4.5.

The ~5 citations in a few weeks likely reflect its role as a citable entry point: it argues that functional QCD is "the only framework" for direct high-density computation, provides a curated CEP-location consensus, and supplies a ready reference for anyone connecting theory to RHIC/FAIR/HIAF programs. Review pieces that fix a community consensus number (here, the CEP window) tend to accrue citations fast as the default reference.

### [arXiv:2606.02714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02714) — Projected Energy Correlators: Two-Loop Jet Functions and NNLL Resummation (Kyle Lee et al.)

The authors extend NNLL collinear resummation of projected N-point energy correlators (ENCs) from N=2,3 up to N=6, in both e⁺e⁻ annihilation and H→gg, matched to NLO. The key deliverable is the two-loop jet function for N=4,5,6, computed semi-analytically via IBP and differential equations, with explicit numerical jet constants cross-checked against Event2 and Eerad3. They add leading non-perturbative corrections governed by two universal soft matrix elements Ω̄_1q, Ω̄_1g, and study α_s sensitivity through ENC/EEC ratios whose log-log slopes track twist-two spin-(N+1) anomalous dimensions.

The paper's citation traction (~5 in weeks) is best read as a supply-side event: it provides missing perturbative ingredients (the higher-point jet constants) that others need to make higher-N correlators usable for precision phenomenology. With CMS already extracting α_s from the three-point correlator inside jets, and Higgs-factory (FCC-ee/CEPC) H→gg motivation explicit, this is positioned as an enabling toolkit for a live α_s-extraction program with complementary systematics — exactly the kind of reference-computation paper that gets cited by follow-up analyses.

### [arXiv:2606.12775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12775) — Are Primordial Black Holes a Natural Dark Matter Candidate? (Stefano Profumo)

Profumo applies three fine-tuning measures (Barbieri–Giudice, Strumia–Rattazzi, and an island half-width ε) uniformly across three non-inflationary PBH mechanisms, six inflationary PBH model classes, and seven particle-DM benchmarks, all evaluated on the observed relic contour. The provocative headline: PBH fine-tuning is set by the *analytic structure of the abundance map*, not by whether DM is a particle or a gravitational relic. Biased-domain-wall PBHs (Δ=9/2) are as natural as off-resonance WIMPs and freeze-in; single-exponential mechanisms cluster at Δ≈ln(T_form/T_eq)≈14–50; only single-field ultra-slow-roll inflation is severely tuned, via a double exponential. It also reconciles the Barbieri–Giudice vs. Iovino–Riotto dispute as answering complementary questions.

The ~5 citations likely stem from the contrarian framing — directly rebutting the common "PBH DM is fine-tuned" dismissal with a quantitative, cross-paradigm protocol. A single-author polemic offering a clean universality-class taxonomy and a memorable structural identity (Δ≈log of a scale ratio) is the kind of claim people cite to endorse, dispute, or position against.

### [arXiv:2606.12533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12533) — Constraining the real scalar singlet extension of the SM (Moritz Bosse et al.)

This is a comprehensive constraint map of the real singlet extension (xSM), combining a state-of-the-art electroweak phase transition treatment (two-loop dimensionally-reduced 3D EFT validated against lattice) with high-scale vacuum stability (three-loop gauge, two-loop Yukawa/scalar RG running) and collider probes: Higgs signal strength, trilinear κ₃, EWPO, and resonant ZZ/di-Higgs searches. Focusing on a heavy singlet, they find SFOEWPT-viable parameter space up to ~1 TeV, show that self-coupling deviations can exceed hZZ deviations by 𝒪(10), and identify a challenging "funnel region" (θ→0) where a two-step transition and vacuum stabilization coexist with vanishing tree-level Higgs-coupling shifts. They project HL-LHC and FCC reach and release a UFO implementation.

The early ~5 citations probably reflect its value as a unified reference: it overlays cosmological requirements with distinct collider handles rather than doing one in isolation, updates prior HL-LHC/100-TeV studies, and ships reusable MadGraph tooling. For a benchmark model this well-studied, a fresh, internally consistent constraint synthesis becomes the natural thing to cite.
