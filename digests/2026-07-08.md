## Themes

### SMEFT, PDFs, and QED Factorization

As we push deeper into the high-mass tails of Run 3 and look toward the High-Luminosity LHC, the traditional decoupling of BSM searches from Standard Model PDF fits is breaking down. Today’s papers signal a major push toward unified, joint fits to prevent new physics signals from being silently "profiled" away by PDF adjustments.

Marzocca, Ubiali, and Yamamoto [arXiv:2607.07791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07791) demonstrate this danger in high-mass Drell-Yan SMEFT analyses. They perform a joint fit of four-fermion Wilson coefficients and PDF nuisance parameters, revealing a severe, highly flavor-dependent degradation in sensitivity. Because first-generation quark operators are highly correlated with the high-$x$ valence-quark luminosities that dominate the high-mass spectrum, profiling the PDFs substantially broadens the SMEFT limits. Operators involving heavier quarks escape relatively unscathed, but the message is clear: if we do not fit PDFs and SMEFT simultaneously, we risk hiding new physics in the PDF uncertainties.

At the same time, the precision of our experimental measurements has surpassed the accuracy of pure QCD factorization. We now require joint QCD+QED factorization frameworks, introducing "Lepton Distribution Functions" (LDFs) to model collision-induced QED radiation. Mantry et al. [arXiv:2607.07811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07811) study this impact on SMEFT constraints in deep-inelastic scattering (DIS), showing that while cross sections receive order-one QED corrections, longitudinal spin asymmetries remain highly robust, making them the ultimate clean observables for future SoLID and Electron-Ion Collider (EIC) runs. A parallel paper by Qiu et al. [arXiv:2607.07664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07664) sets up the formal joint QCD+QED factorization theorems for single inclusive hadron and jet production, deriving the default set of LDFs needed for this next-to-leading-precision era.

### Accidental PQ and SUSY UV Completions

On Monday, Baer et al. delivered a tough verdict showing that LZ direct-detection limits have cornered natural, light higgsino dark matter [arXiv:2607.05648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05648). Today, Baer is back with a highly elegant solution to a broader suite of MSSM headaches [arXiv:2607.07921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07921). Rather than treating the MSSM’s $\mu$ problem, rapid proton decay, and the strong CP problem as isolated issues, they show that imposing anomaly-free discrete $Z_n^R$ symmetries solves them all at once. Crucially, when the $\mu$ term is forbidden by the $Z_n^R$ symmetry, the theory develops an accidental global $U(1)_{\rm PQ}$ symmetry. Breaking this symmetry via a Kim-Nilles mechanism naturally regenerates a weak-scale $\mu$ while producing a DFSZ axion that solves the strong CP problem. 

This theme of linking electroweak-scale SUSY to cosmological relics is echoed by Frolovsky et al. [arXiv:2607.07193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07193). Working in Starobinsky supergravity coupled to the MSSM, they show that the scale of cosmic inflation directly fixes the MSSM soft terms. Under this framework, the measured Higgs boson mass and relic dark matter density uniquely converge on a 3 TeV pure thermal wino. This model is highly testable: its direct-detection cross section lies just below LZ but within reach of next-generation liquid xenon, and its disappearing-track signature would be a definitive target for a 100 TeV collider. To tie this together with UV-complete flavor structures, Fileviez Perez et al. [arXiv:2607.07794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07794) propose a universal seesaw based on a new Abelian gauge symmetry. By forbidding ordinary Yukawa couplings (except for the top quark), they explain fermion mass hierarchies through mixing with a vector-like sector, whilst using a Nelson-Barr block structure to keep the tree-level strong CP phase at zero.

### Axions and ALPs in the Crosshairs

The search for axion-like particles (ALPs) continues to accelerate, with experimentalists and phenomenologists collaborating to carve out the remaining parameter space.

On the experimental front, the Belle II Collaboration [arXiv:2607.07800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07800) has published a leading constraint on ALPs decaying to two photons ($e^+e^- \to \gamma a, a \to \gamma\gamma$) using a massive $408\text{ fb}^{-1}$ dataset. They search for a narrow di-photon resonance, finding no excess and setting the most restrictive limits to date over the $0.17 < m_a < 5.00\text{ GeV}$ range—improving on previous limits by up to a factor of 9.

Meanwhile, Darcy Newmark’s doctoral work [arXiv:2607.07476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07476) showcases the future of liquid argon detectors at beam dumps. Utilizing the 10-ton Coherent CAPTAIN-Mills (CCM) detector, the thesis demonstrates the first event-by-event separation of sub-MeV Cherenkov radiation from scintillation light in LAr. By exploiting this hybrid Cherenkov-scintillation signature, the work achieves unprecedented background rejection, carving out new exclusions for MeV-scale ALPs. This hybrid technique represents a massive step forward for the DUNE and dark sector communities alike. In the astrophysical regime, Cappiello et al. [arXiv:2607.07297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07297) point out that heavy ALPs produced in gamma-ray bursts would decay and form a secondary $e^+e^-$ fireball, reprocessing gamma-rays into isotropic $X$-rays that future wide-field telescopes can detect even without pointing directly at the GRB jet.

### Symmetric Scaffolding for Neutrino Flavor

Following yesterday's focus on neutrino degeneracies and PMNS non-unitarity [arXiv:2607.06108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06108), today's papers focus on the underlying mathematical elegance of neutrino mixing. 

Chaulagain et al. [arXiv:2607.07213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07213) dissect the algebraic structure of three-flavor neutrino oscillations in constant-density matter. By applying the Cayley-Hamilton theorem to the evolution operator and using the Denton-Minakata-Parke (DMP) resummation, they construct closed-form Jacobians in the NuFIT parameter basis. This mathematical work removes the divergence that plagues standard expansion schemes near the MSW resonance, providing the analytical continuity and parameter limits required for the next generation of global fits.

For those trying to explain the mixing patterns from first principles, Hao et al. [arXiv:2607.07865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07865) present the first model-independent study of Modular Littlest Seesaw models based on the finite modular group $\Delta(96)$. By scanning the symmetry-preserving fixed points, they identify 35 highly predictive breaking patterns that yield narrow ranges for neutrino masses and CP phases, offering concrete targets for JUNO, DUNE, and T2HK. Additionally, Xing [arXiv:2607.07311](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07311) introduces a novel "flavor cuboid" parametrization for normal ordering, showing that its cubic limit naturally accommodates both neutrino mass degeneracy and tribimaximal flavor mixing.
