## The vibe today

The field is experiencing a sharp pivot toward structural consolidation. After years of exploring every corner of the MSSM and pushing the limits of LHC triggers, the collective mood has shifted toward "forensic" physics: reconciling theory with precision data, purging degeneracies, and quantifying the actual resource costs of our work. The overwhelming sentiment today is that we have transitioned from a era of "discovery through breadth" to "discovery through fidelity."

There is a palpable sense of architectural cleanup. Whether it’s applying LHC-style on-shell amplitude methods to resolve decades-old BBN tensions, or using transformer-based flavor tagging to squeeze the last percent of uncertainty out of Run 3 data, the community is hardening its infrastructure. The era of loose approximations is ending; when the theory error on $b$-baryon lifetimes or the interpretation of $0\nu\beta\beta$ half-lives can lead us astray, our only recourse is to rebuild from first principles.

## Themes

### The End of the WIMP "Blind Spot"
The net around natural SUSY is tightening into a noose. Baer et al. [arXiv:2607.05648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05648) effectively closed the final remaining door for "natural" neutralino dark matter: the blind spot. By scanning the parameter space for gravity-mediated models, they show that any parametric cancellation suppressing direct-detection signals is fundamentally incompatible with the naturalness condition $\Delta_{\text{EW}} \le 30$. This is a major turning point: it forces the community to choose between abandoning electroweak naturalness or accepting that the LSP must be unstable (or the dark matter sector entirely non-WIMP). This is complemented by the work of Baer et al. [arXiv:2607.07921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07921), which explores how discrete $Z_n^R$ symmetries simultaneously solve the $\mu$-problem and naturally yield DFSZ axions, signaling a pivot toward axionic dark matter as the primary theoretical successor to WIMPs.

### Neutrino Degeneracy and the "Fake" CP Signal
As we move toward the operational phase of DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande, the neutrino community is increasingly haunted by the threat of "fake" signals. Singh et al. [arXiv:2607.06108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06108) provide a sobering analysis of how PMNS non-unitarity can generate CP asymmetries that mimic genuine SM signals, potentially degrading discovery potential by over 60% in certain energy windows. The message is unanimous: single-detector analyses are no longer sufficient. Only by performing joint fits that leverage different matter-effect profiles—as explored in the analytic resummation framework by Chaulagain et al. [arXiv:2607.07213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07213)—can we hope to distinguish between CP violation and BSM-induced mimics.

### Machine Learning at the Reconstruction Edge
We are witnessing the rapid operationalization of ML, moving from "performance papers" to "calibration results." ATLAS's deployment of the GN2 transformer-based tagger in a simultaneous measurement of $b$- and $c$-tagging efficiencies [arXiv:2607.06199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06199) is a masterclass in how to move ML into the high-precision pipeline. This is not just a software update; it is a fundamental change in how we estimate systematic uncertainties in heavy flavor tagging. The infrastructure is maturing, as evidenced by Varni et al. [arXiv:2607.04723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04723), who are pushing hybrid Hough-CNN tracking pipelines to manage the extreme pileup environment of the HL-LHC.

### Heavy Flavor Precision
The theory community is finally resolving the "lifetime tension" in the heavy-flavor sector. Lenz et al. [arXiv:2607.06538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06538) presented NNLO-QCD corrections to the $b$-quark decay that bring predicted $b$-baryon lifetime ratios into alignment with experiment, effectively ending a long-standing headache. This high-precision approach is echoed in the work of Tait [arXiv:2607.05514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05514), who used LHC-style on-shell amplitude methods to resolve the $d(p,\gamma)^3\text{He}$ discrepancy in BBN. It is a striking example of cross-pollination: high-energy formal machinery is the key to solving the most stubborn problems in low-energy nuclear physics.
