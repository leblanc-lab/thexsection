## Spin polarization, Beijing and CERN

Two experiments made the day, both by measuring polarization in channels that were hard to reach. ATLAS reports 4.0σ evidence for a longitudinally polarized $Z$ in electroweak $W^\pm Zjj$ production ([arXiv:2607.20246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20246)) — the direct sequel to last week's doubly-longitudinal $ZZ$ observation, pushing polarization fractions into the VBS-adjacent sector. At the other end of the mass scale, BESIII debuts a genuinely new trick: using secondary scattering off spectrometer material to measure final-state nucleon polarization, and turning it into the first determination of the relative phase between the proton's psionic form factors ([arXiv:2607.19948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19948)). Around these, a UHE-neutrino tension paper hardens the ANITA/KM3NeT/IceCube puzzle, and the neutrino-systematics thread the memory has tracked resurfaces on the energy-reconstruction side.

## Themes

### Polarization and EM measurements at colliders
The ATLAS $WZjj$ result [arXiv:2607.20246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20246) ($f_{X0}=0.32\pm0.09$, SM-consistent) extends the electroweak-symmetry-breaking polarization program that produced the $ZZ$ milestone on July 15 — the doubly-longitudinal fraction is still only bounded ($<0.12$). CMS delivers the most precise LHC measurement of $\gamma\gamma\to\tau\tau$ in ultraperipheral PbPb and tightens $a_\tau$ to $[-0.039, 0.032]$ ([arXiv:2607.20148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20148)). LHCb adds a clean null $CP$ search in $D^+\to\phi\pi^+$ at the $10^{-4}$ level, plus a first hadron-collider look at the $K^0/\bar K^0\pi^+$ amplitude ratio ([arXiv:2607.20276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20276)).

### BESIII's polarimetry gambit and a glueball claim
The pairing of [arXiv:2607.19927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19927) (proof of principle) and [arXiv:2607.19948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19948) (first relative-phase measurement) turns a general-purpose spectrometer into a large-acceptance polarimeter — a method with obvious reuse value across nuclear and particle facilities. Separately, BESIII argues the $X(2370)$ is a flavor-singlet state and the dominant component of the lightest $0^{-+}$ glueball ([arXiv:2607.20366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20366)), from the suppression of $K^*(892)\bar K$. That continues the July heavy-hadron/glueball drumbeat (the $N_f=1$ glueball–$s\bar s$ mixing result of July 21).

### The UHE neutrino anomaly gets a number
Chattopadhyay et al. [arXiv:2607.19487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19487) do the joint ANITA-IV/KM3NeT/IceCube fit and quantify what has been a hand-wave: a diffuse power-law flux is in ~7.5σ tension, and even rare-transient populations stay at 5.9σ, given IceCube's null. This sharpens the KM3-230213A thread the memory has followed (the July 14 grand-unification read of that event). Adjacent, Garcia-Garcia et al. [arXiv:2607.19461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19461) show light neutrinos must be kept in HNL $t$-channel amplitudes — their destructive interference kills LNV signatures, redirecting searches to lepton-flavor-violating $e\mu jj$.

### Neutrino energy reconstruction, again
Lachner et al. [arXiv:2607.20419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20419) show calorimetric energy reconstruction carries an interaction-model-dependent bias from detector non-linearities (Birks quenching, recombination) — ~7–9 MeV in scintillator, 11–18 MeV in argon — separate from the missing-energy bias, and reducible with hybrid tracking+calorimetry. This is the same systematics anxiety as the Dolan review and T2K generator-spread work, now on the material-response side. JUNO's ultrasonic source positioning ([arXiv:2607.20354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20354)) and the ITk gamma-irradiation study ([arXiv:2607.19936](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19936)) round out the detector-hardware end.

## Deep dives

# The Cross Section

### [arXiv:2607.20246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20246) — Evidence for longitudinally polarised $Z$ bosons in electroweak $W^{\pm}Zjj$ production (ATLAS Collaboration)

**Abstract only.** ATLAS reports 4.0σ evidence for a longitudinally polarised $Z$ in electroweak $W^\pm Zjj$ production, combining 140 fb$^{-1}$ at 13 TeV and 164 fb$^{-1}$ at 13.6 TeV in the fully leptonic ($e/\mu$) channel. The integrated fiducial fraction is $f_{X0}=0.32\pm0.09$, consistent with the NLO-EW Standard Model prediction. Upper limits at 95% CL of 0.19 and 0.12 are set on longitudinally polarised $W$ and on doubly-longitudinal $WZ$ events, respectively. The 13.6 TeV fiducial cross-section is measured as $0.374\pm0.077\,(\text{stat})\pm0.053\,(\text{syst})\pm0.009\,(\text{lumi})$ fb per lepton flavour.

**Why it matters:** This is the direct sequel to last week's doubly-longitudinal $ZZ$ observation, extending the electroweak-symmetry-breaking polarization program into the VBS-adjacent $WZjj$ sector — the channel most sensitive to longitudinal-boson scattering that probes unitarization by the Higgs. That the single-longitudinal $Z$ fraction sits squarely on the SM prediction, while the doubly-longitudinal component remains only bounded ($<0.12$), reflects the statistical reach of current datasets rather than any tension. The caveat is intrinsic: a 4.0σ result on $f_{X0}$ with $\pm0.09$ precision and the merged two-energy dataset means the harder doubly-longitudinal target awaits Run 3 luminosity.

### [arXiv:2607.19948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19948) — First Measurement of the Relative Phase between Proton Psionic Form Factors (BESIII Collaboration et al.)

**What they did:** Using $10.09\times10^9$ $J/\psi$ events, BESIII measured the normal polarization $\mathcal{P}_y$ of protons from $e^+e^-\to J/\psi\to p\bar p$ by exploiting *secondary* $pp$ elastic scattering off hydrogen in the beam-pipe mineral-oil layer and MDC inner wall — turning the spectrometer itself into a polarimeter. Requiring a third track (the knocked-out target proton), vetoing quasi-free nuclear collisions via $|p_\text{target}|<50$ MeV/$c$, they isolate 2556 elastic events (15.2% efficiency) and extract, via the SAID analyzing powers, $\sin\Delta\Phi=-0.20\pm0.34_\text{stat}\pm0.11_\text{syst}$ — the first determination of the relative phase between the proton's psionic (complex $G_E^\psi$, $G_M^\psi$) form factors.

**Why it matters:** The relative TL-region phase had been unreachable for nucleons, which lack self-analyzing decays; hyperons ($\Lambda$, $\Sigma$, $\Xi$) had been mapped but the proton and neutron remained blank. Placing the proton near $\Sigma^{+/0}$ and below $\Lambda$, $\Xi^{-/0}$ gives a first SU(3)-symmetry test point. The method — validated by a pseudo-experiment recovering an injected $\sin\Delta\Phi=-0.84$ as $-0.82\pm0.02$ — is reusable at any large-acceptance spectrometer, with $\Sigma^-$ and (with better neutron detection) the neutron as obvious next targets. **Caveats:** the result is statistics-limited and consistent with zero; two $\Delta\Phi$ solutions remain degenerate, resolvable only with a polarized beam ($\mathcal{P}_x$) or antiproton polarization, e.g. at STCF.

### [arXiv:2607.19487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19487) — Four, One, and None: Quantifying the UHE Neutrino Anomaly Across ANITA-IV, KM3NeT, and IceCube (Dibya S. Chattopadhyay et al.)

**What they did:** Chattopadhyay, Argüelles, and Brdar perform the first joint statistical fit of the four near-horizon ANITA-IV events, the single KM3-230213A event, and IceCube's null result, using semi-analytic energy-, direction-, and time-dependent effective areas for all three detectors with realistic exposures (26.6 d for ANITA-IV, 287 d for ARCA-21, 5400 d for IceCube). For a common diffuse power-law flux, no normalization/spectral-index choice reproduces the observed $(4,0,1)$ counts: the best fit predicts ~5 events at IceCube and $\mathcal{O}(10^{-3})$/$\mathcal{O}(10^{-1})$ at ANITA-IV/KM3NeT, a **7.5σ tension** (7.9σ for fixed $E^{-2}$). Modeling rare all-sky transients, with the probability of four favorable ANITA-IV overlaps fixed to $\sim10^{-4}$ and scanning $10^5$ MC realizations, the least-anomalous benchmark (185 sources, 10-day duration) still leaves **5.9σ**.

**Why it matters:** This converts the qualitative ANITA/KM3NeT-vs-IceCube "puzzle" into a hard number, sharpening the KM3-230213A thread. Crucially, the tension is driven by ANITA-IV, not KM3NeT: the ANITA-IV–IceCube-only fit gives 5.8–7.8σ, while KM3-230213A alone is a mild 1.8–2.5σ. The authors conclude neither diffuse flux nor rare identical transients works within the SM, pointing to structured source populations, detector/classification effects, or new physics. **Caveats:** the effective areas are semi-analytic, reproducing collaboration values only within a factor ~2 near the horizon; the trigger-efficiency parametrization and spherical detector approximation (especially for the anisotropic ARCA-21) carry acknowledged modeling uncertainty.
