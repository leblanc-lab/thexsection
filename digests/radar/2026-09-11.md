This week's radar is a single-topic feeding frenzy. Every paper below was posted within days of the LUX-ZEPLIN Collaboration's late-August preprint reporting one nuclear-recoil-like event at $E_R = 248\pm23\pm23$ keV in a 2.84 tonne-year exposure (3.4σ local, 2.6σ global). The community has converged with remarkable speed on a single reading — endothermic/inelastic dark matter, whose threshold kinematics naturally selects a high, localized recoil energy that conventional elastic WIMP spectra cannot reproduce — and is now racing to claim the most predictive particle realization. The result is a tight cluster of cross-citing papers (they reference each other heavily), which explains the near-identical citation counts. This is what a fresh anomaly looks like when it hits a theory community primed with a ready-made mechanism.

### [arXiv:2609.02608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02608) — Dark Matter at the Kinematic Edge: Interpreting the 248 keV LZ Nuclear-Recoil Candidate (Mattia Di Mauro)

Di Mauro provides the most thorough single-author phenomenological recast of the LZ event. Using the published efficiency curves and operator significances, the paper argues that elastic spin-independent scattering fails quantitatively — normalizing to one event near 248 keV would demand ~$1.4\times10^3$ accompanying low-energy recoils — while endothermic inelastic scattering shifts the spectrum into the observed window. Two concrete realizations are worked out: a thermal pseudo-Dirac fermion with off-diagonal vector coupling ($m_\chi\simeq1$ TeV, $\delta\simeq297$ keV, $\sigma_N\simeq6.5\times10^{-43}$ cm²) that ties the direct-detection rate to relic-abundance coannihilation, and a more predictive thermal Higgsino ($1.1$ TeV, $\delta\simeq377$ keV) testable via a gamma-ray line.

At ~41 citations in roughly a month, this reads as the community's reference recast: it carefully catalogs which NREFT operators LZ actually tested, stresses that the event selects "spectral hardness" rather than a unique Lorentz structure, and quantifies halo-tail sensitivity. Its detailed efficiency digitization and explicit relic–direct-detection relation make it a natural citation anchor for the follow-up papers below.

### [arXiv:2609.01475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01475) — Inelastic Dark Matter Signature at High Recoil Energy in LUX-ZEPLIN and CRESST (Liangliang Su et al.)

Su, Yang, and Yang deliver a compact Letter interpreting the event as endothermic inelastic DM, adding a distinctive ingredient: terrestrial propagation. They compute both direct scattering of surviving ground-state halo DM and the excited-state component produced by upscattering inside the Earth, concluding that for $m_\chi\gtrsim500$ GeV and $\delta=\mathcal{O}(300)$ keV the Earth-produced component is kinematically forbidden — leaving direct endothermic scattering on xenon as the signal and eliminating daily modulation. An illustrative two-bin likelihood gives a best-fit $(m_\chi,\delta)\simeq(1.105\,\mathrm{TeV},350\,\mathrm{keV})$ for $\bar\sigma_n=10^{-37}$ cm².

The headline forward-looking claim — testability via the planned CRESST upgrade, whose heavier tungsten target reaches the same transition at lower recoil energy with ~1.5 tonne·day exposure — gives experimentalists a concrete target and likely drives some of its ~41 citations. As one of the earliest posted (Sept 1) interpretations, it also functions as a template others cite when framing the inelastic reading and the CRESST complementarity.

### [arXiv:2609.02807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02807) — A Peccei-Quinn Origin for Inelastic Electroweak Dark Matter after LUX-ZEPLIN (Luca Visinelli)

Visinelli embeds the inelastic electroweak-doublet interpretation in a Peccei–Quinn completion: spontaneous PQ breaking leaves a residual parity that stabilizes the lighter neutral state and generates the Majorana splitting via a heavy PQ-charged singlet, while a KSVZ colored pair supplies the QCD anomaly. Integrating out the singlet yields the dimension-five operator that splits a vectorlike doublet, giving a Higgsino-like off-diagonal $Z$ coupling. The construction realizes a mixed axion–WIMP cosmology: for benchmark doublet masses 287–509 GeV the EW component supplies only 7–21% of the relic density, with the QCD axion providing the rest through misalignment ($v_S\simeq4.4\times10^{11}$ GeV).

The paper is notable for its careful complementarity discussion — solar-capture/IceCube limits, excited-state radiative decay (~20 km decay length, leaving LZ a clean single recoil but enabling luminous scintillator signatures), and collider disappearing-track bounds. Its ~39 citations likely reflect both the appeal of unifying the two leading DM paradigms and its extensive, honest cross-referencing of the competing LZ interpretations, which positions it centrally in the emerging literature web.

### [arXiv:2609.02868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02868) — Inelastic Dark Photon Dark Matter for the LUX-ZEPLIN High-Recoil Event and the Galactic Halo Gamma-Ray Excess (Kimiko Yamashita)

Yamashita attempts a two-anomaly fit: a $U(1)_X$ dark-photon DM at $m_X=420$ GeV that both explains Totani's halo-like Fermi-LAT gamma-ray excess (via a $p$-wave-suppressed, Sommerfeld-enhanced $W^+W^-$ channel) and the LZ event (via inelastic $X+N\to V+N$ with $\delta\simeq316$ keV, saturating the kinematic ceiling for xenon). A second light scalar $\phi'$ mediates the inelastic transition while a dark parity forbids the elastic amplitude that would otherwise violate SI limits.

The ambition — one dark sector, two independent anomalies, same 420 GeV mass — is the clear draw, and at ~35 citations it is well-cited despite requiring a fairly elaborate scalar sector (the paper candidly defers "a full symmetry construction" and a profile-likelihood significance to future work). It leans on the same detector-resolution smearing ($\sigma_E=32.5$ keV) argument as the others. The transparent AI-assistance disclosure is a sign of the times. Its citation count is somewhat inflated by membership in the tightly cross-citing LZ cluster.

### [arXiv:2609.01892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01892) — A PQ-Symmetric High-Scale SUSY Interpretation of the LZ High-Energy Recoil (Wen Yin)

Yin reads the event as near-threshold upscattering of 1.08-TeV thermal Higgsino dark matter, with a neutral-Higgsino splitting of 0.28–0.35 MeV fixing electroweak gauginos at $\mathcal{O}(10^7)$ GeV via the standard integrating-out relation $\delta_0\simeq m_Z^2(s_W^2/M_1+c_W^2/M_2)$. The paper then builds out the full high-scale SUSY picture: gravity-mediated sfermions at $\sim10^7$ GeV, a Kim–Nilles PQ mechanism setting $\mu=1.08$ TeV plus a QCD axion, HSSUSY-checked Higgs mass at $\tan\beta\simeq1.9$–2.2, gauge unification with suppressed proton decay, and avoidance of the gravitino/moduli problems.

The distinctive prediction — a "super-invisible" axion with accidentally cancelled photon coupling ($E/N=2$ from the Higgsino contribution) — plus potential proton-decay reach make this a smoking-gun-oriented scenario. At ~32 citations it is the most model-building-heavy entry, cited as the SUSY-embedded counterpart to the more agnostic Higgsino recasts. The framing throughout is appropriately cautious, treating the single non-blind event as a "working hypothesis."
