### [arXiv:2609.06153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06153) — Exothermic dark matter and the 248 keV nuclear recoil in LUX-ZEPLIN (Howard Baer et al.)

Baer and Barger propose the downscattering alternative to the endothermic Higgsino reading. In their model, the relic is the heavier state of a pseudo-Dirac pair split by |δ| ≃ 350 keV. Because there is no threshold speed, the predicted count shifts by only 0.3% as v_esc runs from 500 to 600 km/s. The endothermic count, by contrast, swings from zero to 143× its central value.

Several constraints follow from the setup:
- **Mediator.** Weak-strength coupling overproduces the event by ~10⁶, which forces a dark-photon mediator.
- **Splitting.** Longevity of the excited state requires |δ| < 2mₑ.
- **Excited fraction.** A coupled-channel calculation (the Born approximation violates unitarity here) gives an excited fraction of 2×10⁻³ to 4×10⁻², which implies ε ~ 10⁻⁶ at a 1 GeV mediator.
- **Ground state.** Upscattering of the ground state excludes |δ| ≲ 300 keV, so the endothermic readings appear as the small-splitting limit of the same model.

The headline test is argon: it predicts ~4–6 events per tonne-year near 250 keV, where the endothermic reading is forbidden at any exposure. It also predicts percent-level rather than near-total annual modulation.

It is one of three exothermic treatments the authors identify, alongside many endothermic ones. It also supplies the quantitative halo-sensitivity argument against endpoint tuning and a sharp discriminator that DEAP-3600 and DarkSide-50 could apply to existing data.
