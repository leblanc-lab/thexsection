## $V_{ud}$ meets its contact terms

The most consequential posting is a pair from Novario et al. [arXiv:2611.18830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18830), [arXiv:2611.18843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18843). They give the first EFT evaluation of $\bar\delta_{\rm NS}$ across 12 superallowed transitions, using coupled-cluster calculations benchmarked against QMC.

- **Scale uncertainty:** now negligible.
- **Three-body terms:** some $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2)$ nuclear matrix elements matter, because they grow coherently as $Z^2$.
- **The floor:** short-distance contact terms in the magnetic Gamow–Teller pieces. One combination fits from data. The other is strongly correlated with $V_{ud}$ itself, which leaves $\delta V_{ud}\simeq6\times10^{-4}$.

The abstracts do not say where this leaves first-row unitarity, so the reader has to open the paper for the verdict. Elsewhere, nucleation theory turns out more robust than this week's GW-forecast worries suggested, and the mass-ordering decomposition gets a general formalism.

## Themes

### Nucleation rates, checked from two sides
Zhou et al. [arXiv:2611.18794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18794) compute the thermal nucleation rate nonperturbatively on the lattice, using multicanonical HMC plus real-time Langevin, and take the continuum limit. The result differs from one loop by only ~8% in a 3D real scalar theory. Gil et al. [arXiv:2611.20938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20938) add higher-dimensional operators to the bounce consistently, prove field-redefinition invariance, and find modest corrections for moderately strong transitions. This week's thread was that GW forecasts are hostage to transition dynamics. Today's evidence suggests the rate is not the weak link; the ambiguity sits further downstream, in wall hydrodynamics (Barni, 11-16).

### Mass ordering, as bookkeeping
Cerrone et al. [arXiv:2611.21252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21252) show that each experiment's wrong-ordering $\Delta m^2_{31}$ is a precision-weighted average over its bins. The weights depend on detector resolution, so each experiment reduces to one intercept, and how much two experiments help each other depends on how far apart their intercepts sit. This is the general machinery behind Machado's 11-11 finding that the 1.9σ hint is a balance of opposing pulls. Cao et al. [arXiv:2611.19416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19416) give a cautionary companion: FRB+CMB $\sum m_\nu$ limits loosen to 0.3–0.4 eV once $w_0w_a$ is allowed.

### Charm hadrons, measured and computed
- **Strong phases:** BESIII, presented by Zeng [arXiv:2611.18944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18944), updates strong-phase inputs for $\gamma$ and charm mixing. The talk also reports the first observation of $C$-even $D\bar D$ pairs above $\psi(3770)$.
- **$D_{s1}(2460)$ structure:** Belle et al. [arXiv:2611.20531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20531) observe $D_{s1}(2460)^+\to D_s^{*+}\gamma$ at >10σ, with a ratio to $D_s^+\gamma$ of 0.74±0.08 (stat. and syst. combined).
- **Lifetimes:** Chia-Wei Liu [arXiv:2611.20747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20747) computes dimension-seven spectator terms at NLO. They make the published $D^+$ width positive, giving $\tau(D^+)=1.41^{+\infty}_{-0.76}$ ps. That upper error is not a typo.

### Proofs and fits, audited
- **Lattice chiral fermions:** Lu et al. [arXiv:2611.20084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20084) show the overlap Weyl fermion behind Lüscher's construction is not reflection positive in 2D or 4D at finite spacing. That bears directly on the Witten/Hsu/Liu chiral-lattice thread from 11-05 and 11-13.
- **GUT no-go:** Chen et al. [arXiv:2611.20386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20386) formalize a SUSY-GUT $R$-symmetry no-go in Lean 4, and find and close a gap in the published proof along the way.
- **Likelihood fits:** Zhu et al. [arXiv:2611.19355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19355) are covered under Worth a look.

## Worth a look
- **[arXiv:2611.18843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18843)** (Novario et al.): The long version of the $V_{ud}$ result, with an explicit roadmap for which nuclear input would break the contact-term degeneracy.
- **[arXiv:2611.21252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21252)** (Cerrone et al.): Extends the mass-ordering sum rule to atmospheric neutrinos in matter. For DeepCore, the intercept separation from JUNO is nearly independent of $\delta_{CP}$ and $\theta_{23}$.
- **[arXiv:2611.18833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18833)** (Foster et al.): A gravitational "Auger effect" drains the first superradiant level of the QCD axion after saturation. It can suppress continuous-GW signals, and the authors say existing spin-down limits need revising.
- **[arXiv:2611.20356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20356)** (Ahmed et al.): Closed-form triple-exchange bispectrum. The Planck template overestimates the amplitude by up to 1.8× and biases the mass low.
- **[arXiv:2611.19355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19355)** (Zhu et al.): Adaptive integrators can make the normalization jump discontinuously, producing artificially low NLLs. The motivating case is a CMS fully-charmed tetraquark search, and tighter tolerances alone don't fix sharply localized terms.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2611.18843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18843) — Ab initio calculations of $\bar\delta_{\rm NS}$ and extraction of $V_{ud}$ using effective field theory (Samuel Novario et al.)

The result is a nuclear-structure floor of $\delta V_{ud}\simeq6\times10^{-4}$, about twice the $3\times10^{-4}$ quoted for the standard $V_{ud}=0.97373(31)$. It comes from the second, unfitted combination of the contact LECs $g^{NN}_{V1,2}$ that renormalize the magnetic GT NMEs, which is strongly correlated with $V_{ud}$.

The main new input is coupled-cluster NMEs for all 12 transitions from $^{10}$C to $^{54}$Co, computed with the 1.8/2.0 (EM) and NNLO$_{\rm sat}$ interactions. The CC uncertainty combines CCSDT-1 triples corrections with $N_{\max}$ 8→10 convergence. Several other ingredients feed the analysis:
- **Three-body Fermi NMEs:** The $Z^2$-enhanced $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2)$ terms come from a density approximation. It reaches $|M^{+\rm 3b}_{F,p}|\sim200$ for $^{54}$Co and matches QMC ($-18.6$ vs. density estimates $-17.7$ to $-20.3$ for $^{14}$O).
- **Single-nucleon input:** $g_V(m_n)=1.01143(12)$, with the γW-box choice worth $7\times10^{-5}$ in $V_{ud}$.
- **Corrections to earlier work:** The authors restore a missing $\mathbf L^{\rm CM}$ term and fix a sign error in the spin-orbit potential. Both changes are numerically non-negligible because of large cancellations.

Caveats:
- For $^{10}$C, the CC $M^{\rm mag}_{\rm GT,p}$ is 30% below QMC. The authors suspect clustering or Hamiltonian differences, and say this degrades the $V_{ud}$ fit quality.
- $\bar\delta_C$ is still taken from the traditional evaluation rather than computed ab initio.
- The extracted text ends before the fit section, so neither the central $V_{ud}$ nor the first-row unitarity verdict could be checked here.

### [arXiv:2611.21252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21252) — Mass ordering synergies and the role of the detector response (Vanessa Cerrone et al.)

The wrong-ordering solution of any experiment is an inverse-variance weighted average of per-bin preferred $\Delta m^2_{31,\rm IO}$. Every experiment then reduces to one intercept $b=\Delta m^2_{31,\rm IO}+\Delta m^2_{31,\rm NO}$, and pairwise synergy is $(b_A-b_B)^2/(\sigma_A^2+\sigma_B^2)$. Intercepts below are in units of $10^{-6}$ eV².

| Experiment | Intercept $b$ | Main dependence |
|---|---|---|
| JUNO | 25.7 at $a_{\rm res}=2.61\%$ (data: 27) | Moves ~10 between 2% and 4% resolution |
| Accelerators | $103.8-11.6(\cos\delta_{CP}+\cos\delta^{\rm IO}_{CP})$ | $\delta_{CP}$ |
| DeepCore ($\nu_\mu$ disappearance, reference) | 119.7 | $\theta_{23}$ |
| ORCA6 | ~11 below DeepCore | Mostly from the shower sample |

Above the MSW resonance the $\nu_\mu$ frequency is $\delta_{CP}$-independent, so the atmospheric intercept barely moves with $\delta_{CP}$. As a result, JUNO+DeepCore (all channels) synergy is nearly fixed. JUNO+NOvA synergy, by contrast, varies by about 2× with the true $\delta_{CP}$.

Caveats:
- The analysis uses statistics only, with solar parameters and $\theta_{13}$ fixed.
- DeepCore is modeled on the 21,402-event golden sample. The 9.3-yr CNN sample lies above the computed band.
- The NOvA–DeepCore estimates are lower bounds.
- Published uncertainties cannot yet separate the accelerator and DeepCore bands.

### [arXiv:2611.18833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18833) — Black Hole Superradiance and the Gravitational Auger Effect (Joshua W. Foster et al.)

Newtonian self-gravity mediates $211+211\to322+$BH transitions, with amplitude coefficient $\mathcal I_G\simeq-0.0914$. That is ~50× the quartic coefficient $\mathcal I_\lambda\simeq+0.00184$. The two have opposite signs because black-hole recoil cancels the uniform field and leaves only the tidal part.

Self-gravity dominates for $f>f_\times\simeq6.1\times10^{16}$ GeV $(\alpha/0.1)$. That puts the QCD axion permanently in the moderate self-coupling regime: cancellation would need $\alpha\simeq0.6$, beyond where 211 is superradiant.

The authors evolve a 33-level model for $10\,M_\odot$, $\chi_0=0.9$, $\alpha_0=0.1$:
- **211 depletion:** 211 drains to $10^{-3}$ of its peak within $10^5$ yr, versus $5\times10^8$ yr without scattering. The two-level estimates are $T_{\rm SG}\sim0.3$ yr against $T_{\rm GW}\sim4\times10^5$ yr at $\alpha=0.1$.
- **Continuous-GW lines at aLIGO design sensitivity:** 211 drops from up to $10^4$ lines to <0.05. 322 drops from ~$10^3$ to <0.1.
- **Spin-down:** $n44$ overtones cap 322 at ≲$10^{-4}$ of the BH mass. 322 spin-down therefore starts earlier but proceeds slower.

The spin-down nuance matters. The authors say the net effect on spin constraints is unclear and needs population studies. Only bounds using levels beyond 211 need revision; supermassive-BH 211-only limits are largely unaffected.

Caveats: the treatment is hydrogenic $\alpha\ll1$ with Newtonian self-gravity. It neglects self-energy level shifts and GW cross-annihilation, and the rates are unreliable for $\alpha_0>0.2$–0.3.
