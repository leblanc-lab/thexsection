## The xenon event meets its adjudicators

Four weeks on, the LZ recoil still owns roughly a sixth of the listings, but the register has shifted again — from fits, to bounds, to concrete discriminators. Two papers today try to end arguments rather than start them: Ghosh et al. [arXiv:2609.15321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15321) show that no plausible halo modeling rescues the thermal higgsino from solar capture, and Baer et al. [arXiv:2609.15782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15782) argue argon settles the endothermic-vs-exothermic question outright. The day's genuine breadth, though, sits away from xenon — a clean BESIII lepton-universality test, a precision-QCD stack running from polarized SIDIS to the proton–neutron mass difference, and quantum information reaching small-$x$ saturation.

### Precision flavor at BESIII and LHCb
BESIII delivers the first observation of $D_s^+\to f_0(980)\mu^+\nu_\mu$ [arXiv:2609.15655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15655), with a $\mu/e$ ratio of $0.92\pm0.13\pm0.08$ — lepton universality intact — and the most precise $D_s\to f_0(980)$ form factor to date. LHCb reports the first observation of $\Xi_b^0\to\Xi^0 J/\psi$ [arXiv:2609.15251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15251), notable for the first full $\Xi^0$ reconstruction at an LHC experiment. Add improved $\eta'\to3\pi$ amplitude analyses [arXiv:2609.15053](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15053) and BESIII cross-section scans; the consolidating-null flavor picture from August holds.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2609.15321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15321) — Confronting the Higgsino Interpretation of the LZ Event with Astrophysical Uncertainties and Solar Capture (Aditya Ghosh et al.)

The higgsino reading of LZ's 248 keV recoil cannot survive the solar-capture bound no matter how the halo is stretched. Ghosh, Chavez, and Kelso compute the expected LZ signal for a 1.1 TeV thermal higgsino (WimPyDD, including the factor-4 cross-section correction of Pospelov–Ramani) across the full Standard Halo Model parameter space and, separately, using $N$-body hydrodynamical halos that include the LMC's imprint on the high-speed tail. IceCube/Super-K non-observation of solar annihilation neutrinos requires the inelastic splitting $\delta\gtrsim 506$–$566$ keV, which for a 248 keV xenon recoil demands lab-frame speeds $v_{\rm min}\sim 950$–$1025$ km/s. In the SHM, the $\delta\gtrsim566$ keV bound requires $v_0\gtrsim240$ km/s independent of $v_{\rm esc}$ — well beyond the $2\sigma$ range of any measured $v_0$ (up to 256 km/s), even at optimistic $\rho_\chi=0.45$ GeV cm$^{-3}$ and with a 5%-probability upward fluctuation. In the LMC-aware simulated halo, $\delta=514$ keV is the largest splitting that yields one event in the 200–300 keV window; all curves fall below the fluctuation threshold by $\delta\sim530$ keV.

**Why it matters:** This is exactly the discriminator mode the digest tracks — an attempt to close the last astrophysical escape hatch rather than add another fit. The thermal higgsino is now doubly cornered: kinematics and solar capture together leave no viable window at $\delta\gtrsim566$ keV.

**Caveat:** Only the weaker $\delta\gtrsim506$ keV bound (tree-level $Z$ up-scatter, not the loop-induced elastic channel) leaves a sliver of SHM viability, and only when both $v_0$ and $v_{\rm esc}$ sit at their upper limits. The LMC-tail extension beyond 950 km/s is a phenomenological exponential, not a simulated result.

### [arXiv:2609.15782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15782) — Argon as the test of two interpretations of the LZ 248 keV recoil (Howard Baer et al.)

Baer and Barger reduce the endothermic-vs-exothermic debate to a single already-recorded measurement. Both interpretations require an internal splitting, but they flow energy oppositely. Read exothermically (pseudo-Dirac downscatter releasing $|\delta|$), the event predicts a nuclear-recoil line in argon at $E_{\rm Ar}=|\delta|\,m_\chi/(m_\chi+m_{\rm Ar})$ — 336 keV for $m_\chi=200$ GeV, ranging 268–525 keV over the exothermic family — at ~3–4 events/tonne-yr, roughly ten times the equivalent xenon rate. Argon wins because its Helm form factor at 336 keV sits in the first diffraction lobe ($F^2=0.056$) while xenon at 248 keV sits near its second-lobe minimum ($F^2=1.6\times10^{-4}$); the factorized rate ratio returns 9.5 at 200 GeV. Read endothermically (ground-state upscatter by the fast halo tail), argon predicts *zero* events: the argon threshold speed exceeds the halo maximum (1224 vs 796 km/s at 1.1 TeV). The ratio depends only on two-body kinematics, the two form factors, and the coupling's charge structure — not the mediator or excited fraction.

**Why it matters:** DEAP-3600 already holds the data. The published 231-day exposure implies ~7 events (full target) or 1.7 (fiducial) at the benchmark rate; the 813-day first run holds ~25 and 6. A reanalysis for single-scatter nuclear recoils between 200–600 keV (near 85 keV$_{\rm ee}$) either finds the line and fixes $m_\chi$ from the two line positions, or kills the exothermic reading — no new detector required.

**Caveat:** The fiducial mass at ~500 photoelectrons is unpublished; expected counts within the WIMP-search fiducial volume are only a few, and background control (shadowed alphas, $^{39}$Ar, radiogenic neutrons) rests on DEAP's own estimates.

### [arXiv:2609.15655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15655) — First Observation of $D_s^+\to f_0(980)\mu^+\nu_\mu$ (BESIII Collaboration et al.)

BESIII observes the semi-muonic decay $D_s^+\to f_0(980)\mu^+\nu_\mu$ (with $f_0\to\pi^+\pi^-$) for the first time, using 7.33 fb$^{-1}$ at $E_{\rm CM}=4.128$–4.226 GeV and a double-tag technique (839k single tags across 15 hadronic modes). From $196\pm22$ signal events they measure $\mathcal{B}=(1.59\pm0.18_{\rm stat}\pm0.11_{\rm syst})\times10^{-3}$. Combined with the earlier BESIII electronic-channel result, the LFU ratio $\mathcal{B}(\mu)/\mathcal{B}(e)=0.92\pm0.13_{\rm stat}\pm0.08_{\rm syst}$ agrees with the SM range 0.94–1.11 — no LFU violation at current precision, and the first such test in a scalar-meson $c\to s$ transition. A dynamical fit (simple-pole form factor, relativistic Flatté line shape) yields $f_+^{f_0}(0)|V_{cs}|=0.490\pm0.059\pm0.025$; averaging with the electronic channel gives $0.500\pm0.016\pm0.020$, and with CKMfitter $|V_{cs}|$ the most precise $D_s\to f_0$ form factor to date, $f_+^{f_0}(0)=0.514\pm0.017\pm0.021$.

**Why it matters:** Beyond extending LFU tests into scalar final states, the branching fraction implies a mixing angle $\phi=(28.0\pm26.6)^\circ$, favoring an $s\bar s$-dominant $f_0(980)$ in the $q\bar q$ picture. The precise form factor discriminates sharply among models: consistent with CLFD/DR/QCDSR but in up-to-$5\sigma$ tension with LCSR, LFQM, and CCQM predictions, all of which now incorporate the finite $f_0$ width. This is the consolidating-null flavor picture the digest notes — LFU intact, precision turned onto hadronic structure.

**Caveat:** The 13% statistical LFU uncertainty leaves ample room; the extracted $\phi$ carries a large error. $\mu/\pi$ misidentification is handled by retaining both track pairings and fitting the wrong-combination shape.
