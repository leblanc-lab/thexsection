## LHCb's CP-violation harvest

The day's weight sits squarely on a trio of LHCb papers dissecting $B^+\to K^+\pi^+\pi^-$ on the 2011–12 dataset ([arXiv:2608.12612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12612), [arXiv:2608.12613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12613), [arXiv:2608.12614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12614)) — an amplitude analysis that lands six CP-violation phenomena as significant for the first time and reorders the $K\pi\pi$ S-wave picture in favor of QCD factorization. This lands the day after yesterday's twin $B$-anomaly flare ($C_9$ transversity and the $B\to PP$ SU(3) fit); the flavor front stays hot, but this is measurement, not tension. Around it, a dense cluster of weak-scale dark-matter model-building, a tomographic-CP top paper extending the season-long QI thread, and two differentiable neutrino toolkits posted the same day.

## Themes

### LHCb's charmless three-body CP violation
The three companion papers share the 3 fb⁻¹ sample but split the story: [arXiv:2608.12612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12612) delivers the amplitude analysis with three independent S-wave treatments (isobar, K-matrix, quasi-model-independent) that agree; [arXiv:2608.12613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12613) resolves the long-standing $K^*_0(1430)$ S-wave puzzle by relaxing unitarity assumptions, eliminating the ad-hoc $f_X(1300)$; and [arXiv:2608.12614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12614) is the headline — discovery of CP violation in $\rho(770)^0K^+$, first evidence in $\pi\pi$ rescattering and in a spin-3 resonance, and CP violation in S–P and S–D wave interference driven by long-distance dynamics. This is the data-side counterpart to yesterday's semileptonic and non-leptonic anomalies.

This is the collider-QI program the memory has tracked from $t\bar t$ to TauPolaris, now aimed at CP violation rather than entanglement per se.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2608.12614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12614) — Observation of several sources of CP violation in $B^+\to K^+\pi^+\pi^-$ (LHCb collaboration et al.)

Working the full 3 fb⁻¹ Run 1 sample (115,102 candidates, 88.7% purity), LHCb performs an amplitude analysis of $B^+\to K^+\pi^+\pi^-$ with three independent treatments of the troublesome $K\pi$ and $\pi\pi$ S-waves (isobar/GLASS, K-matrix, and a first-of-its-kind quasi-model-independent binning of two crossing partial waves). All three agree, and six CP-violation phenomena clear observation-level significance at once. The headline first observations: quasi-two-body CPV in $B^+\to\rho(770)^0K^+$, $\mathcal{A}_{CP}=+0.280\pm0.022\pm0.029$ (minimum 5.9σ across S-wave models); and in $B^+\to f_2(1270)K^+$, rejected at 8.2–9.1σ at amplitude level. First evidence appears for CPV in the elastic $\pi\pi\!-\!\pi\pi$ rescattering S-wave below the $4\pi$ threshold ($\mathcal{A}^{\rm S}_{CP}=+0.665\pm0.032\pm0.053$), in S–P interference with $\rho(770)$ (5.4σ), in S–D interference near the $f_2(1270)$ (≥4.4σ), and — a genuine first — in a spin-3 state, $\rho_3(1690)^0$ (≥3.3σ).

Notably, no CPV is seen in $\rho^0$–$\omega$ mixing, contrary to several predictions; this dilution explains why the fitted $\rho(770)$ asymmetry exceeds the model-independent P-wave value (0.150±0.022). A large positive asymmetry immediately above $\pi\pi$ threshold resists all three models, hinting at sizeable $c\bar c$-loop or isospin-2 contributions. Systematics are comparable to statistical uncertainties throughout — good model control, but a signal that Run 1 statistics can no longer fully exploit. This is the measurement-side counterpart to the season's flavor anomalies, sharpening reference points for $\gamma$ extraction and $B\to K\pi$-type isospin sum rules.

### [arXiv:2608.12463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12463) — Probing CP-violating top-quark dipole moments with tomographic observables (Maxim Perelstein et al.)

Perelstein, Shaik and Youn recast $t\bar t$ spin tomography as a CP-violation search. Symmetry fixes which Fano coefficients a CP-odd (chromo-)electric dipole can populate: the normal polarization difference $\Delta B_n$ and the antisymmetric normal-plane correlations $A_{rn}$, $A_{kn}$, all exactly zero in the SM (three-loop suppressed). They compute the production density matrix in closed form for $e^+e^-$, $q\bar q$ and $gg$, build the statistically optimal CP-odd observable $O_{\rm opt}=f_1/f_0$ (proven to saturate the Cramér–Rao bound), and carry the analysis to Delphes detector level. Projected 95% CL reach on inverse couplings: 10–15 TeV for the electroweak electric dipoles $d_\gamma,d_Z$ at FCC-ee (365 GeV, 3 ab⁻¹), and 40 (200) TeV for the top chromo-EDM at LHC Run 2 (HL-LHC) — exceeding the conventional triple/quadruple-product observables (e.g. the CMS $O_1$ search) used today. $d_\gamma$ projects mainly onto $A_{rn}$, $d_Z$ onto $\Delta B_n$; the optimal weight combines them for ~40 TeV parton-level FCC-ee reach.

A sharp negative result accompanies this: they prove a no-go theorem that the top–antitop discord asymmetry $\Delta\mathcal{D}$ (and geometric discord, steering asymmetry) vanishes identically for the dipoles, contradicting a recent claim that advertised it as a CP probe — the reported nonzero values are CP-even absorptive artifacts. Caveats: reach at FCC-ee threshold is diluted by poor top-direction reconstruction ($\sim40^\circ$, dilution factors 0.24–0.40); systematics are assumed subdominant, and the EFT-consistent linear-order truncation neglects dimension-eight-degenerate terms.

### [arXiv:2608.12793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12793) — High-precision measurement of the space-like $\eta'$ transition form factor (BESIII Collaboration et al.)

BESIII measures the single-virtual space-like $\eta'$ TFF via single-tag two-photon fusion $e^+e^-\to e^+e^-\eta'$ on 20.3 fb⁻¹ at 3.773 GeV, reconstructing $\eta'\to\pi^+\pi^-\gamma$ and $\pi^+\pi^-\eta$. The result spans $Q^2\in[0.1,6.0]$ GeV² in 18 fine bins, reaches better than 3% precision below 1.5 GeV², and provides the first direct determination at $Q^2<0.3$ GeV² — filling the gap between BaBar (>4 GeV²) and L3 (<0.15 GeV², which inferred $Q^2$ indirectly from the $\eta'$ transverse momentum). Tight control of the untagged-photon virtuality ($Q^2_{\rm miss}\in[0,0.08]$ GeV², with a +1.0% correction to $Q^2_{\rm miss}=0$) is the key improvement; total systematics run 2.5–4.7% on the cross section, halved on the TFF.

The extracted values are the most precise to date and lie systematically above earlier data — attributed to the larger, less-controlled $Q^2_{\rm miss}$ of prior measurements. Crucially, the data agree with lattice QCD (BMW) and holographic (LMR) predictions while sitting above data-driven dispersive models. Since LQCD and holographic approaches also predict a larger $a_\mu^{\eta'}$ than the data-driven evaluation, this input feeds directly into the hadronic light-by-light contribution to muon $g-2$ — where LQCD HLbL now runs ~50% above the 2020 white-paper number — and the authors flag that existing data-driven analyses may warrant re-evaluation. The result also constrains the $\eta'$ gluonic component and $\eta'$ production models.

## Citation radar

This week's radar points to the speculative frontier of flavor model-building, where a single-paper octonionic construction is drawing early attention less for a confirmed result than for staking out sharply falsifiable predictions in the neutrino sector.

### [arXiv:2606.27836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27836) — Leptonic CP Conservation and the Quark CP Phase from Octonionic Flavor Structure (Bishnu Gupta Teli et al.)

Working within the octonionic unification programme — one Standard-Model generation realized on complexified octonions via the Clifford algebra $\mathcal{C}l(6)$, extended to three generations by $G_2$ automorphisms and ladder-operator rotors — Teli and Singh prove a localization theorem for where a CP-violating phase can live. Their central claims are asymmetric and clean: the quark (1,2) transport is the occupation flip of a single ladder mode, forcing $A_d = A_u^*$ and the exact one-parameter law $\phi_{12} = -2\chi$, with the programme's geometric rotor sitting at the maximal, quadrature-balanced point $|\phi_{12}|=\pi/2$. For leptons they prove a "reality theorem": every charged-lepton and neutrino transport amplitude is real for the entire admissible transport class, so leptonic Dirac CP is conserved ($J_\ell=0$, $\delta^\ell_{\rm CP}\in\{0,\pi\}$) unless a Higgs "bridge" mixes the identity line with the lepton-flavor plane — the sole, uncomputed loophole. A minimality assumption on the neutrino Jordan spectrum $(-\delta_\nu,0,+\delta_\nu)$ then selects inverted ordering, $m_{\beta\beta}\simeq19$ meV and $\Sigma m_\nu\simeq0.10$ eV.

Notably, the authors themselves quantify "the lean," conceding that current global fits disfavor their inverted-ordering-plus-conserved-CP package at roughly $3\sigma$ per axis, with DESI cosmology also in tension. The ~5 citations two months out most plausibly reflect internal traffic within this tightly-knit programme (many references are the authors' own companion papers), amplified by the paper's unusually explicit falsification table tied to JUNO, DUNE, Hyper-K, and next-generation $0\nu\beta\beta$. The reason for broader uptake is not yet visible from the text; the appeal is a concrete, near-term-testable prediction rather than an established measurement. (The acknowledgments also flag heavy reliance on AI tools in the derivations.)
