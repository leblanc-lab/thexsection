## JUNO leans normal

JUNO [arXiv:2609.37895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37895) is the day's firmest result. It uses 207 live days and 8,294 IBD candidates. The measurement gives $\Delta m^2_{31}$ to about 1% and the best solar parameters yet. Adding the long-baseline $\Delta m^2_{31}$ constraints favors normal ordering at ≥2.1σ for any CP phase, with a Bayes factor of 10.2. That is an indication, not a determination, and the collaboration says so.

On the flavor side, last week's standing question about the LHCb beauty-strange state already has a quantitative answer. Albaladejo et al. [arXiv:2609.38022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38022) predicted it before the measurement. The LZ recoil drew five papers again, this time on halos and decaying parents.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2609.37895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37895) — Measurement of Neutrino Oscillation Parameters at JUNO and Indication of the Neutrino Mass Ordering (The JUNO Collaboration)

JUNO used 207.2 live days, 8,294 IBD candidates, and a 52.5 km mean baseline. It measures $\Delta m^2_{31} = +2.509^{+0.027}_{-0.025}\times10^{-3}$ eV² under NO and $-2.482^{+0.025}_{-0.026}\times10^{-3}$ eV² under IO, a precision of about 1% with Feldman–Cousins intervals. It also reports $\sin^2\theta_{12} = 0.3036\pm0.0064$ (2.1%) and $\Delta m^2_{21} = (7.388\pm0.078)\times10^{-5}$ eV² (1.1%).

This is the first analysis to fold in TAO near-detector data (90,280 IBDs) alongside Daya Bay for the unoscillated spectrum. Three independent groups ran blinded analyses, and their results were consistent.

The mass-ordering statement is a reactor–accelerator tension test. NuFit long-baseline $\Delta m^2_{31}$ pulls under each hypothesis give $\Delta\chi^2_{\rm IO-NO} = 4.86$. Pseudo-experiments that scan $\delta_{\rm CP}$ turn this into $p = 0.0197$, or 2.1σ one-sided (2.3σ two-sided). An independent Bayesian MCMC gives $\kappa = 10.18$.

The caveats are structural:
- **JUNO alone cannot pick the minimum.** Its $\Delta\chi^2$ profile has several degenerate local minima in $\Delta m^2_{31}$. The Daya Bay constraint is what selects the physical one; JUNO then sets the precision.
- **The NMO evidence is not JUNO-internal.** It rests on the mismatch with long-baseline experiments.
- **Systematics are starting to matter.** Statistics still dominate, but Bi–Po backgrounds and detection efficiency are now significant contributors.
- **Energy resolution is slightly worse than simulated.** It is about 3.5% at the ⁶⁸Ge peak, a bit above the MC prediction.

A by-product is the world's largest geoneutrino sample, at $73\pm11$ TNU.

### [arXiv:2609.38022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38022) — The new LHCb beauty-strange state as the bottom partner of the $D_{s0}^*(2317)$ (Miguel Albaladejo et al.)

LHCb's narrow $\bar B_s^0\pi^0$ peak sits at $5698.9\pm1.5\pm0.6$ MeV, 74 MeV below the $B^-K^+$ threshold, with 343 signal candidates. Two unitarized coupled-channel models were tested against it:
- **Model I:** leading-order Weinberg–Tomozawa plus a bare $b\bar s$ state, fitted to $\bar B^{(*)}K$ lattice levels.
- **Model II:** NLO HMChPT, with its low-energy constants fixed by charm-sector lattice scattering lengths and carried over by heavy-quark flavor symmetry.

Isospin breaking enters through physical masses, $\pi^0$–$\eta$ mixing and $m_u\neq m_d$, and adds no free parameters.

**Predictions with no LHCb input** land 1.1σ (Model II) and 1.3–1.7σ (Model I) from the observed mass. **Freeing one parameter** (the cutoff Λ or the subtraction constant $a_b$) reproduces the zero-width reference fit, with χ²/dof = 1.22 for 84 degrees of freedom. The required shifts are about 1.1–1.3σ, and joint fits with the lattice data cost only Δχ²_LQCD ≈ 1.1–2.5.

Properties of the state in the fitted models:

| Quantity | Model I | Model II |
|---|---|---|
| $\bar BK$ compositeness | ~45–50% | ~60% |
| Isospin-violating width | 34 keV | 82.5 keV |
| $1^+$ partner mass | 5748.8 MeV | 5751.7 MeV |

The factor of two between the widths is a rough measure of model dependence, and radiative decays are not included.

The key point is the $1^+$–$0^+$ splitting of 49–52 MeV, which is essentially the $B_s^*$–$B_s$ mass difference (49.4 MeV). The observed peak therefore cannot discriminate between the two assignments and may contain both states. $\bar B_s^0\gamma$ searches are the direct test.

The authors are careful on several points:
- A good fit does not prove a molecular state.
- The lattice inputs are at unphysical quark masses, and the predictions carry no systematic uncertainties.
- The same NLO amplitudes are in tension with ALICE $D\pi$ femtoscopy in the charm sector.
- The resolution kernel was extracted from LHCb's published figure.

### [arXiv:2609.37563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37563) — The four-loop quark-to-quark splitting function in QCD (Thomas Gehrmann et al.)

This paper gives the first fully analytic, all-$x$ four-loop pure-singlet $P_{\rm ps}^{(3)}$. Combined with the non-singlet result (cited here as the same authors' [arXiv:2604.09534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09534)), it completes $P_{qq}$ at N³LO.

The method follows these steps:
1. Compute off-shell quark operator matrix elements, including mixing with gluon and gauge-variant operators.
2. Reduce integrals by IBP over finite fields and solve differential equations in an auxiliary parameter $t$ to reach high Mellin moments.
3. Fit an ansatz of harmonic sums up to weight six to those moments.
4. Invert the Mellin transform to harmonic polylogarithms up to weight six.

The result reproduces the known fixed moments through $n=22$. It also reproduces the $\zeta_3$ contribution at $n=2$ that arises from a spurious $(n-2)$ pole.

New content includes the previously unknown leading-power small-$x$ coefficients of $\ln x/x$ and $1/x$. There is also a discrepancy with the double-log resummation of Davies et al.: the $\zeta_2\ln^4 x$ coefficient lacks a $(d_F^{abcd})^2/N_c$ term there. The two agree at leading colour, matching the pattern seen in the non-singlet case, and the origin is unexplained.

The practical impact on evolution is modest. The existing moment-based approximation agrees with the exact result within its uncertainties over $10^{-5}<x<1$. The main gain is that the approximation uncertainty for this entry of the evolution matrix is gone, while the gluon entries remain approximate.

The ancillary files include a numerical fit accurate to $10^{-5}$ absolute and $10^{-8}$ relative. The exact $\gamma_{qq}^{(3)}$ may also help reconstruct $\gamma_{gg}^{(3)}$ through reciprocity.
