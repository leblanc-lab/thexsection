## The keV floor and the dead cone

Two experimental firsts carry the day, both clean measurements rather than anomalies. XENONnT reports the first solar-neutrino detection via electron scattering at the keV scale ([arXiv:2608.29450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29450)), rejecting background-only at 5σ and extracting a $pp$ flux down to 17 keV — the lowest energy threshold any neutrino experiment has reached. CMS finds evidence for the dead-cone effect in $b$-jets at 4.3σ ([arXiv:2608.29412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29412)), the direct manifestation of quark-mass suppression of collinear radiation — a nice follow-on to last week's heavy-quark energy-correlator work from Vitev. Alongside them, a genuinely improved B→Xsγ prediction and a diffuse spread of hadron-structure and quantum-computing methods.

## Themes

### Neutrino and rare-event milestones
Beyond XENON's keV threshold, Super-Kamiokande pushes $p\to\ell^+X$ (single antilepton plus invisible massless particle) lifetime limits to $1.7\times10^{33}$ yr, factors of ~2 better ([arXiv:2608.30361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30361)), and IceCube sets what are in places the strongest constraints on large extra dimensions and SM copies from 10.7 yr of upgoing $\nu_\mu$ ([arXiv:2608.29746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29746)). On the softer end, Qu et al. characterize atmospheric-NC "singles" for JUNO-like scintillators ([arXiv:2608.28095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28095)), and HAWC flags two horizontal track events as candidate Earth-skimming neutrinos through a volcano at 2.1–2.5σ ([arXiv:2608.28951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28951)) — intriguing but statistics-thin.

### B→Xsγ and precision flavor
The week's flavor weight is a companion pair from the Misiak group. Czaja et al. [arXiv:2608.29864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29864) compute the NNLO corrections with exact $m_c$ dependence — hundreds of four-loop cut diagrams with two mass scales — removing the interpolation uncertainty that sat in every prior analysis, and Misiak et al. [arXiv:2608.29863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29863) fold this into $\mathcal{B}_{s\gamma}^{\rm SM}=(3.54\pm0.14)\times10^{-4}$, matching experiment and pushing the Type-II charged-Higgs bound to $M_{H^\pm}>670$ GeV. This sits against the persistent R(D*) tension and the N³LO $|V_{ub}|$ push from last week — the inclusive-radiative side, at least, is fully consistent. Yang et al. [arXiv:2608.29666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29666) add a Bayesian N⁵LO estimate for the hadronic $W$ width.

### Quantum observables and quantum computing
The collider quantum-information thread continues: Mejia Alvarez et al. [arXiv:2608.30037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30037) build quantum-steering-ellipsoid observables for top-pair spin states, mapping dimension-6 operators to geometric deformations. On the simulation side, Gálvez-Viruet et al. [arXiv:2608.30375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30375) compute fragmentation functions from the light-front QCD Hamiltonian on a quantum simulator, and Kim et al. [arXiv:2608.31173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31173) simulate Markovian and non-Markovian open-system dynamics for heavy-ion probes via an auxiliary pseudomode — the quantum-simulation-for-QCD strand the memory has tracked all month.

### Hadron gravitational form factors
A structure cluster centered on gravitational form factors: kaon GFFs and mechanical structure in a three-flavor NJL model ([arXiv:2608.29848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29848)) and meson D-form factors in the linear sigma model ([arXiv:2608.30434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30434)), plus a lattice determination of the $m_q$ correction to $\pi^0\to\gamma\gamma$ confirming $\pi^0$-$\eta$-$\eta'$ mixing enhancement ([arXiv:2608.31024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31024)).

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2608.29450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29450) — First Measurement of Solar Neutrinos through Elastic Neutrino-Electron Scattering at the keV Scale (XENON Collaboration et al.)

XENONnT reports the first detection of low-energy solar neutrinos via elastic neutrino–electron scattering in a dark-matter TPC, using 2.46 t·y from the SR0 and SR1b science runs over a recoil-energy ROI of [1, 140] keV. An unbinned extended-likelihood fit rejects the background-only hypothesis at 5.0σ and yields a solar $pp$ flux of $(10.2\pm2.0)\times10^{10}\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}s^{-1}}$, with the 1 keV electron-recoil threshold translating to sensitivity down to 17 keV neutrino energy — the lowest neutrino detection threshold on record. Roughly 89% of the ROI signal is $pp$, ~10% $^7$Be, the rest sub-percent. Discovery power comes mainly from [1, 60] keV. The enabling advance is radon reduction: dedicated distillation cut $^{222}$Rn to ~0.9 µBq/kg, comparable to the neutrino rate, with the $^{214}$Pb/$^{222}$Rn activity ratio ($0.66\pm0.03$) fixed by a $^{222}$Rn calibration.

Why it matters: it cements liquid-xenon TPCs — built for WIMPs — as genuine keV-scale neutrino detectors, complementing the recent $^8$B CEνNS observations and pointing toward XLZD sub-percent $pp$ measurements for electroweak and solar-metallicity tests. Caveats are honest and several: the flux sits 1.9σ above Borexino; the fit relies on the FEA-stepping signal model (the more complete RRPA is only available below 32 keV); an unexplained "tritium-like" excess overlaps the highest-S/B region below 20 keV and is left free; and adding $^{14}$C at its maximal conceivable level drops significance to 4.3σ (Borexino consistency to 1.2σ). Total uncertainty is 20%, split evenly between systematic and profile-likelihood.

### [arXiv:2608.29412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29412) — Evidence for the dead-cone effect in bottom quark initiated jets in pp collisions at √s = 13 TeV (CMS Collaboration)

CMS finds evidence for the dead-cone effect — mass suppression of collinear gluon radiation within $\Delta R_{\rm dead}=m_q/E_q$ — in $b$-jets from dileptonic $t\bar t$ events, using 59.8 fb$^{-1}$ at 13 TeV. The emission density $\rho(\Delta R)$ is measured via iterative Cambridge–Aachen declustering onto the Lund jet plane, in two jet-$p_T$ bins (40–80, 80–200 GeV). Two novelties sharpen the measurement: a tag-and-probe selection of $b$-jets from $t\bar t$ that avoids flavor-tagging substructure biases (91% $b$-quark purity, agreeing with generator-level selection to 2% versus 3–10% for direct DeepJet tagging), and a transformer encoder that partially reconstructs charged $b$-hadron decay products (95.6% signal efficiency) to restore the angle-ordered emission history. Compared to a HERWIG7 sample with the $b$-mass set to zero in the showering kernel, the data disfavor the massless model at 4.3σ ($p=8\times10^{-6}$) in the 40–80 GeV bin, while the nominal massive shower fits well ($p=0.57$).

Why it matters: this is a direct imprint of quark mass on the parton shower in $b$-jets — following ALICE's $D^0$-tagged first observation and CMS/LHCb charm and beauty measurements — and provides a clean benchmark for logarithmically-accurate shower development and analytic Lund $b$-plane calculations. It dovetails with the recent heavy-quark energy-correlator work. Caveats: significance is confined to the lower-$p_T$ bin; at 80–200 GeV the narrower dead cone leaves no significant discrimination (nominal $p=0.03$, massless $p=0.01$). The massless-kernel comparison also shows hadronization plays a sizable role in the collinear suppression, so the effect is not purely perturbative.

### [arXiv:2608.29863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29863) — The inclusive $\bar B \to X_s\gamma$ decay rate with higher precision (M. Misiak et al.)

Misiak et al. deliver an upgraded SM prediction $\mathcal{B}_{s\gamma}^{\rm SM}=(3.54\pm0.14)\times10^{-4}$ for $E_\gamma>1.6$ GeV, a ~4% total uncertainty. The headline improvement is retiring the long-standing $m_c$-interpolation: the dominant $\mathcal{O}(\alpha_s^2)$ interference terms $\hat G_{17}^{(2)}$, $\hat G_{27}^{(2)}$ are now computed with exact charm-mass dependence via four-loop cut diagrams (companion paper [arXiv:2608.29864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29864)), enhancing $\mathcal{B}_{s\gamma}$ by 4.2% relative to the interpolated value — exceeding the previously assigned ±3% interpolation error by 1.4×. The $\mathcal{O}(\alpha_s)$ rate is also rendered formally complete by adding four- and five-body $(\bar s b)(\bar q q)$ contributions (~−0.2%), and HQE matrix elements, $m_b$ and $|V_{cb}|$ are taken from the latest kinetic-scheme semileptonic fit including $\mathcal{O}(\alpha_s^3)$. Including $\mathcal{O}(\alpha_s^2)$ cuts the $\mu_b$-scale variation to ±1.3% over [1.5, 10] GeV.

Why it matters: the prediction sits in perfect agreement with the experimental average $(3.49\pm0.19)\times10^{-4}$, and translates into an absolute (any-$\tan\beta$) 2HDM-II charged-Higgs bound $M_{H^\pm}>670$ GeV at 95% C.L. — stronger than comparable LHC direct searches. A convenient linear formula $\mathcal{B}_{s\gamma}\times10^4=(3.54\pm0.14)-8.51\,\Delta C_7-2.16\,\Delta C_8$ is provided for generic BSM fits. Against the persistent $R(D^*)$ tension, the inclusive-radiative sector is now fully SM-consistent. Caveat, flagged by the authors: the exact-$m_c$ $\hat G_{17,27}^{(2)}$ still omit the photon-energy cut (expected minor, given endpoint-peaked spectra), and PDG's $E_0$ extrapolation factors from experimental cuts down to 1.6 GeV await updating, ideally via SIMBA.
