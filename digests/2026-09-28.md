## Simulation plumbing, and a quiet LZ

The LZ recoil drew five to seven papers a day all last week and none today. What remains is mostly infrastructure. The CaloClouds group summarizes point-cloud fast simulation, and a Parnassus surrogate arrives for FCC-ee's CLD detector. Two coordinated optimal-transport papers take on negative-weight mitigation. The one genuinely new kind of bound comes from neutron stars. Yin et al. [arXiv:2609.30335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30335) exclude a finite *interval* of dark-matter triggering rates, where earlier survival arguments gave one-sided limits.

## Themes

### Optimal transport for negative weights
Hay et al. [arXiv:2609.30357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30357) resample NLO events in cells defined by an IRC-safe metric and find that OT-based distances group kinematically similar events best. The demonstration is on Z+jets. Jain et al. [arXiv:2609.30418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30418) supply the matching validation tool, a "Cross-Section-Mover's Distance" that compares full phase-space reweightings unbinned rather than through 1D histograms. Read them as method plus figure of merit. Nearby, Dimitrakopoulos [arXiv:2609.31296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31296) brings MiNLO to full off-shell $t\bar tW^+$ in HELAC-NLO. It lands a week after Pozzoli's two-loop $t\bar tW$ amplitude.

### Surrogates replacing Geant4
Qureshi et al. [arXiv:2609.30775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30775) train conditional flow matching on CLD Geant4+Pandora output. The surrogate beats Delphes and preserves $b/c/s/q$ tagging. It runs at 1.2 ms per event on a GPU and is public. This follows last week's ALEPH→SLD Parnassus transfer. Buss et al. [arXiv:2609.30403](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30403) survey CaloClouds3 (120× Geant4 on one CPU), CaloHadronic, and AllShowers, which covers twelve particle types in one model. They report cross-geometry transfer needing two to three orders of magnitude fewer showers. This is the review companion to last week's CaloClouds II transfer paper, from the group that also raised the Jevons'-paradox caution.

### Phase transitions, heard at high frequency
Berthiaume et al. [arXiv:2609.30366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30366) present Hybrid Partial Dressing, a finite-temperature resummation that is two-loop exact at all temperatures. It avoids the overlapping-momentum problem and reproduces the two-loop dimensionally reduced potential where that applies. Chen et al. [arXiv:2609.31495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31495) add a microscopic high-frequency GW component from particle–graviton production off runaway walls. Jödicke et al. [arXiv:2609.30318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30318) propose MAGGIE, a lumped-element kHz–MHz detector on a 14 T Hamburg magnet projecting ~$10^{-19}$ strain after a year. Last week's BBN-deuterium papers constrained the same supercooled transitions from the cosmology side.

### Spin and information in hadronic final states
Belmonte et al. [arXiv:2609.31425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31425) use $t\bar t$ spin correlation as a search variable for top-philic invisible mediators. Binned in visible mass, $\mathcal D(M^{\rm vis}_{t\bar t})$ beats rate and kinematics alone, for both exclusion and scalar/pseudoscalar discrimination. Adelaja et al. [arXiv:2609.31499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31499) bound a classical conditional mutual information between multiplicities in Mueller's dipole cascade. They report H1-derived cumulants far below the single-dipole cascade value, which reads as a caution for the entanglement-entropy picture of DIS.

## Worth a look
- **[arXiv:2609.30775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30775)**: A public FCC-ee surrogate that keeps flavor tagging intact at three orders of magnitude faster than full sim. Likely to show up in feasibility studies quickly.

# The Cross Section — Deep dives

*Note: of the four "worth a look" picks, full text was supplied for only three. There is no entry below for [arXiv:2609.30366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30366) (Hybrid Partial Dressing).*

### [arXiv:2609.30775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30775) — Parnassus for the CLD Detector: A Generative Machine-Learning Surrogate for Detector Simulation and Reconstruction at the FCC-ee (Umar Sohail Qureshi et al.)

**What they did.** A two-stage conditional flow-matching model maps generator-level particles to CLD PandoraPFA objects:

- **Event stage:** a residual MLP generates multiplicity, $H_T$ and missing-momentum components.
- **Particle stage:** a cross-attention DiT with about 5M parameters generates the objects themselves.
- **Outputs:** kinematics, species, and $d_0, z_0$ with their significances.
- **Conditioning:** the truth production vertex, which is what lets the model reproduce displaced heavy-flavor tails.

It was trained on $Z\to q\bar q$ and $b\bar b$ at 91.2 GeV, with $c\bar c$ and $s\bar s$ held out as out-of-distribution tests.

**Results.**

- **Distance to full simulation:** Parnassus is closer than Delphes for every observable, metric and flavor, typically by an order of magnitude. For example, $W_1(d_0/\sigma_{d_0})$ runs 0.14→0.41 from $q\bar q$ to $b\bar b$, against 2.06→4.94 for Delphes.
- **Tagging:** a transformer tagger reproduces Pandora AUCs to within about 1%. For a tagger trained on full simulation and applied to surrogate jets, accuracy drops only from 0.694 to 0.682.
- **Speed:** full simulation plus reconstruction costs 6.0 s per event. The surrogate costs about 39 ms per event on one CPU core and about 1.3 ms on an A100. The abstract quotes 1.2 ms.

**Why it matters.** Delphes's CLD card overestimates flavor separability: it reaches 0.732 accuracy against Pandora's 0.693. It also mis-models species fractions and $m_{jj}$. FCC-ee flavor-physics projections built on Delphes may therefore be optimistic. The authors frame this as their own caution about compensating effects in the transfer test.

**Caveats.**

- Parnassus slightly underestimates separability. At 80% $b$-efficiency, the $b$-vs-$c$ mistag is 1.58% against 1.00% for Pandora.
- $b\bar b$ is reproduced worse than the held-out $c\bar c$ and $s\bar s$ channels.
- Validation covers only the Z pole.
- Hyperparameters are taken unchanged from the CMS configuration.
- The HitPF variant shows residual impact-parameter deviations.

### [arXiv:2609.31425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31425) — Quantum probes of invisible particles in top-quark pair production at the LHC (Blanca Belmonte et al.)

**What they did.** This is a dileptonic $t\bar t+Y_{0,1}$ study at 14 TeV and 3 ab$^{-1}$, with the invisible mediator at 10–100 GeV. The selection is $E_T^{\rm miss}>100$ GeV and $m_{T2}^{\ell\ell}>90$ GeV. It reduces $t\bar t+tW$ to 0.04% while keeping 1.3% (scalar) and 4.0% (vector) of the signal at 10 GeV.

Four alternative analyses of the same sample are compared, and not combined:

- the inclusive yield;
- $dN/dM^{\rm vis}_{t\bar t}$;
- $dN/dD_{\rm evt}$;
- the fiducial $\mathcal D(M^{\rm vis}_{t\bar t})$, fitted with a Gaussian likelihood.

**Results.**

- **Exclusion ordering:** yield < $M^{\rm vis}$ < $D_{\rm evt}$ < $\mathcal D(M^{\rm vis})$, for both mediator spins and all masses.
- **Robustness of the angular variable:** $D_{\rm evt}$ with 50% shape uncertainty still beats $M^{\rm vis}$ with 20%.
- **Coupling structure:** toy-calibrated scalar/pseudoscalar separation is also best with $\mathcal D(M^{\rm vis})$. The advantage grows at higher mediator mass, where rate-based discrimination degrades.

**Why it matters.** Spin information carries reach that rate and kinematics alone do not capture, which moves collider QI from demonstration to search tool.

**Caveats.**

- The helicity frame is built from generator-level top momenta, which is truth-assisted.
- $E_T^{\rm miss}$ is generator-level.
- There is no detector simulation, and diboson and Z+jets backgrounds are neglected.
- The gain rests on $\delta\mathcal D_{\rm syst}=0.015$ and is lost at about 0.05.
- After cuts, $\mathcal D$ is no longer a density-matrix quantity.
- The axial-vector case is deferred because of unitarity issues.

### [arXiv:2609.30335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30335) — Metastable Neutron Stars as Transient-Rate Detectors of Heavy Dark Matter (Ziwen Yin et al.)

**What they did.** For neutron stars in a metastable hadronic mass window, the present conversion rate is $\mathcal R_{\rm tr}=n_{\rm NS}f_{\rm meta}\Gamma_\chi e^{-\Gamma_\chi t_{\rm meta}}$. This rate peaks at $\Gamma_\chi=1/t_{\rm meta}$. The benchmarks are:

- $n_{\rm NS}=3\times10^{15}$ Gpc$^{-3}$;
- $f_{\rm meta}=0.01$;
- $t_{\rm meta}=1$ Gyr;
- $\mathcal R_{\rm SGRB}=2\times10^3$ Gpc$^{-3}$yr$^{-1}$.

Requiring $\mathcal R_{\rm tr}$ below the SGRB rate excludes $7\times10^{-11}<\Gamma_\chi<4\times10^{-9}$ yr$^{-1}$. An interval exists only if $C<1/e$; the benchmark gives $C=0.067$.

**Resulting DM bounds.** With $U_{\max}=10$ GeV and gravitationally focused halo DM:

| Channel | Excluded band |
|---|---|
| Annihilation ($m_\chi\gtrsim5$ GeV) | $8\times10^{-34}<\langle\sigma v\rangle(5\,{\rm GeV}/m_\chi)^2<5\times10^{-32}$ cm$^3$/s; disfavors thermal relics at $4\times10^3$–$3\times10^4$ GeV |
| Decay | $3\times10^{29}<\tau_\chi(m_\chi/10\,{\rm GeV})<2\times10^{31}$ s |
| Inelastic scattering | $\sigma_{\rm inel}(10\,{\rm GeV}/m_\chi)$ between $2\times10^{-80}$ and $10^{-78}$ cm$^2$ |
| Macroscopic DM | narrow band $\sigma\approx(1.3$–$1.6)\times10^{-27}$ cm$^2$ (with weak $m_\chi^{1/19}$ scaling), for $4\times10^{-7}\lesssim m_\chi\lesssim2\times10^{14}$ g |

**Why it matters.** The bound needs no individual star to be identified as hadronic. The allowed rapid-trigger branch has no analogue in survival-based bounds.

**Caveats.**

- Every conversion is assumed to yield an observable SGRB, and all SGRBs are attributed to isolated sources ($f_{\rm iso}=1$).
- A single $t_{\rm meta}$ is used.
- Energy is assumed to couple maximally to the nucleation mode ($\epsilon_{\rm bub}\sim1$).
- Only hadronic deposition counts; electromagnetic showers are too diffuse.
- The local halo density of 0.4
