## Themes

### Light Higgsino Evasion Routes
The community continues to grapple with the tightening net around natural supersymmetry. In the last week, we saw how LZ direct-detection limits have severely cornered traditional light Higgsino dark matter (as detailed by Baer et al. in [arXiv:2607.05648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05648) and [arXiv:2607.07921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07921)). Today, the search for a way out of this corner proceeds along two complementary fronts: future lepton colliders and specialized LHC topologies. 

Hamaguchi et al. [arXiv:2606.31013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31013) provides a powerful counter-strategy to the LZ limits. They point out that while direct detection is highly sensitive when gauginos are light, its sensitivity collapses for heavier gauginos because Higgsino-gaugino mixing is suppressed. In this exact regime—where the Higgsino scattering cross-section drops deep below the neutrino fog—precision electroweak measurements of oblique parameters ($M_W$ and $\sin^2\theta_{\mathrm{eff}}$) at future $e^+e^-$ colliders can step in, cleanly probing highly compressed Higgsinos up to $500\text{ GeV}$. On the LHC side, Belyaev et al. [arXiv:2607.00243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00243) tackles the difficult compressed limit using a two-component scalar dark matter model (using a 3-Higgs Doublet Model framework), demonstrating that a dedicated search for soft opposite-sign dimuons accompanied by a hard jet can probe these elusive, compressed mass spectra.

### Quantum Information as an Experimental Lens
Quantum information theory has transcended its role as a toy model framework and is now being deployed to solve practical, real-world issues in experimental data analysis. 

A prime example is the work of Dixit et al. [arXiv:2606.31996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31996), which addresses the "fake" signals and degeneracies currently plaguing three-flavor neutrino analyses (a major theme in recent weeks, cf. [arXiv:2607.06108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06108)). By mapping three-flavor neutrino oscillations to qubit-like representations, they show that the local minima of entanglement concurrence identify energy regions where the flavor state is closest to separability. Aligning the benchmark oscillation regions of NO$\nu$A and T2K with these minimum entanglement regions allows for a cleaner, joint parameter extraction that dramatically reduces the tension between the two experiments. 

Meanwhile, the concept of "Magic"—a measure of how far a state is from being a easily-simulated stabilizer state—is popping up across the spectrum. Li et al. [arXiv:2606.31501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31501) shows that direction-averaged Magic in low-energy neutron-proton scattering is minimized at the CP-conserving point. This suggests a deep connection between fundamental symmetries and quantum computational complexity. In a similar vein, Alvarado et al. [arXiv:2607.00229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00229) tracks the production of magic states through electroweak $Z$ bosons and dark photons, finding that Bhabha scattering is highly sensitive to electroweak effects on the underlying stabilizer state classes.

### The Small-$x$ Gluon Wigner Trilogy
In a remarkable single-author tour de force, Lei Wang has posted three papers [arXiv:2606.31708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31708), [arXiv:2606.31712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31712), [arXiv:2606.31754](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31754) targeting the spatial and momentum distribution of gluons at small $x$. This represents a massive theoretical push to prepare for the spatial imaging capabilities of the EIC. 

In [arXiv:2606.31708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31708), Wang proposes using azimuthal energy-flow moments in DIS dijet production to cleanly extract the elliptic gluon Wigner distribution without the soft-radiation leakage that plagues exclusive diffractive dijets. In [arXiv:2606.31712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31712), the author moves to the evolution of this distribution, demonstrating that the collinearly improved Balitsky-Kovchegov (IMST-BK) evolution alters the shape and node positions of the elliptic signal, rather than acting as a simple normalization shift. Finally, [arXiv:2606.31754](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31754) constructs a normalized gluon Husimi phase-space distribution, showing that if you resolve it with a coarse-graining that adapts to the saturation scale $Q_s(Y, b)$, the momentum distributions collapse onto a single scaling curve—revealing an emergent local scaling law in the high-density regime.

### Next-Generation High-Energy Precision
The push to sub-percent precision at colliders is forcing theorists to compute terms that were once deemed impossibly complex. 

Gehrmann-De Ridder et al. [arXiv:2606.31286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31286) presents the first fully differential calculation of boosted Higgs-strahlung ($WH$ production) at N$^3$LO in QCD. This is a monumental achievement: the N$^3$LO corrections alter the boosted regime by $+2\%$, lying entirely outside the scale variation bands of the previous NNLO calculation, while reducing the residual scale uncertainty to under $1\%$. 

On the experimental tracking front, Lee et al. [arXiv:2607.00087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00087) achieves a major milestone by presenting the first complete, collinear-logarithmically matched calculations of jet substructure observables *on tracks*. By leveraging track-level tracking information rather than calorimeter clusters, this framework bypasses the spatial resolution limits of calorimetry, opening up a new regime of high-precision QCD measurements at the LHC.

### Human-AI Co-Piloting in the Theory Stack
A quiet revolution is happening in how we build and execute our calculations. AI is moving from a tool for analyzing data to a co-developer of theoretical infrastructure. 

Xiao et al. [arXiv:2606.31214](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31214) introduces *EasyScan_HEP 2*, an upgrade designed specifically to be "agent-ready." It exposes machine-readable command-line and config interfaces, allowing an LLM AI assistant to convert natural-language user requests directly into complex, multi-program parameter scan workflows. 

On the calculation side, Kitazawa et al. [arXiv:2606.31492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31492) explicitly credits a human-AI collaboration for the development of high-order hopping-parameter expansion algorithms in lattice QCD. By utilizing a trie data structure designed in tandem with an AI coding agent, they successfully computed the daunting $\kappa^{12}$ term, demonstrating that AI can be a highly effective partner in pure mathematical physics.
