## Same data, different error bars

Elmer et al. [arXiv:2610.30239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30239) reconstruct the triplet PDF three ways and get comparable central values with markedly different uncertainties. Morales-Zapien et al. [arXiv:2610.30510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30510) keep OGLE's Magellanic events and efficiencies fixed and weaken the leading PBH bound by up to ~6× just by updating halo and lens models. Both carry the week's error-budget thread, from scale variations to LZ's ER tail, into ML fits and astrophysical limits. The sharpest measurement is Belle/Belle II's first observation of $\Lambda_c^+\to\Lambda K^+\eta$, at a rate well above SU(3)$_F$ expectations. There are no 248 keV papers today, the second blank day this week.

## Themes

### Assumptions inside the error bar
Elmer et al. [arXiv:2610.30239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30239) compare Gaussian processes, NN ensembles and tangent-kernel fits for the triplet PDF. Their normalised-bias diagnostic shows calibration depends on the correlations of the predictive covariance, not just its pointwise width. Morales-Zapien et al. [arXiv:2610.30510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30510) find the LMC halo is one of the levers on the microlensing bound, the same input this week's LZ halo-tail fight turned on, and ask that compact-object limits be quoted with their modeling assumptions. Lind [arXiv:2610.31103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31103) simulates fictitious top mesons in Pythia 8.3 and puts the MC mass 240–330 MeV below the pole mass. That matters now that Ball et al. showed any threshold-excess number is also a statement about $m_t$.

### Inference engines, sequential and pretrained
Bahl et al. [arXiv:2610.30245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30245) extend SBI from couplings to masses and widths by iteratively concentrating simulations where the data point. At equal training budget they get better-calibrated likelihoods for $A\to ZH$ and $Y\to HH$. Magel et al.'s CURTAIN [arXiv:2610.30894](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30894) and Li et al. [arXiv:2610.31180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31180) push foundation models into neutrino telescopes and TPC tracking. Hackett et al. [arXiv:2610.30338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30338) find that learned neutrino–nucleus cross sections need flux nuisances once appearance channels enter. This continues the month's pattern of ML tooling moving from agents into the inference layer itself.

### Axions, off the haloscope
Bottaro et al. [arXiv:2610.30238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30238) show $^{229}$Th nuclear clocks respond *linearly* to "imperfect" axions, where explicit PQ breaking or CEDMs shift the potential minimum. An Earth-sourced static field would also allow fifth-force searches. Bao et al. [arXiv:2610.30250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30250) treat the haloscope quantum mechanically. They find the photon-number distribution is not Poisson, and that a virialized Gaussian state can be told apart from a coherent state or BEC. Bose et al. [arXiv:2610.30255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30255) use supernova ALPs gravitationally bound to the remnant neutron star, whose decays reheat it. The cold PSR J2144−3933 then gives the strongest $g_{a\gamma}$ limits at 1–400 MeV. Together they extend September's widening of the axion program into new detection channels.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2610.31377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31377) — Measurements of the branching fractions of $\Lambda_c^+\to\Lambda K^+\pi^0$ and $\Lambda_c^+\to\Lambda K^+\eta$ decays at Belle and Belle II (Belle et al.)

Belle and Belle II (980 + 570 fb$^{-1}$) report the first observation of $\Lambda_c^+\to\Lambda K^+\eta$ at >10σ statistical significance, from 1107 ± 45 signal events. Relative to the reference mode, the measured ratio is $\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{B}(\Lambda\pi^+\pi^0)=(8.73\pm0.35\pm0.43)\times10^{-3}$, giving $\mathcal{B}=(6.20\pm0.25\pm0.31\pm0.30)\times10^{-4}$. The three SU(3)$_F$ predictions are $(1.9\pm0.6)$, $(0.45\pm0.10)$ and $(0.59\pm0.24)\times10^{-4}$. The measurement exceeds them by 5.5σ, 11.2σ and 10.1σ respectively. The companion SCS mode is measured as $\mathcal{B}(\Lambda K^+\pi^0)=(1.29\pm0.05\pm0.05\pm0.06)\times10^{-3}$. This agrees with BESIII and with the 2024 fit that includes the color-symmetric Hamiltonian (1.36 ± 0.25), and sits well below the earlier 3.5–4.5 × 10⁻³ predictions. The precision is markedly improved over BESIII's ±0.27 statistical error.

So the same SU(3)$_F$ fit that now gets $\Lambda K^+\pi^0$ right undershoots $\Lambda K^+\eta$ by an order of magnitude. That is the charm-baryon entry in the season's flavor-symmetry strain noted in the digest. The caveat is visible in the paper itself. The two-body invariant-mass spectra deviate significantly from phase space, and efficiencies are reweighted to the data Dalitz distribution. The predictions assume non-resonant, S-wave-dominated meson pairs, so intermediate resonances in $\Lambda K^+\eta$ (smaller phase space) could account for the excess rather than a failure of the symmetry itself. A Dalitz analysis would discriminate. On the experimental side, the η systematic is dominated by photon reconstruction (4.4%), and there is a 4.8% reference-BF uncertainty.

### [arXiv:2610.30239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30239) — Faithful Uncertainties in ML-Based Inverse Problems: GPs, NNs and NTKs for the Triplet PDF (Nina Elmer et al.)

Methods that agree on the central $T_3$ disagree on what their error bars mean. The setup is a closure test on BCDMS $F_2^p-F_2^d$, using 248 points with the full covariance, NNPDF4.0 truth, and a data region of $x\gtrsim0.07$. The normalised bias $R_b$ is computed on a single $L_1$ realisation:

| Model | $R_b$ (full covariance) | $R_b$ (diagonal only) |
|---|---|---|
| RBF GP | 1.08 | 0.38 |
| Gibbs GP | 0.86 | 0.53 |
| NTK, trained | 0.67 | — |
| NTK, at initialisation | 9.5 | — |
| $L_2$ NN ensemble | 0.74 | — |
| $L_1$ NN ensemble | 2.84 | — |

The $L_1$ ensemble excludes data uncertainty by construction, so its large value is expected. Dropping covariance correlations shifts $R_b$ non-monotonically, so pointwise width alone does not determine calibration. Two other failure modes stand out:
- **Heteroscedastic loss.** It learns broad diagonal variances, which the authors read as likelihood misspecification rather than faithful uncertainty.
- **Endpoint preprocessing.** It sets the small-$x$ answer. The fit at $\alpha=1$ versus $\alpha=0.5$ is identical in the data region but diverges below 0.07. Preprocessed GP variance vanishes at the endpoints by construction, giving bands that miss the truth.

In the HERA extrapolation test, the initialisation NTK degrades NC-EM by $\Delta n_\sigma\approx5.0$, while all other models stay below 1. The authors are candid about the limits. With $n_{\rm eig}$ = 4–19, the test rules out gross underestimates but cannot certify faithfulness. A single seed visibly changes the reconstructions, and the $L_2$ ensemble is only NNPDF-inspired.

### [arXiv:2610.30510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30510) — Same Events, Weaker Bound: Model Dependence in Magellanic Microlensing Limits on Primordial Black Holes (David Morales-Zapien et al.)

The authors hold OGLE's 13 events, efficiencies and $t_E$ posteriors fixed and change only the rate model, inside OGLE's own relaxed likelihood. The benchmark uses a flattened MW halo ($q_h=0.72$, $\rho_\odot=0.30$ GeV cm⁻³), a cored Burkert LMC halo, Ibata disks, and a lighter LMC with a shallower IMF. It weakens the 20-year relaxed limit by up to 1.6 near 0.32 $M_\odot$. A deliberately extreme stress test weakens it by up to 3.4.

The headline "~6×" (6.2 for the benchmark, 13.2 for the stress test) is relative to the strict limit, so it also folds in OGLE's own event-origin difference. In the planetary-mass window, the factors are 1.19 and 2.39 for smooth PBHs, and 3.4 and 6.0 for 30 pc clusters under a Lyα shot-noise cap. A one-at-a-time ablation at 0.45 $M_\odot$ shows the IMF matters most ($R=0.68$), while the heavier disks push the other way (1.10). LMC-induced disequilibrium *raises* the lensing-weighted halo density by about 6%, undercutting a common critique.

The caveats are explicit:
- Parameters were chosen to suppress the rate and are fixed, not marginalized.
- The IMF endpoints are not a joint confidence region.
- The stress test conflicts with 6D tracer masses.
- Its isothermal LMC halo fails the LMC mass constraints.

Oddly, the weaker limits coexist with a larger preference for nonzero $f$ near 0.03 $M_\odot$: $\Delta\chi^2=9.2$–10.2, versus 3.7 in OGLE's analysis.
