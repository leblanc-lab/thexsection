## Relic supernova neutrinos, at 2.6σ

Super-Kamiokande [arXiv:2610.35619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35619) reports an indication of $\bar\nu_e$ from the diffuse supernova neutrino background, using 5003 days of data. The best-fit flux is $3.6^{+1.6}_{-1.5}$ cm⁻²s⁻¹ above 13.3 MeV, 2.6σ from null, and the significance holds across spectral models. It is not a discovery, but it is the first hint of a signal that has only ever produced limits. LHCb supplies the day's firmer result: the $T_{c\bar c1}(4430)^-$ seen in $D^{*-}D^0$, extending the season's strong spectroscopy run. On the theory side, the weight sits in flavor textures, with three papers deriving Yukawa structure from unification or non-invertible symmetries.

## Themes

### Textures from new symmetries
Hall et al. [arXiv:2610.33152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33152) note that $y_\tau=y_b$ and $y_\mu=3y_s$ both hold near $10^6$ GeV. They read this as low-scale Pati–Salam with $\mu\to e$ conversion as the direct test. One of their two theories predicts $|V_{cb}|=0.042\pm0.002$, a number that has to face the month's inclusive/exclusive $|V_{cb}|$ split.

Two papers use fusion algebras instead:
- Distler et al. [arXiv:2610.34720](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34720) fit mass textures well, but no algebra they study fits masses and mixings together.
- Borah et al. [arXiv:2610.35587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35587) get inverted ordering with $m_3=0$. That lines up with Gluza et al.'s finding last week that most mixing schemes prefer inverted ordering.

### LLMs graded on QFT
Liu et al.'s QFT-Bench [arXiv:2610.33134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33134) has 75 graduate-to-research-level problems. Frontier models exceed 95%, but their chains of thought often fail to justify the correct answers they reach. Saad's proton_agent [arXiv:2610.33593](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33593) routes GUT running and proton decay through a deterministic backend and runs on local open models. Both continue the agentic thread that last week produced FeynFacet's analytic NLO results.

### Inelastic DM beyond xenon
Kim et al.'s dark-Higgs model for both the LZ event and the GCE [arXiv:2610.25423](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25423), covered on 10-20, reappears in today's listing. Two papers take inelastic kinematics past the 248 keV event itself:
- Amarasinghe [arXiv:2610.35789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35789) maps the neutrino fog for inelastic scattering on xenon. In some regions it vanishes entirely; in others its onset jumps discontinuously with mass and splitting.
- Chiang et al. [arXiv:2610.35620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35620) use NGC 1068 to bound threshold-induced spectral deficits in AGN spikes.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2610.35619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35619) — Indication of Electron Antineutrinos from the Diffuse Supernova Neutrino Background (Kamiokande Collaboration et al.)

Super-Kamiokande fits 5003.0 days (Oct 2008–Mar 2026, phases SK-IV through VIII, including the Gd-loaded phases) and finds a DSNB flux of $3.6^{+1.6}_{-1.5}$ cm⁻²s⁻¹ ($4.8^{+2.2}_{-2.0}$ events/yr) for $E_\nu>13.3$ MeV, assuming the Horiuchi+09 6 MeV spectral shape. The background-only hypothesis is disfavored at 2.6σ (one-sided). The Kresse+21 and Ashida+23 shapes give 2.7σ and 2.6σ. The Gd phases alone give 2.2σ and the pure-water phases 1.4σ. The simpler model-independent count over 13.3–31.3 MeV gives only 1.8σ (p = 0.036). The best-fit flux sits at the upper end of the Horiuchi+09 range of 2.1–3.9 cm⁻²s⁻¹.

On the digest's question of where the gain comes from:
- **Background rejection.** An ML NC classifier raises IBD efficiency from 79% to 89% and halves the residual NCQE fraction from 10% to 5%.
- **Systematics.** A T2K beam sample cuts the NC rate uncertainty from 70% to 45%. The neutron-multiplicity uncertainty falls from 30–40% to 10–30%, and the ⁹Li remaining-rate error from 50% to 30%.
- **Thresholds.** The $N_n=1$ threshold drops from 15.5 to 11.5 MeV.

The paper says only that the combined effect more than doubles the median discovery potential. The provided text does not split that gain by component.

The key caveat is in Appendix B. With the old $N_n\neq1$ threshold, the pure-water phases show an extra contribution at 15.5–21.5 MeV, comparable to the expected signal. Including it shifts the fitted DSNB rate by about 1σ, which is more than statistics should allow. The collaboration attributes this to mismodeled spallation and raises that threshold to 21.5 MeV, at a 13.9% cost in expected signal. That is conservative, but it also shows the spallation model is not yet under control at the sensitivity now being reached.

### [arXiv:2610.35239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35239) — Observation of the $T_{c\bar c1}(4430)^-$ state in $B^0\to D^{*-}D^0K^+$ decays (LHCb collaboration et al.)

LHCb's amplitude analysis uses 6912 candidates from 9 fb⁻¹, with about 5% background. A model containing only $D^0K^+$ resonances describes the $D^0K^+$ and $D^{*-}K^+$ spectra but misses a dip near 4.4 GeV in $m(D^{*-}D^0)$ ($\chi^2=82.1$ over 64 Dalitz bins).

Adding the $T_{c\bar c1}(4430)^-$ with its mass and width fixed gives $2\Delta$NLL $=-43.0$, which is 6.2σ statistical and 5.3σ after systematics. The state appears through destructive interference rather than as a peak. Its own fit fraction is $(1.3\pm0.4)\%$, while its interference with the QMI S-wave is $(-6.6\pm1.3)\%$. $J^P=1^+$ is preferred over the alternatives at more than 5σ.

Other results:
- With mass and width free, the fit gives $4424\pm21^{+12}_{-6}$ MeV and $145\pm39\pm17$ MeV. These sit 1.8σ and 0.7σ from the $\psi(2S)\pi$ values, so "consistent" carries a mild mass pull.
- Substituting the $T_{c\bar c1}(4200)$ gives only 3.6σ. With both states included, the 4200 drops to 1.6σ.
- The $T_{c\bar c1}(3900)$ and $T^*_{\bar c\bar s1}(2900)$ are not significant (fit fractions below 0.24% and 0.32% at 95% CL).

This is the first open-charm decay of the state. The measured width ratio $\Gamma(D^{*-}D^0)/\Gamma(\psi(2S)\pi^-)=0.96\pm0.29^{+0.22}_{-0.48}{}^{+0.57}_{-0.30}$ disfavors dominance of either channel. The authors note that theory predictions for this ratio are lacking.

The main caveat is that the dominant systematic is amplitude modeling. The overlapping $D^0K^+$ $1^-$ wave, which carries about 75% of the fit fractions, is described by splines, with a K-matrix alternative used as the systematic variation. The two diverge in phase above 3 GeV.

### [arXiv:2610.33152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33152) — A Low Scale for Quark–Lepton Unification (Lawrence J. Hall et al.)

Running the Standard Model Yukawas at two loops, the authors find that $y_\tau=y_b$ holds at $\mu_3=5.20^{+0.90}_{-0.76}\times10^5$ GeV and $y_\mu=3y_s$ at $\mu_2=7.20^{+3.69}_{-2.33}\times10^5$ GeV. The ratio $\mu_2/\mu_3$ is stable under three-loop running (1.39 → 1.40). The Georgi–Jarlskog relation $y_e=y_d/3$ arrives later, at about $3\times10^8$ GeV. For contrast, at $2\times10^{16}$ GeV the three ratios are off by 30–70%.

A sparse Pati–Salam texture produces all three relations. An $SU(4)$-singlet bidoublet fills the 12, 21 and 33 entries, and an adjoint fills the 22 entry. However, tree-level vector-leptoquark exchange in $K_L\to\mu e$ requires $M_X\gtrsim2.5\times10^6$ GeV, which is above $\mu_{2,3}$. That leaves two routes:
- **Electron mass from elsewhere.** The heavier-generation relations then survive at $(5$–$7)\times10^5$ GeV, with $K\to\mu\mu$ as the leading probe.
- **Two fixed-parameter theories that keep all three relations:**
  - The "123" theory reaches unification through flavor diagonalization alone, at $4.6^{+1.2}_{-0.9}\times10^6$ GeV.
  - The "12" theory has symmetric Yukawas and unifies at $1.5^{+0.7}_{-0.5}\times10^7$ GeV. It predicts $|V_{cb}|=0.042\pm0.002$; the paper compares this only to the PDG value $0.0411\pm0.0012$. It also needs an 11% charged-scalar threshold, a negligible up-sector 2–3 rotation, and $\sin\beta\approx0.07$–0.17.

The $\mu\to e$ conversion rate is $\mathrm{CR_{Al}}\approx2.8\times10^{-17}(10^7\,\mathrm{GeV}/M_X)^4$. Mu2e and COMET Phase II reach about $8\times10^6$ GeV, covering most of the 123 range; Mu2e-II and AMF would cover both theories.

Caveats:
- About 75–90% of the variance in the unification scales comes from $m_d$ and $m_s$.
- The leading-order texture predicts $|V_{us}|=0.219$ against a measured 0.2243; matching it needs a relative phase of about 92° between the up and down light blocks.
- Scalar contributions to $\mu\to e$ conversion are omitted.
- In $G_{422}$, nucleon-decay bounds of several $10^6$ GeV can arise, but they depend on assumed Planck-suppressed operator coefficients.
