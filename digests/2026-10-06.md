## Red dwarfs reject the G-step

Sakstein et al. [arXiv:2610.04048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04048) test Hubble-tension fixes that change Newton's constant abruptly in the recent past. They use fully convective red dwarfs, whose thermal timescales match the proposed look-back times, and eight eclipsing-binary mass-radius measurements. A 4% step at 73 Myr is excluded at 3.1σ, and an 11% step at 130 Myr at 4.0σ. Most of the rest of the day is spin and polarization observables, plus two papers arguing that exclusive QCD rates should be squared at the amplitude level. That pair is the latest entry in last week's "corrections the size of errors" file. LZ stays at a trickle of three papers.

## Themes

### Polarization as the measurement
- Bachmann et al. [arXiv:2610.03855](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03855) show that parton-level polarization fractions set the sensitivity limit for polarized cross sections, and that a network can learn them. They demonstrate this on ZZ.
- Hoppe et al. [arXiv:2610.06698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06698) add polarized loop-induced processes to SHERPA+RECOLA.
- Bi et al. [arXiv:2610.04971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04971) build a $bW$ spin density matrix, analyzing the $b$ spin through $\Lambda_b$ decays. They report gains of 8× on $V_R$ and about 6× on $g_L$ at the HL-LHC.

On the formal side, Beane et al. [arXiv:2610.06791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06791) find that entanglement power and non-local magic both depend, at leading order, on one geometric quantity. That gives the season's "quantum magic" thread a common footing.

### Keep the whole square
Gong et al. [arXiv:2610.04557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04557) argue that infrared divergences cancel amplitude by amplitude in exclusive processes, so the full modulus square of the truncated amplitude is the right observable. They show it keeps NNLO charmonium rates positive and convergent. Zhan et al. [arXiv:2610.04293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04293) apply the same idea to $J/\psi+\gamma$ in ultraperipheral collisions. The conventional spectrum turns negative at high $p_T$, the complete square raises integrated rates by 11.6–18.4%, and the scale bands shrink by 36–67%.

### LZ: the Sun, then the LHC
Freese et al. flagged last week that the solar bound depends on elastic scattering. Jeong et al. [arXiv:2610.05893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05893) attack the annihilation side instead. Axion-mediated annihilation into dark photons suppresses the solar neutrino signal while capture stays efficient, at the price of Higgsinos making up a few percent of the dark matter. Anwar et al. [arXiv:2610.06756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06756) recast the ATLAS monojet search and exclude most elastic EFT readings below about a TeV. Kahlhoefer et al. [arXiv:2610.06159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06159) show that neural ratio estimation can marginalize over halo models at no extra cost, which bears on the halo argument behind every LZ fit.

### FCC-ee, channel by channel
Altmannshofer et al. [arXiv:2610.04309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04309) use polarized Z-pole $\Lambda_b\to\Lambda\nu\bar\nu$ to reach $|C_R|^2-|C_L|^2$ and ${\rm Im}(C_LC_R^*)$, combinations the mesonic modes cannot see. ## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2610.04048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04048) — Stars Would Remember a Recent Gravitational Transition: Testing G-step Resolutions of the Hubble Tension with Red Dwarfs (Jeremy Sakstein et al.)

Both Hubble-motivated $G$-step benchmarks are disfavoured: $\Delta G/G_0=+0.04$ at 73 Myr (EGSM) at 3.1σ, and $+0.11$ at 130 Myr (OGSM) at 4.0σ. The profile likelihood has its minimum at $\Delta G=0$ for both transition times.

The mechanism is a radius memory in fully convective red dwarfs. Homology gives an equilibrium radius $R\propto G^{0.324}$, while the fast adiabatic response gives $R\propto G^{-1}$. The perturbation then relaxes on $t_{\rm relax}\simeq0.05\,t_{\rm KH}\sim10^8$ yr, which is comparable to the proposed look-back times. A drop from larger $G$ therefore leaves today's stars inflated.

The analysis uses:
- MESA tracks with an implemented $G$-step;
- a per-star starspot covering fraction treated as a nuisance parameter;
- eight CHEOPS detached-eclipsing-binary M dwarfs (0.1–0.31 $M_\odot$, periods ≥5 d) with mass–radius correlations $\rho=0.53$–0.90.

This gives $\Delta\chi^2=9.52$ (EGSM) and 16.41 (OGSM). Because the spot fractions sit at their physical boundary, the significances come from a parametric bootstrap rather than Wilks' theorem.

The test is independent of the disputed solar and terrestrial modelling arguments.

**Caveats:**
- **Boundary stars drive the result.** All of the leverage comes from the four stars pinned at $f_{\rm spot}=0$, where spots can no longer absorb the extra inflation.
- **One star dominates.** Dropping J2343+29 cuts the significances to 2.45σ (EGSM) and 1.90σ (OGSM).
- **Model systematics matter.** A coherent 1% downward shift of the model radii lowers $\Delta\chi^2$ to 5.72 and 10.92.
- **Only positive steps are tested.** Both benchmarks have $\Delta G>0$.

### [arXiv:2610.04557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04557) — Violation and Restoration of Probability Positivity in Perturbative QFT (Bin Gong et al.)

The central argument concerns exclusive processes with colourless and neutral external states. There, each renormalized loop amplitude is IR-finite on its own and no KLN sum over unresolved states is needed. Gong et al. conclude that truncating the squared amplitude is an inherited QED habit, and that $|\sum_{l\le M}\mathcal A^{(l)}|^2$ is the right observable.

They recast eleven published NNLO results in the form $\Gamma=\Gamma_{\rm LO}|1+a_s(c_1+ic_2)+a_s^2(c_3+ic_4)|^2$. In every case both complex coefficients have modulus below 1, with the largest, 0.67, for $J/\psi+\eta_c$. Selected ratios $\Gamma/\Gamma_{\rm LO}$ at NLO, NNLO:

| Channel | Amplitude-level | Squared-level |
|---|---|---|
| $J/\psi\to3\gamma$ | 0.276, 0.126 | −0.02, −0.44 |
| $\Upsilon\to3\gamma$ | 0.426, 0.190 | 0.28, −0.11 |
| $e^+e^-\to J/\psi J/\psi$ | 0.40, 0.058 | 0.26, −0.38 |

$J/\psi\to3\gamma$ goes negative at NLO in the traditional expansion. $\Upsilon\to3\gamma$ and double-$J/\psi$ go negative at NNLO. Zhan et al. apply the same prescription to ultraperipheral $J/\psi+\gamma$.

**Caveats, visible in the paper itself:**
- **Partial higher orders.** The NLO square keeps $|\mathcal M_1|^2$ but not the same-order $2{\rm Re}(\mathcal M_0^*\mathcal M_2)$, so it contains incomplete $\mathcal O(\alpha_s^2)$ terms.
- **Convergence is judged inside the modulus.** The rates themselves still fall sharply from NLO to NNLO: by a factor 2.2 for $J/\psi\to3\gamma$ and about 7 for double $J/\psi$.
- **Positivity is not accuracy.** Positivity is guaranteed by construction, which does not show the result is closer to the true value.
- **Mostly a reframing.** The numbers re-express the group's own earlier calculations, with $\alpha_s$ re-chosen for the B-factory channels.

### [arXiv:2610.04309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04309) — Angular Observables in the $\Lambda_b\to\Lambda\nu\bar\nu$ Decay (Wolfgang Altmannshofer et al.)

The full cascade $\Lambda_b\to\Lambda(\to p\pi^-)\nu\bar\nu$ is described by five angular coefficients, $S_{1\text{–}4}$ and $A_5$; all but $S_1$ need $\Lambda_b$ polarization. Z-pole $\Lambda_b$ supply it: LEP measured $\mathcal P=-0.40\pm0.14$.

The coefficients split by what they probe:
- **Chirality:** $S_1$ and $S_2$ go as $|C_R|^2-|C_L|^2$, which the mesonic modes cannot see.
- **CP phase:** $A_5$ is a T-odd CP asymmetry proportional to ${\rm Im}(C_LC_R^*)$, with no flavour tagging or time dependence needed.

Mesonic data fix only $|C_L\pm C_R|$, so they confine the couplings to a ring via $(|C_R|^2-|C_L|^2)^2+4({\rm Im}\,C_LC_R^*)^2=|C_R+C_L|^2|C_R-C_L|^2$. The baryonic observables pick the point on that ring.

SM predictions:
- branching ratio $(7.59\pm1.03)\times10^{-6}$, dominated by form-factor and CKM uncertainties;
- $\langle S_1\rangle=-0.926\pm0.018$;
- an $S_2$ zero crossing at $12.6\pm1.2$ GeV² that is independent of the Wilson coefficients, and so tests the form factors.

Projected FCC-ee reach, assuming a 10% branching-ratio measurement:
- **Real couplings:** $S_1$ and $S_2$ break the four-fold real-coupling degeneracy even at ±0.5 uncertainty on the angular observables.
- **Complex couplings:** at ±0.1 all four benchmarks are distinguishable from the SM. At ±0.5 the $A_5$ sensitivity is largely lost and only the pure right-handed benchmark survives.

This bears directly on the Belle II $K^+\nu\bar\nu$ excess, which the authors put about 2.7σ above the SM.

**Caveats:**
- **No dedicated angular estimates.** The ±0.1–0.5 uncertainties are assumed scans.
- **Simplified meson comparison.** The mesonic side is a 5% toy estimate.
- **Only right-handed currents show up.** A pure rescaling of $C_L$ leaves every angular observable unchanged.
- **Dark-sector explanations not covered.** Light invisible states are explicitly excluded, although several Belle II interpretations rely on them.
