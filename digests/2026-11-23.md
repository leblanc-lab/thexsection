## Toponium and a pseudoscalar must interfere

The year's longest interpretive fight asks whether the $t\bar t$ threshold excess is NRQCD $\eta_t$ or a top-philic CP-odd Higgs. Djouadi et al. [arXiv:2611.27704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27704) show the two readings cannot be fitted separately. An $A$ near $2m_t$ sits in the same colour-singlet ${}^1S_0$ channel with the same Coulomb interaction, so the two must interfere. Unitarity then fixes the line shape through a single complex ratio $\kappa$, with an exact amplitude zero just above $M_A$. Fits that simply add toponium and $A$ templates are therefore wrong by construction. Also today: an LLM agent delivering new Feynman-integral bases, a 6.3σ strike against the Körner–Pati–Woo rule, and the first NNLO+PS generator for $e^+e^-$.

## Themes

### Zeros from interference
For $A$ decaying to tops, Djouadi et al. find $\mathrm{Im}\,\kappa=-1$ exactly and $|\kappa|\approx1.5$–1.7. An $A$ carrying half the excess would shift the toponium peak by 10–70%. Cesarotti et al. [arXiv:2611.27681](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27681) make the general case: in strongly coupled UV completions, destructive interference can put an amplitude zero parametrically below the heavy threshold. Absorptive effects turn that zero into a dip, and its position, depth and curvature constrain the UV. The two papers appeared independently on the same day.

### Agents, where the answer can be checked
Petit Rosas et al. [arXiv:2611.26688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26688) had Claude Opus 5.5 work autonomously for days. It produced ε-pole-free bases for a two-loop five-point family with 18 elliptic sectors and an ε-factorized basis for a radiative-jet-function superfamily with a K3 sector. The authors pick the task because the output is verifiable, which is the standard Alimena argued for on 11-19. That contrasts with last week's Codex reproduction of CMS $J/\psi$ cross sections, which carried a 21% RMS spread. Bonnet-Guerrini et al. [arXiv:2611.27079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27079) find a jet foundation model whose classification flips when one soft neutral particle is added. They trace the infrared-safety failure to mixed reconstruction thresholds in pretraining. They then repair it either by removing three sparse-autoencoder directions or by letting an agent edit the weights directly in 50 queries.

### Heavy-hadron rules under strain
Di Wang et al. [arXiv:2611.27716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27716) find that the KPW+SU(3)$_F$ prediction for $\Xi_c^0\to pK^-$ overshoots the measured rate by 17±6×, a 6.3σ discrepancy in a joint fit. Shirui Zhao et al. [arXiv:2611.27363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27363) predict $D_{s1}(2460)$ radiative ratio 0.57, below Belle's 0.74±0.08 from 11-17. They read the state as a $^1P_1$/$^3P_1$ mixture with a possible tetraquark component. Jiaxing Zhao et al. [arXiv:2611.27014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27014) fit bottomonium and lattice $\Omega_{bbb}$ jointly, and the fit favours Y-shaped over Δ-shaped confinement.

### Lepton-collider generators
König et al. [arXiv:2611.26718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26718) build MiNNLO for lepton collisions and apply it to dijets in Powheg. Dave et al. [arXiv:2611.27883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27883) match YFS soft-photon resummation to NLO for radiative-return $\mu\mu/\pi\pi$. They agree with BabaYaga at the per-mille level but differ from KKMC by up to 2%, which matters for HVP inputs.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2611.27704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27704) — A pseudoscalar Higgs boson interfering with toponium: a QCD analysis of $gg\to A\to t\bar t$ near threshold (Abdelhak Djouadi et al.)

Djouadi and Ellis show that an $A$ near $2m_t$ must interfere with $\eta_t$, and that its line shape is a Fano profile fixed by one complex number $\kappa$. Both live in the colour-singlet ${}^1S_0$ channel and share the same Coulomb Green function. If $A$ decays only to $t\bar t$, unitarity forces $\mathrm{Im}\,\kappa=-1$ exactly and independently of $g_{Att}$. The narrow-width rate then changes by $\delta=-2/|\kappa|^2$, with an exact amplitude zero at $\epsilon=-\mathrm{Re}\,\kappa$. Coulomb resummation enhances $\Gamma_A$ by 1.2–3.5×, which gives resummed $\kappa\approx-1.14$ to $-1.37$ minus $i$, so $|\kappa|\approx1.5$–1.7: maximal distortion. The zero then sits at $\epsilon\approx1.1$–1.4, and the ${}^1S_0$ rate drops 70–90% relative to signal alone.

Because toponium lies below $M_A$, where $\mathrm{Re}\,\kappa<0$, the interference there is constructive. For $f=50\%$, the $\eta_t$ peak rises by 1.69, 1.19 and 1.12 at $M_A=350$, 365 and 380 GeV. The threshold excess over toponium is 0.95, 1.22 and 1.45 coherently, versus 1.57, 1.44 and 1.41 incoherently. An $A$ at 350 GeV carrying half the excess is thus essentially invisible. At 20% $m_{t\bar t}$ resolution, toponium and toponium+$A$ differ mainly in normalization.

Caveats are stated plainly:
- The hard process is LO without $K$-factors.
- The Coulomb potential is LO with a constant $\Gamma_t$.
- The matching to fixed order is an interpolation.
- Top decays and spin correlations are not simulated.

Peak enhancements still move by up to ~20% under these variations. The paper also retracts the robustness of its own earlier wide-window probe, which shifts from ≈4 to ≈3, or ≈6 with a frozen form factor.

### [arXiv:2611.26688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26688) — Agent-guided construction of Feynman integral bases (Pau Petit Rosas et al.)

Claude Opus 5.5, driven by two "skills" (roughly 4,500 lines of instructions and 15k lines of mostly Mathematica code), produced bases for two families. For each, the authors state that no such basis existed before. Every candidate passes a deterministic gate before acceptance: an exact-in-$\varepsilon$ IBP reduction on a kinematic slice, an honesty check, and an invertibility check. An agent error can therefore waste CPU time but cannot enter an accepted basis.

**Two-loop five-point QED pentabox with $m_e,m_\mu$** (seven scales, 232 masters):
- **Classification.** The agent classified 80 sectors as canonical and 18 as elliptic. Five of the canonical sectors need nested roots.
- **Pole removal.** It started from 23,254 entries with $1/(a+b\varepsilon)$ poles. After ~155 accepted master changes over eight days, the census of offending entries was empty.
- **Result.** The final system is pole-free and at most quadratic in $\varepsilon$. Its diagonal blocks are $\varepsilon$-factorized in all 75 non-nested canonical sectors.

**Radiative-jet-function superfamily** (124 masters):
- **K3 sector.** On a second pass, the agent identified a K3 sector, which the authors confirmed.
- **$\varepsilon$-factorized form.** It then reached full $\varepsilon$-factorized form in ~4 days with no dedicated skill. That change of basis uses 8 elliptic periods, 3 K3 periods and 108 DE-defined functions.

Total list-price costs were ~\$1.8k and ~\$1.0k.

Caveats:
- The five-point DE exists only on slices; its full kinematic reconstruction is outstanding.
- Neither basis is optimized for evaluation.
- Humans intervened once substantively: the agent wrongly concluded two blocks couldn't be factorized because it held higher-sector bases fixed.

### [arXiv:2611.27716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27716) — Challenging the Körner–Pati–Woo Theorem in Charmed Baryon Decays (Di Wang et al.)

KPW+SU(3)$_F$ links $\Lambda_c^+\to\Xi^0K^+$ to $\Xi_c^0\to pK^-$. Using BESIII partial waves, the relation predicts $\mathcal B(\Xi_c^0\to pK^-)$ at $17.2\pm6.0$ times LHCb's measured value. A five-observable joint fit gives $\chi^2_{\min}=45.2$ for 2 dof, a 6.3σ discrepancy. The fit has to drag $\mathcal B(\Lambda_c^+\to\Xi^0K^+)$ from $5.5$ to $1.38\times10^{-3}$, a 5.9σ pull.

Rescuing KPW requires large SU(3)$_F$ breaking:
- **$pK^-$ channel:** S/P-wave magnitude shifts of at least 76% and relative phases of at least 17.8°.
- **Decuplet sector:** predicted $\alpha(\Lambda_c^+\to\Delta^{++}K^-)\in[-0.91,-0.83]$ against a measured $+0.55\pm0.04$, a 16–20σ sign flip. Fixing it needs a P–D phase shift of at least 93.5°.

The theoretical argument is qualitative. Colour-sextet diquarks embedded in larger singlets, via gluons or baryon–meson colour space, evade the valence-colour projection.

Caveats:
- BESIII also allows an S-wave-dominated $\Xi^0K^+$ solution.
- Branching fractions alone give a wider ratio range, $[5.6\pm1.5,\,18.7\pm5.0]$.
- The companion $\Sigma^+K^-$ test depends on the disputed $\Xi_c^0\to\Xi^-\pi^+$ normalization.

The actionable output is a set of isospin-only KPW relations, where breaking is expected at ~1%. These cover eight $\Lambda_c^+$ modes and include the requirement $\Delta^+\eta^{(\prime)}=0$. One already looks strained: the predicted $\mathcal B(\Delta^+K_S^0)=(2.99\pm0.13)\times10^{-3}$ sits above BESIII's $<2.54\times10^{-3}$.

*Full text for the Barenboim et al. ([arXiv:2611.27985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27985)) and König et al. ([arXiv:2611.26718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26718)) picks was not supplied, so no deep dive is given for them.*
