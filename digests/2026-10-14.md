## IceCube closes Planck-suppressed decoherence

IceCube's decoherence search [arXiv:2610.14144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14144) is the day's cleanest result. It uses 368k atmospheric neutrinos at 0.5–100 TeV, an order of magnitude higher in energy than earlier searches, and finds nothing. It excludes natural Planck-suppressed decoherence for every $E^n$ scaling with $0\le n\le3$, the first Planck-scale exclusion at $n=3$. Separately, LZ model-building rose to five papers, after last week's one or two a day. Flavor proceedings say the $R(D^{(*)})$ world-average tension is still there despite last month's Belle II easing.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2610.14144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14144) — Planck-Scale Constraints on Quantum Gravity-Induced Neutrino Decoherence with IceCube (R. Abbasi et al.)

IceCube sees no oscillation damping in 368,071 up-going $\nu_\mu$ events (10.7 yr, $E_\text{reco}$ 0.5–100 TeV). The sample is the same as the sterile-neutrino analysis, now at 99.9% purity and split into starting and throughgoing tracks. The decoherence fits use a Lindblad operator with a single rate $\Gamma(E)=\Gamma_0(E/1\,\text{TeV})^n$, tested in two forms: "state selection" (all eight off-identity entries $\Gamma$) and "phase perturbation." The 90% CL limits at $E_0=1$ TeV are $\Gamma_0<2.4\times10^{-15}$, $4.6\times10^{-16}$, $7.3\times10^{-19}$ and $6.1\times10^{-21}$ eV for $n=0$–3. Against the 2024 IceCube result (quoted at $E_0=1$ GeV), the gains are about 13× at $n=2$ and about 30× at $n=3$. At $n=3$ the limit falls below the natural expectation $\Gamma_0=E_0^n/M_\text{Pl}^{n-1}$ for the first time, so a cubic-scaling effect would need extra suppression or a non-power-law form. The $n=2$ case matters most for theory, because it is the scaling found in string-motivated black-hole and D-foam calculations.

Caveats:
- **$n=3$ margin.** Evaluating the paper's formula at $E_0=1$ GeV gives about $6.7\times10^{-30}$ eV for the natural expectation, against a limit of $6.05\times10^{-30}$. That is a marginal crossing, not a deep exclusion.
- **$n=0$.** The new limit is actually looser than 2024 ($2.36$ vs $1.17\times10^{-15}$ eV).
- **Model assumptions.** CPT conservation is assumed, since IceCube cannot separate $\nu$ from $\bar\nu$. The $D$ matrix is diagonal, and decoherence is taken as independent of matter density.
- **Fit quality.** Pre-unblinding goodness-of-fit p-values were only 4–5%.

### [arXiv:2610.17177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17177) — MadNIS, Ready for Production at LO (Theo Heimel et al.)

MadNIS now covers full $pp$ processes with all their partonic subprocesses, and it runs untuned from defaults set by a three-iteration VEGAS survey. It will be MadGraph7's LO default. The main additions are:
- learned sampling of symmetry permutations, and of flavour with a PDF prior;
- merging of matrix elements, channels and, optionally, subprocesses;
- buffered training on partially unweighted events;
- concurrent training and generation on GPU.

On an H100 with training excluded ($\kappa_\infty$), MadNIS beats VEGAS on every benchmark, by 1.27× ($t\bar t$ dileptonic) up to 247× ($\ell\ell+3j$). Distributions match a sliced MG5aMC baseline in pulls, including the tails.

The amortization numbers the digest flagged look like this for $t\bar t+nj$ on an A100. Break-even with VEGAS comes at $9\times10^6$, $1.5\times10^5$ and $6\times10^3$ events for $n=1,2,3$, after 8 s, 11 s and 1.4 min of training. At $10^3$ iterations, the speed-ups are already 2.9, 8.9 and 16.

Caveats:
- **Training cost of the headline numbers.** The process-zoo figures come from trainings of up to 6.7–8.8 h.
- **Retraining.** Any change of process, cuts or parameters requires a new training.
- **CPU.** CPU training is more than 10× slower in wall-clock time.
- **Open choices.** When to merge subprocesses is unresolved, and the defaults are "likely to evolve" (alpha release v0.2.1).
- **Low multiplicity.** At low multiplicity, VEGAS combined with MadSpace mappings already captures most of the gain.

### [arXiv:2610.15248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15248) — When hadrons collide: di-$J/\psi$ structure in the final state (Eric S. Swanson (University of Pittsburgh))

Swanson gets a $\chi^2/\text{ndf}=56/50$ fit to the LHCb di-$J/\psi$ spectrum (6.2–7.7 GeV) without introducing any states. The production model is a compact octet–octet $cc\bar c\bar c$ source in $2^{++}$, rescattering through ten charmonium-pair channels. Quark-exchange kernels come from a constituent model whose parameters are fixed by the charmonium spectrum, and the scattering is solved to all orders with a Kohn–Hulthén–Kato resonating-group method.

The structures map onto thresholds:
- a $J/\psi J/\psi$ threshold enhancement in the $X(6600)$ region;
- a dip from flux lost to the $J/\psi\psi(2S)$ channel, followed by a peak at 6.9 GeV;
- a 7.2 GeV shoulder between the $J/\psi\psi(3S)$ and $\psi(2S)\psi(2S)$ thresholds.

Only three background parameters are free ($a$, $c$, $\lambda$), with DPS fixed to LHCb's template. The fit needs both an incoherent remainder ($c=0$ gives 90/51) and a corrected continuum slope ($\lambda=0$ gives 152/51). The amplitude has poles at $6809-68i$ and $7102-57i$ MeV. Swanson argues these reflect a repulsive-shell/attractive-core interaction rather than tetraquarks.

Caveats:
- **CMS fit.** CMS is fitted less well (117/55), and its dips are deeper than the model's.
- **Missing and spurious features.** The model undershoots the 6.9 GeV peak by 15–20% and predicts an unobserved $\psi(2S)\psi(2S)$ bump near 7.4 GeV.
- **Omitted physics.** Quark annihilation is left out, which removes absorption into $D^{(*)}\bar D^{(*)}$. $J/\psi\psi(3770)$ is excluded because it couples only through tensor forces.
- **$\Upsilon\Upsilon$ test.** The prediction gives broad features at 19.3 and 19.7 GeV and a sharp dip near 20.05 GeV. Because an earlier loop-cusp model predicted the same enhancements, this tests threshold rescattering in general, not this model specifically.
