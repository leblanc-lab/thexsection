## Flavor, precision not tension

After a week where the flavor front ran on anomalies, today it runs on consolidation. LHCb posts an improved $\phi_s$ from $B^0_s\to J/\psi\pi^+\pi^-$ ([arXiv:2608.14119](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14119)), landing squarely on the SM; Huang et al. [arXiv:2608.14341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14341) supply the last missing factorization ingredient for $b\to s\ell\ell$ at leading power — the theory-side counterpart to the $C_9$ story that peaked mid-week; and a kaon global fit ([arXiv:2608.14474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14474)) folds in the new $K^+\to\pi^+\nu\bar\nu$ and LFU observables. The standout curiosity is elsewhere: Alves et al. [arXiv:2608.13736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13736) argue neutrino oscillations are not blind to absolute mass after all. A lighter day overall, weighted toward theory and tooling.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2608.13736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13736) — Beyond Δm²: Absolute Mass Sensitivity in Neutrino Oscillations (Gustavo F. S. Alves et al.)

The headline is conceptual: the oscillation phase, expanded beyond leading order in mᵢ/E, contains a term ∝ (mᵢ⁴−mⱼ⁴)L/E₀³ = Δmᵢⱼ²(mᵢ²+mⱼ²)L/E₀³, so oscillations *do* carry absolute-mass information through the mass-squared *sum*. The authors show this cannot be recovered from naive plane-wave prescriptions — equal-energy, equal-momentum, and various t↔L replacements disagree at NLO — and derive it properly with a wave-packet treatment that fixes each eigenstate's mean energy/momentum from beta-decay kinematics (an effective two-body decay N_A→(N_B e)+ν̄). The production-dependent correction enters only at the per-mille level (E₀/m_A ≲ 10⁻⁴). Applied to JUNO's 52.5 km reactor baseline, a χ² fit to ~9.5×10⁴ IBD events over six years yields sensitivity m₁ < 305 keV (1σ), 434 keV (2σ) with oscillation parameters profiled; the solar channel dominates because its broad dip lands in JUNO's high-statistics band and survives energy smearing intact.

Why it matters: this overturns the textbook statement that oscillations measure only Δm². It is a genuinely new, mechanism-independent absolute-mass probe, sensitive to a different combination than beta decay (m_νe), 0νββ (m_ββ), or cosmology (Σmᵢ). The honest caveat is scale: at few-hundred-keV, this is ~10⁴× weaker than KATRIN (0.45 eV) and ~10⁷× weaker than cosmology (0.053 eV), and the sensitivity is limited by a degeneracy in which the NLO shift mimics a rescaling Δm₂₁²→Δm₂₁²(1+m₁²/2E₀²), broken only by the 1/E₀² energy dependence.

### [arXiv:2608.14341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14341) — Enhanced Three-Particle Contribution to Electroweak Penguin B-Meson Decays (Yong-Kang Huang et al.)

Using SCET with two-step QCD→SCET_I→SCET_II matching, the authors compute for the first time the subleading-twist correction to B→{K,π}ℓ⁺ℓ⁻ from the three-particle twist-three B-meson distribution amplitude at NLO, plus new NNLO corrections to the spectator-scattering (C-type) and weak-annihilation topologies and the O(α_s) D-type spectator term. This is billed as the last missing ingredient for a complete leading-power factorization proof of the b→{s,d}ℓ⁺ℓ⁻ matrix element. The three-particle convolution integrals over the two soft momenta are shown to converge, and the new jet function J_C^(3P) is validated by a second, method-of-regions calculation. Numerically, the newly included leading-power effects shift both real and imaginary parts of the top-sector amplitude T_π⁰ by O(30%) in q²∈[1.5, 4.0] GeV², driven by cancellation among A- and B-type pieces; the largest phenomenological impact is on the direct-CP-asymmetry differences ΔA_CP(B→Pℓℓ), shifted 15–20% at large recoil, where the nontrivial strong phase of J_C^(3P) dominates.

Why it matters: this is the theory-side counterpart to the week's C₉ story — tightening the leading-power factorization control that any short-distance NP interpretation of b→sℓℓ requires. The honest caveat is that it does not resolve the anomaly: branching fractions for B⁰→K⁰ℓℓ and B⁺→K⁺ℓℓ "remain in sharp tension" with Belle/CDF/LHCb/CMS at large recoil, and the new B⁺→π⁺ℓℓ predictions actually favor old LHCb data over the latest full-Run-1+2 result (1.1–2.5σ).

### [arXiv:2608.14119](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14119) — Improved measurement of CP violation in B⁰ₛ→J/ψπ⁺π⁻ decays (LHCb collaboration et al.)

LHCb performs a time-dependent, flavour-tagged amplitude analysis of ~94,000 B⁰ₛ→J/ψπ⁺π⁻ signal decays from the full Run 2 6 fb⁻¹ sample (38 fs decay-time resolution, 4.5% tagging power), extracting φₛ = −0.077 ± 0.034 ± 0.007 rad, |λ| = 0.993 ± 0.026 ± 0.007, and Γ_H = 0.610 ± 0.002 ± 0.004 ps⁻¹. The π⁺π⁻ spectrum is modelled with six resonances plus a nonresonant term — adding an f₂(1565) contribution to a small ~1.6 GeV peak relative to earlier work — with the CP-odd f₀(980) carrying 72.5% fit fraction. Combining with Run 1 gives φₛ = −0.046 ± 0.031 rad; folding in all other LHCb channels yields φₛ = −0.041 ± 0.017 rad (χ²/ndf = 35/30), consistent with the J/ψφ average and improving precision by ~15%.

Why it matters: against a week where the flavour front ran on anomalies, this is a clean null. The result sits squarely on the SM prediction φₛ^SM = −0.0376₋₀.₀₀₀₅⁺⁰·⁰⁰⁰⁶ rad, and no direct CP violation is seen (|λ| compatible with 1). The main caveat is explicit in the text: subleading penguin contributions are neglected throughout — externally constrained to a Δφₛ = 5.0 ± 4.6 mrad shift from B⁰→J/ψρ⁰ — and the production asymmetry A_P is fixed to zero (a ±2% variation is taken as systematic). Systematics remain subdominant to the 34 mrad statistical uncertainty on φₛ.
