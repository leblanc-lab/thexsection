## Gell-Mann's weak magnetism, seen in the neutron

Camarasa-Domene et al. [arXiv:2610.26792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26792) separate the weak-magnetism form factor from $\lambda$ by combining β-asymmetry data with the neutron lifetime and superallowed $\mathcal{F}t$ values. They get $g_M=4.47(87)$ against the CVC prediction of 4.71, and exclude zero at 5.1σ. The significance stays between 5.0 and 6.1σ across variations of the neutron dataset, despite its known internal tensions. This is a reanalysis of existing data rather than a new measurement, but it is a clean one. Elsewhere, IceCube delivers precision rather than limits, the Galactic Center excess meets the dwarfs, and LZ model-building returns after yesterday's zero.

### The Galactic Center, and LZ again
Kim et al. [arXiv:2610.26648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26648) test 18 years of Fermi data against 80 diffuse models. In their best-fit model, limits from 30 dwarf spheroidals exclude the 68% GCE regions for $b\bar b$, $c\bar c$, light quarks, gluons, $4b$ and $2b2\tau$. Only τ-rich channels and light-mediator $4b$ cascades survive. Last week Abazajian et al. argued against a shared origin for the halo excess and the LZ event. Kim et al. [arXiv:2610.25423](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25423) build exactly that shared origin anyway: in their exothermic dark-Higgs model, the 26 GeV dark-photon branch matches the southern-sky GCE, and CMB limits exclude the 1 GeV branch at its likelihood minimum. Tan et al. [arXiv:2610.25925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25925) find a 1.85 GeV $[ud][ds][su]$ hexaquark for the invisible-dibaryon reading of LZ, which they concede depends on the OPE truncation.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2610.26792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26792) — First observation of weak magnetism in neutron decay (Miriam Camarasa-Domene et al.)

The weak-magnetism form factor comes out as $g_M = 4.47(87)$, which excludes $g_M=0$ at 5.1σ and sits 0.3σ from the CVC value of 4.71. The β asymmetry alone cannot separate $g_M$ from $\lambda$. The authors fix $\lambda = -1.27598(61)$ independently, using $r_n\tau_n(1+3\lambda^2)=2\mathcal{F}t$ with:
- $\tau_n=878.36(43)$ s, the PDG bottle average with $S=1.81$;
- $\mathcal{F}t=3070.01(1.90)$ s from $^{26m}$Al alone, reanalyzed with updated charge radii.

They then reinterpret the asymmetry data as an effective $\tilde A$ that carries explicit $g_M$ dependence. Two refinements matter:
- They evaluate the energy moments over each experiment's actual window rather than the full phase space. This raises the $g_M$ sensitivity relative to the earlier 3σ global fit.
- The linearized version, $g_M = 4.40(50)_{\mathcal{F}t}(39)_{\tau_n}(59)_{\tilde A}$, shows that all three inputs contribute comparably to the error.

The robustness checks are the main content. The significance ranges across dataset choices as follows:

| Dataset choice | $g_M$ | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Lifetime set including beam values ($S=2.3$) | 4.63(92) | 5.0σ |
| Adding the $a$, $B$, $\lambda_{AB}$ correlations | 4.85(85) | 5.7σ |
| PERKEO III + UCNτ only | 4.68(81) | 5.8σ |
| Post-2012 asymmetries only (no scale factor needed) | 5.00(82) | 6.1σ |

Fitting the spectral shape of $A(E_e)$ would tighten $\sigma(g_M)$ by only 3–7%, based on Asimov studies. A recast of the PERKEO III Fierz likelihood gives a consistent 5.07(92).

Caveats:
- The baseline $\mathcal{F}t$ rests on a single transition. It carries a $\delta_{\rm NS}$ error (1.44 s) that is comparable to its statistical error.
- The $\lambda$ determination relies on the SM relation between $\tau_n$ and $\mathcal{F}t$. The result is therefore a consistency test within the SM, not a model-independent extraction.

### [arXiv:2610.24068](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24068) — Measurement of the neutrino cross section across the TeV scale using IceCube data (Rasmi Hajjar et al.)

The analysis uses only the public eight-year IceCube upgoing-track sample: 305,735 events, more than 99.9% pure $\nu_\mu$ CC. It extracts the average ν/ν̄ CC cross section in five bins from 150 GeV to 40 TeV. Earth absorption and the event energy distribution break the flux–cross-section degeneracy. Relative to CSMS, the normalizations are:

| Bin | Normalization |
|---|---|
| 0.15–0.36 TeV | $1.11^{+0.12}_{-0.13}$ |
| 0.36–1.17 TeV | $1.22^{+0.24}_{-0.17}$ |
| 1.17–3.79 TeV | $1.11^{+0.15}_{-0.12}$ |
| 3.79–12.3 TeV | $0.92^{+0.15}_{-0.12}$ |
| 12.3–40 TeV | $1.01^{+0.15}_{-0.13}$ |

All bins are within 20% and consistent with the SM. This bridges the energy-resolved collider points (up to about 2 TeV) and the astrophysical ones (above about 10 TeV). Between 360 GeV and 2 TeV the uncertainties already beat FASER's.

Freeing ν and ν̄ separately gives a non-zero ν̄ cross section above 360 GeV at only 1.6σ (p = 0.06). That drops to 1.1σ if $\sigma_\nu>\sigma_{\bar\nu}$ is enforced, because it is partly driven by an anticorrelated swap in bin 4 (ν: 0.40, ν̄: 2.1).

The main caveat for anyone treating this as a reference curve is the first bin. It carries a 16% Gaussian prior from fixed-target data, and that prior anchors the atmospheric flux normalization. Without it:
- the fit prefers a lower flux;
- the other four bins shift up by 20–30%;
- the first bin lands about 2σ above CSMS.

So the result is limited by the flux–cross-section anticorrelation and is partly calibrated by accelerator input. A tenfold exposure forecast gives under 10% on the average cross section and more than 7σ for ν̄.

### [arXiv:2610.26648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26648) — Dark Matter Interpretations of the Galactic Center Gamma-Ray Excess with 18 Years of Fermi-LAT Data and the FL16Y Source Catalog (Sang Hwan Kim et al.)

The GCE is re-extracted from 17.7 years of FRONT-only data with FL16Y masks, scanning 80 GALPROP diffuse models. Model X fits best, ahead of the next model by $\Delta(-2\ln\mathcal L)=72$. Systematics come from a full 14×14 covariance built from 22 Galactic-plane control regions.

Against new combined limits from 30 dwarfs, with J-factor uncertainties profiled, the picture is:
- **$b\bar b$ and other hadronic channels:** the best fit is (39.5 GeV, $1.2\times10^{-26}$ cm³/s) with χ² = 27.5/12. Its entire 68% region lies above the dSph limit, as do those for $c\bar c$, light quarks and gluons.
- **Effective four-body channels:** the 68% regions for $4b$ and $2b2\tau$ are fully excluded.
- **$\tau^+\tau^-$:** the best fit (9.3 GeV, χ² = 22.8/12) sits just 0.67% above the limit, and part of its 68% region survives.
- **$4\tau$:** the best fit is 8.7% above the limit, and part of its 68% region survives.
- **$4b$ cascades:** these fit best near $r=m_\phi/m_\chi\simeq0.3$. Per the abstract, part of the 68% region stays below the limits for $r\lesssim0.4$.

AMS-02 antiprotons are generally weaker than the dwarfs and constrain only part of the $4b$ cascade region. That antiproton likelihood uses the BIG propagation model and includes only the experimental covariance; adding propagation-model uncertainties could weaken it further.

For GCE model-building, including today's dark-Higgs construction, this leaves a τ-rich or light-mediator corridor.

Caveats are substantial:
- The exclusions are conditional on Model X and a fixed γ = 1.2 gNFW J-factor.
- The 80-model envelope, a factor 3–4 above 1 GeV, is shown but not propagated.
- Even the best fits have χ²/dof ≈ 2.
- Extending the dwarf data from 14.3 to 17.7 years weakens the high-mass limits for every catalog tested, and the authors do not yet understand why.
