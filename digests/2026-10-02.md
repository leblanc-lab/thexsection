## Fractional determinants, two uses

Pisarski [arXiv:2610.00472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00472) proposes that below $T_\chi$ the topological charge is fractional in units of $1/N_c$. Fractional powers of $\det\Phi$ then enter the chiral Lagrangian. The consequence is a chiral transition that is generically second order, with exceptions at $N_f=1$ and $N_f=3$, and the prediction is testable with 2+1 lattice data. Giacosa et al. apply the same device to glueball decays the same day. The rest of the day is spread thin: LZ falls to three papers, all at high mass scales, and Drell-Yan gets a lattice-fed precision upgrade. A second paper in three days also argues against Dark Dimension graviton phenomenology.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2610.00472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00472) — Fractional anomalous determinants and the chiral phase transition (Robert D. Pisarski)

Pisarski proposes a "syncretic" chiral Lagrangian whose anomalous sector changes across $T_\chi$. Below $T_\chi$, topological charge is taken to be fractional in units of $1/N_c$, so terms $\mathcal F_j(\Phi^\dagger\Phi)\,[(\det\Phi)^{j/N_c}+\text{h.c.}]$ appear. The prefactors are fractional powers of $\det\Phi^\dagger\Phi$, chosen so that each term behaves like $\Phi^2$ as $\Phi\to0$. Above $T_\chi$, only integer powers survive. The argument rests on assuming that in the Veneziano limit the free energy depends on $\theta/N_c$. This yields a Witten–Veneziano generalization to arbitrary $N_f/N_c$ in which the $\eta'$ stays massive at $T_\chi$ without a large $\xi_1\det\Phi$. The predicted transition order by flavor number:
- **$N_f=1$:** crossover ($Z(2)$-like).
- **$N_f=2$:** second order, $O(4)$.
- **$N_f=3$:** still first order because the cubic $\det\Phi$ remains, but possibly very weakly so, since $\xi_1$ can be small.
- **$N_f=4$:** a $\mathcal Q=2/3$ term could induce a weak, Halperin–Lubensky–Ma-type first-order transition.

The model is motivated by lattice data that find no first-order signal for $N_f=3$. One example is Zhang et al.'s susceptibility scaling exponent $\alpha\approx0.39\pm0.04$, which fits neither first order ($\alpha=3$) nor $Z(2)$ ($\alpha\approx1.96$). Another is the measured $b_2\approx-0.19/N_c^2$, read here as a dense liquid of fractional instantons. The proposed test is to fit the 2+1-flavor ratio $\mathcal R_{\pi,a_0}(T)$ to $\sigma_s^{4/3},\sigma_s^{2/3},\sigma_s$ between 150 and 300 MeV.

Caveats are substantial, and the author flags them himself:
- The $\mathcal F_j$ are unknown, and the ansatz is called "rank supposition." FRG work already indicates additional invariants are needed.
- The non-degenerate case relevant to the proposed lattice test is deferred to a separate paper.
- QCD is "blithely" assumed close to the Veneziano limit.

### [arXiv:2610.02169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02169) — Drell–Yan lepton pair production from low to high transverse momentum (Beno\^it Assi et al.)

The prediction is N³LO matched to approximate N⁴LL′ $q_T$ resummation, with unknown four-loop boundary terms estimated in large-$\beta_0$. Its main novelty is the nonperturbative input. The Collins–Soper kernel is taken from continuum-limit lattice QCD over $0.15\lesssim b_T\lesssim0.8$ fm, giving $c_0=0.032\pm0.012$ GeV² at $B_{NP}=2$ GeV⁻¹. The only parameter fitted to Drell–Yan data is a Gaussian width, $\kappa_{NP}=3.0\pm0.5$ GeV⁻².

Comparison with ATLAS 13 TeV data:
- **Agreement:** deviations are at most 1.3% below 10 GeV, 2.0% for 10–30 GeV, and 3.5% above 30 GeV, with χ²/bin of 0.2, 1.0 and 1.6 in those regions.
- **Lattice uncertainty:** the $c_0$ uncertainty contributes up to 2.6% in the first bin and at most 0.8% elsewhere.

The calculation is transferred to event samples by maximum-entropy reweighting. Of 170 tabulated moments in $r_T$ and $\phi_{\rm acop}$, 14–19 are selected, giving event-local multipliers $\propto\exp(\sum\lambda_j g_j)$ that are strictly positive.
- **Sherpa:** the reweighted sample matches the calculation to about 2% in a typical bin, against a prior that was off by up to 40%. The effective sample size is essentially unchanged (7.6% → 7.3%).
- **POWHEG:** agreement is about 3% in a typical bin, with effective sample size held at 99%. The largest deviation from data below 200 GeV falls from 19% to 6%.

Caveats:
- The prediction is about 8× less precise than the data in a typical bin, and PDF uncertainties (a few percent) are assessed elsewhere.
- The moment covariance is treated as diagonal.
- Positivity applies to the multiplier; the Sherpa prior itself retains signed weights.
- Between 200 and 900 GeV the ungated POWHEG weight overshoots the data by 4–40%.
- Extension to $W$ production is proposed but not done, which matters for any $m_W$ application.

### [arXiv:2610.01825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01825) — Near Threshold Kaluza-Klein Graviton Decays and the Dark Dimension (Kevin Langhoff)

On shell, the massive cubic KK-graviton vertex reduces to the double copy of the Yang–Mills vertex evaluated at massive momenta. The mass term cancels against the $\Box$ terms, and the cancellation follows from the field redefinition $h=e^H-1$. As a result the amplitude vanishes at threshold, and the decay is pure $d$-wave with $\Gamma\propto p^5\propto Q^{5/2}$. Relative to the $s$-wave rate assumed in the dark-dimension cascade literature, the suppression is $\sim(Q/\mu)^2\sim10^{-24}$ for $Q\sim1$ meV and GeV parents.

Consequences for the flat dark dimension:
- **Cascade:** summing over a flat tower with the completeness bound on $g_{mn\ell}$ saturated, the mass-distribution peak moves by less than 0.1% by matter–radiation equality ($\delta n=100$, $R=0.05$–$0.2\,\mu$m).
- **Dark matter:** GeV-scale gravitons then live only $\sim10^5$ yr, which excludes KK gravitons as the dark matter.
- **Irreducible background:** freeze-in still produces $\rho_h/\rho_{\rm DM}=1.3\times10^{-6}(T_{\rm RH}/5\,{\rm MeV})^3(m_{\rm KK}/5\,{\rm meV})^{-1}$. COMPTEL and EGRET MeV gamma-ray data then cap $T_{\rm RH}$.
- **Combined bound:** with $T_{\rm RH}\ge5.96$ MeV from BBN+CMB+BAO, a viable window survives only for $R\lesssim0.2\,\mu$m ($m_{\rm KK}\gtrsim1$ eV). That is roughly three orders of magnitude below the $R\sim\Lambda^{-1/4}$ expectation.

Caveats: the analysis covers flat or weakly warped geometries only, and the cascade estimate uses a saturated coupling bound. The threshold suppression was found independently by Lee, Randall and Riojas (the Wednesday paper). A factor-10 improvement in MeV telescopes such as AMEGO-X or e-ASTROGAM would tighten the bound further.

### [arXiv:2609.06153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06153) — Exothermic dark matter and the 248 keV nuclear recoil in LUX-ZEPLIN (Howard Baer et al.)

Baer and Barger propose the downscattering alternative to the endothermic Higgsino reading. In their model, the relic is the heavier state of a pseudo-Dirac pair split by |δ| ≃ 350 keV. Because there is no threshold speed, the predicted count shifts by only 0.3% as v_esc runs from 500 to 600 km/s. The endothermic count, by contrast, swings from zero to 143× its central value.

Several constraints follow from the setup:
- **Mediator.** Weak-strength coupling overproduces the event by ~10⁶, which forces a dark-photon mediator.
- **Splitting.** Longevity of the excited state requires |δ| < 2mₑ.
- **Excited fraction.** A coupled-channel calculation (the Born approximation violates unitarity here) gives an excited fraction of 2×10⁻³ to 4×10⁻², which implies ε ~ 10⁻⁶ at a 1 GeV mediator.
- **Ground state.** Upscattering of the ground state excludes |δ| ≲ 300 keV, so the endothermic readings appear as the small-splitting limit of the same model.

The headline test is argon: it predicts ~4–6 events per tonne-year near 250 keV, where the endothermic reading is forbidden at any exposure. It also predicts percent-level rather than near-total annual modulation.

At 44 citations since its September posting, it has likely become the default citation for the exothermic branch. It is one of three exothermic treatments the authors identify, alongside many endothermic ones. It also supplies the quantitative halo-sensitivity argument against endpoint tuning and a sharp discriminator that DEAP-3600 and DarkSide-50 could apply to existing data.
