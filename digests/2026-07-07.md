## The vibe today

A neutrino-heavy day with a strong precision-theory undertow. If there was a single conversation dominating the boards, it was the one the memory has been tracking all week: as JUNO, DUNE, and Hyper-Kamiokande approach reality, the community is increasingly anxious about what could *mimic* a genuine CP-violating signal. Today that worry arrived in triplicate — non-unitarity, Lorentz-invariance violation, and quasi-Dirac splittings each get their own long-baseline study, and each concludes, in its own way, that the standard three-flavor picture is fragile in exactly the places DUNE hopes to measure. This is no longer a boutique concern; it reads like a coordinated stress test of the entire CP-discovery program.

The other half of the day belongs to the ongoing project of tending QCD's foundations — hadron spectroscopy, molecular states, femtoscopic correlation functions, and the machinery of precision predictions. Here the mood is one of consolidation rather than surprise: a comprehensive instanton-vacuum framework, resonance-aware QCD+EW matching, a new fast library for gluon-initiated processes, and a fresh attempt to wrestle the muon $g-2$ hadronic contribution out of mutually incompatible $e^+e^-$ datasets. And, quietly notable, ATLAS lands the *second* GN2 calibration paper of the week — the transformer wave keeps rolling.

Underneath both currents, the day carried an unusual amount of reflection about the field itself: a global data-preservation report, a multi-agent framework for auditing AI-generated analysis code, and a historical essay on the birth of particle cosmology. A thoughtful, infrastructure-minded day rather than a bombshell one.

## Themes

### Neutrino oscillation degeneracies & fake CP

The week's "fake CP" thread crests today. Singh et al. ([arXiv:2607.06108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06108)) — already flagged in the memory as a benchmark — show that PMNS non-unitarity can generate sizeable CP asymmetries even at CP-conserving $\delta_{CP}$, exceeding the genuine signal (~130%) in specific energy windows near 1.5 GeV, and demonstrate that a joint DUNE + Hyper-K analysis shrinks the degenerate $(\delta_{CP},\varphi_{21})$ space by up to 16×. Deka et al. ([arXiv:2607.06513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06513)) run the parallel exercise for Standard-Model-Extension LIV coefficients, finding that $a_{ee}$, $a_{\tau\tau}$, $a_{e\mu}$, $a_{e\tau}$ can drag DUNE's CP discovery below $3\sigma$ over a large fraction of $\delta_{CP}$. And Boudjema et al. ([arXiv:2607.06862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06862)) add the quasi-Dirac scenario — nearly degenerate sterile pairs from small lepton-number violation — constrained now with NOvA and T2K and forecast for DUNE. Three different new-physics dials, one shared conclusion: the CP measurement is only as clean as your assumptions about what else is in the Hamiltonian. This is the direct continuation of last week's degeneracy-breaking theme, and the answer keeps being "combine detectors."

On the experimental side, BESIII reports first observation of $J/\psi,\psi(3686)\to p\bar p K^0_S K^0_S$, with the branching-ratio double flavoring theory by 4.6σ ([arXiv:2607.06070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06070)).

### Precision QCD+EW machinery for HL-LHC

The "sub-percent precision demands joint frameworks" storyline from last week gets concrete tooling. Flower et al. present the first automated MC@NLO matching of NLO QCD+EW to an interleaved QCD+QED shower, with a resonance-aware modification for Drell-Yan ([arXiv:2607.06059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06059)) — squarely in the lineage of the Lepton-Distribution-Function push the memory noted. ### Axions doing double duty

Two papers push single axions to carry multiple cosmological burdens. Delaunay et al. build a technically natural apparent-phantom dark energy from a $\mathbb{Z}_N$-axion coupled to $N$ dark-QCD copies, simultaneously setting the DM relic abundance and reproducing DESI's evolving-$w$ preference ([arXiv:2607.06774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06774)). Unwin shows an intermediate-scale R-axion can evade the DFK Planckian-$f_R$ bound via a mixed uplift and, if the R-symmetry carries a QCD anomaly, double as the QCD axion with $f_R\sim10^{11}$ GeV yielding the observed DM ([arXiv:2607.06375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06375)). Both echo last week's habit of leaning on discrete symmetries to solve several problems at once.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2607.06108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06108) — Non-Unitarity Effects and Fake CP Violation in Neutrino Oscillation Experiments

Using GLoBES with a custom non-unitary probability engine, Singh et al. quantify how a single off-diagonal PMNS deviation $\alpha_{21}=|\alpha_{21}|e^{i\varphi_{21}}$ fakes CP violation at DUNE and Hyper-K. The headline: at each experiment's oscillation maximum the fake asymmetry is a mild perturbation (1.7% of the genuine swing at DUNE's 2.4 GeV, 4.5% at Hyper-K's 0.5 GeV for $|\alpha_{21}|=0.01$), but in suppressed-genuine windows near 1.5–1.6 GeV the fake-to-genuine ratio exceeds unity — 129% at DUNE, 134% at Hyper-K, with a second 129% window for Hyper-K at 2.0 GeV. In these bins an $A_{CP}$-only analysis cannot separate real from fake CP, and the sign of $\varphi_{21}$ is invisible in the broad-peak region. The degeneracy analysis shows Hyper-K alone leaves $\varphi_{21}$ essentially unconstrained at $\ge2\sigma$ (matter effects too weak to reject the wrong ordering, $\chi^2_{\rm min}=4.4$), while DUNE's 1300 km matter lever arm pins it ($\chi^2_{\rm min}=486$). Their combination shrinks the $3\sigma$ allowed $(\delta_{CP},\varphi_{21})$ area to $0.059\pi^2$ — 16.3× smaller than Hyper-K alone.

Why it matters: this is the week's reference point for "fake CP." It concretely localizes where non-unitarity is dangerous (specific energy bins, not the oscillation maximum) and confirms the recurring prescription — combine complementary baselines rather than trusting either detector. Caveat: only the minimal single-parameter $\alpha_{21}=0.01$ scenario is explored, and the combined $3\sigma$ area is marginally *larger* than DUNE alone, an honestly-flagged artifact of Hyper-K's flat directions overlapping DUNE's contour edge.

### [arXiv:2607.06862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06862) — Probing Quasi-Dirac Neutrino Oscillations at Long Baseline Experiments

Boudjema, Deppisch & Pattanaik embed the quasi-Dirac scenario — small lepton-number-violating Majorana mass $\mu$ splitting a near-degenerate sterile pair — in an inverse-seesaw-motivated $3+2$ framework, deriving analytic $\nu_\mu\to\nu_e$ and $\nu_\mu\to\nu_\mu$ probabilities with the sterile splitting $\Delta m^2_{54}\propto\mu$ driving a slow interference term. Simulating NOvA, T2K and DUNE in GLoBES with full systematics, they map sensitivity in $(\theta_{sa},\Delta m^2_{54})$. Current NOvA and T2K each reach $\theta_{sa}\approx0.1$; combined, $\approx0.08$. DUNE alone dominates, probing $\theta_{sa}\approx0.05$ — roughly a factor-two improvement — with approximately *uniform* coverage across $\Delta m^2_{54}$ because its long baseline averages out the rapid sterile oscillations. NOvA/T2K instead show a resonance enhancement near $\Delta m^2_{54}\sim\Delta m^2_{31}\sim2.4\times10^{-3}$ eV², plus degeneracy valleys at $\theta_{sa}\approx\theta_{23}\approx0.4$ where the $5\nu$ model mimics $3\nu$.

Why it matters: this adds quasi-Dirac splittings to the week's "what mimics CP" catalogue, now constrained with *real* NOvA/T2K data rather than forecasts alone. A key structural result is that quasi-Dirac sensitivity is intrinsically coupled to $\delta_{CP}$: the $5\nu$ appearance probability carries a $c_{sa}$ suppression factor modulating the CP-dependent amplitude, so DUNE's quasi-Dirac and CP programs necessarily inform one another. Caveats: flavour-universal sterile mixing ($\theta_{sa}$ common to all angles) and vanishing sterile CP phases are assumed throughout — both acknowledged as simplifications that could resolve or deepen the identified degeneracies. Current NOvA/T2K cannot exclude the scenario across benchmark phases and orderings.

### [arXiv:2607.06059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06059) — A resonance-aware MC@NLO QCD+EW-matched calculation of lepton-pair production

Flower, Roper & Schönherr present the first automated MC@NLO matching of NLO QCD+EW to an *interleaved* QCD+QED parton shower, built on Catani-Seymour dipoles in SHERPA. The core technical contribution is a resonance-aware modification: dipoles spanning a colour/charge-neutral $s$-channel resonance are switched off (via $\Theta$-functions on emission scale $t_{\rm res}\sim\Gamma_r^2$ and virtuality measure $\Delta_r$) when an emission is hard enough to resolve it, preventing spurious recoil-induced distortions of the resonance lineshape. They also identify that standard interleaved matching leaves uncancelled $\mathcal{O}(\alpha_s\alpha)$ terms — the QCD Sudakov $\bar\Delta\ll1$ multiplying QED dipoles drives the photon $p_\perp$ spectrum *negative* over 15–40 GeV — and cure it with an extra physically-motivated Sudakov on the H-events (dubbed MC@NLO$^{\rm sud}$), at the cost of a formally higher-order shift to the inclusive cross section.

Validated against fixed-order NLO QCD+EW and against MC@NLO QCD+YFS, the method gives, for $pp\to e^+e^-$, a resonance-aware distortion of the dressed dilepton mass of a few per mille (0.2% below the $Z$ peak, 0.5% at $m_{ee}\approx60$ GeV) and a percent-level change in hard-photon $p_\perp$, rising to +15% at $p_{\perp,\gamma}\approx1$ TeV from missing ISR-FSR interference. Why it matters: Drell-Yan underpins calibration, PDF extraction and normalization, so this is likely to become standard HL-LHC precision machinery. Caveat: the resonance-aware implementation is currently process-specific (though the algorithm is general), and colour/charged resonances need new emitter/spectator splitting functions left to future work.
