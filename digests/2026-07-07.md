## The vibe today

It is a pragmatist's day on the arXiv. As the high-energy community prepares for the High-Luminosity LHC and next-generation dark matter detectors, we are seeing a shift away from wide-eyed model building toward the cold, hard business of optimization and cross-disciplinary cleanup. 

The ongoing pressure on WIMPs is no longer just a theoretical worry; it is forcing a methodical, forensic search for any loophole left in the MSSM. When theorists aren't trying to rescue naturalness, they are importing sophisticated, high-energy formal machinery—like on-shell amplitude methods—to clean up messy, low-energy nuclear physics problems that have split Big Bang Nucleosynthesis calculations for years. On the experimental side, the focus is squarely on efficiency. We are seeing a concerted effort to integrate machine learning into the earliest stages of hardware triggers and raw tracking pipelines, while simultaneously grappling with the literal, environmental cost of generating the billions of simulated events required to analyze this future data.

## Themes

### No Hiding Spot for Natural WIMPs
We have been watching the LZ direct-detection limits slowly squeeze the life out of traditional natural higgsino dark matter. While theorists have recently pivoted to discrete symmetries to generate DFSZ-type axions as the "real" dark matter (e.g., Baer et al. [arXiv:2607.07921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07921)), some had hoped that parameter-space "blind spots"—where cancellations in direct-detection couplings suppress event rates—might still salvage stable neutralinos in natural SUSY. 

Today, Baer et al. [arXiv:2607.05648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05648) close this loophole. In an exhaustive sweep of gravity-mediated NUHM2/NUHM3 models, they show that the blind spots compatible with naturalness (where the superpotential $\mu$ parameter remains near the weak scale) are already ruled out by LHC soft-dilepton searches and Higgs mass constraints. The surviving blind spots lie deep in the *unnatural* region where $\mu \gg M_{\text{gaugino}}$. The verdict is sobering: if you want electroweak naturalness, stable light higgsinos cannot be rescued by blind spots. This puts immense pressure on WIMPs and strongly points toward scenarios with unstable light higgsinos or massive entropy dilution.

### Tracking and Tagging at the Edge
The community's transition from high-level physics calibrations to low-level, high-efficiency raw reconstruction is accelerating. Following ATLAS’s recent deployment of the transformer-based GN2 flavor tagger, we now have the official performance paper by the ATLAS Collaboration [arXiv:2607.05322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05322) using Run 3 data at $\sqrt{s} = 13.6$ TeV. GN2 is a massive leap forward, offering up to a factor of two to three rejection of light/charm jets compared to its predecessor, DL1d. 

But flavor tagging is only as good as the tracks fed into it, and HL-LHC pileup threatens to swamp classical tracking algorithms with spurious combinations. Addressing this, Varni et al. [arXiv:2607.04723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04723) present a hybrid tracking pipeline that pairs the fast, geometry-driven Hough transform with convolutional neural networks. By feeding the Hough parameter space directly into a CNN without intermediate data transformations, they suppress false track candidates on the fly under realistic HL-LHC pileup. This hybrid model preserves the predictable speed of classical geometric methods while exploiting the classification power of neural networks.

### Amplitude Methods and Functional QCD Meet Low-Energy Nuclear Physics
In a beautiful example of theoretical cross-pollination, tools developed for high-energy scattering and formal field theory are being deployed to resolve long-standing headaches in low-energy nuclear physics. 

A prime example is Tait [arXiv:2607.05514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05514), who tackles the primordial deuterium-proton fusion rate ($d(p,\gamma)^3\text{He}$). This is the key reaction limiting the precision of primordial deuterium abundance predictions in BBN. By constructing an effective field theory using modern on-shell amplitude methods—which bypasses the traditional Lagrangian formulation to directly constrain the S-matrix—Tait identifies that the discrepancy can be traced to a single natural-sized next-to-leading-order electric dipole contact term. 

On the hadronic side, Chang et al. [arXiv:2607.04871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04871) deliver the first functional QCD calculation of the pion distribution amplitude using the functional renormalization group in the large-momentum effective theory framework. Pushing the pion momentum to $P_z = 4.5$ GeV to eliminate light-cone extrapolation errors, they extract a second-order moment ($\langle \xi^2 \rangle_\pi = 0.267$) that is significantly smaller than existing lattice-LaMET determinations, bringing functional QCD into closer alignment with other nonperturbative approaches.

### Robustness in the Hunt for Lepton Number Violation
As we push deeper into the next generation of neutrinoless double-beta decay ($0\nu\beta\beta$) experiments, the conversation is shifting from "how sensitive is our isotope?" to "how do we interpret a signal if we see it?". 

Ishidoshiro [arXiv:2607.04932](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04932) addresses this with a robust, operator-level framework ($\nu\text{DoBe}$) to convert half-life limits across different isotopes into constraints on low-energy lepton-number-violating (LNV) coefficients. Crucially, the work quantifies how stable these limits are under the notorious uncertainties of nuclear matrix elements (NMEs). While the light-Majorana neutrino exchange mechanism remains highly stable across different NME models, short-range dimension-nine operators show severe sensitivity to the choice of isotope and NME calculation method. This provides a clear, quantitative guide for which isotopes are actually useful for ratio-based tests to pin down the underlying UV physics after a potential discovery.
