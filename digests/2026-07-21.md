## Rare kaons and a first exotic Reggeon

Two experimental results carry the day, both clean and both in channels that have been chased for years. NA62 posts an updated $K^+\to\pi^+\nu\bar\nu$ branching ratio of $(9.6^{+1.9}_{-1.8})\times10^{-11}$ over 2016–2024 — 20% precision on the golden mode, and consistent with the SM ([arXiv:2607.16413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16413)). Meanwhile COMPASS/JPAC report the first observation in high-energy scattering of an *exotic Reggeon*, above $5\sigma$ in both $\eta\pi^-$ and $\eta'\pi^-$, most likely tied to the $\pi_1(1600)$ ([arXiv:2607.17850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17850)). Around these, a substantial first-order-phase-transition cluster picks up exactly the systematics thread that ran through July 17, and heavy-flavor spectroscopy keeps consolidating.

## Themes

### Rare kaons and exotic mesons
The NA62 measurement ([arXiv:2607.16413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16413)) is the headline: the world's most precise handle on a mode where the SM prediction is exceptionally clean, still sitting on top of theory. The COMPASS exotic-Reggeon observation (Alexeev et al. [arXiv:2607.17850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17850)) is the more novel result — an unbinned high-mass analysis disentangling the exchange mechanism, giving the spin-exotic $\pi_1$ program a production-side foothold. Two very different corners of QCD, both reporting firsts.

### First-order transitions and their gravitational waves
A coherent methods cluster, all sharpening the microphysics-to-spectrum map. Dorsch et al. [arXiv:2607.18207](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18207) benchmark the fluid ansatz against WallGo for wall velocities, finding agreement for mild transitions but a discrepancy once scattering enters and non-linearities matter as $\alpha\to1$. Kierkla et al. [arXiv:2607.18233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18233) show that in supercooled conformal models the daisy-resummation scheme is strongly disfavored — its theoretical error swamps LISA reconstruction uncertainty. An et al. [arXiv:2607.16398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16398) revise the UV scaling of heavy-particle production in boosted wall collisions, and Barbini et al. [arXiv:2607.18107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18107) deliver lattice GW spectra for biased domain-wall networks. This directly extends July 17's wall-friction and cosmic-string-reach papers: the message keeps being that GW "signals" hinge on poorly controlled non-equilibrium inputs.

### Ultralight and dark-sector detection
Kalia et al. [arXiv:2607.16342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16342) review the "Earth transducer" effect — using the planet's radius rather than an apparatus size to probe EM-coupled ultralight DM below $\sim3\times10^{-14}$ eV. Brax et al. [arXiv:2607.17727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17727) build repulsive ultralight DM from a Hosotani/Scherk-Schwarz construction, and Rosenzweig et al. [arXiv:2607.18221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18221) propose correlated dual-species comagnetometry for high-frequency background suppression. Olea-Romacho [arXiv:2607.18185](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18185) uses wide binaries in Boötes I to bound extended dark objects. The diversifying-search mood, now firmly on the detection side.

### Heavy-flavor spectroscopy consolidation
Luo et al. [arXiv:2607.17923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17923) use HQET sum rules to assign quantum numbers to excited $\Xi_c'$/$\Omega_c$ states and predict four resolvable $P$-wave $\Sigma_c$ states overlapping the observed $\Sigma_c(2800)/\Sigma_c(2900)$ — a direct theory follow-up to LHCb's July 14 discovery of two new $\Sigma_c^0$ states. Gui et al. [arXiv:2607.17294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17294) find a large glueball–$s\bar s$ mixing angle ($\sim41°$) in $N_f=1$ lattice QCD, and several QCD-sum-rule pentaquark and doubly-heavy-baryon analyses round it out.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2607.16413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16413) — Measurement of the branching ratio of the $K^+\to\pi^+\nu\bar\nu$ decay (NA62 Collaboration)

NA62 reports $\mathcal{B}_{2023\text{-}2024}(K^+\to\pi^+\nu\bar\nu)=(7.2^{+2.3}_{-2.1})\times10^{-11}$ from 33 candidates against $22.9\pm1.1$ SM signal and $12.0^{+2.9}_{-2.2}$ background events, then combines with 2016–2022 to obtain $(9.6^{+1.9}_{-1.8})\times10^{-11}$ — 20% precision and consistent with SM predictions near $8\times10^{-11}$. Across 2016–2024, 84 observed events against $30^{+4}_{-3}$ background give background-only rejection exceeding $6\sigma$, and the SM-observation significance passes $5\sigma$ for the first time. The 2023–2024 data were taken at lower intensity (440 MHz vs. the 580 MHz design max), sitting on a broad signal-per-spill maximum around 450 MHz where DAQ dead time and accidental vetoes are balanced; this improved the random-veto efficiency by 1.14 and roughly halved several relative backgrounds. New deep-learning tools — a transformer-based 4D GTK tracker (false-track rate 13%→4%, purity 87%→97%) and a ResNet-based calorimetric $\pi/\mu$ classifier (~2% higher pion ID, 40% lower $\mu\to\pi$ misID) — sharpen the selection. A KTAG upgrade to H$_2$ radiator cut material budget from 3.9% to 0.7% X$_0$.

Why it matters: this is now the world's most precise handle on the theoretically cleanest FCNC kaon mode (SM intrinsic uncertainty ~3%, dominated by long-distance charm), and it lands squarely on the SM. The dominant background remains upstream (accidental $K^+$/π matching), estimated fully data-driven with ~30% uncertainty. NA62 expects a further ~50% more signal events after 2025–2026 running.

### [arXiv:2607.17850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17850) — First Observation of an Exotic Reggeon (G. D. Alexeev (for JPAC Collaboration) et al.)

*(Abstract only.)* COMPASS presents high-statistics measurements of peripheral $\eta\pi^-$ and $\eta'\pi^-$ production in $\pi^- p\to\eta^{(\prime)}\pi^- p$, performing for the first time an unbinned analysis of the high-mass region to disentangle the exchange mechanisms governing production. They report the first observation in high-energy scattering of an exotic Reggeon, at significance exceeding $5\sigma$ in both channels, most likely related to the spin-exotic $\pi_1(1600)$.

Why it matters: this establishes a production-side foothold for the $\pi_1$ spin-exotic program, complementing the decades of resonance-spectrum work. Where earlier analyses focused on identifying the exotic state in the produced system, disentangling the *exchange* mechanism gives independent evidence that exotic quantum numbers propagate in the $t$-channel — a genuinely new handle on QCD's exotic sector. As only the abstract is available, the details of the exchange decomposition and systematic treatment cannot be assessed here.

### [arXiv:2607.18233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18233) — Theoretical uncertainties in reconstructing model parameters with gravitational waves from supercooled phase transitions (Maciej Kierkla et al.)

Taking the classically conformal U(1)$_X$ model as representative of strongly supercooled transitions, the authors scan $\{g_X,M_X\}$ over $\mathcal{O}(10^4)$ points and compute GW spectra with two nucleation-rate schemes: a 4D daisy-resummed potential with dimensionally-estimated prefactor, and a full NLO calculation (two-loop 3D-EFT matching plus exact scalar and vector fluctuation determinants, "NLOdet"). Injecting the resulting signals into LISA and running a Fisher-matrix reconstruction of the fundamental parameters, they find the daisy scheme strongly disfavored: the two methods disagree by $\mathcal{O}(10\%)$ in the reconstructed gauge coupling $g_X$, an order of magnitude larger than the daisy RG-scale ambiguity ($\mathcal{O}(1\%)$) and more than an order of magnitude above the sub-percent Fisher reconstruction uncertainty. The hierarchy is $\delta_g^{\rm exp}\sim\delta_g^{\bar\mu,\rm NLOdet}\ll\delta_g^{\bar\mu,\rm daisy}\ll\delta_g^{\rm meth}\sim\mathcal{O}(10\%)$. Crucially, no reasonable RG-scale choice reconciles daisy with NLOdet — contradicting a recent claim that scale-shifting suffices — because the discrepancy stems from genuinely missing higher-order physics (the scale-shifting gauge modes require exact determinants, not a derivative expansion). $M_X$ is essentially unaffected, since it just sets the low-$T$ Hubble scale.

Why it matters: this extends the digest's recurring message — GW "signals" from first-order transitions hinge on poorly controlled non-equilibrium/thermal inputs. For high-SNR LISA targets, theoretical diligence, not detector sensitivity, limits the inverse problem. Caveats: dynamical prefactor uses the no-damping approximation (neglecting back-reaction), and the hierarchy inverts in the lower-SNR PTA band.
