## Top pairs reach genus two

Buccioni et al. [arXiv:2610.32543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32543) deliver the complete two-loop QCD amplitudes for $q\bar q$ and $gg\to t\bar t$ in analytic form. They keep full $m_t$ and $N_c$ dependence and work from 417 master integrals. The function space includes several elliptic curves and a genus-two hyperelliptic curve. The authors say this is the first amplitude where genus-two iterated integrals survive in the four-dimensional finite remainder. They also supply high-precision threshold expansions in a public evaluator. Threshold is where the toponium-vs-continuum argument sits, and Ball et al. showed last week that any excess there is entangled with $m_t$. Elsewhere, the Galactic Center excess gets its most deflationary reading yet, and the 248 keV LZ event loses another candidate explanation.

## Themes

### Dark-sector excesses, three verdicts
Sands et al. [arXiv:2610.32845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32845) build diffuse emission from MHD Milky Way analogs with time-dependent cosmic-ray transport. The gap between instantaneous and time-averaged emission reaches several times the GCE, and some snapshots resemble it in both spectrum and morphology. Coming after Abazajian's foreground dependence (10-19) and Kim's narrow dSph corridor (10-20), this reads the excess as a look-elsewhere fluctuation.

Aristizabal Sierra et al. [arXiv:2610.32747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32747) use LZ's empty recoil region to set new bounds on atmospheric-neutrino up-scattering to sterile fermions above ~50 MeV. Fitting the 248 keV event this way needs couplings at the edge of perturbativity. That echoes September's exclusion of neutrino-scattering readings.

Pointing the other way, Tekverk et al. [arXiv:2610.31881](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31881) find SIDM subhalos match all three gravitational-imaging perturbers far more often than CDM subhalos. This quantifies last week's finding that none of 5000+ CDM subhalos is dense enough.

### Transverse spin from several sides
- Kang et al.'s FeynFacet [arXiv:2610.31745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31745) uses AI agents to derive new analytic NLO hard functions for transverse spin transfer.
- Yang et al. [arXiv:2610.32387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32387) show the mass-induced $m\,h_{1T}/Q$ transversity term cancels exactly, so inclusive DIS has no net twist-3 transversity.
- Cao et al. [arXiv:2610.32715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32715) propose a fragmentation energy correlator whose transverse-spin dependence probes the chiral-symmetry-breaking scale.
- Han et al. [arXiv:2610.32741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32741) isolate gluon-transversity GPDs in deuteron TCS through their angular dependence.

All of this continues September's EIC/JLab-facing nucleon-structure push.

### Axions back in the cavity
After last week's off-haloscope proposals, Gao et al. [arXiv:2610.31717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31717) return to the cavity. They pre-excite the mode so the signal is linear in $g_{a\gamma}$ and claim KSVZ/DFSZ sensitivity with current technology. The case rests on simulations, so check the noise treatment.

Radford et al. [arXiv:2610.32091](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32091) add axion electrodynamics to OSIRIS. They find the previously proposed co-linear instability disappears under a consistent reduction, while a counter-propagating Floquet branch survives.

Bodeker et al. [arXiv:2610.31756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31756) add an $\mathcal O(\sqrt\lambda)$ soft-scalar correction to the high-temperature topological susceptibility in SU(2).

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2610.32543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32543) — Analytic two-loop corrections to top-quark pair production at hadron colliders (Federico Buccioni et al.)

Buccioni, Nega, Sauer, Tancredi and Wagner give the complete two-loop $q\bar q\to t\bar t$ and $gg\to t\bar t$ amplitudes in analytic form, with full $m_t$, $N_c$, $n_l$ and $n_h$ dependence, through $\mathcal O(\varepsilon^2)$. The 789 $gg$ and 218 $q\bar q$ two-loop diagrams reduce to 417 masters. These are cast into $\varepsilon$-factorised differential equations spanning 13 elliptic curves and one genus-two curve, which lives in the non-planar sector nB463. Up to weight four, 300 of the 449 independent differential forms enter the finite remainder. None of the quadratic-type elliptic forms survive, but 55 of the 85 genus-two forms do. That is the basis for the "first genus-two finite remainder" claim; the curve itself had been identified earlier.

Boundary values are fixed at threshold. The authors run AMFlow at $(x_1,x_2)=(10^{-4},2\cdot10^{-4})$ with 100–300 digits, match to generalised series, then fit constants with PSLQ. The elliptic and genus-two constants resist any known basis and are kept numerically. The public `hqpAmp` code evaluates renormalised finite remainders across phase space, switching between double and quad precision.

On practical impact, NNLO $t\bar t$ already ran on numerical grids, and the $q\bar q$ channel was already analytic. The gains here are:
- control and speed;
- $\mathcal O(\varepsilon^{1,2})$ terms for three-loop IR studies;
- a stress test of canonical-basis methods beyond elliptic.

The paper does not address bound-state effects. Its threshold expansions are fixed-order input that toponium-vs-continuum fits would still need to combine with a Coulomb/NRQCD treatment.

### [arXiv:2610.32845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32845) — The Galactic Center Gamma-Ray Excess and the Look-Elsewhere Effect (Isabel S. Sands et al.)

Sands and Hopkins analyse two FIRE-2 Milky Way analogs, m12f and m12m (m12i is dropped for its warped disk), with spectrally resolved CR transport. They compare each snapshot over 460–20 Myr lookback against a baseline "template": the mean of the snapshots whose total ROI flux is below the median. For every ROI (10°, 20°) and line-of-sight cut (0, 2, 7 kpc), the Fermi GCE lies inside the 25th–75th percentile band of residuals, and many residuals are several times larger. One m12f snapshot matches the GCE in spectrum and is roughly spherical inside 10°, produced by at least three clustered supernova episodes. The physical driver is that CR diffusion across the central kpc takes ~1 Myr, comparable to the timescale of ISM disruption, so steady-state templates are structurally inadequate.

The caveats are substantial, and the authors state most of them:
- **Star formation.** Central SFRs are 1–2 orders of magnitude above the CMZ, and total diffuse flux is a few to 10 times Fermi's. The residual amplitude is therefore compared against a much brighter galaxy.
- **Sample size.** There are only two galaxies and no AGN, so no frequency or look-elsewhere statistic can be computed.
- **Baseline choice.** A baseline built from below-median snapshots biases residuals positive.
- **Comparison method.** Residuals here are not a template fit; the authors' proposed test is to run actual Fermi pipelines on the simulations.

A potentially useful discriminant is that the excesses are typically flanked by in-plane flux deficits.

### [arXiv:2610.31745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31745) — FeynFacet: AI agents for analytic NLO QCD (Zhong-Bo Kang et al.)

Kang, Vogelsang and Zhang use LLM agents to write and run the process-specific programs for analytic NLO calculations. Physicists fix the observables, projections and HVBM conventions, and the finished programs run without further model calls. The pipeline:
- spin and colour algebra done in parallel with FeynCalc, FORM and Tracer;
- reverse unitarity for the real-emission phase space;
- dimensional shifts to remove evanescent numerators;
- Kira IBP at symbolic $d$, then SubTropica for the masters.

The new results are NLO transverse-spin-transfer hard functions:
- all seven $q^\uparrow b\to c^\uparrow X$ classes through $\alpha_s^3$, including the regular-only $q^\uparrow g\to\bar q^\uparrow X$ from identical-quark interference;
- $\ell q^\uparrow\to q^\uparrow X$ through $\alpha_{\rm em}^2\alpha_s$, with the scattered lepton integrated.

They find $\hat\sigma_{T,XX}=\hat\sigma_{T,NN}$ in every channel, so the spin dependence is purely $\cos(\phi_a-\phi_c)$.

Validation reproduces published UU/LL $pp$ and $\ell p$ results, double-transverse $pp$, and the one-loop TMD matching and soft functions. The checks on the new results are weaker than on the benchmarks:
- The $pp$ transfer results agree across the three algebra routes only at test points. Crossing from incoming-TT is symbolic only at Born level.
- The $\ell p$ result has no external benchmark, only Born normalisation, pole cancellation and RG checks.
- All three routes share the same generated amplitudes, reduction and masters, so common-mode errors would go undetected.

As a test case for agentic theory work, the release of code alongside the coefficients is what makes it auditable.
