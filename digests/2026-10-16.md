## A threshold excess, fitted with the PDFs

Ball et al. [arXiv:2610.21479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21479) take the $t\bar t$ threshold excess, the thing CMS's toponium result made a headline, and ask what it looks like in a global PDF analysis. They fit ATLAS and CMS differential data with correlated PDF, $m_t$ and $\alpha_s$ uncertainties. They find nothing at 8 TeV and just over 2σ at 13 TeV. They also find a strong $\Delta$–$m_t$ correlation. That is a much quieter reading than CMS's 6.1σ, from a different handle on the same region. Elsewhere, LZ gets one paper that moves the argument from the solar bound to the halo, and two precision inputs (lattice HVP, solar gallium) land on the SM side.

## Themes

### Threshold, toponium, and the inputs they depend on
Ball's result is a reminder that any $t\bar t$-threshold claim has to be quoted jointly with $m_t$. It also bears on the toponium-vs-CP-odd-scalar question. Around it sit precision-QCD building blocks. Gehrmann et al. [arXiv:2610.20820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20820) deliver all three-loop four-point masters with one off-shell leg, including a 371-integral family, which is the missing piece for N³LO $V$/$H$+jet. Seymour [arXiv:2610.22102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22102) damps spurious singularities in dipole subtraction. Alsairafi [arXiv:2610.21813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21813) compares decay modelling in $t\bar t t\bar t$.

### LZ: now the halo tail is on trial
Graham et al. [arXiv:2610.21041](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21041) show how the thermal Higgsino could survive the solar-neutrino bound, the constraint most 248 keV models have been designed around. It needs a different high-velocity dark-matter tail from the LMC-motivated simulations. That shifts the argument from model-building to halo simulations, which echoes the halo-marginalization point from 10-06. The paper also proposes heavy-element paleodetectors and large neutrino detectors as tests that do not depend on xenon.

### Anomalies meeting their inputs
- Elliott et al. [arXiv:2610.22219](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22219) use the Sun as a calibrated source and find no gallium anomaly: SAGE+GALLEX/GNO at $66.1\pm3.1$ SNU against $67.5^{+1.4}_{-1.1}$ expected.
- Alexandrou et al. [arXiv:2610.22224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22224) (ETMC) get $a_\mu^{\rm HVP}=(714.9\pm8.1)\times10^{-10}$ at about 1%, agreeing with other lattice groups and widening the gap to pre-CMD3 data-driven values.
- Li et al. [arXiv:2610.20945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20945) get a QCD sum-rule $a_\tau^{\rm HVP}$ of $(3.501\pm0.048)\times10^{-6}$, and their combined $a_\mu^{\rm HVP}$ sits on the lattice side too.

### Hadronization and tooling
Janik et al. [arXiv:2610.20793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20793) use tensor-network string breaking to motivate the Lund area law. Bonanno et al. [arXiv:2610.20747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20747) mitigate topological freezing at 0.03 fm with stochastic normalizing flows. That is the first lattice-side fix for the sampling problem under the strong-CP lattice program. Bierlich [arXiv:2610.21897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21897) shows Lorentz boosts silently lose precision in single-precision records and air-shower codes, though not in ordinary double-precision collider work.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2610.21479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21479) — Determining the Threshold Excess in Top-Quark Pair Production through a Global PDF Analysis (Richard D. Ball et al.)

**The fit.** Ball and ter Hoeve float a threshold excess Δ alongside $m_t$, $\alpha_s(m_Z)$ and the PDFs. They use the Theory Covariance Method on ATLAS and CMS $m_{t\bar t}$-differential data, measured against fixed-order NNLO QCD with NLO EW and missing-higher-order uncertainties. The Δ prior is deliberately broad, at 200% of a 6.43 pb (13 TeV) / 2.19 pb (8 TeV) central value.

**Results.**
- **13 TeV:** $\Delta = 14.14^{+6.44}_{-6.31}$ pb and $m_t = 172.85^{+0.59}_{-0.58}$ GeV, which is 2.2σ from the continuum. The $m_t$ error is 2.3× larger than in their Δ-free fit, and the correlation is $\rho(\Delta,m_t)=0.84$.
- **8 TeV:** $\Delta = 0.57\pm3.83$ pb, or $3.28^{+2.66}_{-2.32}$ pb with positivity imposed, with $\rho=0.69$. They attribute the energy dependence to the gluon being probed at higher $x$ at 8 TeV.
- **Conditioned on $m_t=172.5$ GeV:** $\Delta = 11.14^{+4.08}_{-4.41}$ pb. This is close to the ATLAS (9.3 pb) and CMS (8.8 pb) toponium cross sections but with larger errors, which the authors suggest come from the PDF correlation.

**Why it matters.** Any threshold-excess number is effectively a statement about $m_t$ as well. Conversely, $m_t$ fits that ignore Δ absorb part of the excess.

**Caveats.**
- No individual 13 TeV dataset deviates significantly except CMS dilepton, at about 2σ. The excess only appears in the combination, whose central value exceeds every individual one; the authors explain this as a BLUE-type correlated-combination effect.
- The 68% intervals use only replicas with Δ > 0.
- Bin migrations from the top width are neglected.
- The ATLAS 13 TeV inputs use 36 fb⁻¹.
- Δ is defined relative to fixed-order NNLO, not NRQCD.

The proposed way to break the Δ–$m_t$ degeneracy is to add spin-correlation observables to the fit.

### [arXiv:2610.21041](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21041) — Testing the inelastic dark matter interpretation of the LZ event with heavy-element experiments (Peter W. Graham et al.)

**The halo question.** For the 1.1 TeV thermal Higgsino, the 248 keV event's preferred splitting depends on the halo model, but stays below the solar-capture bound (δ ≲ 566 keV) in the standard ones:

| Halo model | Efficiency window | Preferred δ (keV) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| SHM | LZ ROI (0–250 keV) | 340–360 | Excluded by solar bound |
| Simulated LMC | LZ ROI | 495–515 | Excluded by solar bound |
| SHM / LMC | Full 0–650 keV | Contours drop below the Higgsino line | Disfavoured |
| LMC tail cut 100× ($w=0.0026\%$) | Full | 440–510 | Moves up to the Higgsino line, still excluded |
| LMC tail extended to $v_{\max}=1100$ km/s at 10% flux | Full | 500–600 | Partly survives |

Two numbers frame the last two rows:
- The 100× flux reduction corresponds to $w=0.0026\%$, two orders of magnitude below the usual benchmarks.
- The 10% flux of the extended tail sits an order of magnitude below the lowest flux current simulations resolve.

The authors' conclusion is that the Higgsino question becomes a halo-simulation-resolution question.

**Proposed tests.**
- **Pb paleodetectors (Laurionite, 60 cm³, 15 nm SAXs):** cover most of the event contour.
- **Two different elements:** fix $m_\chi$ and δ through the apex recoil energy.
- **Mineral-age scans:** the signal-to-noise should peak at 50–100 Myr if the tail comes from the LMC.
- **JUNO with a (20 m)³ Pb or ²³⁸U shell:** covers the 566–600 keV window, and measures δ directly from the photon line. Varying the shell yields the decay length, which is calculable for the Higgsino.

**Caveats.**
- The analysis rests on a single event.
- The contours are relative likelihoods, not goodness of fit.
- The efficiency is assumed to stay near 1 up to 650 keV, beyond LZ's conservative ROI.
- The extended tail is admittedly speculative.

### [arXiv:2610.22219](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22219) — Gallium Anomaly and Solar Neutrinos (Steven R. Elliott et al.)

**The calibration.** Elliott and Robertson use the Sun as a calibrated source. Its strength is fixed by Borexino elastic-scattering fluxes (⁷Be, CNO), the SNO neutral-current ⁸B flux, and a luminosity-constrained pp flux of $5.966(27)\times10^{10}$ cm⁻²s⁻¹. Survival probabilities use JUNO and Daya Bay mixing, and solar models enter only through the MSW density profile.

**Result.** The predicted rate is $67.5^{+1.4}_{-1.1}$ SNU against a measured $66.1\pm3.1$ SNU from SAGE and Gallex/GNO, a ratio of $0.980^{+0.050}_{-0.049}$. The radioactive-source experiments give $0.817^{+0.028}_{-0.048}$. More than 90% of the solar rate comes from the ground-state cross section derived via detailed balance.

**Why it matters.** This disfavours the proposed ~20% factorization breakdown (Cadeddu et al.), which sits roughly 3.5σ from the solar ratio, and any error in the ⁷¹Ga cross section more generally. It also validates the extraction and counting efficiencies of SAGE, Gallex and BEST.

**Correction to the digest's framing.** This removes the mundane explanations, not the anomaly. The authors conclude that its "source evidently lies elsewhere" and say nothing against sterile neutrinos.

**Caveat.** The solar rate is dominated by pp neutrinos (40 of 67.5 SNU). The test therefore weights the cross section differently from the source experiments.

A single paper, but a clear signal: attention is concentrated on LZ's 248 keV nuclear-recoil candidate, which has a 3.4σ local and 2.6σ global significance. ### [arXiv:2609.06760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06760) — Solar Capture Tests of Inelastic Dark Matter after the LZ High-Recoil Event (Mattia Di Mauro et al.)

Di Mauro and Shaikh propagate three LZ-fitting interpretations through Solar capture, post-capture orbital evolution, and IceCube annihilation limits.

- **Thermal Higgsino.** The authors independently confirm the Pospelov–Ramani exclusion, matching the thermalized endpoint to within 1–2 keV. IceCube requires δ ≳ 0.51–0.56 MeV, while the LZ-preferred splitting is about 377 keV. The gap survives the uncertainty in the loop-induced elastic cooling cross sections and a deliberately pessimistic WW/ZZ treatment.
- **Pseudo-Dirac vector model.** The model captures plenty of dark matter, C⊙ = 1.53×10²⁰ s⁻¹, roughly at the naive equilibrium scale for the IceCube b-bbar limit. However, annihilation requires one χ₁ and one χ₂, and this two-state kinetics caps Γ_A at about 4.4×10¹⁴ s⁻¹, roughly 1.7×10⁵ below the limit.
- **Neutron-philic 𝒪₄ model.** Capture is starved because it can proceed only on trace odd-neutron isotopes, leaving the equilibrium rate a factor of about 154 below the limit.

The paper flags real caveats: b-bbar is used only as a proxy for the annihilation spectrum, and re-excitation cycles need a full phase-space treatment.

With ~52 citations since its September posting, the likely draw is its function as a scorecard. It closes off the most predictive interpretation while stating plainly that endothermic explanations are not generically excluded. That gives both skeptics and model-builders something to cite. The paper itself references seven recent Solar-capture analyses of the event, pointing to a crowded, fast-moving subliterature. The citing papers themselves aren't visible from the text, so this reading is inferred.
