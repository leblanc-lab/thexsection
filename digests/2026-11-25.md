## Calabi–Yau periods in electron g−2

Forner et al. [arXiv:2611.32854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32854) compute every master integral in the four-loop ladder family of the QED electron self-energy. The kernels involve sunrise elliptic curves and a Calabi–Yau threefold. On shell, this single family already spans all the non-polylogarithmic constants of the planar four-loop electron $g-2$.

It anchors a day heavy on integral technology, including a second agent-plus-verifier framework for canonical bases. Also notable: Djouadi carries Monday's toponium $\kappa$ formalism into heavy MSSM Higgs searches. Monday's toponium paper [arXiv:2611.27704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27704) and yesterday's HEP-Ex-Bench [arXiv:2611.28194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28194) reappear in today's listing; both were covered earlier this week.

## Themes

### Four-loop integral technology
- **Higher residue pairings.** Brunello et al. [arXiv:2611.32544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32544) build differential equations directly from intersection numbers, with no IBP reduction. They obtain canonical DEs for massless planar ladders that are new at four loops.
- **Diphoton with a top loop.** Bera et al. [arXiv:2611.32553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32553) give two-loop $pp\to\gamma\gamma$ amplitudes with a closed top loop, with elliptic sectors handled without a canonical basis. They ship a C++ hard function.
- **CANON.** Jian et al. [arXiv:2611.33100](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33100) let an agent pick basis-construction methods while independent programs verify each proposal and force retries on failure. This is the same deterministic-gate design as Petit Rosas's Opus run (11-23), though demonstrated only on two- and three-loop families.

### Energy correlators, bounded and resolved
- **Positivity bounds.** Ricci et al. [arXiv:2611.31587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31587) derive the complete set of positivity constraints on multi-point correlators. They use them to bound three-point multipoles in QCD from LEP one- and two-point data.
- **Quarkonium.** Ee et al. [arXiv:2611.31445](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31445) give the first full LO analytic quarkonium EEC. The channel shapes are ordered by the $c\bar c$ pair's angular momentum $J$, which LHC forward coverage exposes better than sPHENIX.
- **Boosted bosons.** Jobilal et al. [arXiv:2611.33057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33057) reach N³LL in the small-acoplanarity limit, with Collins-function sensitivity in $Z\gamma$.

These are natural theory partners for ATLAS's all-charged-particle EEC from last week's OmniFold release.

T2K [arXiv:2611.32106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32106) adds its first Gd-loaded Super-K data and still favours normal ordering, near-maximal CP violation and, slightly, the upper octant. ## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2611.32854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32854) — The Four-Loop Ladder Integral Family for the QED Electron Self-Energy (Felix Forner et al.)

All 160 master integrals (72 sectors) of the planar four-loop ladder family are now in canonical form. Their on-shell boundary values reproduce every non-polylogarithmic constant class in the planar four-loop electron $g-2$, plus two new independent weight-seven constants.

On their maximal cuts, 53 sectors are polylogarithmic, 18 are elliptic, and one, the equal-mass four-loop banana, carries a Calabi–Yau threefold. Even so, the threefold leaks into most of the 27 extra functions $G_i$ needed for $\epsilon$-factorisation. It enters through couplings to eyeball and double-eyeball sectors, and to the cat-eye sector, where two differently parametrised sunrise curves already couple on the maximal cut. The resulting differential equation has 100 kernels, of which only 13 are dlog.

Boundary constants were fitted from AMFlow inputs to 300–350 digits:
- **At $p^2\to\infty$:** pure alternating MZVs through weight seven, with no geometric trace.
- **On shell:** alternating MZVs and polylogs at sixth roots of unity. Sector 1145 needed 4000 digits from series transport (6700 orders on 39 patches).
- **Plus** the Laporta non-polylogarithmic basis.

Numerical transport evaluates all 160 integrals through $\epsilon^7$ at any $p^2$ in 0.06–0.28 s, with errors between $6\times10^{-10}$ and $7\times10^{-7}$.

Caveats:
- This is one family, not $g-2$ itself.
- The two extra constants are left numerical. They may not enter the physical result, or they may obey relations visible only at higher precision.
- The $z=0$ constant basis is conjectured.
- Removing logarithms at non-MUM points works here but is unproven in general.

The authors also note that this basis construction lends itself to AI agents, which fits today's CANON theme.

### [arXiv:2611.09666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09666) — Interference effects in MSSM Higgs boson searches at low $\tan\beta$ (Abdelhak Djouadi et al.)

Djouadi and Ellis show that narrow ($\Gamma/M\sim1$–2%) heavy $H/A$ resonances at low $\tan\beta$ still interfere strongly with the $gg$ continuum. The size of the effect is set by the Fano ratio $\kappa$ of resonant to non-resonant amplitude at the peak, not by $\Gamma/M$. The digest ties this $\kappa$ to Monday's toponium formalism.

Suppressed $\cos(\beta-\alpha)$ couplings plus $t\bar t$-dominated widths make $|\kappa|\propto 1/M_A^2$, nearly independent of $\tan\beta$. At $\tan\beta=2$:

| $M_A$ | $\lvert\kappa\rvert$: $ZZ$ / $hh$ / $Zh$ | Rate increase |
|---|---|---|
| 600 GeV | 4.5 / 9 / 3 | $ZZ$ +20%, $hh$ +10% |
| 800 GeV | 2.7 / 6 / 2.4 | $ZZ$ +50%, $hh$ +17% |
| 1 TeV | 2 / 4.6 / 2 | — |

At 500 GeV the $ZZ$ increase is 9%. The enhancement comes from the absorptive part of the top box above $2m_t$, which gives Im$\,\kappa>0$, so exact $m_t$ dependence is essential.

Applied to ATLAS's 2024 hMSSM summary, the contours shift as follows:
- $ZZ$ moves by $\le4$ GeV.
- $Zh$ moves by $\Delta\tan\beta\approx0.2$.
- $WW$ does not move.
- The high-mass $hh$ edge at $\tan\beta=1$ moves from 686 to 706 GeV.

Only the $hh$ strip at $M_A\approx560$–710 GeV still matters, since $h$-coupling fits exclude $M_A\lesssim560$ GeV. For HL-LHC masses of 600 GeV–1 TeV the effect becomes $\mathcal{O}(1)$.

Caveats:
- The calculation is LO, with Goldstone equivalence for the longitudinal amplitudes.
- Detector resolution is modelled as Gaussian, with a fixed-background fit.
- The ATLAS contours were digitised from a figure.

### [arXiv:2611.31359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31359) — Superdaisy Resummation in Theories with Broken Gauge Symmetries (Martin M\"unzenberg et al.)

Partial-dressing (tadpole) resummation is extended to the gauge and ghost sectors of spontaneously broken theories. The construction works in a general $R_\xi$ gauge, with separate gap equations for the transverse, longitudinal and gauge-direction modes. It solves for $Z$–photon mixing self-consistently and keeps the full mass dependence of the thermal self-energies. Earlier SM gauge gap studies used the high-$T$ expansion with $g'=0$. Recent 2HDM PD work, which the digest links to Zhao et al., kept gauge bosons at leading Debye masses.

The provided text breaks off before the numerical section, so the results below come from the abstract:
- Beyond-$\mathcal{O}(T^2)$ corrections matter mainly for the gauge thermal masses and only slightly shift the scalar spectrum.
- Self-consistent longitudinal dressing lowers the SM barrier.
- Dressing the transverse modes removes it entirely, consistent with the lattice crossover.

The authors' own power counting limits this. The transverse thermal mass scales as $g^2T$, so the effective coupling $\gamma_T\sim1$ near $h\to0$, giving $\mathcal{O}(1)$ uncertainty exactly where the transition is decided. Lollipop diagrams and momentum-overlap (sunset/bubble) corrections are also omitted. Mixed gauge–scalar and fermion bubbles lack the vertex suppression that justifies this omission in the scalar case. The paper therefore establishes that transverse modes control the transition's character, but does not yet give a quantitative barrier.
