## Upgrade I's first rare-decay result

LHCb [arXiv:2611.00230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00230) uses 18 fb⁻¹ of 2024–25 data from the upgraded detector for three results:

- the first single-experiment evidence for $B^0\to\mu^+\mu^-$;
- the world's most precise $B_s^0\to\mu^+\mu^-$ branching fraction;
- the most precise $B_s^0\to\mu^+\mu^-$ effective lifetime.

All three agree with the SM, alone and combined with Runs 1–2. That fits the season's split in flavor. The cleanest exclusive channels keep landing on the SM, while the live tensions sit in $C_9$ and $|V_{cb}|$. The abstract gives no numbers, so open the paper to see how far Run 3 moves things. CMS adds a $|V_{cb}|$ measured from top decays. The rest of the day's weight is in FPGA-budget ML and in whether predicted phase transitions actually complete.

## Themes

### $|V_{cb}|$ without hadronic inputs
CMS [arXiv:2611.01274](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01274) extracts $|V_{cb}|=(4.59^{+0.44}_{-0.48}{}^{+0.64}_{-0.76})\times10^{-2}$ from $W\to cb$ in $t\bar t$. ### Transformers in 147 ns
- **SCRAMJet.** Sharma et al. [arXiv:2611.00086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00086) reach 71.4% JetClass accuracy on a VU13P. That is 6.1 points above the best resource-constrained model, using an eighth of the operations.
- **SpikePID.** Tsoi et al. [arXiv:2611.00104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00104) beat an 8.6× larger MLP on K/π cluster counting with a 529-parameter spiking network.
- **Localized anomalies.** Mendiratta et al. [arXiv:2611.00234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00234) show a calorimeter flow's global likelihood misses localized signals that a regional latent score catches.

After LEAF (10-21) through CoReNet yesterday, the open question is what survives quantization, not whether models can shrink.

### Which transitions finish
Chiang [arXiv:2611.01634](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01634) studies the type-II seesaw. The usual $v_c/T_c>1$ criterion flags degeneracies that never complete. The transitions that do complete are weak, sphaleron-unsafe and far below LISA, and they depend strongly on the RG scale. Araújo et al. [arXiv:2610.40354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40354) find genuine two-peak spectra in two-field conformal models. This continues yesterday's idealization-dropping GW papers: a GW forecast is only as good as its transition dynamics.

### Agents, with receipts
Gao et al. [arXiv:2611.03373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03373) have an agent reproduce a CEPC Bell-observable analysis to ~2% without being given the published numbers as targets. It then traces the detector-level shift mainly to muon isolation. Zhao's CELEBI [arXiv:2611.01788](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01788) pins each result to an immutable analysis state. After QFT-Bench showed correct answers with unfaithful reasoning (10-27), provenance is the right thing to build.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2611.00230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00230) — Evidence for the decay $B^0\to\mu^+\mu^-$ and improved measurements of the decay $B^0_s\to\mu^+\mu^-$ (LHCb collaboration et al.)

With 18 fb⁻¹ of 2024–25 Upgrade I data, LHCb sees a $B^0\to\mu^+\mu^-$ excess at 3.0σ, with $\mathcal{B}=(1.26^{+0.51}_{-0.47}{}^{+0.06}_{-0.05})\times10^{-10}$. Combined with Runs 1–2, this becomes $(1.14^{+0.42}_{-0.39}{}^{+0.06}_{-0.05})\times10^{-10}$ at 3.2σ. The SM predicts $(1.03\pm0.05)\times10^{-10}$, and the prior limit was $<1.6\times10^{-10}$ at 90% CL.

For $B_s^0$, Run 3 alone gives $(3.31^{+0.31+0.15}_{-0.30-0.13})\times10^{-9}$. The combined fit gives $(3.25^{+0.26+0.13}_{-0.25-0.12})\times10^{-9}$, sitting somewhat below the SM's $3.66\pm0.14$.

On the digest's question: the ratio is now measured rather than bounded, but loosely. The combined result is $\mathcal{R}_{\mu\mu}=0.035^{+0.014}_{-0.012}$, against $0.0280\pm0.0016$ in the SM and in any MFV theory. At ~40% precision, that is not yet a meaningful test of non-CKM flavour structure.

The effective lifetime is $1.42\pm0.18\pm0.02$ ps from Run 3 and $1.60\pm0.16\pm0.01$ ps combined. Both are still statistically dominated, and $\mathcal{A}_{\Delta\Gamma_s}^{\mu\mu}=0.91\pm1.92$ is unconstrained within its physical range.

Caveats visible in the paper:
- The $B_s$ branching fraction assumes $\mathcal{A}_{\Delta\Gamma_s}^{\mu\mu}=+1$. Using −1 instead raises it by 9.2%.
- The dominant $B_s$ systematic (3%) is $f_s/f_d=0.247\pm0.008$ at 13.6 TeV, taken from an LHCb paper still in preparation.
- The $B^0$ systematic (4%) comes from misID and semileptonic background modelling.
- The $B_s$ yields from the lifetime and branching-fraction fits agree only at the 1.7σ level.

### [arXiv:2611.01158](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01158) — Exothermic Higgs portal: from LUX-ZEPLIN recoil to Gamma Rays (Subhojit Roy et al.)

(Authors are Roy and Zupan.) The model is a complex singlet split into $\phi_{1,2}$ by a few hundred keV, coupling only through an off-diagonal Higgs portal $g\,\phi_1\phi_2 H^\dagger H$. The relic abundance fixes $g$ and leaves $r_2\simeq0.5$ of the dark matter in the excited state today.

At the benchmark ($m_{\phi_1}=100$ GeV, $\Delta m=600$ keV, $g\simeq0.0515$), the predictions are:
- **LZ rate:** $\phi_2 A\to\phi_1 A$ down-scattering gives $N_\epsilon\simeq1.63$ efficiency-weighted events in the 2.84 t·yr exposure. This uses the Standard Halo Model and needs no velocity tail.
- **Diphoton decay:** $\phi_2\to\phi_1\gamma\gamma$ has $\tau\simeq4.5\times10^{23}$ s, about 40× above a recast INTEGRAL/SPI bound. That recast neglects inter-bin correlations and template systematics. About 83% of the photons fall above COSI threshold.
- **Dwarf annihilation:** $\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\rm equiv}=2.26\times10^{-26}$ vs a Fermi-LAT limit of $3.34\times10^{-26}$ cm³/s. That is a thin margin, and the dwarfs exclude $m_{\phi_1}\lesssim72$ GeV.
- **Solar capture:** capture leaves the Sun's population mostly in $\phi_1$. The resulting WW rate is ~$10^{-13}$ of the IceCube limit. The captured-$\phi_1$ × halo-$\phi_2$ channel was not evaluated.

The main caveat is spectral. A xenon diffraction minimum near 270 keV pushes the predicted peak to ~180 keV. The model expects 1.18 events in 100–200 keV but only 0.22 in 215–270 keV, where the candidate sits. The total yield also depends strongly on nuclear modelling: 1.63 events with the default response, 4.70 with a Helm form factor, and 1.61 with chiral EFT. No event-level likelihood is performed.

### [arXiv:2611.00086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00086) — SCRAMJet: An Ultra Low Latency JetClass-Scale Transformer for the HL-LHC Level-1 Trigger (Aadeesh Sharma et al.)

The model is a single-block transformer with rank-2 Linformer attention, $D=16$, and 64 constituents, trained with per-parameter learned fixed-point quantization. It reaches 71.43±0.62% accuracy on ten-class JetClass with 0.157M MACs. That is 6.1 points above PHAT-JeT at one-eighth the MACs. On a VU13P it meets timing at 147 ns (21 cycles) using 25.7% of LUTs, and the RTL is bit-exact over 1.3M outputs.

The transferable result is the "virtual channel" mechanism. The quantizer masks zero-width channels only at inference, so training sees them while the circuit does not. One checkpoint scores 72.32% in training mode but 65.2% at inference, and 72.35% with the mask disabled; the mask explains the whole gap. All 7,015 sub-zero-width parameters sit inside attention.

The fix is a floor on the *activation*-quantizer bit-widths; the inherited constraint only covered weight quantizers. At EBOPs matched to within 0.12%, the floor lifts accuracy from 66.38% to 69.48%.

Caveats:
- That same floored design compiles to 42% more adder-graph units, so EBOPs can rank designs opposite to their real cost. Width cuts are the reliable lever: halving $D$ removed 38.8% of compiled cost at no accuracy loss.
- Macro AUC (0.9289) still trails PHAT-JeT by 0.010.
- Results come from a 2M-jet training subset with three seeds.
