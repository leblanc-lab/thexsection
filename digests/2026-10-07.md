## Three bids for the B_s1

Two more groups interpret LHCb's $B_{s0}^*(5700)$ today. With last week's Albaladejo et al., all three now place the $1^+$ partner between 5740 and 5755 MeV. That agreement says less than it seems. Albaladejo already noted that the $1^+$–$0^+$ splitting essentially equals the $B_s^*$–$B_s$ difference, so the partner mass is nearly fixed by heavy-quark spin symmetry. The models actually differ on widths and on how much of the state is a bare $b\bar s$ core. The rest of the day is spectroscopy-heavy, with steady precision inputs and one LZ paper.

## Themes

### Molecular dressing of the $B_{s0}^*$
Hao et al. [arXiv:2610.07106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07106) find that a compact quark model overshoots the mass by ~120 MeV. Adding $BK$ coupled channels pulls the pole to ~5727 MeV and explains the narrow width. Sánchez-Illana et al. [arXiv:2610.07171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07171) work in the hidden-gauge formalism and predict five further states. A bare component cuts the $B_s^0\pi^0$ width from 50 keV to 10–20 keV. Albaladejo's models gave 34–82.5 keV, so the isospin-violating width is now the discriminating number. Kinugawa et al. [arXiv:2610.08340](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08340) add the general theory of how Coulomb interactions modify compositeness universality near threshold.

### Multiquark methods, and a glueball radius
Zhang et al. [arXiv:2610.07818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07818) diagonalize a potential model exactly on a lattice. Note that this is a discretized quark model, not lattice QCD. They find a $cc\bar c\bar c$ $0^+$ state 33 MeV below $\eta_c\eta_c$ and claim it is robust against the choice of potential. Jiang et al. [arXiv:2610.06979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06979) read $X(3790)$ and $G(3900)$ as $P$-wave $D\bar D^{(*)}$ configurations. Mamo [arXiv:2610.08545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08545) gives the first holographic glueball gravitational form factors. The scalar Breit mass radius of 0.276 fm sits close to Issifu et al.'s 0.28 fm from last Thursday, though the radius definitions may differ.

### Perturbative inputs for the next machine
Maier et al. [arXiv:2610.08282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08282) compute four-loop top-mediated QCD corrections to $\Delta r$ and $\Delta\kappa$. The resulting $m_W$ shift is a few hundred keV, which the authors call relevant at FCC-ee. Guzzi et al. [arXiv:2610.07233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07233) lay out CT's route to partial-N³LO fits. Their evolution still uses approximate four-loop splitting functions. Chen et al. [arXiv:2610.07145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07145) resum the back-to-back EEC plateau through N³LL in $\mathcal N=4$ SYM and interpolate it to strong coupling. They do this with a "human-in-the-loop agentic workflow," another entry in last week's LLMs-inside-the-calculation thread.

### Fast, but not lossy
Pizzichemi et al.'s HybridMC [arXiv:2610.08054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08054) parametrizes optical transport for LHCb's ECAL Upgrade II and gains a factor of 150–480 in speed. The authors explicitly position it against generative flash sims, which cannot reproduce the photon arrival times that tens-of-ps timing needs. This is a useful counterpoint to the surrogate enthusiasm of 09-28. Bustamante's Magnus [arXiv:2610.07159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07159) covers the oscillation side.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2610.07106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07106) — Molecular Structure Effects in Low-Lying P-wave Bottom-Strange Meson Spectrum (Wei Hao et al.)

A compact $b\bar s$ picture cannot reach the $B_{s0}^*(5700)$: the plain confinement+OGE model puts the $0^+$ at 5851 MeV. One-loop ($\alpha_s^2$) corrections to the OGE potential lower it only to 5816 MeV, still ~120 MeV above LHCb's $5698.9\pm1.5\pm0.6$ MeV. Coupling the bare state to $BK$/$B^*K$ via $^3P_0$ vertices supplies the missing shift and brings the $0^+$ to 5727 MeV, within ~30 MeV of data. Near-threshold phase-space suppression accounts for the narrow width. The same machinery puts the $1^+$ partner at 5755 MeV ($B^*K$-dominated), close to Nowak–Zahed's chiral-doubling $5747\pm2$ MeV. The $j_\ell=3/2$ states stay essentially compact. The authors give E1 widths of 45 keV for $B_{s0}^*\to B_s^*\gamma$ and ~55–58 keV for the $1^+\to B_s^{(*)}\gamma$ modes, which they propose as the Run 3 discovery channels.

Caveats:
- **Molecular content not quantified.** The paper does not report a $B_s^0\pi^0$ width. That is today's discriminating number against Albaladejo et al. and Sánchez-Illana et al. It also gives no explicit compositeness, despite promising to quantify the admixture.
- **Radiative widths may be mislabeled.** The quoted 58/55 keV values come from the Table 4 rows for $B'_{s1}(1P)$. The $B_{s1}(1P)$ rows, which correspond to the state assigned to 5747 in Table 3, give 50.9/34.6 keV.
- **Bare-state wavefunctions.** All radiative widths use quark-model wavefunctions, not the dressed ones.
- **$B_{s2}^*$ width is a fit, not a test.** The $^3P_0$ strength $\gamma_0$ was fitted to the $B_{s2}^*(5840)$ width, so reproducing its 1.5 MeV is not an independent check.
- **Residual offset.** The $0^+$ still misses by ~30 MeV.

### [arXiv:2610.07818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07818) — Fully charmed tetraquarks on the Lattice with controlled errors (Zhenyu Zhang et al.)

The headline result is a $J^P=0^+$ $cc\bar c\bar c$ state at $5934.8(13)$ MeV, $33.2(13)$ MeV below $\eta_c\eta_c$, and therefore strong-decay-stable. The authors discretize a standard OGE+linear potential model on a periodic lattice and diagonalize it with Lanczos ("LQPM"). This is a lattice-discretized quark model, not lattice QCD. Benchmarks are good:
- $\eta_c$, $J/\psi$ and the 2S states land within a few MeV of experiment.
- $\Omega_{ccc}=4817.3(9)$ MeV agrees with NNQS and lies below the variational GEM value of 4823.

They also find a $1^+$ only 10.9 MeV below $\eta_cJ/\psi$, which they flag as potential-dependent. The $2^+$ sits 24 MeV above $J/\psi J/\psi$, and they connect it to the CMS near-threshold $2^+$ structure. A bound $0^+$ contradicts essentially every prior model, including DeepQuark. The authors attribute their ~500 MeV lower masses to enforcing full antisymmetry with non-symmetric spatial parts. Restricting to symmetric spatial functions recovers NNQS (~6414–6508 MeV).

The "controlled errors" claim deserves scrutiny:
- **Coarse spacing.** The tetraquark is computed at $a\approx0.2$ fm, a spacing at which the supplement says charmonium has *not* converged. Finer spacings "do not converge."
- **Threshold mismatch.** The quoted 5968 MeV threshold equals twice the fine-lattice $\eta_c$ mass. The text does not show it evaluated at $a\approx0.2$ fm.
- **Small boxes, poor fits.** Only five boxes are used, up to 1.8 fm. The extrapolation adds 16 MeV beyond the largest box, and the fits have $\chi^2/\text{dof}=15$–28. The ±1.3 MeV error is fit-only.
- **Scattering-like color structure.** The color ratio converges to the 2:1 value characteristic of meson–meson scattering states.

### [arXiv:2610.08282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08282) — Relations between electroweak parameters to order $\alpha_s^3$ (Andreas Maier et al.)

Maier and Marquard complete the top-mediated four-loop QCD corrections to $\Delta\bar r$ and $\Delta\kappa$ beyond the leading $\Delta\rho$ term. They supply the missing pieces:
- the four-loop flavour-singlet polarization functions;
- top-mass corrections to the $bb$ correlator;
- a sixth low-energy term in the axial correlator.

The expansion in $M_Z^2/m_t^2$ is taken to $M_Z^{10}/m_t^{10}$, with negligible truncation error. The new $\alpha_s^3 m_t^{\le0}$ terms shift $M_W$ by ≈ −0.8 MeV with an on-shell top mass and ≈ −0.2 MeV in $\overline{\rm MS}$. For comparison, FCC-ee projects 0.16 MeV systematic and 0.18 MeV statistical uncertainty, so these terms are relevant at that precision.

Two points matter for projections:
- **Scheme dependence.** In $\overline{\rm MS}$ the subleading $\alpha_s^3\bar m_t^0$ term (−0.22 MeV) exceeds the formally leading $\alpha_s^3\bar m_t^2$ term (−0.03 MeV). The factor-of-four scheme spread at this order is itself a theory uncertainty at the FCC-ee scale.
- **Five loops needed.** Extrapolating the on-shell pattern, the authors expect $\alpha_s^4 M_t^2$ to contribute several hundred keV. That makes five-loop $\rho$ desirable.

Scope is pure QCD on top/bottom loops, and the numerical study neglects the $\Delta r$–$\Delta\bar r$ difference. Results are supplied in electronic form.
