## Four papers, one LZ event

LZ's single 248 keV nuclear recoil picked up four independent interpretations today, three of which land on nearly the same point: a ~1.1 TeV higgsino split by about 350 keV. Su et al. get there from generic endothermic dark matter ([arXiv:2609.01475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01475)), while Fan et al. ([arXiv:2609.01504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01504)) and Freese et al. ([arXiv:2609.01583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01583)) argue the higgsino case specifically, where the off-diagonal $Z$ coupling fixes the inelastic cross section rather than leaving it free. Agreement that tight suggests the event's kinematics are doing most of the work, not the models. Around it: last week's agentic-software wave acquires standards, and CMS posts a first on the Higgs width.

## Themes

### One event, four models
The cluster splits by mechanism, not conclusion. Wu et al. ([arXiv:2609.01590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01590)) add that the same 1.1 TeV thermal point fits the mild Fermi-LAT Galactic-centre preference and predicts a line near H.E.S.S. sensitivity. Lou et al. ([arXiv:2609.01592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01592)) take the other road — fermionic dark-matter absorption on xenon, a 247 MeV particle giving a monoenergetic 248 keV recoil — then recast KamLAND's neutron-emission channel and exclude their own benchmark. The self-inflicted null is the most useful paper of the five.

### Agentic tooling gets standards
Last week's HEPToolBench pair has turned into a program. Menzo et al. ([arXiv:2609.00107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00107)) argue the stable parts of agentic workflows should become versioned, citable "scientific operations" behind common protocols, with machine-readable contracts to stop independently built tools composing badly. Sun et al. ([arXiv:2609.01095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01095)) supply the empirical half: grounding generation in a ROOT software knowledge graph lifts first-attempt execution from 58.5% to 76.0% across 275 tasks, at ~1% extra cost. Young et al. ([arXiv:2609.00611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00611)) carry the same instinct into pre-training, one point-cloud recipe transferring across LArTPC, collider TPC, and water Cherenkov.

### A Higgs width, and things to build
CMS constrains $\Gamma_\mathrm{H}$ through H→γγ–continuum interference for the first time ([arXiv:2609.01385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01385)), reaching $<92$ MeV observed — the tightest on-shell bound to date, still far above the 4.1 MeV prediction. Citron et al. propose GRENDEL ([arXiv:2609.00152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00152)), 110 m of scintillator bars in the PX56 drainage gallery above CMS, reaching 0.2–15 GeV long-lived particles with no civil engineering.

### Strong CP, reviewed and probed
Ai takes stock of the strong-$CP$ problem ([arXiv:2609.00620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00620)), a convenient anchor for the thread running since last week's lattice pair. Paul et al. ([arXiv:2609.00123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00123)) propose axion-induced third-harmonic generation from colliding petawatt beams, tuned by beam angle rather than apparatus volume; Li et al. ([arXiv:2609.00128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00128)) find accreting axion clouds emit discrete lines at odd multiples of the bound-state energy.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2609.01385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01385) — Constraint on the Higgs boson width in the diphoton final state using signal-background interference in proton-proton collisions at $\sqrt{s}$ = 13 TeV (CMS Collaboration)

CMS turns the interference between $gg\to H\to\gamma\gamma$ and the continuum $gg\to\gamma\gamma$ amplitude into a width measurement, using 138 fb$^{-1}$ from 2016–2018. The interference distorts the $m_{\gamma\gamma}$ lineshape and shifts its peak by an amount that grows with $\Gamma_\mathrm{H}$, so fitting that shift across categories binned in diphoton $p_\mathrm{T}$ and BDT output constrains the width without ever leaving the on-shell region. The result is $\Gamma_\mathrm{H}<92$ MeV observed (138 expected) at 95% CL, against a SM prediction of 4.1 MeV and a previous best direct limit of 330 MeV.

Why it matters: the tightest existing number, $\Gamma_\mathrm{H}=3.0^{+2.0}_{-1.5}$ MeV, comes from comparing on-shell and off-shell $H\to VV$ rates — which assumes the couplings governing the two regimes are the same, exactly the assumption new physics would break. On-shell rates alone cannot do better on their own, because a width inflated by a factor $\alpha$ through undetectable decays is degenerate with rescaling every Higgs coupling by $1/\alpha^{1/4}$. Interference breaks that flat direction using on-shell data only, so the weaker limit is doing more model-independent work than the raw number suggests. Caveats: sensitivity still sits more than an order of magnitude above the SM value, and the dominant experimental systematics are photon energy scale and resolution — which can mimic the very peak shift being measured.

### [arXiv:2609.01504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01504) — Higgsino Above the Sea of Fog (JiJi Fan et al.)

Fan et al. read LZ's single $248\pm23\text{(stat)}\pm23\text{(sys)}$ keV event as inelastic higgsino scattering. Ordinary spin-independent elastic scattering cannot produce it: an event at 250 keV would have been preceded by thousands at lower recoil energy. Scattering between two nearly degenerate neutral states changes the kinematics, and the required minimum velocity — $v_\mathrm{min}\approx[350\sqrt{E_R/250\,\mathrm{keV}}\,(m_\chi+m_N)/m_\chi + 425\sqrt{250\,\mathrm{keV}/E_R}\,(\delta/350\,\mathrm{keV})]$ km/s — puts the scatterer squarely in the high-speed tail of the local halo. A 1.1 TeV thermal higgsino with $\delta\approx350$ keV works: under the Standard Halo Model the recoil spectrum starts near 170 keV, peaks around 200 keV, and integrates to 0.3 events in the 215–270 keV signal window.

The theoretical payoff is that the splitting is not a free parameter — it is induced by higgsino–gaugino mixing, so $\delta\approx350$ keV pushes binos and winos to $10^3$–$10^4$ TeV, and avoiding large logarithmic corrections to the higgsino mass drags the heavy Higgs doublet to the same scale: a heavy but *unsplit* spectrum, apart from the light higgsino. The caveats are the authors' own and substantial. The high-velocity tail is highly uncertain; under an LMC-influenced halo with a fatter tail, $\delta$ must instead exceed 350 keV. They also note the annual modulation peaks near June 2 and the event landed on June 16, 2023 — suggestive, but this is one event.

### [arXiv:2609.01592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01592) — Fermionic Dark Matter Absorption and the High-Energy Event in LUX-ZEPLIN (Yuanchao Lou et al.)

The same event by a different mechanism, and then a self-inflicted null. Lou et al. propose neutral-current absorption of a fermionic dark matter particle on a xenon nucleus: for $m_\chi\simeq247$ MeV the coherent process deposits a monoenergetic 248 keV nuclear recoil, matching LZ with no appeal to the velocity tail. But the momentum transfer, $q\simeq m_\chi\sim p_F^\mathrm{Xe}\simeq262$ MeV, resolves individual nucleons, so an incoherent single-nucleon knockout channel with a broad 200 keV–100.2 MeV spectrum comes along unavoidably. That correlated coherent–incoherent pair is the model's distinctive signature, and the primary knockouts are argued to fail standard LZ selection cuts, keeping the low-energy spectrum clean. One effective coupling at $\Lambda\simeq11.5$ TeV, $\sigma_{\chi N}^\mathrm{NC}\simeq1.07\times10^{-46}$ cm$^2$, accounts for the single event.

Then they go looking where the incoherent channel would show. Recasting KamLAND's neutron-emission channel $\chi+{}^{12}\mathrm{C}\to\nu+n+{}^{11}\mathrm{C}^*$ at $m_\chi=247.5$ MeV yields 90% CL bounds of $\Lambda_{90}^V\simeq80$ TeV and $\Lambda_{90}^S\simeq73$ TeV — both far above the 11.5 TeV the LZ interpretation requires, excluding the benchmark for vector and scalar operators alike. The authors call these bounds preliminary and valid "within the present signal and detector response treatment," but the gap is wide. Worth reading precisely because it builds the case and then dismantles it, and because it shows large-volume scintillators now constrain xenon-anomaly interpretations.
