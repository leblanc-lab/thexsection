## The LZ event, read elastically

Since September, almost every model of the 248 keV LZ recoil has used inelastic scattering, and each one has been built to survive solar capture. Bera et al. [arXiv:2611.15957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15957) drop the mass splitting entirely. Their dark matter is a Majorana fermion whose only electromagnetic interaction is an anapole moment, and the momentum dependence alone hardens the recoil spectrum enough to fit the event. The same coupling gives the relic abundance at ~300 GeV, and the model predicts more events at lower energies, which the full LZ run will test. The abstract checks collider and indirect-detection limits but doesn't mention solar capture, which is where earlier readings of this event were excluded. Lin et al. [arXiv:2611.16689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16689) recompute solar capture itself, and Dai et al. [arXiv:2611.16553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16553) add a gauged $B-L$ two-scalar model with a three-bin likelihood fit; the 11-03 Higgs-portal model gave rate estimates but no likelihood.

## Themes

### Loop counts go up
- Li et al. [arXiv:2611.15366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15366) compute QED charge, mass and field renormalization analytically at six loops for arbitrary $N_f$, and say the methods open the way to six-loop QCD.
- Gehrmann et al. [arXiv:2611.15836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15836) give full-color three-loop $V$+jet amplitudes, whose finite remainders live in a much smaller function space than the underlying integrals.
- Two results aimed at lepton colliders: Haisch et al. [arXiv:2611.15807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15807) compute $S$ and $T$ at two loops in the $Z_2$ singlet model three independent ways, and Devoto et al. [arXiv:2611.17171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17171) supply two-loop mixed QCD–EW corrections to $e^+e^-\to b\bar b$.

This continues September's precision-QCD run, now carried into electroweak observables.

### Grover for haloscopes
Bogorad et al. [arXiv:2611.14641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14641) show that wavelike dark matter can act as a repeatable phase gate over many coherence times. A Grover search over $N_{\rm bins}$ frequency bins then gains a factor of $\sqrt{N_{\rm bins}}$. Bao et al. [arXiv:2611.14629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14629) work out a transmon version; reaching the QCD axion band takes $10^6$ entangled qubits. Soto Alcaraz et al. [arXiv:2611.15878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15878) use a two-node quantum network to tell an isotropic halo from a Gaia-like anisotropic one, which works only once Earth's rotation sweeps the baseline across the sky. This adds a quantum-algorithm route to the haloscope readout work covered on 11-05.

### Supercooling, with consistency checks
Zhang et al. [arXiv:2611.14593](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14593) find that three different criteria for a transition completing share the same boundary. Close to that boundary, though, a homogeneous cosmology can't be justified, so the claimed arbitrarily strong monopole suppression doesn't hold up. Chaudhuri [arXiv:2611.16845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16845) finds a fast, strongly supercooled electroweak step in scale-invariant models. The LISA signal depends on the acoustic GW description still applying in that regime. Cassidy et al. [arXiv:2611.15856](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15856) find that GW and di-Higgs predictions survive added dimension-six operators, but the Higgs trilinear does not. Same lesson as 11-03: a GW forecast is only as good as its transition dynamics.

### Top SMEFT, two ways to slice
ATLAS [arXiv:2611.16347](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16347) fits 33 Wilson coefficients across nine top-associated processes and sees no significant deviation. It also compares generic object-counting categories with process-targeted regions; the targeted regions give better sensitivity for most coefficients. Kumari et al. [arXiv:2611.15274](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15274) make a parallel point about taggers. Taggers with similar AUC differ by up to 3× in discovery significance on CMS Open Data, and the particle transformers extend the $Z'$ mass reach by 400 GeV.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2611.15957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15957) — The Curious Case of Anapole Dark Matter at LZ: Minimal, Elastic and Testable (Sourav Bera et al.)

A Majorana fermion whose only electromagnetic coupling is a hypercharge anapole moment, $a_\chi\,\bar\chi\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\chi\,\partial^\nu B_{\mu\nu}$, fits LZ230616 with purely elastic scattering. The interaction reduces to the NREFT operators $\mathcal{O}_8$ and $\mathcal{O}_9$. Their momentum dependence keeps the spectrum falling slowly at high recoil, which compensates for the halo-tail suppression. The fit is a binned Poisson likelihood over LZ's public 2.84 t·yr data, with three S1c windows of 32 $\delta$ bins each. The background is fixed to LZ's post-fit model, and the signal templates come from rescaling LZ's $\mathcal{L}_{10}$ templates with NEST. Local significance from toys rises from 1.8σ at 100 GeV to 2.9σ at 300 GeV and saturates near 3.1σ above 500 GeV. The thermal-relic contour crosses the best-fit line at $a_\chi \simeq 1.05\times10^{-6}$ GeV$^{-2}$ and $m_\chi \simeq 300$ GeV, or 215–422 GeV within the 68% band. That point is allowed by an ATLAS 139 fb$^{-1}$ monojet recast and by white-dwarf heating limits.

Solar neutrinos are not computed here. The authors cite a celestial-probes study that included them and state that the white-dwarf limits are the most stringent in this mass range.

The prediction is sharp. At the full LZ exposure of 15.3 t·yr, the benchmarks reach 2.6–3.2σ (Asimov), mostly from the 250–500 phd window, which is nearly background-free and does not contain the observed event. XLZD would reach ≳9σ (11.7σ at 300 GeV).

Caveats:
- **Low predicted count where the event sits.** At current exposure the model predicts only 0.064 events in the 500–600 phd window, against a background of 0.0097.
- **EFT validity.** The EFT sits near its unitarity limit at LHC energies.
- **UV completions are strained.** A tree-level vector completion needs $\epsilon g_\chi \sim \mathcal{O}(1)$, which implies a strongly coupled dark sector. A loop completion needs charged states below about 100 GeV.

### [arXiv:2611.16689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16689) — Dark matter evolution in stellar objects: A Boltzmann transport approach (Yen-Hsun Lin et al.)

The classical isothermal treatment of solar capture fails badly when nuclear scattering is rare. The method orbit-averages the Boltzmann equation into a master equation on an $(E, L)$ grid of 192×48 cells. It evolves capture, orbit-to-orbit scattering, evaporation and s-wave annihilation together, and is valid because there are far fewer than one collision per orbit.

For SD scattering at 100 GeV and $\sigma_{\chi p}=10^{-50}$ cm², captured DM never thermalizes. Its central density ends up more than six orders of magnitude below the isothermal value, because freshly captured particles sit on wide orbits, often reaching beyond the solar surface. The classical treatment overestimates the annihilation rate by more than 2× below about $10^{-46}$ cm² (SD) or $10^{-49}$ cm² (SI). SD is suppressed much more than SI because hydrogen transfers energy poorly to heavy DM. The evaporation mass matches the classical value down to about $10^{-47}$ cm² and falls below it at smaller cross sections. Orbit anisotropy inherited from the halo changes the annihilation rate by less than 4%.

For the inelastic Higgsino at 1.1 TeV, the result goes the other way. The full evolution confirms the equilibrium relation $2\Gamma_A \simeq C_c$. Including the thermal motion of solar nuclei opens capture channels that are closed for nuclei at rest. With the IceCube 10-year $W^+W^-$ limit ($\Gamma_A < 2.9\times10^{19}$ s$^{-1}$), the bound becomes $\delta \gtrsim 592$ keV, compared with 552 keV for nuclei at rest. The heavier elements earlier studies added (Zn, Ge, Pb, U) shift this by only about 0.1 keV.

Caveats:
- **Conservative channel choice.** The neutrino signal uses only $W^+W^-$, which has a branching ratio of 0.54.
- **Simplified Sun and grid.** The Sun is static, planets are ignored, and grid choices move $\Gamma_A$ by $\mathcal{O}(1)$.
- **Limited scope.** Cross sections are taken as velocity-independent SI/SD. The suppression results therefore cannot be carried over directly to momentum-dependent operators such as the anapole above.

### [arXiv:2611.16347](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16347) — Multi-approach SMEFT analyses of top-quark associated production in leptonic final states with ATLAS (ATLAS Collaboration)

*Abstract only.* ATLAS fits 33 dimension-six Wilson coefficients simultaneously across nine processes using 140 fb$^{-1}$ of Run 2 data:
- $t\bar t\gamma$, $t\bar tZ$, $t\bar tW$, $t\bar tH$, $t\bar tt\bar t$
- $tZq$, $tWZ$, $tHq$, $tHW$

The operators cover top–boson, four-quark and top–lepton interactions, including complex coefficients. Object definitions are harmonized across processes, and detector-level SMEFT effects are treated consistently. No significant deviation from the SM is found, and 95% intervals are set.

The methodological result is the comparison of two event categorizations: generic object counting versus orthogonal regions targeted at individual processes. The targeted design gives the best sensitivity for most coefficients. The abstract does not give the size of the gain, the coefficients for which the generic categories do as well or better, or any numerical limits.
