## The muon collider's neutrino byproduct

The freshest thing today is a coincidence: two independent groups posted re-evaluations of the neutrino flux at a 10 TeV muon collider, and both conclude the intensity is far larger than earlier estimates — enough to make the machine an accidental neutrino factory. Around that, the collider-quantum-information program keeps spreading into new final states, ATLAS delivers a genuine first in forward-rapidity jet suppression, and the numerical bulk sits, as it did over the weekend, in hadron spectroscopy. One pointed reanalysis paper is worth flagging for anyone who cites the ATLAS doubly-charged-Higgs limit.

## Themes

### Neutrinos as a muon-collider byproduct
Burk et al. [arXiv:2608.02714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02714) find fluxes around the interaction region nearly two orders of magnitude above prior estimates for the latest 10 TeV design, with tau-neutrino events reachable in a ton-scale forward detector. Choi et al. [arXiv:2608.02718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02718) build a dedicated Monte Carlo (MINT) for the beam dynamics and forecast $\mathcal{O}(10^9)$ interactions per year in a ~3-tonne volume 5 km out, plus the secondary muons and wrong-sign backgrounds that come with it. A new thread — the neutrino-physics case for a machine sold as an energy-frontier precision tool.

### Collider quantum information, still spreading
Ai et al. [arXiv:2607.28346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28346) show that missing neutrinos need not kill spin tomography: casting the visible measurement as a coarse-grained POVM, they prove that in $\tau^+\tau^-\to\pi\pi+\nu\bar\nu$ only the antisymmetric $C_{nr}-C_{rn}$ combination is unidentifiable, and give a template-free unfolding. Zhang et al. [arXiv:2608.03131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03131) compute Bell variable, concurrence and negativity for $\Lambda\bar\Lambda$/$\Sigma\bar\Sigma$/$\Lambda_c\bar\Lambda_c$ at BESIII. This is the same program that carried Erdogan et al.'s $t\bar t$ tomography two days ago, now reaching hyperons and invisible final states.

### Heavy-ion, forward and small
ATLAS [arXiv:2608.03261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03261) reports the first inclusive jet suppression at forward rapidity ($2.8<|y|<3.6$) in Pb+Pb, finding stronger suppression at $2.8<|y|<3.2$ than in the central region — a new handle on the geometry and flavor dependence of energy loss. Pucillo for ALICE [arXiv:2608.02874](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02874) adds first strangeness-enhancement results in OO, extending the "how small can QGP be" question that LHCb's $\Upsilon$ and ALICE's charm $v_2$ opened over the weekend. Fukushima et al. [arXiv:2608.03945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03945) dissect why longitudinal spin-polarization sign depends on the reference-frame formulation.

### Hadron spectroscopy bulk
The day's count leans on molecular and exotic-state model work: $D^*\bar D^*$/$B^*\bar B^*$ bound states via Bethe-Salpeter (Li et al. [arXiv:2608.03104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03104)), Faddeev calculations for $\phi NN$/$J/\psi NN$ (Zhang [arXiv:2608.03162](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03162)), $X(2370)$ as a glueball (Wang [arXiv:2608.03362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03362)), and $X(6900)$ nuclear photoproduction as a structure probe (Paryev [arXiv:2608.03374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03374)). Plus real data: LHCb's new $B_c^+\to DX$ measurements [arXiv:2608.03453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03453), including a first observation.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2608.02718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02718) — The Forward Neutrino Flux and its Secondaries at a 10 TeV Muon Collider (Ju-Yeol Choi et al.)

Using a purpose-built Monte Carlo (MINT) that propagates and decays muons through a realistic IMCC hybrid lattice (v0.9 straight section + v0.6 arcs), Choi et al. characterize the forward $\nu$ flux from the $\pm180$ m straight sections around the IP. The headline: $\sim7\times10^{18}$ $\nu+\bar\nu$/year through a benchmark 3.2-tonne, 43 g/cm² fiducial volume at 5 km, giving $\mathcal{O}(10^9)$ neutrino-nucleus interactions/year plus $\sim5\times10^5$ $\nu$-$e$ scattering events. Crucially, the beam divergence ($\mathcal{O}(0.1$–$1)$ mrad) dominates over the intrinsic $1/\gamma\sim0.02$ mrad opening angle, **washing out the "prism" energy-angle correlation** claimed in preliminary studies — a concrete correction to earlier flux modeling. Secondary muons from CC interactions in upstream rock give $\sim2$ penetrating, >99.9%-polarized muons ($\langle E_\mu\rangle\simeq1.2$ TeV) per bunch crossing per detector — a background overlapping the neutrino signal in time, but also a potential wide-band polarized TeV muon source. Secondary $\nu_\tau$ (charm, ITD, tridents, $D_s^*$) yield only $\sim0.2$ CC events/year: unobservable.

Why it matters: this is the concrete simulation behind today's neutrino-factory framing, quantifying both the physics opportunity (electroweak precision, HNL searches reaching $|U_{\mu N}|^2\simeq2\times10^{-11}$ near 15 GeV, dipole couplings to $\sim4\times10^{-10}$ GeV⁻¹) and the radiation/background reality. Caveats are stated plainly: the lattice is optimized for luminosity, not neutrinos; the BSM reach curves are event-yield targets *before* backgrounds and efficiencies; and recovering the prism effect would need a ~100× larger $\beta$-function. Results are declared compatible and complementary to the independent Burk et al. study.

### [arXiv:2607.28346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28346) — Collider Spin Tomography with Missing Neutrinos (Tengyu Ai et al.)

Ai et al. recast spin tomography under invisible particles as a coarse-grained POVM on the production density matrix, proving that information loss is governed by the *kernel* of the visible-data map, not by the number of kinematic solutions. Applied analytically to $e^+e^-\to\tau^+\tau^-\to\pi^+\pi^-\nu\bar\nu$, the twofold ambiguity leaves exactly **one** unidentifiable direction: the antisymmetric transverse correlation $C_{nr}-C_{rn}$. The differential rate, both polarization vectors, and the remaining fourteen spin coefficients are all recoverable from the pion distribution alone. They build a template-free, self-consistent fixed-point unfolding where fold weights are set by the data-reconstructed density matrix rather than a flat 50/50 average. Closure tests on $10^8$ events ($\sim9.3$ ab⁻¹) show the flat average is visibly biased even in the production-angle distribution, while iterations converge to truth on the identifiable subspace — confirmed in both SM and a deliberately large anomalous tau-dipole benchmark ($a_\tau=0.01-0.02i$), proving it is not an SM template fit. Power-iteration/Arnoldi analysis pins the sole marginal ($\lambda\simeq1$) mode to $C_{nr}^-$, with the next eigenvalue $\simeq0.96$ (contracting).

Why it matters: this extends the collider-quantum-information program (following the $t\bar t$ tomography flagged two days ago) to final states with missing energy — tau pairs, but also, they note, fully leptonic $WW$ and dileptonic $t\bar t$. Entanglement/CHSH claims survive: concurrence and CHSH are reported as positivity-constrained intervals over the null direction, still yielding truth-consistent sensitivity. Caveat: the clean one-dimensional kernel is specific to the $\pi\nu$ channel; multi-hadron modes require numerical POVMs, and results are closure tests, not full sensitivity projections with detector effects.

### [arXiv:2608.03261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03261) — Measurement of forward jet suppression in Pb+Pb collisions at $\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=5.02$ TeV with ATLAS (ATLAS Collaboration)

(Abstract only.) ATLAS reports the **first** inclusive jet $R_{AA}$ at forward rapidity in heavy-ion collisions, for anti-$k_t$ $R=0.4$ jets with $p_T>74$ GeV in $2.8<|y|<3.2$ and $3.2<|y|<3.6$, using 1.72 nb⁻¹ Pb+Pb and 255 pb⁻¹ $pp$ at 5.02 TeV. Jets are significantly suppressed; suppression increases monotonically from peripheral to central collisions with only mild $p_T$ dependence. The key comparison: suppression is **stronger** in $2.8<|y|<3.2$ than in the previously measured central region $|y|<2.8$. In the most forward bin ($3.2<|y|<3.6$) the precision is insufficient for a conclusive statement.

Why it matters: forward jets probe more asymmetric parton kinematics — different flavor mix (more quark-initiated, higher-$x$), different path geometry, and different local medium density — so the stronger-than-central suppression is a genuinely new lever on parton energy loss, complementing today's small-system QGP thread (ALICE OO strangeness, weekend $\Upsilon$/charm-$v_2$ results). Caveat visible in the abstract: the most-forward bin's limited statistics leaves the extrapolation of the trend to $|y|\sim3.4$ open, so the flavor/geometry interpretation rests mainly on the $2.8<|y|<3.2$ interval.
