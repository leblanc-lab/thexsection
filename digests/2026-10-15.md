## B→DP wants 60% SU(3) breaking

Bhattacharya et al. [arXiv:2610.20611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20611) refit $\bar B\to DP$ together with $\bar B\to\bar DP$, adding the direct and indirect CP asymmetries that appear when $D^0$ and $\bar D^0$ interfere. The disagreement with the SU(3)$_F$-limit SM stays above 5σ. The SU(3)$_F$ breaking needed to absorb it grows from ~20% with branching ratios alone to ~60% with all observables. At that size, "breaking" stops being a small correction to the flavor-symmetry expansion. The result sits beside August's $B\to PP$ SU(3)$_F$ puzzle, now on the charm side. Elsewhere, $|V_{cb}|$ gets a proposal that avoids $B$ decays entirely. LZ drops to one paper after yesterday's five.

## Themes

### Flavor symmetry and $|V_{cb}|$
Bhattacharya et al. supply the tension. Marzocca [arXiv:2610.18961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18961) proposes measuring $|V_{cb}|$ through $b$-tagged charged-current DIS, $e^-c\to\nu_e b$. He projects ~0.8% at LHeC and 0.7% at FCC-eh, with systematics unrelated to the inclusive/exclusive fight in semileptonic decays. The EIC reaches only 20–30%. De Fazio et al. [arXiv:2610.19917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19917) summarize the Frascati semileptonic workshop on CKM status, form factors and LFU, which is a useful reference while $R(D^{(*)})$ and $|V_{cb}|$ both remain open.

### Quantum information in scattering
Chu et al. [arXiv:2610.18955](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18955) note that stabilizer Rényi entropy depends on an unspecified phase frame for helicity states. They define a rephasing-invariant version, and tree-level gluon scattering saturates its two- and three-channel ceilings. This gives the season's "magic" thread a convention-independent definition. Ji et al. [arXiv:2610.19711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19711) treat soft-photon decoherence of a pion-pair density matrix as a flow in detector resolution. They tie the Lindblad jump operator to the soft Wilson line and recover real–virtual cancellation at the density-matrix level.

### Hadron-structure inputs
Yang et al. [arXiv:2610.19415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19415) respond to the Fantômas claim of underestimated small-$x$ pion PDF uncertainties. With an extended H1+ZEUS leading-neutron dataset, they strongly disfavor a vanishing pion gluon. Alexandrou et al. [arXiv:2610.20362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20362) add $N\sigma$ interpolators to suppress excited states in $\sigma_{\pi N}$. Since $\sigma_{\pi N}$ was the hadronic input Bao et al. hedged with FLAG-vs-Roy–Steiner last week, the eventual number matters.

### Dark-matter detectors, other jobs
Amaral et al. [arXiv:2610.18971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18971) use solar-neutrino data from LZ, XENONnT and PandaX-4T. They report that these data already constrain part of the complex-NSI explanation of the NOvA–T2K tension. COSINE-100 (Kim et al. [arXiv:2610.19668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19668)) pushes NaI to a 0.49 keV threshold with an MLP noise filter. Deka [arXiv:2610.18974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18974) is the day's only LZ paper: a 45 GeV exothermic freeze-in model, worked through several non-instantaneous reheating histories.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2610.20611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20611) — A global analysis of $\bar B\to DP$ and $\bar B\to\bar DP$ decays (Bhubanjyoti Bhattacharya et al.)

Adding $D^0$–$\bar D^0$ interference observables pushes the SU(3)$_F$-breaking requirement from ~20% to ~60%. The SU(3)$_F$-limit fit to all 25 observables (15 branching ratios, 6 direct and 4 time-dependent CP asymmetries, with $\beta$, $\beta_s$, $\gamma$ fixed to world averages) gives $\chi^2_{\min}/{\rm dof}=153.4/12$. The branching ratios alone are already bad: $\bar B\to DP$ gives 105/5. Splitting by strangeness, the $\Delta S=0$ fit is excellent (0.2/1), but $|T_1/T_0|-1=0.15\pm0.03$ and $|C_1/C_0|-1=0.36\pm0.13$.

Allowing first-order breaking diagrams but capping their ratios at 30% leaves $\chi^2_{\min}=17.0$, and every ratio saturates the cap. The dominant pulls come from $\mathcal A_{CP}(B^-\to D_{CP}\pi^-)$ ($\chi^2=10.3$) and $S(\bar B^0\to D^-\pi^+)$. Raising the cap to 60% brings $\chi^2_{\min}$ down to 2.8. The remainder is irreducible:
- ~1.0 from an isospin-related pair of branching ratios;
- ~1.4 from the measured $S(D^\pm\pi^\mp)$ central values exceeding the bound $2\kappa/(1+\kappa^2)=0.0341$ that their own branching ratios imply.

Alongside the ~1000% needed in $B\to PP$, this is a second system where the symmetry expansion is under strain.

Caveats:
- The 30% and 60% fits use 35 parameters for 25 observables, so there are no degrees of freedom. The case for >30% breaking rests on cap saturation and the $\chi^2$-vs-$R$ scan.
- Many fitted ratios are poorly determined (e.g. $r_{T_1}=0.6\pm0.5$).
- The $\bar B\to\bar DP$ sector has only five measured branching ratios.
- Isospin breaking is ignored.

### [arXiv:2610.18961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18961) — Measuring $V_{cb}$ at electron-proton colliders (David Marzocca)

The projection is a sub-percent $|V_{cb}|$ from $b$-tagged charged-current DIS. The fit uses binwise ratios of $b$-tagged to flavour-inclusive CC counts in $(x,y)$. It profiles projected PDFs, the other CKM elements, top background and tag calibration, with no $|V_{cb}|$ prior or unitarity input. Projected precision:

| Collider | Luminosity | Unpolarised | Polarised |
|---|---|---|---|
| LHeC | 1 ab$^{-1}$ | 0.89% | 0.79% ($P_e=-0.8$) |
| FCC-eh | 2 ab$^{-1}$ | — | 0.67% |
| EIC | — | — | 22–32% |

At the EIC, $|V_{cb}|=0$ remains allowed within 3σ. For comparison, the PDG gives $|V_{cb}|_{\rm excl}=39.5\pm0.5$ and $|V_{cb}|_{\rm incl}=42.0\pm0.5$ (×10⁻³), so the LHeC number would be competitive with orthogonal systematics.

Calibration, not statistics, sets the floor. At the LHeC the error grows from 0.27% (statistics only) to 0.49% with CKM, PDF and top uncertainties, then to 0.79% once tag calibration is added. The $b$-efficiency calibration enters as roughly $\delta_b/2$; its correlation with $\mu_{cb}$ is −64%. Halving calibration widths gives 0.60%, and a better tagger on top gives 0.55% at the LHeC and 0.40% at FCC-eh. Using today's PDF4LHC21 instead of projected PDFs degrades the LHeC result to 1.0%. Mistag-shape uncertainties of 5% and 10% give 0.91% and 1.0%.

Caveats:
- The calculation is Born-level with flat tag responses.
- Radiative $b$ production and photoproduction are not treated.
- The neutral-current background is only modelled via an assumed electron-loss probability.

### [arXiv:2610.18971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18971) — Direct Detection of Solar Neutrinos to Probe Tensions and Break Degeneracies in Long-Baseline Neutrino Experiments (Dorian W. P. Amaral et al.)

For nucleon-only NSI, current LZ and XENONnT $^8$B CE$\nu$NS data already exclude part of the complex-$\mathcal E_{e\tau}$ region that resolves the NOvA–T2K tension ($|\mathcal E|\sim0.2$, $\phi\sim3\pi/2$). They do not yet reach the $\mathcal E_{e\mu}$ region.

A 200 t·yr xenon detector (XLZD or PandaX-xT) with a 1 keV$_{\rm nr}$ threshold and 6% $^8$B flux uncertainty would nearly cover the $\mathcal E_{e\tau}$ 1σ region. For electron-only couplings, LZ's ER data are not yet sensitive.

The degeneracy argument is structural. Oscillations see only differences of diagonal couplings, while scattering sees absolute couplings. Even after profiling over $\mathcal E_{\mu\mu}$, current NR (and, for electron-only couplings, LZ ER) limits cut into DUNE's degenerate $(|\mathcal E_{e\tau}|,\mathcal E_{ee}-\mathcal E_{\mu\mu})$ region. They do not reach the $\mathcal E_{\tau\tau}-\mathcal E_{\mu\mu}$ region.

Caveats:
- Results depend strongly on the assumed $(\eta,\varphi)$ coupling configuration.
- The NR analyses assume no background, with the 12% $^8$B flux uncertainty dominant.
- XENONnT ER data are excluded because its pp flux, $(10.2\pm2.0)\times10^{10}$ cm⁻²s⁻¹, sits 2.6σ from Borexino. The NSI region it prefers is largely excluded by LZ.
