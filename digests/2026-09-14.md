## The higgsino, from halo back to Run 2

For three weeks the higgsino has been a xenon story — one 248 keV recoil, fit and refit. Today Athron et al. [arXiv:2609.12914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12914) put it back where it is actually constrained: a global fit over 34 Run 2 LHC searches and 63 measurements. LZ traffic has meanwhile thinned to a handful of model-building follow-ups, and the day's real breadth sits elsewhere — a gravitational-wave phase-transition cluster, a strong pair of haloscope results, and a genuine CMS light-pseudoscalar search. The month-long recoil spectacle is losing its grip on the front page.

## Themes

### The LZ event thins to model-building
Three follow-ups arrived, all trying to keep inelastic DM viable against the solar-capture bound. Okada et al. [arXiv:2609.13038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13038) use a scotogenic $U(1)_X$ with isospin violation to suppress solar capture while keeping the xenon signal; Cabo-Almeida et al. [arXiv:2609.13130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13130) break the mass–cross-section relation with low-reheating freeze-in. Lee et al. [arXiv:2609.12045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12045) stand out for a genuinely different mechanism — invisible neutron-pair annihilation into a baryon-number-2 scalar, giving a monochromatic recoil with a common reconstructed mass across isotopes. The escape-hatch pattern continues, but the volume has dropped sharply from last week.

### Electroweakinos, confronted with the LHC
Athron et al. [arXiv:2609.12914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12914) deliver the most complete collider summary of the MSSM electroweakino sector to date: for a light bino, $m_{\tilde\chi_2^0}\gtrsim760$ GeV; adding a light gravitino pushes exclusions toward 1 TeV, though a Higgsino-dominated $\tilde\chi_1^0$ needs only ~650 GeV. Individual ATLAS/CMS compressed-spectrum excesses can each be fit, but not simultaneously. This is the empirical backdrop the LZ-higgsino fits have mostly been ignoring.

### Gravitational waves as a dark-sector ledger
A real cluster. Puchades-Ibáñez et al. [arXiv:2609.12098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12098) compute NLO nucleation for a supercooled transition and find it shifts the PTA-inferred gauge coupling by 9–13% — a concrete warning about first-order-PT interpretations of NANOGrav. An et al. [arXiv:2609.12867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12867) use LVK O1–O4a to constrain inflationary PBH mechanisms via scalar-induced GWs, already beating conventional abundance bounds. Alongside: Khalaf et al. [arXiv:2609.12052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12052) probe ultralight scalars through superradiance in LVK low-mass-ratio mergers, and Betancourt Kamenetskaia et al. [arXiv:2609.12806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12806) reconstruct a DM equation of state from inspiral dynamical friction. Ghosh's GW atlas [arXiv:2609.12664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12664) is explicitly phenomenological.

### Haloscopes and axion flavor
The axion detection wing from last week keeps producing. The DALI collaboration posts both a dark-photon exclusion — $\chi\lesssim6.8\times10^{-14}$ near 28.5 μeV, the strongest lab bound in that band [arXiv:2609.12775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12775) — and the resonator architecture behind it [arXiv:2609.12776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12776). On the model side, Cox et al. [arXiv:2609.12635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12635) build minimal flavoured DFSZ axions with unit domain-wall number, reconstructing the full quark Yukawa sector from measured masses and CKM.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2609.12914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12914) — What has the LHC told us about the electroweakino sector of the MSSM? (Peter Athron et al.)

GAMBIT's most complete collider recast of the MSSM electroweak sector to date: a frequentist global fit over 34 Run 2 LHC searches, 63 Run 2 measurements (via Contur), and LEP cross-section limits, scanning $M_1$, $M_2$, $\mu$, $\tan\beta$ with all other sparticles decoupled. The headline is that a genuine general bound now exists where the 2018 fit found none — for a light bino $\tilde\chi_1^0$, exclusion reaches $m_{\tilde\chi_2^0}\gtrsim760$ GeV in both the $(m_{\tilde\chi_2^0},m_{\tilde\chi_1^0})$ and chargino planes. Adding a light ($m_{3/2}=1$ eV) gravitino pushes exclusions to $(m_{\tilde\chi_1^0},m_{\tilde\chi_2^0})\sim(900,1000)$ GeV, but a Higgsino-dominated LSP survives down to $\approx650$ GeV. The much-discussed ATLAS soft-dilepton excess ($\Delta m\sim20$ GeV) is reproduced as a weak preference near $m_{\tilde\chi_2^0}\approx200$ GeV, but the CMS zero-lepton and soft-lepton searches disfavour exactly the ATLAS-preferred region — no scenario fits both simultaneously.

Why it matters: this is the empirical backdrop the month of LZ-higgsino fits has largely ignored. The Higgsino-degenerate corner that survives at ~650 GeV is precisely where compressed dark-matter scenarios live, and closing it at small $\Delta m$ is conceded to be "very challenging" even at HL-LHC. Honest caveats: signal yields use LO+LL (not NLO) cross-sections, monojet and long-lived-particle searches are omitted, the $b$-jet regions of ATLAS_2BoostedBosons are not implemented, and the $\tilde G$-EWMSSM best fit rests on a 36 fb$^{-1}$ CMS diphoton excess that newer full-Run-2 photon searches do not corroborate.

### [arXiv:2609.12683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12683) — Search for a light pseudoscalar Higgs boson in boosted $\mu\mu\tau\tau$ final states (CMS Collaboration)

CMS searches $H\to aa\to\mu\mu\tau\tau$ in 138 fb$^{-1}$ of 13 TeV data, where the large $m_H$–$m_a$ hierarchy collimates each $\tau\tau$ pair. The key methodological advance is dedicated boosted-ditau reconstruction: modified HPS seeding for $\tau_\mu\tau_h$/$\tau_e\tau_h$ (efficiency gains up to factors of 2–4 at high $m_H$), plus a new deep-network tagger, DeepDiTau, that classifies AK4 jets as $\tau_h\tau_h$ at 70% efficiency and ~1% mistag — enabling the high-rate all-hadronic channel for the first time. Combining four $\tau\tau$ channels covers 94% of $a\to\tau\tau$ decays. No excess is seen. Model-independent 95% CL limits on $\sigma_H\times\mathcal{B}/\sigma_{\rm SM}$ reach $5\times10^{-5}$ to $2.7\times10^{-4}$ for $m_a=3.6$–21 GeV at $m_H=125$ GeV, and — for the first time at the LHC in $\tau\tau$ final states — extend to a heavy scalar up to $m_H=1$ TeV, where limits span $3.5\times10^{-4}$ to $1.5\times10^{-3}$ for $m_a=3.6$–50 GeV.

Why it matters: this is the day's genuine light-pseudoscalar collider result, complementary to the LZ/axion model-building. It sets the most stringent $\mathcal{B}(H\to aa)$ limits to date for $3.6<m_a<4.2$ GeV in Type-I/II 2HDM+S, improves on prior $\mu\mu\tau\tau$ results by factors 2–5, and opens the heavy-scalar $\to aa$ window entirely. Caveats: sensitivity degrades at low $m_a$ where decay products fully merge, $\Upsilon$ resonances contaminate near $m_a\approx10$ GeV, and above 4.2 GeV the charm-tagging analysis remains stronger.

### [arXiv:2609.12775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12775) — Constraints on Galactic Dark Photons from a DALI Prototype (Javier De Miguel et al.)

A proof-of-principle DALI haloscope — a 20-plate yttria-stabilized zirconia Fabry–Pérot resonator read out by an antenna, run at fixed $\lambda/8$ spacing with $Q_L\approx2200$ near 6.907 GHz and $T_{\rm sys}\simeq59$ K — reinterprets 36 hours of data (originally an axion search) as a dark-photon search. No magnetic field is needed since kinetic mixing converts dark photons at the dielectric discontinuities. After baseline removal, iterative RFI masking, grand-spectrum stacking with the lab-frame line shape, and an end-to-end injection efficiency of $\eta_{\rm e2e}\simeq0.60$, no significant excess survives. Fourteen retained clusters all fall below adopted confidence and are flagged for rescan. The result excludes kinetic mixing $\chi\lesssim6.8\times10^{-14}$ at 28.54 μeV (6.902 GHz), across 88.1% of the unmasked 28.482–28.610 μeV band at 90% CL — claimed as the strongest laboratory bound in this frequency range.

Why it matters: it fits today's haloscope theme (alongside DALI's companion resonator paper), demonstrating that the open Fabry–Pérot architecture — whose cross section decouples from resonance frequency — can deliver competitive high-frequency limits with mature commercial hardware. As a magnet-free dark-photon channel, it is a cheap, fast complement to cavity haloscopes. Caveats are explicit: only a single frequency step (one plate spacing), a scaled-down prototype (~1:100 in area), an undercoupled antenna ($\beta\simeq0.36$), assumption that dark photons saturate the local density with randomly-oriented polarization ($\kappa^2=1/3$), and a residual $0.09\sigma$ baseline systematic.
