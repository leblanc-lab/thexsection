## Lattice precision, and code that writes itself

Two currents carry the day. The first is precision lattice: Boyle et al. [arXiv:2607.22358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22358) deliver the first lattice QCD+QED calculation of the structure-dependent radiative correction to the $\pi$ and $K$ lepton-universality ratios $R_{e/\mu}$ — the exact input PIONEER and NA62 need to interpret their measurements. The second is the automation thread the memory has tracked since LQCDMaster: two more papers hand LLM agents the keyboard, this time for amplitude computation and full SMEFT collider pipelines. Around them, ATLAS reports evidence for boosted $H\to\tau\tau$ ([arXiv:2607.22193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22193)) and Auger sharpens the muon puzzle into a concrete demand on hadronic interactions ([arXiv:2607.22323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22323)).

## Themes

### Agents at the console
Gu et al. [arXiv:2607.21629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21629) treat scattering amplitudes as programs and let coding agents search over analytic representations, reporting an 805× speed-up on a scored grid and matrix-element engines that close the gap to compiled MadGraph. Two more weak-scale searches get read through EFT, as the memory flagged this becoming standard. Duarte et al. [arXiv:2607.21711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21711) recast a CMS same-sign-dimuon heavy-neutrino search into the first exclusion limits on the agnostic $\nu$SMEFT parameter space. Brivio et al. ### Topological defects and their waves
Yamada [arXiv:2607.21701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21701) simulates global $\mathbb{Z}_N$ string networks with baryon-vertex junctions, finding scaling (not frustration) and $\Omega_{\rm GW}\propto\mu^2(N^2-1)^2$ for $N\lesssim8$. Kanda et al. [arXiv:2607.22481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22481) show global-monopole annihilation emits Nambu-Goldstone bosons peaking at horizon scale, with a pNG dark-matter estimate. A quiet continuation of the defect-network thread that has run through July.

## Deep dives

### [arXiv:2607.22358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22358) — First Lattice QCD Determination of Lepton-Flavor-Universality Ratios in Light-Meson Leptonic Decays (Peter Boyle et al.)

*(abstract only)* Boyle et al. deliver the first lattice QCD+QED calculation of the structure-dependent (SD) radiative correction to $R_{e/\mu}$ — the electron-to-muon leptonic-width ratio — for both $\pi$ and $K$, working at the physical pion mass and in the continuum limit. Using the infinite-volume reconstruction method with Coulomb-gauge photons to suppress statistical noise and finite-volume effects, they obtain SM predictions $R_{e/\mu}^\pi = 1.23501(10)\times10^{-4}$ and $R_{e/\mu}^K = 2.47653(34)\times10^{-5}$ — sub-permille precision on the previously dominant hadronic uncertainty.

Why it matters: $R_{e/\mu}$ is a benchmark LFU test, and the $O(0.1\%)$ SD correction was the limiting theory input as PIONEER and NA62 push toward matching precision. This calculation supplies exactly that ingredient from first principles, replacing model-dependent estimates. It is the SM number those experiments will now quote against. As an abstract-only entry, the systematic budget of the continuum/QED extrapolations isn't visible here, but the quoted errors already sit well below current experimental sensitivity.

### [arXiv:2607.22193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22193) — Boosted $H\to\tau\tau$ cross-section measurements with ATLAS (ATLAS Collaboration)

*(abstract only)* ATLAS reports evidence for Higgs production at $p_T^H > 300$ GeV in the di-hadronic-$\tau$ final state, combining full Run 2 (140 fb$^{-1}$, 13 TeV) with a partial Run 3 sample (162 fb$^{-1}$, 13.6 TeV). Standard hadronic-$\tau$ reconstruction loses efficiency when the two $\tau$s collapse into a single large-radius jet, so the analysis deploys dedicated boosted di-$\tau$ reconstruction and identification plus multivariate discriminants. The inclusive boosted measurement reaches observed (expected) significance of 3.8 (3.3)σ — evidence-level.

Why it matters: this extends $H\to\tau\tau$ sensitivity into a high-$p_T$ regime that probes the Higgs coupling structure and is sensitive to BSM contributions that grow with momentum transfer. Results are cast in the simplified-template-cross-section framework, with differential $p_T^H$ measurements for gluon fusion and an inclusive VBF cross section — directly usable for EFT interpretation. The observed significance modestly exceeds expectation, consistent with SM within the still-limited boosted statistics.

### [arXiv:2607.21629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21629) — Scattering Amplitudes as Programs: Self-Evolving Search for Theory and Event Generation (Yi Gu et al.)

Gu and Krippendorf treat amplitude evaluation as a program-search problem: a repository-scale coding agent (OpenAI Codex) acts as mutation operator inside an external generate–evaluate–select loop with *frozen* correctness gates and objectives, evolving executable amplitude programs. Three benchmarks. (1) Scaling: from a BCFW seed, search rediscovers Berends–Giele currents, then CSW, then a fixed-$k$ split-helicity transfer dynamic program with $O(n)$ source work, hitting an 805× geometric-mean speed-up over 34 timing cells. (2) Structural: NMHV rational forms are reorganised into R-invariant-style cells and glued supercells, lifting a cancellation-conditioning score from 0.376 to 0.605 and collapsing 8–13 terms to 2–3. (3) Tree complexity: across twenty QCD/EW processes, exact arithmetic falls 47.7× (geometric mean 11.68×), with a 5.9× post-hoc Python runtime gain. In matched pure-gluon tests ($n=4\to6$), the evolved engine's lead over a Sherpa-Comix exact-sum call grows 10×→277×, while its gap to `-O3`-compiled MadGraph Fortran narrows 77.7×→13.7×. Total cost: 115 candidates, ~$112, 8.15 agent-hours.

Why it matters: with HL-LHC projections assigning ~20% of ATLAS CPU to event generation, matrix-element kernels are a real bottleneck, and this shows agent-driven search can traverse genuine changes of *representation* — not just tuning — with fully archived, inspectable lineages. This is the digest's "agents orchestrating" thread made concrete. Caveats are stated plainly: these are tree-level, single-stack, isolated-kernel benchmarks; the Comix comparison targets exact colour/helicity sums (Comix can sample), no symbolic divisibility proof accompanies the gluing, and native generator integration plus end-to-end throughput remain untested.
