## The vibe today

A quiet-volume, high-variety day — 32 papers, no single collaboration bombshell, but an unusually rich spread of the field's standing preoccupations. If yesterday belonged to axions and quantum information, today the flavor physicists reclaimed the room: rare kaon and B decays, CKM unitarity, QED corrections to leptonic modes, and a lattice attack on $K_L\to\mu\mu$ all landed together, and the through-line is precision — squeezing new-physics reach out of well-measured channels rather than hunting for bumps.

The other headline is cosmological and faintly competitive: the "$H_0$ World Cup," a two-paper systematic bake-off pitting fourteen proposed Hubble-tension solutions against a common dataset. It's the kind of referee's-verdict paper the field has been waiting for as the tension crosses $7\sigma$, and it delivers a clear hierarchy. Around these two poles, the day carries a strong gravity-and-cosmology undertow — extra GW polarization modes from Weyl and gauge-field gravity theories, evaporating cosmologically-coupled black holes, topological-defect networks — and the axion thread the memory has tracked all fortnight keeps flowing, now in the guise of fields asked to do double cosmological duty.

And a genuinely nice sociological note: two independent open-data reanalyses posted today, one on CMS Run 2015D and one on DELPHI's 1990s $Z$ sample. The data-preservation ethos the DPHEP report championed last week is visibly paying dividends. A diffuse, tool-tending, breadth-over-depth morning — but a satisfying one.

## Themes

### Flavor precision and rare decays

The densest cluster today. Dery [arXiv:2607.13136](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13136) identifies a genuine CKM blind spot: a uniform rescaling of the $b$-column that unitarity-triangle fits are structurally insensitive to, since B physics over-constrains the triangle's angles but only calibrates its normalization. The punchline is that $|\varepsilon_K|$ already constrains this flat direction at the level of the direct measurement — promoting kaons to the leading probe of a scenario B factories can't see. Abada et al. [arXiv:2607.13142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13142) tackle the Belle II $B^+\to K^+\nu\nu$ excess head-on, showing that lepton-number-violating SMEFT operators can't explain it without severe fine-tuning of neutrino masses — unless one adds a light right-handed neutrino, which reopens the window. Cornella et al. [arXiv:2607.13144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13144) build a factorization theorem for QED corrections to $B^-\to\tau^-\bar\nu_\tau$, exploiting the heavy $\tau$ to get a structurally simpler EFT than the muon case, with naively-large $\Lambda_{\rm QCD}m_B/m_\tau^2$ terms cancelling. On the lattice side, Chao et al. [arXiv:2607.14077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14077) and En-Hung Chao's collaborators show how to fix the extra low-energy constants that appear in a three-flavor treatment of the two-photon $K_L\to\mu^+\mu^-$ long-distance contribution. Rounding it out, Prasad et al. [arXiv:2607.13743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13743) compute polarization observables in $\tau\to VV\nu$. Collectively this extends the precision-flavor lineage the memory has followed — the $b\to s\ell\ell$ and Belle II $b\to u$ work of the past two weeks — but pushes it toward CKM structure and QED systematics rather than the $C_9$ debate.

### The H0 World Cup

Schöneberg et al.'s twin papers [arXiv:2607.13282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13282), [arXiv:2607.13283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13283) are the day's most consequential cosmology entry: a systematic, common-framework contest of fourteen tension solutions against current CMB (ACT/SPT), BAO (DESI) and supernova data, judged by both frequentist and Bayesian criteria. The verdict is unambiguous — early dark energy and early modified gravity win, shifting the inferred $H_0$ toward $70$ and cutting the residual SH0ES discrepancy to $\sim2.5$–$3.6\sigma$, while varying the electron mass lands intermediate and both enhanced-radiation and late-time scenarios fail to beat $\Lambda$CDM. This is the natural sequel to the scattered dark-energy work the memory noted (the smooth sign-switching DE model of July 6, the DESI evolving-$w$ threads) — but now as a refereed tournament rather than individual pleas. Paper II's robustness gauntlet is the part model-builders will actually cite.

### Extra gravitational-wave polarizations

A coherent modified-gravity cluster, all asking the same question: does the LVK/LISA/Taiji era see polarization modes beyond GR's two tensor states? Ghilencea et al. [arXiv:2607.13146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13146) work out Weyl quadratic gauge gravity, finding two extra vector modes from the Weyl gauge field's transverse fluctuations, and stress the analysis must be done around a de Sitter background to get the GW content right. Xu et al. [arXiv:2607.13483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13483) target the transverse scalar (breathing) mode of Gravitational Quantum Field Theory with Taiji, using a null-response channel to isolate it cleanly. And Hua et al. [arXiv:2607.13066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13066) simulate a hybrid domain-wall/cosmic-string network in a $3:1$ $U(1)$ model, with preliminary GW spectra. Three different UV frameworks, one shared bet: gravitational-wave polarimetry as a discriminator among gauge theories of gravity. This isn't a thread the recent memory has tracked — it reads as a fresh coalescence.

### Axions and inflatons carrying cosmological loads

The axion diversification the memory has flagged for weeks continues, today with fields asked to solve more than one problem at once. Bittar et al. [arXiv:2607.13128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13128) identify a "weak axion" as the phase of the SM's anomalous $U(1)_{B+L}$, whose radiatively-stable potential — controlled by two Weinberg operators and a spontaneously-broken $S_3$ lepton symmetry — comes out parametrically near the dark-energy scale, with the amplitude set by the departure from tribimaximal mixing so that Hyper-K and DUNE's $\delta_{\rm CP}$/$\theta_{23}$ measurements directly test it. Deng et al. [arXiv:2607.13592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13592) use an axion-like inflaton's derivative coupling to produce heavy right-handed neutrinos during inflation and preheating, feeding non-thermal leptogenesis. And Grimaud et al. [arXiv:2607.13637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13637) assess ALP-photon oscillation signatures in stacked VHE gamma-ray spectra of AGN behind galaxy clusters, targeting the neV window with H.E.S.S.-class telescopes. The Bittar paper is the standout: tying dark energy to observable neutrino mixing is a rare falsifiable handle on quintessence.

### Open data, reanalyzed

Two independent analyses today lean entirely on archived data, a quiet vindication of the preservation push. Rasineni [arXiv:2607.13771](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13771) runs a mono-$Z$ dark-matter search on CMS Run 2015D open data using neural spline flows for likelihood-ratio scoring across a 37-dimensional feature space — methodologically novel even if statistics-limited. Fan et al. [arXiv:2607.13804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13804) mine DELPHI's 1992–1995 $Z\to$ hadrons sample for the first measurement of $\Xi^-/\bar\Xi^+$ production in energy-ranked jets, finding gluon/quark ratios consistent with JETSET. Neither is a blockbuster, but together they show open data functioning as intended — as a live resource for both new methods and new observables, exactly the payoff the DPHEP Global Report argued for last week.

### [arXiv:2607.13283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13283) — The $H_0$ World Cup II: A comprehensive competition between proposed Hubble tension solutions (Nils Sch\"oneberg et al.)

*(Abstract only.)* An updated systematic bake-off — the sequel to the "$H_0$ Olympics" — subjecting fourteen representative alternatives to $\Lambda$CDM to a common analysis of current CMB (ACT/SPT), BAO (DESI) and Type Ia SN data, now with the Hubble tension quoted above $7\sigma$. The contenders span four mechanisms: late-time expansion modifications, modified recombination, extra pre-recombination radiation, and early non-radiative energy injection. Every model is scored with both Bayesian and frequentist tension/performance metrics, with the neutrino mass sum left free, and tested for whether adding curvature or CPL dark energy improves the fit. Leading contenders face a robustness gauntlet: alternative CMB likelihoods and multipole cuts, different SN samples, LSS information, and BBN constraints.

Per the digest, the verdict favors early dark energy and early modified gravity (shifting $H_0$ toward 70 and cutting the SH0ES residual to $\sim2.5$–$3.6\sigma$), places varying-electron-mass models intermediate, and finds enhanced-radiation and late-time scenarios failing to beat $\Lambda$CDM. **Why it matters:** it converts a scattered literature of individual tension "solutions" into a refereed, common-framework ranking — the reference tournament model-builders will cite, with Paper II's robustness tests being the operative part. The honest caveat is intrinsic to any such contest: conclusions depend on the chosen dataset combination and priors, which is precisely why the robustness suite is included; results not visible here beyond the digest summary.

### [arXiv:2607.13128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13128) — Dark Energy and Neutrino Flavor from the Weak Axion (Pedro Bittar et al.)

Bittar and Wagner identify dark energy with a "weak axion" — the phase of a complex scalar carrying the SM's anomalous $U(1)_{B+L}$ charge — and show its potential can be radiatively stable at the meV scale. Electroweak-instanton contributions come out $\sim10$ orders of magnitude too small ($\Lambda_{\rm inst}^4\simeq(6\times10^{-6}\,{\rm eV})^4|c_{BL}|^3$), so the dominant potential arises from two inequivalent Weinberg operators generating an axion-dependent Majorana mass. A spontaneously-broken $S_3$ lepton permutation symmetry enforces the orthogonality condition ${\rm Tr}[M_\Phi^\dagger M_\star]=0$, which simultaneously kills the quadratic divergence and the leading $T_\nu^2 m_\nu^2$ thermal force, pushing the first axion-dependent Coleman-Weinberg term to quartic order in neutrino masses. The amplitude vanishes in the exact tribimaximal limit, so the observed departure sets the scale: $\Lambda_\nu\simeq2.3\,{\rm meV}\sqrt{|\cos\delta_{\rm CP}/0.52+\sin(\theta_{23}-\pi/4)/0.055|}$, landing in the 1–4 meV range for current oscillation data.

**Why it matters:** this is a rare falsifiable quintessence model — the dark-energy scale becomes a prediction from PMNS parameters, with $\delta_{\rm CP}$ accounting for $\sim80\%$ of the variance, so Hyper-K/DUNE directly test it. It also predicts $m_{\beta\beta}\simeq1.5$–3.7 meV (below next-gen reach) and behaves as instability-free thawing quintessence for $f\sim M_{\rm pl}$. **Caveats, stated frankly:** it does not address the cosmological-constant problem (vacuum energy assumed zero), faces a "severe axion-quality problem" requiring $|\Delta V_{\rm UV}|\lesssim\rho_{\rm DE}$, and needs near-Planckian $f$ — treated as low-energy assumptions rather than derived. NuFit and long-baseline (T2K/NOvA) best-fits currently map to different $\Lambda_\nu$.

### [arXiv:2607.13758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13758) — Evidence for sequential $\Upsilon(nS)$ suppression in light ion collisions (CMS Collaboration)

CMS reports the first measurements of $\Upsilon(1S,2S,3S)$ in oxygen-oxygen and $\Upsilon(1S,2S)$ in neon-neon collisions at $\sqrt{s_{NN}}=5.36$ TeV, with a $pp$ reference at the same energy (integrated luminosities 8.4 nb$^{-1}$, 0.81 nb$^{-1}$, 456 pb$^{-1}$). Double ratios of excited-to-ground-state yields relative to $pp$ come out below unity in OO: $D_{21}=0.664\pm0.055\pm0.007$, $D_{31}=0.392\pm0.077\pm0.023$, $D_{32}=0.59\pm0.12\pm0.04$ — the last below unity by 3.2$\sigma$, the headline "evidence" for sequential suppression in a light-ion system. In NeNe, $D_{21}=0.27\pm0.12\pm0.01$, lower than OO by 3.0$\sigma$. The OO $\Upsilon(3S)/\Upsilon(2S)$ ratio is compatible with PbPb across $p_T$ and integrated, supporting a common QGP-medium interpretation.

**Why it matters:** it extends state-dependent bottomonium suppression to $\langle N_{\rm part}\rangle\approx10$, a system size an order of magnitude below PbPb, reinforcing that QGP-like final-state effects need not require large volumes — bearing on the long-running debate over deconfinement in small systems. **Caveats the paper flags:** the $D_{32}$ significance is modest (3.2$\sigma$); event-activity trends "should be carefully interpreted" since multiplicity alone does not fix suppression (head-on OO can rival PbPb particle production per participant); and the `shinchon` transport model "significantly underestimates" the measured suppression, hinting at missing medium dynamics.
