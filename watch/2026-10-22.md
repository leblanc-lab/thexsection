## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2610.29439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29439) — How Far Can Foundation Models Be Distilled for Efficient Jet Tagging? (Jay Agarwal et al.)

This is a controlled distillation scan on the top-tagging benchmark. Six teachers (ParT, MIParT, L-GATr, L-GATr-slim, OmniLearned-S, JetClass-pretrained ParT-f.t.) are each distilled into five ~200k-parameter students, with standard Hinton KD and fixed α=0.7, T=2 for every pair. All 30 pairs improve AUC and R50. The mean R50 gain rises with teacher strength, from 9% (ParT teacher) to 40% (ParT-f.t.), independent of shared symmetry or architecture. The Lorentz-equivariant students learn more from non-equivariant ParT-f.t. than from L-GATr. To separate pretraining from strength, they use an L-GATr-slim ensemble trained from scratch, which matches OmniLearned-S in AUC; its students do about as well. This undercuts the pretraining attribution in the closest prior work (Benyas et al., OmniLearned-L → Deep Sets+GNN).

The scaling results are the headline:
- **20k L-GATr-slim** distilled from ParT-f.t. reaches AUC 0.98729, R50 596±38, R30 2517±122. That beats every from-scratch tagger in their comparison at 5.7 MFLOP/jet, versus 197–1868 for the teachers.
- **1.8k L-GATr-slim** matches ParT's AUC (0.98567 vs 0.98571) and exceeds its R30 (1872 vs 1586).
- **181k L-GATr-slim** matches ParT-f.t. (AUC 0.98772) at about 1/7 the FLOPs and roughly 2× GPU throughput.
- **1.2k Transformer** gains nothing from any teacher, a capacity-gap floor that the equivariant architecture avoids.

The inference-cost benchmark shows pair features dominate cost at fixed parameter count. The ParT and MIParT students run at 26k and 12k jets/s on GPU, versus 70k for the plain Transformer. Students also inherit teacher-specific false positives, and for the least-confident top jets distillation pulls students toward teacher errors.

Caveats:
- Only one binary benchmark and one untuned loss.
- Reproduced teachers fall somewhat below published values (ParT-f.t. R50 644 vs 691). The LorentzNet baseline is notably weak (413 vs 498 published).
- The depth study uses single runs, and three teachers' students come from one teacher seed.
- All timings are FP32. TF32 visibly degrades small L-GATr-slim models, because Minkowski products of collinear momenta are precision-sensitive. That matters for deployment and quantization.
- CPU latency barely scales with size. Deep Sets+GNN (quoted from its published performance) remains the fastest model on CPU.
