## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2611.33221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33221) — Continuous Universal Fake Factors for jet-to-tau background estimation (Lei Guo et al.)

**Relevance to this topic:** This is a tau fake-background paper. Its link to jet substructure is narrow. The fake-factor (FF) networks use associated-jet track multiplicity, track width and C₁ as inputs. The UFF baseline uses track multiplicity as its quark/gluon template variable, standing in for the published q/g discriminant.

**What they did:** CUFF extends ATLAS's Universal Fake Factor (UFF) method. It uses the ATLAS 2015–16 Open Data (36.6 fb⁻¹), restricted to 20 < p_T^jet ≤ 60 GeV.
- Four reference regions: SS W, tt̄, and two multijet regions split by NNJvt (MJ-pass, MJ-fail).
- Each reference region gets a network that learns the Loose fake factor plus the passing-score density. The density is a mixture of 8 Beta components, i.e. a degree-7 Bernstein basis.
- Any Tau-ID threshold is then obtained by integrating that density, with no retraining.
- A second network sets per-candidate weights over the four references. Its inputs include an order-invariant 14-entry summary of the target fail-Loose sample, so no template fit is needed.

**Results:**
- Known-proportion mixtures (Medium ID): closure of +0.7% to +4.3%; the learned combination itself shifts yields by only −0.62 to +0.13 pp.
- Validation regions (Medium ID): underprediction of −21.85 ± 1.58% (N_b = 0) and −45.84 ± 6.03% (μ3j).
- Versus UFF adapted to public inputs (Loose ID): −19.7% vs −55.1% in N_b = 0; −50.2% vs −43.5% in μ3j.
- Shapes: CUFF is closer to data in 5 of 7 observables per region. This includes H_T, which is not a model input (D_shape reduced by 4.30 ± 0.76 pp and 5.57 ± 1.49 pp).

**Caveats:**
- The quoted error budget is dominated by the MC transfer variation, roughly [−70, +58] pp. Some influential μ3j groups contain only 8–9 MC ID records. The validation discrepancies sit inside this range, which is a weak test.
- The MJ-fail reference alone fails closure by +15.5%.
- No efficiency scale factors are applied, and detector, PDF and generator uncertainties are not estimated.
- Systematics are not propagated to the four intermediate thresholds.

### [arXiv:2611.34815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34815) — Heavy-flavor transverse energy-energy correlators in the color-glass condensate (Zhong-Bo Kang et al.)

**What they did:** A leading-order Color Glass Condensate (CGC) calculation of the cross-branch hadron–hadron transverse energy-energy correlator (TEEC) for forward QQ̄ production in pA collisions.
- Retains the quark mass, exact massive kinematics and the full azimuthal dependence, with no correlation-limit approximation.
- Includes splitting both before and after the shockwave; target correlators are taken at large N_c.
- Fragmentation functions cancel via the momentum sum rule. This requires the collinear prescription p⃗_h = ξ p⃗ and complete sums over hadrons in each branch. The observable is therefore idealized: it assumes branch assignment is possible.
- Target: MV initial condition from the HERA fit of Casuga et al. (Q²_s0 = 0.061 GeV², γ = 1), evolved with running-coupling BK. Nuclear scaling is Q²_s0,A = c A^{1/3} Q²_s0, with c between 0.5 and 1.

**Kinematics and reach:**
- RHIC: charm at √s_NN = 200 GeV, η = 3.5 (STAR forward). TEEC-weighted ⟨x_t⟩ ≈ 1.4×10⁻³.
- LHC: charm and bottom at 5.02 TeV, η = 4 (LHCb). ⟨x_t⟩ ≈ 3×10⁻⁵ (charm) and 9×10⁻⁵ (bottom).
- RHIC bottom is dropped because ⟨x_t⟩ ≈ 10⁻².

- R_pA^Σ is suppressed and rises with τ, i.e. more weight away from back-to-back, consistent with saturation-scale broadening.
