## Muon collider

### [arXiv:2607.16904](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16904) — The muon collider: expected physics, technological solutions, and the prospect of a 21 km ring at the UNK site (L. V. Dudko)

Single-author review (Dudko) that is 90% a consolidation of the existing IMCC/MuCol case, with one genuinely new element: a site-specific proposal to reuse the excavated-but-never-equipped 20,772 m UNK tunnel at Protvino as a muon-collider ring. The physics and technology sections are a competent restatement of known numbers (MuCol reference parameters; 0.75% on σ(H→bb̄) at 3 TeV full-sim; Higgs width pinned to (−0.41%,+2.1%) at 10 TeV via forward-μ ZZ-fusion tagging with |η|<6; δλ₃ ~4% at 10 TeV, ~1–1.5% at 30 TeV; thermal wino covered with ~70 fb⁻¹, higgsino needing ~8 ab⁻¹; MICE transverse-cooling demonstration; HTS/REBCO magnet roadmap). Nothing new there — treat it as a reference index.

The novel content is Section 4. Using the rigidity relation with R≈3306 m and a dipole packing factor calibrated to the IMCC 10 TeV lattice (f≈0.66; as-built UNK-II gives f≈0.59), the author maps arc fields onto √s: conventional NbTi/Nb₃Sn (7–8.3 T) → ~8–11 TeV, HTS (10–16 T) → ~12–21 TeV. The one physically interesting argument is the luminosity scaling: at fixed √s, ℒ ∝ B̄ ∝ 1/C, so the 20.8 km ring delivers only ≈0.55× the 11.4 km reference luminosity at 10 TeV — a deficit that closes only by running the fixed ring at the top of its energy range (B̄≈10.5 T at 21 TeV).

Caveats are stated plainly by the author: no CDR exists, the entire front end (driver, target, 6D cooling, acceleration) must still be built (and dominates cost), the reach table is pure extrapolation of published curves at assumed design luminosity, and the neutrino-flux constraint *worsens* with energy — the 20–60 m tunnel depth is shallower than IMCC dose studies assume, with decay-plane neutrinos surfacing 16–28 km out in populated regions. Also flagged: institutional (CERN–Russia cooperation suspended, IMCC closed to Russian labs). Bottom line for this track: no new physics or accelerator result, but a concrete tunnel-reuse scenario and a clean 1/C luminosity argument worth noting alongside the LHC/SPS reuse options.
