## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2610.10734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10734) — The hidden sunrise in the energy-energy correlator (Matthew D. Schwartz et al.)

This paper completes the analytic NNLO EEC in 𝒩=4 SYM. Ref. [1] had left one unevaluated two-fold integral F_R(ζ); this work evaluates it.

**Polylogarithmic pieces.**
- Partial fractioning in the inner variable splits F_R into three parts: F_red₁ (purely polylogarithmic), F_K₁, and F_R₂.
- F_R₂ has to be kept as a whole, because red₂ and K₂ separately diverge like (1−z̄)⁻² log²(1−z̄).
- The polylog remainder P(ζ) = F_red₁ + Δ consists of HPLs of weight ≤5 on the alphabet {ζ, 1−ζ}. Notably, the √ζ letter present at NLO is absent, and the weight is non-uniform.

**Elliptic sector.**
- The two square-root discriminants Q₁ and Q₂ are related by the Möbius map w = z̄/(z̄−1). Both are isomorphic to the equal-mass sunrise curve under S = (ζ−1)/ζ, and the j-invariants match identically.
- Under this map the physical region 0<ζ<1 lands in the Euclidean sunrise region. The odd-looking singular point ζ = −1/8 is the 9m² threshold.
- The coupled system for F_K₁, F_R₂ and a companion integral S₀ involves 𝓛 = ζ∘L_PF∘ζ⁻¹, which is second order. This bounds the result at elliptic: no K3 or Calabi–Yau geometry can appear.
- Subtracting the coupled equations shows Δ = F_R₂ − F_K₁ is polylogarithmic, giving F_R = 2F_K₁ + P.
- S₀ is written as weight-5 iterated Eisenstein integrals for Γ₁(6). The letters are ω₀ and ω₁ plus two new modular-weight-3 letters, ψ₁ω₀ and ζψ₁ω₀.

**Numerics and the collinear limit.**
- Evaluation is by truncated q-series, with the collinear limit ζ→0 at q→0. About 75 terms give 50 digits at ζ = 3/11 (q₀ ≈ 0.17).
- Per their error estimate N_dig ≈ N log₁₀(1/q₀) − 3, convergence degrades toward back-to-back, where q→1.

**How central this is to the topic.** The relevance to jet substructure is indirect: this is a full-angle, fixed-order result in 𝒩=4. The value for jets is mainly a clean collinear-limit expansion and a template for the QCD elliptic sector, which the authors assert but do not compute.

**Caveats.**
- The provided text is truncated before the bootstrap section, so I can't assess those results.
- Much of the technical work was done with AI under human supervision.

### [arXiv:2610.12408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12408) — A compact, interrogable Tree Tensor Network Jet Tagger on Lund declustering trees (Fabrizio Napolitano et al.)

**Architecture.** A single shared trilinear core C ∈ ℝ^{6×χ×χ×χ} is contracted recursively along the C/A declustering tree. Inputs are the five LundNet5 coordinates in a projective-sphere encoding, plus a shared MLP residual. Optional QCD gates (α_s and splitting functions) and Sudakov gates modulate the tree message. At χ = 8 the model has 4014 parameters, against about 391k for LundNet5 and 366k for ParticleNet. Samples are particle-level Pythia (training) and Herwig (transfer), 2M jets per generator.

**Performance:**

| Task | Evaluation | TTN | LundNet5 | ParticleNet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| q/g | native AUC | 0.8917 | 0.8941 | 0.9014 |
| q/g | Pythia→Herwig AUC | 0.8114 | 0.8098 | 0.8107 |
| top | native AUC | 0.9531 | 0.9574 | 0.9592 |
| top | native 1/ε_B at ε_S = 0.5 | 132 | 187 | 204 |
| top | Pythia→Herwig AUC | 0.9472 | 0.9474 | 0.9479 |

- Transfer AUCs agree within 0.002 across all three taggers in both tasks.
- The physics gates change AUC by at most 0.0011, so they bring no measurable gain.

**Bond-dimension and k_t-cut scans.**
- q/g is nearly flat in χ, while top tagging improves with χ.
- The default configuration has no k_t cut and is therefore *not* IRC safe. Imposing ln(k_t/GeV) > −2 restores IRC safety at a cost of ≤0.003 AUC.
- Raising the cut to 1 narrows the Pythia→Herwig gap; in top tagging the transfer AUC even exceeds the native one.

**What's new: interrogability.** Because the same map acts at every node, a tree truncated at depth D is processed with the learned vacuum state, exactly as at leaves during training.
- Pythia/Herwig latent divergence concentrates at depths 10–15 and at ln k_t < 0, mostly in gluons.
- In the primary plane the divergence sits at ln(1/Δ) > 4.

**Caveats.**
- All results use a single training seed.
- Baselines are not parameter-matched.
- The smaller relative transfer loss partly reflects the TTN's lower nominal performance.
- The readout was never trained on truncated trees.
- There is no detector simulation.
