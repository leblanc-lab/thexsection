## Optimal transport

### [arXiv:2608.12795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12795) — Fine-tuned Normalizing Flows for ALICE Zero Degree Calorimeter Fast Simulation (Emilia Majerz et al.)

**Centrality caveat up front:** this is a fast-simulation/normalizing-flows paper, not an OT paper. "Optimal transport" enters only through the evaluation metric — the mean 1-Wasserstein distance between per-photomultiplier photon-count distributions (Eq. 2, averaged over m=5 checkerboard-mask channels via inverse-CDF integration). There is no OT-based training objective, no Sinkhorn regularization in this work, and no transport-map machinery; W₁ is used purely as a scalar figure of merit. (Note ref. [3] in their bibliography is a Sinkhorn autoencoder, but that is prior art, not this paper.)

What they actually did: MAF-based conditional NF with rational-quadratic-spline transforms, pre-trained on the full imbalanced GEANT4 ZN dataset (306,780 samples) then fine-tuned per particle type (γ, n, Λ, K⁰_S, Σ⁺) via two gradual-unfreezing schemes. A two-stage pipeline (NF for normalized shower shape + BNN for total photon count) plus a four-way ensemble reaches W₁ = 1.61 ± 0.02 over the full test set, beating baselines (1.70, 1.76).

The genuinely useful OT-adjacent contribution is their critique of W₁ as a ZDC surrogate metric: because the same input maps to many valid responses, a global 1-Wasserstein score can reward marginal distributional match while ignoring conditional input→output structure. They introduce conditional weighted MAE, a shrinkage-stabilized dispersion ratio, and a Jaccard co-activation error to capture what W₁ misses. Caveat they flag: bootstrap significance is weak outside γ (small per-input samples), and photon-count prediction, not the flow, is the accuracy bottleneck.
