## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2611.06824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06824) — Cross-Moment Consistency and the Fifth Adler Coefficient in Hadronic Tau Decays (Gauhar Abbas et al.)

**Relevance: essentially off-topic.** This paper is about perturbative QCD for inclusive hadronic τ decays (the Adler function and τ spectral moments). It contains no jets or substructure, and the keyword match is spurious. The only indirect link to your topic is α_s. The authors say high-scale QCD observables, presumably including event shapes and jet rates at FCC-ee, would test α_s running against low-scale τ determinations. They do not develop this.

For completeness, the results:

- **Transfer identity.** The FOPT Adler-to-moment map is triangular with unit diagonal for normalized weights. A common error in the forecast of c₅,₁ therefore shifts every moment by the same absolute amount. A multi-weight 10% test then reduces to a single tolerance, set by the moment with the smallest coefficient (here W_P2).
  - This resolves c₅,₁ only to about ±24.
  - The CIPT analogue is far weaker: ±770.
- **Defect-sign obstruction.** The defects of the direct Padé/Shanks forecast are positive and growing. Any continuation of the Adler sequence that preserves that sign therefore gives c₅,₁ ≥ c₄,₁²/c₃,₁ = 378.03. Values near 280 require extra structure that reverses the sign, such as UV renormalons.
- **Provenance of 283.** The often-quoted Beneke–Jamin value of 283 is a moment-space linear extrapolation of FOPT term ratios. It is an input to their main five-parameter renormalon model, not an output of it.
- **Conditional estimate.** A pure-power BJ-type ansatz with a cross-order closure test gives c₅,₁ = 248 ± 30 (struct) ± 74 (method). The central value is just the exact four-parameter fit.
- **Phenomenological impact.** Δc₅,₁ = 80 shifts each FOPT moment by about 8.7×10⁻⁴, roughly 0.4% of δ⁽⁰⁾.

**Caveats** (stated by the authors):

- The blind one-order-lower postdiction misses c₄,₁ by 30%.
- Adding BJ's full cut structure empties the admissible set, so the closure test rejects the more complete model.
- A misplaced leading singularity at u = 3/2 still passes the closure test.
