## Muon collider

### [arXiv:2607.26020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26020) — Probing Lepton-Flavor-Violating Four-Lepton Operators at a Muon Collider (Sukanta Dutta et al.)

**Abstract only.** A SMEFT study of charged LFV from dimension-six four-lepton operators in $\mu^+\mu^-\to e^\pm\tau^\mp$, $e^\pm\mu^\mp$, $\mu^\pm\tau^\mp$ at $\sqrt{s}=3,10,14$ TeV, with beam polarization and hadronic $\tau$ reconstruction. Using an optimal-observable analysis of angular distributions and a global fit over the relevant operator set, they project sensitivities of $C/\Lambda^2\sim(0.6\text{–}1.6)\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-2}$, up to an order of magnitude beyond current limits. The multi-energy, multi-polarization combination is highlighted as the lever that breaks correlations among Wilson coefficients — the concrete methodological point for anyone tracking how a muon collider's LFV reach depends on running scenario.

For this topic the paper is squarely on-target: it's a projection specific to a multi-TeV muon collider, exploiting the $s$-channel LFV production that only a muon-beam machine gives directly. Caveats limited by abstract-only: no statement of assumed integrated luminosity per energy point, no detail on the $\tau$-reconstruction efficiency/background model behind the quoted reach, and the sensitivities are presumably from a truth- or fast-sim level fit rather than full detector simulation. The "exceeds current limits by an order of magnitude" claim should be read against which specific low-energy LFV constraint (e.g. $\mu\to e$, $\tau\to\ell$) each operator direction is compared to.

## Jet substructure

### [arXiv:2607.25893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25893) — The calibration of large-radius jets using the Run 2 dataset with the ATLAS detector (ATLAS Collaboration)

**Abstract only.** ATLAS Run 2 ($\sqrt{s}=13$ TeV) calibration of large-radius jets built from Unified Flow Objects and Soft Drop–groomed. Combines an MC-based jet energy/mass response correction with in-situ methods validating the JES/JMS and their resolutions in data. Residual JES/JMS uncertainty is ~1% for $p_T$ up to 1 TeV and 2–3% up to 2 TeV.

Central to the topic on the systematics side rather than the algorithm side: this is the calibration input that sets the achievable precision for boosted $W/Z/t/H$ tagging and BSM searches using groomed substructure. The UFO+Soft Drop combination and the sub-percent-to-few-percent JMS control are the numbers to carry forward when assessing substructure-based analyses' systematic floors. Abstract gives no breakdown of the in-situ methods used or the mass-scale behavior across the grooming parameter space, so the per-observable substructure calibration detail (e.g. $D_2$, $\tau_{32}$) isn't visible here.
