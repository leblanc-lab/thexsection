## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2611.17801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17801) — Novel observables for exploring jet quenching effects in individual jets (Jin-Wen Kang et al.)

The authors boost each jet's constituents into the jet frame of Baty–Gardner–Li (z* along the jet axis) and compute two shapes in the plane transverse to the axis: transverse thrust τ⊥ = 1 − T⊥, and linearized transverse sphericity S⊥ = 2λ₂/(λ₁+λ₂). They use the linearized tensor because it is IRC safe. Setup:

- JEWEL 2.6.0, pp and 0–10% Pb+Pb at 5.02 TeV.
- Anti-kt R=0.4 jets with p_t > 60 GeV and |η| < 1.6.
- Hadron constituents with p_t > 0.3 GeV; event-level constituent subtraction.

Results:

- **With recoil:** τ⊥ and S⊥ both shift to larger values, meaning a more azimuthally isotropic j⊥ configuration. η* shows peripheral enhancement and core suppression.
- **Without recoil:** the τ⊥/S⊥ modification reverses (more pencil-like), and η* migrates toward the axis.
- **j⊥ spectrum:** softens in both cases, so it gives no evidence of intra-jet broadening.
- **Raising the constituent p_t threshold** progressively weakens the enhancement. This points to soft medium-response hadrons as the driver.

The pitch is a scale-invariant, multi-particle probe of medium response that complements jet shapes and angularities.

Caveats:

- Everything is single-model. JEWEL's free-streaming recoil is a limiting case, and they say so.
- There are no detector effects, no realistic background fluctuations, and no quantified sensitivity versus existing observables. Ratio magnitudes are only shown in figures.
- They concede that raising the p_t threshold also strips soft shower particles, so it is not equivalent to dropping recoil.
- My own note, not in the paper: with E-scheme axes the constituent j⊥ sum to zero by construction, which presumably shapes the τ⊥ distribution. The authors do not discuss this.

### [arXiv:2611.18202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18202) — N³LO Prediction for the Radiative B⁻ → γℓ⁻ν̄ Decay (Yong-Kang Huang et al.)

This paper matched on "jet function" in the SCET sense. It is not about collider jets or substructure; it is relevant mainly if you follow multi-loop jet-function technology.

They compute the three-loop photonic radiative jet function J_γ two independent ways in Feynman gauge, and the two agree:

- **SCET operator matrix element:** 1372 diagrams, Kira reduction to 68 masters. The 46 nontrivial masters were evaluated with AMFlow plus PSLQ at 80 digits and also analytically via HyperInt.
- **Hard-collinear expansion of full QCD.**

A new photon-singlet structure appears at three loops, with color factor ∝ d^{abc}d^{abc}. It is finite and L-independent.

Numerical results:

- At L=0: J_γ ≈ 1 − 3.527a_s − 64.86a_s² − 1588.9a_s³. At μ = 1.5 GeV the successive orders are −9.8%, −5.0%, −3.4%.
- **Form factor:** F_LP = 0.340 → 0.318 → 0.300 going from NLO+LL to N³LO+NNLL. Three-loop matching alone gives −5.96%.
- **LCDA-shape dependence of the jet term:** −4.2%, +2.5%, −5.8% for α = 2.0, 1.7, 2.5.
- **λ_B from Belle R_π:** 501 → 462 → 430 MeV across orders. The jet coefficient alone moves it from 449 to 430 MeV.

Caveats:

- Only the leading-power factorization is treated.
- No scale-variation bands are shown; results are at reference scales only.
- The Belle input is weak: R_π = (1.7 ± 1.4)×10⁻².

### [arXiv:2611.18206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18206) — RG evolution of the B-meson LCDA and radiative jet function at three loops (Ze Long Liu et al.)

This is also SCET jet-function work, not jet substructure. It is an independent group's simultaneous calculation of the three-loop Lange–Neubert kernel plus the three-loop radiative jet function. That gives a cross-check of the inputs used in [arXiv:2611.18202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18202).

Method:

- The LCDA is written as the discontinuity of a matrix element with an inverse covariant-derivative propagator (reverse unitarity).
- IR poles of the on-shell partonic matrix element are removed by the known heavy-light form-factor factor Z_hl. No off-shellness or mass regulator is needed, so gauge invariance is preserved.
- The calculation uses 702 diagrams, 556 masters, and canonical DEs with 14 letters. The final h^(3)(x) contains only logs, Li₂ and Li₃.

Checks:

- Predicted poles from 1/ε⁶ to 1/ε² match the explicit calculation.
- An independent jet-function calculation (3348 diagrams, 69 quasi-finite integrals, AMFlow check) has all poles removed by the derived Z_J. This establishes three-loop RG consistency.
