## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2610.13634](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13634) — A machine-learning method for the estimation of the background from jets misidentified as hadronic tau lepton decays (CMS TAU-25-001) (CMS Collaboration)

**How central to this topic:** peripheral. This is a background-estimation method for jet→τh fakes. Jet structure enters only through simple proxies, not substructure observables.

**What they did.** MUFFIN replaces binned fake factors N_pass/N_fail with a continuous transfer factor, the ratio of calibrated XGBoost class probabilities.
- The nominal model is a four-class BDT (data pass/fail, genuine-τ simulation pass/fail). Genuine-τ contamination is subtracted inside the weight: w = (P_pass^data − P_pass^τsim)/(P_fail^data − P_fail^τsim).
- No simulation of the targeted fake process is used for the data-derived QCD and W+jets factors.
- Inputs, in order of feature importance: τh decay mode, then p_T(jet)/p_T(τh), then p_T(τh), N_jets, N_b, η, φ, and a data-period label. The p_T(jet)/p_T(τh) ratio is the one substructure-like handle, capturing how much of the seed jet's momentum the HPS candidate captures.
- Temperature scaling gives T* ≈ 0.99–1.00, so the BDT outputs are essentially already calibrated.
- Statistical uncertainty comes from a 50-model bootstrap ensemble.

**Results.** Data are 62.4 fb⁻¹ at 13.6 TeV. In validation-region closure, MUFFIN gives a lower mean |Poisson pull| than fake factors in τhτh and τμτh. The gains are largest in η and decay mode (which fake factors marginalise over) and at high p_T. The abstract quotes up to 70% lower jet→τh systematic uncertainty. Total jet→τh yield uncertainty is 6% (τhτh) and 5% (τμτh). The analysis-dependent extrapolation term (≲10%) dominates; the flat non-closure term is 5%.

**Caveats.**
- W+jets and tt̄ are validated only in simulation, since there is no clean data validation region.
- A residual ΔR non-closure in τμτh resembles the fake-factor behaviour, pointing to a missing input variable.
- Pull values appear only in the figures.
- The provided text truncates during the signal-region application section.

### [arXiv:2610.13876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13876) — Precision Tokenization for Multi-Modal Data in High Energy Physics (Jeffrey Krupa et al.)

**What they did.** Residual-VQ-VAE tokenizers with Transformer encoders and decoders, scanned over codebook size K, code dimension d and number of residual stages Q. Modalities are ATLAS JetSet tracks (20 features) and COCOA tracks, topoclusters, and calorimeter cells (8×8 patches with occupancy and energy loss). Fidelity is set against information fraction I = Q·log₂K/(32D).

**Headline numbers.** Reaching the 1% jet p_T IQR/median target:
| Modality | Configuration | Compression | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| JetSet | (32768, 8, 4) | 10.7× | 0.82% |
| COCOA tracks | (4096, 8, 3) | 4.4× | 0.68% |
| COCOA topoclusters | (4096, 8, 6) | 3.6× | 0.39% |
| COCOA cells (best, misses target) | not stated | 11× | 1.24% |

- Learned tokenizers need up to 2.4× fewer bits than per-feature binning.
- The in-context (Transformer) encoder beats an MLP: 0.82% vs 1.27%.

**Design rules.** Use multiple residual stages (single-stage collapses), use large K, scale d with the bit budget and the target's intrinsic dimension, and bin (η,φ) outside the quantizer when coordinates dominate the feature set.

**Downstream tasks.**
- **Particle flow:** with positions binned outside the quantizer, IQR/median is 0.086 vs 0.0845 for continuous inputs (about 2% relative).
- **b-tagging:** at 60 bits per constituent, tokens retain 73–93% of continuous-input rejection. At ε_b = 0.7, light-jet rejection is 1170 vs 1370 and τ-jet rejection 354 vs 451.
- **Degradation at 30 bits:** charm rejection drops to 19.1 vs 42.0, so high-rejection tails are where tokenization costs most.

**Caveats.**
- The resolution metric measures tokenizer error only, comparing reconstructed constituents with themselves before tokenization.
- COCOA tracks are smeared, not reconstructed.
- Table 4's COCOA-track value of 0.068% looks inconsistent with Table 1's 0.68% (a unit slip is likely).
- No substructure observables (e.g. mass, N-subjettiness) are checked explicitly.

**Link between the two papers.** Both swap hand-binned representations of jet-constituent information for learned ones. The tokenization paper quantifies what that costs; MUFFIN shows what it gains for a data-driven background estimate.
