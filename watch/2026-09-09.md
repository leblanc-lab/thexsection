## Muon collider

### [arXiv:2609.08392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08392) — Prospects for precision Higgs boson measurements at a 10 TeV muon collider (Paolo Andreetto et al.)

Full detector-level projections (MUSIC concept, GEANT4 + machine-induced BIB/IPP overlay, ACTS + Pandora PF, kt R=0.5 jets) for the IMCC baseline: two experiments × 10 ab⁻¹ (20 ab⁻¹ total), VBF-dominated production (~2×10⁷ single-H, 7×10⁴ HH). Headline statistical sensitivities on effective cross sections: H→bb̄ 0.18%, H→WW* (semileptonic) 0.35%, H→γγ 2.6%, H→ZZ*→qqμμ 4.2%, H→μμ 6.9%. Combining bb̄/γγ/μμ mass fits gives Δm_H ≈ 19 MeV (competitive with HL-LHC's ~21 MeV). Double-Higgs: HH→bbbb at 4.2%, HH→bbWW* at 14%, translating to a projected ~5% precision on the trilinear self-coupling λ₃ from bbbb — the standout number, since HL-LHC is stuck at [−26%,+29%] on κ₃.

For this topic the paper is the current reference benchmark: it moves the μC Higgs case from parton-level cross-section studies to full reconstruction with realistic BIB, and gives channel-by-channel efficiencies (e.g. 60% b-tag with 22% c / 0.9% light mistag) and background models one can actually reuse. Key caveats stated openly: all quoted numbers are statistical only, and H→bb̄ will be JES-dominated (0.49% from a 0.06% JES uncertainty) once systematics enter; electron reconstruction is not yet optimized, so H→ZZ*→4ℓ and the electron HH→bbWW channel (53% vs 22% for muons) are deferred/conservative; τ_had reconstruction is absent. The λ₃ extraction relies solely on bbbb and on the WW-fusion sample rescaled by 1.103 for ZZ-fusion.

### [arXiv:2609.08918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08918) — Probing the Inert Scalar Sector of the Inert Doublet Model via Vector-Boson Fusion at a Muon Collider (Abdesslam Arhrib et al.)

Studies three previously-unexamined 2→4 VBF channels in the IDM at √s = 3, 10, 20 TeV: μ⁺μ⁻→H⁺H⁻νν̄ (WW fusion), and the associated HH±μ∓ν and AH±μ∓ν (WZ/Wγ fusion), with full 2→4 matrix elements in MadGraph5 (no EVBA). They first dissect the WW→H⁺H⁻ and WV→H⁺S_i subprocesses, showing longitudinal WLWL dominance and a strong destructive interference among diagrams (individual contributions ~10³ pb collapse to ~1 pb in the full amplitude), enforcing correct high-energy behavior. Cross sections rise sharply with energy: H⁺H⁻νν̄ reaches O(10) fb at 10 TeV and tens of fb at 20 TeV; the associated channels reach O(10³) fb. Delphes-level cut-based analysis (no BIB/pileup) for three benchmark points with BR(H±→HW±)=1 gives, at 10 ab⁻¹: [WH][WH]νν̄ significance 33 (10 TeV) / 58 (20 TeV); [WH]Hμν a huge 2000–4000 (dominated by large signal σ); [WH][ZH]μν 164 / 276. The 3 TeV stage is marginal for H⁺H⁻ (S=1.37) but strong for the associated modes.

For the muon-collider topic this extends the VBF new-physics program beyond the earlier AAνν̄ study to charged and mixed inert states, establishing 10/20 TeV as decisive. Caveat, stated by the authors: this is an idealized sensitivity — pileup and MuC beam-induced background are not simulated, and the enormous significances (especially BP2) reflect raw signal rates under optimistic detector assumptions rather than a realistic backgrounded analysis.

## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2609.06686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06686) — Likelihood-Based Unsupervised Anomaly Detection in CMS Dijet Events (Bhavishya Chebrolu (VIT-AP University et al.)

Trains a rational-quadratic neural spline flow (10 blocks, 256 hidden units, 8 bins) on 61 high-level dijet/event observables from CMS 2011B HT AOD open data (√s=7 TeV, 378,278 events, ~0.5 fb⁻¹), scoring events by −log p(x) with no signal model, MC, or sidebands. Selecting the 99.9th percentile (379 events) and running KS tests across all 61 features, the largest population-level deviations are overwhelmingly jet-substructure variables: leading-jet girth (D=0.575), τ₁/τ₂/τ₃ (0.548/0.541/0.518), the ECF ratio (0.508), τ₃₂ (0.498), τ₂₁ (0.441), width (0.386), then jet mass (0.373) and HT (0.343). The anomalous events show no m_jj resonance — a sliding-window bump hunt gives a look-elsewhere-corrected global p=6.0×10⁻⁴ (test stat 8.79), which they explicitly decline to call evidence.

For jet substructure the relevance is real but the framing is honest: substructure observables emerge as the dominant discriminants of the "non-QCD-like" tail, and the paper gives a useful discussion of why ECFs (recoil-free, subjet-free, IRC-safe) and N-subjettiness give coherent signals. Important self-stated caveats: the flow is trained and scored in-sample (no held-out split, acknowledged likelihood-scale bias); selecting the tail of a multivariate density trivially sculpts the marginals, so KS p-values (e.g. 10⁻¹¹⁹ for girth) are diagnostic only, not significances; and the τ₂₁/τ₃₂=0 spikes are known PF low-multiplicity artifacts. Methodological/exploratory contribution rather than a substructure measurement per se.
