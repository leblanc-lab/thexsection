### [arXiv:2610.14221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14221): Energy-energy correlators within Z-tagged jets in pp collisions at √s = 13 TeV (LHCb)

LHCb reports the first EECs in Z+jet events at forward rapidity:
- **Data and selection:** 5.4 fb⁻¹ from 2016–18; Z→μμ; anti-kT R=0.5 jets with 20<p_T<100 GeV and 2.5<y<4. The jet must be the leading jet with Δφ_{Z-jet}>7π/8. Charged hadrons need p_T>0.25 GeV.
- **Observables:** the inclusive EEC, plus like-sign (LS) and opposite-sign (OS) EECs, each normalised to the inclusive one, and their difference.
- **Weights:** p_T^i p_T^j/p_T,jet². These are p_T weights, not energy weights.
- **Unfolding:** a simultaneous 3D IBU in (R_L, p_T,jet, w_pT), with the weight unfolded before projection. That is a methodologically notable choice.
- **Transition peak:** in R_L⟨p_T⟩ the peak sits at 2.74±0.25, 2.71±0.50 and 2.67±0.22 GeV across the three p_T bins. This scaling is consistent with Λ_QCD/p_T.
- **Theory comparison:** the predictions of Barata et al. use a nonperturbative parameter fixed from e⁺e⁻. They reproduce the peak position, which LHCb frames as e⁺e⁻↔pp universality for quark jets.
- **Charge dependence:** the LS−OS difference is negative everywhere, and OS correlations are enhanced as R_L falls toward the transition. Pythia describes this, and the paper reads it as string-breaking-like.
- **Free-hadron region:** for p_T<50 GeV the shape is flat, matching ALICE. Within uncertainties it is also compatible with STAR's negative slope, so it does not settle that tension.

Why it matters: this is a quark-enriched pp baseline, complementing gluon-heavier inclusive-jet EECs (CMS/ALICE) and charm-tagged EECs (ALICE). It sets up flavour-dependence comparisons.

Caveats:
- The quark fraction is asserted from the Compton-dominance argument but not quantified.
- The unfolding prior is Pythia 8.186, which is old.
- Pair efficiencies are only 41–66%.
- Systematics reach 47% in the lowest p_T bin.
- With p_T>50 GeV the free-hadron region is too short to say anything.

### [arXiv:2610.15919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15919): RINO: Renormalization Group Distillation with No Labels

RINO is DINOv2-style self-distillation in which the views are exclusive-kT clusterings of the same jet.
- **Views:** N∈{2,3,4,6,8,16} plus unclustered. The teacher sees N∈{6,8,16}; the student additionally sees the coarse and unclustered extremes.
- **Loss and backbone:** the iBOT and KoLeo terms are kept, on an 8.4M-parameter transformer.
- **Protocol:** pretrain on JetClass QCD only, fine-tune on JetNet, evaluate out-of-distribution (OOD) on JetClass.

Results, OOD top accuracy with an MLP head:

| Model | Accuracy |
|---|---|
| RINO | 0.847±0.003 |
| MPMv1 | 0.800 |
| Supervised | 0.786 |

- **Label efficiency:** with 1% of labels RINO reaches 0.797, matching full supervision.
- **Zero-shot H→4q AUC:** 0.894, versus 0.859 for MPMv2.
- **Clustering ablation:** kT 0.839 > C/A 0.796 > anti-kT 0.693. This is the substructure-relevant result: emission-scale-ordered coarse-graining matters, not merely multiscale views.
- **Contrastive variant:** swapping JetCLR augmentations for kT views gives +11.4%.
- **Backbone geometry:** the DINO backbone stays scale-aware while the head enforces invariance.
- **Domain shift:** W₁ between source and target embeddings drops roughly 3×.

Caveats:
- This is sim→sim.
- Pretraining overlaps the test domain.
- Absolute accuracies are well below in-domain (~0.93).
- Inputs are kinematics only.
- **Selection on the target:** the headline teacher schedule was selected on OOD JetClass top accuracy. That is mild target leakage; the pre-tuning 0.839 is the cleaner number.

### [arXiv:2610.17351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17351): QGrAT: Quantum Grade-Aware Transformer for Jet Tagging

QGrAT is a quantum top tagger.
- **Attention:** pairwise IsingZZ gates whose angles are grade-wise 𝒞ℓ(1,3) inner products of features built from constituent four-momenta: p·P, p, p∧P, a weighted trivector T_i, and |det|.
- **Ancilla:** a Hadamard-test ancilla applies controlled ZZZ rotations weighted by the triple product (p_i·p_j)(p_i·p_k)(p_j·p_k).
- **Readout:** in-circuit.
- **Best configuration:** N=10 constituents, grades 0–3 plus the ancilla, 12 qubits, 144 parameters, 12k training jets.

| Model | Params | AUC | 1/ε_B at ε_S=0.5 | 1/ε_B at ε_S=0.3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| QGrAT (grades 0–3 + ancilla) | 144 | 0.944±0.002 | 35.7 | 96.8 |
| L-GATr-Slim | 144 | 0.940±0.010 | 30.4 | 75.1±53.8 |
| Full minimal L-GATr | 1215 | 0.958 | — | 192 |

- **Ancilla effect:** the three-body ancilla is the main driver, raising 1/ε_B at ε_S=0.5 from 19.9 to 35.7.
- **Grade 4:** adds nothing.

Caveats:
- The classifier is not Lorentz equivariant; only the coupling angles are invariant, while the component encoding is frame dependent.
- Triple products are standardized per batch, including at evaluation, so scores depend on batch composition.
- Results come from noiseless state-vector simulation.
- The baseline variance is huge.
- Everything sits about an order of magnitude below SOTA rejection (~1300–2500).

The interesting takeaway is an explicit three-body invariant buying rejection in a tiny model.
