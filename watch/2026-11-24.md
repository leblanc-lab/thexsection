## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2611.31011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31011) — Lorentz symmetry in self-supervised representation learning (Andreas Hermansen et al.)

The authors pre-train with LeJEPA on 100M JetClass jets and no labels. LeJEPA's loss combines (1−λ) times a view-invariance MSE to the per-jet mean embedding with λ times SIGReg, an Epps–Pulley characteristic-function test over 256 random projections, using λ = 0.02. They compare a plain Transformer (ParT-style inputs plus register tokens) against L-GATr-slim (with beam/time spurions), at a matched d = 128 and 6 encoder layers. Views come from JetCLR augmentations (collinear splits, p_T-dependent η/φ smearing, 30% dropout) or RINO-style k_T subjet views, optionally plus global SO⁺(1,3) boosts and rotations.

The key result is that latent Lorentz invariance is decisive:
- **Without boosts/rotations:** linear probes collapse to 0.33–0.48.
- **With boosts/rotations:** they recover to 0.76–0.77.
- **Spurion-free L-GATr-slim (fully invariant):** stays at about 0.763 regardless of augmentation, and gives the best MLP probe (0.806). Dropping beam-axis symmetry breaking costs nothing here.

Best configurations (linear / MLP probe):

| Model | Linear | MLP |
|---|---|---|
| LeJEPA, Transformer | 0.762 | 0.792 |
| LeJEPA, L-GATr-slim | 0.774 | 0.800 |
| SSFM masked-particle modelling | 0.635 | 0.715 |
| RINO | 0.533 | 0.648 |
| Next-token prediction | 0.430 | 0.554 |

Other findings:
- A multivariate-Laplace SIGReg target beats the Gaussian target (0.774 vs 0.723 linear).
- Masking plus SO⁺(1,3) alone already reaches 0.709.
- LeJEPA wins in low-data fine-tuning, in CWoLa top-vs-QCD SIC at 1% FPR, and in transfer to the top-tagging dataset (no transfer numbers are quoted in the text).
- SSFM beats RINO on probes, but the order flips under fine-tuning, so probe accuracy is an imperfect proxy.

**Caveats:**
- Models are small, and scaling is explicitly left open.
- The baselines were modified to emit a single pooled token. The authors note this is not comparable to the original attention-pooled NTP/SSFM evaluations.
- Only one pre-training run per model; seeds vary only in fine-tuning.
- Everything is Delphes simulation.
- No fully supervised reference number is quoted in the text.

### [arXiv:2611.28180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28180) — Machine-learning-assisted photon pairing for neutral-pion reconstruction at future Higgs factories (Yi Wang et al.)

This paper is only adjacent to the topic. It selects resolved π⁰→γγ pairs, including inside hadronic Z/H final states. The connection to jets is that it repurposes ParticleNet and ParT with a symmetric per-pair readout u_ij = [h_i+h_j, |h_i−h_j|, p_ij] in place of the jet-level head.

Setup:
- Simulation is Delphes with the CEPC 4th-concept card, with the photon threshold lowered from 0.5 to 0.01 GeV.
- Samples are Z→ττ and Z→qq̄ at 91.2 GeV, and ννH and ZH(Z→qq̄) at 240 GeV.
- The figure of merit is efficiency × purity.

At the reference resolution (α = 0.03, β = 0.01), ParT reaches 0.863, 0.662, 0.568 and 0.526 across the four samples. The optimized mass window gives 0.539, 0.329, 0.275 and 0.231, i.e. ParT gains of +60% to +128%. ParticleNet gains +53% to +95%, while BDT and MLP sit around +26% to +45%.

The gain is almost entirely purity. For example, in ννH:

| Method | Purity | Efficiency |
|---|---|---|
| Mass window | 35.4% | 77.7% |
| ParticleNet | 67.7% | 71.0% |
| ParT | 76.5% | 74.2% |

The advantage persists out to α = 0.25 (ParT 30.4% vs mass window 15.0% in ZH). The transformer needs large training samples: ParT on Z→qq̄ goes from 47.6% at 10⁴ events to 66.1% at 9.5×10⁵, and at low statistics both graph models can fall below the mass window. Learning curves show a two-stage rise driven by purity. For jet work, the main relevance is cleaner π⁰ inputs for particle-flow and hadronic-τ/ρ reconstruction in dense jets.

**Caveats:**
- No exclusive pairing is applied, so a photon can enter several selected pairs.
- Efficiency is conditional on both photons being reconstructed and truth-matched.
- Merged or unresolved photons are not addressed.
- Pair features differ between models, so the gain from event context is not isolated (the authors say so).
- Most training-size points are single runs.
- Models are retrained per resolution setting, so detector transfer is untested.

### [arXiv:2611.30429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30429) — Revealing Nuclear Shape through Identified-Hadron Flow Decorrelations (Hadi Mehrabpour et al.)

This is not a jet paper; it presumably matched on correlation vocabulary. It is heavy-ion flow phenomenology, and the summary is kept short.

The study uses TRENTo initial conditions, viscous hydrodynamics (η/s = 0.08) and UrQMD. It computes p_T-dependent factorization-breaking coefficients r₂ and r₂,₂ for π, K and p in central U+U at 193 GeV and Ne+Ne at 5.36 TeV, scanning quadrupole deformation β₂ and triaxiality γ.

The authors define "crossing coordinates": the p_T where the π/K and π/p ratios of these coefficients intersect, and the common ratio value there. Different (β₂, γ) values trace distinct trajectories in this plane, with neighbouring configurations separated at Z ≈ 2–5 in simulation. For example, U+U with r₂ spans P_cross ≈ 1.58–1.74 GeV and R_cross ≈ 1.09–1.23. Replacing UrQMD with SMASH preserves the qualitative trends.

**Caveats:**
- The approach is model-only.
- Nonflow and self-correlations are not treated.
- r₂,₂ relies on an equal-normalized-fourth-moment approximation.
- Ne+Ne α-clustering is not modelled.
