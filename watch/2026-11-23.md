## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2611.26688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26688) — Agent-guided construction of Feynman integral bases (Pau Petit Rosas et al.)

**Relevance:** this paper matched on "jet function" and is not really about jets or substructure. The "radiative jet function" here is an SCET operator matrix element with Wilson lines. It enters the factorization of QED form factors at next-to-leading power in the fermion mass. The paper is about building master-integral bases, not jet observables.

**What they did:**
- They wrote two Claude Code "skills": `maximal-cut-basis` and `pole-free-basis`.
- Each skill is about 2k lines of instructions plus 6–9k lines of mostly-Mathematica code.
- They call out to Kira, FiniteFlow, LiteRed, LiteIBP and BaikovPackage.
- Every candidate basis is gated by an exact-in-ε IBP reduction at a kinematic slice.

**Jet-function superfamily (the part relevant to you):**
- 124 masters in 70 sectors, 4 scales (m², s, q², b₀).
- The agent classified 63 sectors as canonical and 6 as elliptic, and identified one K3 sector, which the authors confirmed.
- It reached a differential equation that is pole-free in ε and at most quadratic in ε.
- It then reached a fully ε-factorized form using the method of Ref. [24], for which no dedicated skill had been written. That change of basis involves 8 elliptic periods, 3 K3 periods and 108 auxiliary functions.
- Cost: roughly 207 agent-hours and about US$1040 at list prices, mostly on a laptop.

**Five-point QED family** (e⁺e⁻→μ⁺μ⁻γ, 7 invariants): 232 masters, 18 elliptic sectors, 5 sectors needing nested square roots. The ε-polynomial form was reached after about 155 accepted basis changes over 8 days.

**Caveats:**
- The bases are not optimized for evaluation.
- The five-point equation was obtained only on slices; it has not been analytically reconstructed.
- One human intervention corrected a wrong "impossible" conclusion by the agent.

**Takeaway for jets:** if you follow NLP mass-factorization ingredients, the jet-function masters are released as ancillary files.

### [arXiv:2611.27324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27324) — A resolution-based framework for coherent and decoherent jet energy loss in NLO pQCD (Peng-Hui Hu et al.)

This is the paper that is actually on topic today.

**Setup:**
- pp baseline from NLOJet++: 2→2 at NLO for inclusive and reclustered jets; 2→3 at NLO for r_g and jet mass.
- Pb+Pb adds EPPS21 nPDFs, Glauber geometry, CLVisc hydro and Higher-Twist radiative energy loss.
- Out-of-cone loss is handled analytically. A Gaussian q̂-broadening kick is convolved with the jet-cone boundary k_c, which yields a Marcum Q₁(a,b) factor: the Rician probability that the gluon ends up outside the cone.

**Key new ingredient: coherence criterion.** Final-state partons are sequentially clustered by pair invariant mass Q_ij ≈ √(p_T^i p_T^j) θ_ij. Pairs with Q_ij < Q_c lose energy as one color source; otherwise they lose it independently. Two variants are used:
- a step form, Θ(Q_med − Q_ij);
- a smooth form, 1 − exp[−(Q_med/Q_ij)²], derived from an inverse-Rayleigh distribution of Q_c.

**Results:**
- **PYTHIA validation:** jets have about 1 coherent cluster below 200 GeV, about 2 prongs at 300–800 GeV, and ≥3 above 900 GeV.
- **Inclusive jet R_AA:** coherent-only loss (with α_s tuned to the lowest-p_T ATLAS point per centrality) under-suppresses at high p_T. Adding the Q_ij-based decoherence fits the data with Q_med = 10–20 GeV. A fixed angular criterion (θ_c = 0.2) only shifts R_AA overall.
- **Reclustered jets (ATLAS R = 1.0, R_sub = 0.2):** the same Q_med reproduces the multi-subjet R_AA; single-subjet jets stay effectively coherent.
- **r_g and m/p_T:** coherent-only loss gives a flat R_AA. Decoherence generates the measured substructure dependence, and the smooth form fits better than the step form.

**Caveats:**
- Jets contain at most 2 partons (inclusive) or 3 (substructure), so suppression of multi-prong jets is underestimated at the highest p_T.
- α_s is refit per configuration (0.190 vs 0.167).
- Jet flavour is assigned from LO quark/gluon fractions.
- IRC safety is argued only for the step form at large Q_c; the authors say a complete demonstration is still needed.
- The smooth form is explicitly phenomenological.
- The relation Q_c² ~ p_T²/(q̂L³) is a dimensional guess, not derived.

## Muon collider

### [arXiv:2611.26844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26844) — Unstable particle collisions in space and time (Irene Corral Lorences et al.)

**Problem addressed:** Corral Lorences, Liu, Panico and Wulzer target the non-integrable t-channel singularity in processes like μ⁻μ⁺→e⁻ν̄_eW⁺. There an on-shell ν_μ from muon decay scatters off the other beam. Earlier work showed that finite beam size regulates the singularity (the "neutrino beam" picture). It did not give a principled definition of the muon-initiated cross section; previous proposals used ad hoc phase-space exclusion.

**Formalism:**
- They extend the Kotkin et al. wave-packet formalism with a new Lorentz-scalar Wigner density, defined in terms of the classical momentum P = m p/√(p²).
- They define the classical beam limit as κ → 0, with κ ~ 1/beam size (about 0.02 eV for a 10 μm beam), required to satisfy κ ≪ mass gap.
- The transition probability becomes the Wigner densities convolved with a collision function 𝔠(S, −p̃²).
- Terms analytic at p̃ = 0 are short-distance. At leading order they reproduce σ·ṽ
