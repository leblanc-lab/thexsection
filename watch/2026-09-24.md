## Muon collider

### [arXiv:2609.26870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26870) — The LHC is not enough: the LZ High-Recoil Event at FCC-hh and a Muon Collider (Benedikt Maier et al.)

Maier and Spannowsky take the LZ 248 keV nuclear-recoil candidate (2.6σ global) as motivation for a class of collider targets: pure $Y\neq0$ electroweak multiplets with endothermic $Z$-mediated scattering and sub-$2m_e$ neutral splitting. That splitting makes the neutral transitions invisible. Thermal masses run from 0.58 TeV (scalar doublet) to 11.5 TeV (scalar quintuplet). Within the leading-operator EFT, a charged-state trace sum rule caps $\Delta_{\rm min}\lesssim 0.17$ GeV for the quadruplets and quintuplets, which implies $c\tau\gtrsim 18$–27 mm. Most of the new quantitative work is FCC-hh: a disappearing-track rescaling model, plus monojet and Drell–Yan arguments. The muon-collider section is a **compilation of published reaches** (mainly Refs. [29, 32, 67]) mapped onto this benchmark set. There is no new muon-collider simulation.

The muon-collider case, as mapped here:
- **Why it suits these targets:** lifetime-independent mono-γ/mono-W recoil searches. Disappearing-track reach approaches $\sqrt{s}/2$ for long-lived states but only ~$0.3\sqrt{s}$ for the mm-scale doublet. Beam-induced-background-inclusive disappearing- and long-track studies cover the thermal Higgsino and wino at 10 TeV.
- **Coverage table:**
  - fermion doublet: ≥3 TeV
  - scalar doublet: 6 TeV (disappearing tracks)
  - fermion triplet: ≥10 TeV
  - scalar triplet: 14 TeV (missing invariant mass; needs sub-percent systematics)
  - fermion quadruplet: ≥14 TeV (recoil channel needs sub-percent systematics, otherwise tracks)
  - fermion quintuplet: ≳20 TeV
  - scalar quintuplet: not reached at 30 TeV
- **Headline:** 30 TeV covers the full fermionic family.

The useful framing is that recoil channels settle the doublet and triplet without knowing the charged spectrum, which is unpredicted once custodial-breaking operators are allowed.

Caveats:
- Recoil projections assume 0–1‰ systematics, with 1% quoted only where it changes the conclusion.
- The HTML I received truncates mid-§VI, so the detailed muon-collider discussion and caveats section were not available.
- The authors themselves note that a full-density thermal Higgsino is in tension with solar-capture limits ($\delta m_0\gtrsim0.51$ MeV) and with the empty LZ high-energy sideband.

## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2609.26899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26899) — Towards Foundation Models on Hardware Accelerators for Particle Physics (Maya Benyas et al.)

The authors distil the 423M-parameter OmniLearned-Large model into Deep Sets students on the standard Pythia+Delphes top-tagging dataset ($p_T$ 550–650 GeV). The teacher was pretrained on >1B jets across 210 classes, then fine-tuned to AUC 0.9880 and $1/\epsilon_B$ = 3365 at $\epsilon_S$ = 30%. The distillation loss uses α = β = 0.5 and T = 4.

Results at 30% signal efficiency:

| Change | Rejection |
|---|---|
| Adding one message-passing layer ($\log m_{ij}$, $\log\Delta R_{ij}$, $\log k_{T,ij}$ on the top-64 constituents; 11k → 17k params) | 748 → 1980 |
| Distillation vs. hard-label cross-entropy (same student) | 1216 → 1980 |
| Distilling from the pretrained teacher vs. a from-scratch teacher of the same architecture | 1020 → 1980 |

Accuracy moves by only ~0.2–0.3 points across these comparisons, so the pretraining benefit sits almost entirely in the ROC tail. The pretrained teacher itself has ~4× better rejection than its from-scratch counterpart, and roughly half of that gain survives into the student. This is the first demonstration that the *pretraining* benefit, not just the teacher's capacity, transfers through distillation. The best student reaches 93.98% accuracy versus 94.38% for OmniLearned-Small, with 155× fewer parameters.

Quantization:
- **Post-training quantization at 8 bits** is destructive: 59% accuracy for the message-passing student, and chance level at 4 bits.
- **Quantization-aware training at 8 bits** (Brevitas) recovers 93.95% accuracy and 1787 rejection.

Caveats:
- No HLS/FPGA synthesis, latency or resource numbers yet.
- The message-passing student costs 14.3M MACs per jet versus 305k for plain Deep Sets. That 47× gap is what will matter against a µs-scale trigger budget, and the paper does not yet address it.
- There is a single hyperparameter point, and K = 64 was not optimized.
- Only the saturated top-tagging benchmark is tested.
