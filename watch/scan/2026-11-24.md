### [arXiv:2611.28194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28194) — HEP-Ex-Bench: Evaluating Agentic Performance in High Energy Physics Experiment Data Analyses with Sealed Worlds (Andrzej Nov\'ak et al.)

_Watchlist hit (Philip Harris, Michael Kagan) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

Agentic systems are increasingly being explored for automating experimental high-energy physics measurements, but the correctness of their results can today be reliably evaluated only via extensive expert review. To address this, we propose a method to perform detailed quantitative evaluations of agent-driven analyses through the use of blind analysis and injection tests, which are critical in a typical expert review. Agents develop a data analysis pipeline on a public dataset, while the evaluation is performed on programmatically generated sealed private datasets where signals are injected and simulation settings varied within realistic detector uncertainties. By requiring agents to provide a single workflow that is evaluated across these "sealed worlds", we are able to test the accuracy and robustness of the workflows. We demonstrate the approach on four tasks built from CMS open data and present it as a way to reliably benchmark scientific work in experimental high-energy physics. At the time of writing, the best-performing model, Gemini 4 Argon, reaches 60% of the score of the expert reference analyses on average, ahead of GPT 6 Astra at 38% and Claude Opus 5.5 at 31%. In individual sessions on one of the tasks, Gemini 4 Argon exceeds the reference. The benchmark is hosted at https://hep-ex-bench.github.io

### [arXiv:2611.28199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28199) — A Transformer-based Method for Neutrino Event Generator Reweighting (Andrew Cudd et al.)

_Watchlist hit (Ben Nachman, Vinicius Mikuni) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

The ability to test the effects of different neutrino event generators on simulation-based analyses is currently bottlenecked by the computational cost of propagating simulated events through expensive detector simulations. One way to circumvent this limitation is to reweight events produced with one generator to replicate the predictions of another. However, existing methods for doing this in neutrino physics tend to be limited to only a few observables at a time. We present a transformer-based architecture that can reweight between neutrino event generators based on nearly the entire particle stack, providing reweighted events that are valid over a greatly expanded phase space. We present results with events generated from multiple GENIE configurations and NuWro as examples, and we introduce an energy distance-based metric for evaluating the results in a multidimensional, unbinned manner. Our results show promising performance for realistic applications and provide a method for comparing the performance of different reweighting techniques.
