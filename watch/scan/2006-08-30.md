## Authors to watch

### [arXiv:hep-ph/0608319](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0608319) — Extraction of the atmospheric neutrino fluxes from experimental event rate data (M. C. Gonzalez-Garcia et al.)

_Watchlist hit (Juan Rojo) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

The precise knowledge of the atmospheric neutrino fluxes is a key ingredient in the interpretation of the results from any atmospheric neutrino experiment. In the standard atmospheric neutrino data analysis, these fluxes are theoretical inputs obtained from sophisticated numerical calculations. In this contribution we present an alternative approach to the determination of the atmospheric neutrino fluxes based on the direct extraction from the experimental data on neutrino event rates. The extraction is achieved by means of a combination of artificial neural networks as interpolants and Monte Carlo methods.

## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:hep-ph/0608324](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0608324) — Signature of heavy Charged Higgs Boson at LHC in the 1 and 3 prong Hadronic Tau Decay channels (Monoranjan Guchait et al.)

_Watchlist hit (hadronic tau) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

We have done a fast simulation analysis of the charged Higgs signal at LHC in the 1 and 3 prong hadronic $τ$-jet channels along with the $t \bar t$ background. The $τ$ polarization was effectively used to suppress the background in both the channels. Combining this with appropriate cuts on the $p_T$ of the $τ$-jet, the missing $E_T$ and the azimuthal angle between them reduces the background below the signal level. Consequently one gets a viable charged higgs signal up to a mass range of 600-700 GeV at moderate to large $\tanβ$.
