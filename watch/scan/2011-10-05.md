## Authors to watch

### [arXiv:1110.1043](https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.1043) — W+jets, Z+jets, multijets and new physics searches (Christoph Englert et al.)

_Watchlist hit (Tilman Plehn) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

W+jets, Z+jets and QCD multijet production processes at hadron colliders are backgrounds to many searches for physics beyond the Standard Model which involve leptons and missing energy in the final state. We review the current theoretical and experimental status of these processes at the LHC. Furthermore, we discuss several methods that allow for reliable predictions for these processes in the context of new-physics searches.

## Muon collider

### [arXiv:1110.1051](https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.1051) — Study of multiple scattering in high magnetic fields (Daniel M. Kaplan et al.)

_Watchlist hit (muon collider) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

Muon cooling for a neutrino factory or muon collider can be achieved using low-Z absorbers in strong focusing fields. Proposed cooling lattices place absorbers in solenoidal fields ranging up to 30 to 40T. The cooling performance of these lattices is determined by the interplay of ionization energy loss and Moliere scattering, but Bethe's classic treatment of Moliere scattering ignores the helical motion of charged particles in solenoidal fields. When this motion is taken into account, the performance of these lattices can be better than predicted by simulations using the standard treatment.

## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:1110.1055](https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.1055) — Measurement of the top pair invariant mass distribution at 7 TeV and search for new physics (Salvatore Rappoccio)

_Watchlist hit (jet reconstruction) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

An overview of searches for new physics in the ttbar sample from the CMS Collaboration is presented with data collected at the Large Hadron Collider at sqrt(s) = 7 TeV. There are several searches presented, including same-sign dilepton signatures, semileptonic signatures, and all-hadronic signatures, the latter of which uses advanced jet reconstruction techniques.

### [arXiv:1110.1094](https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.1094) — Jet substructure in ATLAS (David W. Miller)

_Watchlist hit (jet substructure, jet grooming, jet algorithms, boosted top) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

Measurements are presented of the jet invariant mass and substructure in proton-proton collisions at sqrt{s} = 7 TeV with the ATLAS detector using an integrated luminosity of 37 pb-1. These results exercise the tools for distinguishing the signatures of new boosted massive particles in the hadronic final state. Two "fat" jet algorithms are used, along with the filtering jet grooming technique that was pioneered in ATLAS. New jet substructure observables are compared for the first time to data at the LHC. Finally, a sample of candidate boosted top quark events collected in the 2010 data is analyzed in detail for the jet substructure properties of hadronic "top-jets" in the final state. These measurements demonstrate not only our excellent understanding of QCD in a new energy regime but open the path to using complex jet substructure observables in the search for new physics.
