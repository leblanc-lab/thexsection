## Authors to watch

### [arXiv:2611.17418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17418) — Subleading beauty effects in Higgs production at small transverse momentum (Rebecca von Kuk et al.)

_Watchlist hit (Iain Stewart) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

We study $b$-quark mass effects at small Higgs transverse momentum $q_T$ where the Higgs is produced in gluon fusion via a closed $b$-quark loop. In contrast to top-quark loops, which can be integrated out at a high scale, the $b$-quark mass, $m_b$, induces a second small scale $m_b \sim q_T \ll m_H$ such that standard $q_T$ resummation does not apply in this region, which includes the peak of the distribution. The resummation of mass effects for the $gg\to H$ form factor, which has been studied previously, applies to the $q_T$ spectrum in the limit $q_T \ll m_b$. Unlike this case, for $q_T \sim m_b$, real emissions can resolve the $b$-quark loop, requiring a more complicated simultaneous $m_b$ and $q_T$ factorization. It involves new next-to-leading-power $m_b$ and $q_T$ dependent beam and soft functions, and the corresponding bare factorization theorem suffers - as expected - from endpoint divergences. We use soft-collinear effective theory to explicitly compute all amplitudes with a $b$-quark loop and an additional real soft or collinear emission. Our results provide important insight into the nontrivial structure of the endpoint divergences and show how their expected cancellation happens in the sum of all amplitudes separately for soft and collinear emissions. Interestingly, we find that at this order the endpoint-divergent contributions have a separable dependence on $q_T$ and $m_b$, providing strong motivation that in the future their cancellations can be made manifest in the factorized cross section. As an important further cross check we show that the sum of all SCET amplitudes correctly reproduces the full-QCD one-emission amplitudes expanded in the relevant soft or collinear limit, which also requires nontrivial contributions involving massive Glauber quarks in the effective-theory calculation.
