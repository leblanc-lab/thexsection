### [arXiv:2610.36052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36052) — The Harmonic Structure of Multiparticle Scattering: Amplitude Projection and Non-Renormalization (Nathaniel Craig et al.)

_Watchlist hit (Nathaniel Craig) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

We develop a projection formalism for four-dimensional massless scattering amplitudes based on the construction of harmonics on multi-particle phase space. This provides a generalization of the partial-wave decomposition valid for any number of particles. Applying this formalism to calculations of on-shell cuts, we show that any cut calculation can be rewritten as a sum of products of projected coefficients and derive a condition for this sum to vanish. Using the on-shell renormalization group equation in combination with our projection formalism, we derive new non-renormalization theorems for effective field theories valid at general loop orders.

### [arXiv:2610.36073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36073) — Regression on high-dimensional phase space (Andrew J. Larkoski)

_Watchlist hit (Andrew Larkoski) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

Machine learning tasks in particle physics are typically expressed in terms of regression, or relaxation toward some optimal solution as defined by an objective or loss function. In this paper, we introduce a large multiplicity expansion for analyzing regression tasks, which enables significant analytic understanding. For concreteness, we study regression with energy flow polynomials (EFPs), a complete linear basis of infrared and collinear safe observables on Lorentz invariant phase space. While the expectation value of any EFP scales like a constant in the large multiplicity limit, we prove that the covariance on uniform phase space of any two EFPs is highly suppressed and scales like the inverse of the squared multiplicity. This very unintuitive result relies on detailed cancellation guaranteed by the interplay of permutation invariance, momentum conservation, and rotational invariance. This scaling of the covariance with multiplicity further implies that the coefficients in an expansion of EFPs must scale quadratically in multiplicity. We present an example of regression with EFPs as applied to $e^+e^-\to$ hadrons events, and show how the regression can be solved analytically through the complementary expansions of the large multiplicity limit and of familiar fixed-order perturbation theory of QCD.

### [arXiv:2610.37398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37398) — An end-to-end framework for uncertainty-aware validation of generative models (Giada Badaracco et al.)

_Watchlist hit (Philip Harris) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

Density estimation with generative models is a common task in the physical sciences, for example in background estimation in particle physics. Existing machine learning techniques, however, lack methods to quantify uncertainties that can be propagated into a statistical test. That limits the validation of generative models to standard goodness-of-fit tests, in which the generative model is treated as exact (point-null hypothesis). But a model fitted to a finite training set is never exact, so if its uncertainties are neglected, any sufficiently powerful goodness-of-fit test will eventually reject it once the test set is large enough to resolve this estimation error. We propose SIGMA, an end-to-end framework combining density estimation with principled uncertainty quantification. We use Gaussian mixture models, for which uncertainties are tractable, and further construct a new goodness-of-fit test in which these uncertainties are profiled as nuisance parameters to account for the statistical error of the fit, promoting the conventional point-null test to a composite one. A rejection then signals genuine mismodeling rather than a test set large enough to resolve the error of the fit, provided the propagated uncertainties faithfully cover that error. We demonstrate the framework on a controlled two-dimensional toy model and on a collider physics benchmark modeling the QCD background process. With uncertainties included, the same model can be trusted on test sets up to an order of magnitude larger than the standard test allows. Our work thus provides a statistically grounded basis for a robust use of generative models in the physical sciences.
