## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:1105.6287](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.6287) — Precise measurement of the top-quark mass from lepton+jets events at D0 (The D0 Collaboration et al.)

_Watchlist hit (jet energy scale) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

We report a measurement of the mass of the top quark in lepton+jets final states of ppbar->ttbar data corresponding to 2.6 fb^{-1} of integrated luminosity collected by the D0 experiment at the Fermilab Tevatron Collider. Using a matrix element method, we combine an insitu jet energy calibration with our standard jet energy scale derived in studies of γ+jet and dijet events and employ a novel flavor-dependent jet response correction to measure a top-quark mass of mt = 176.01 +/- 1.64 GeV. Combining this result with our previous result obtained on an independent data set, we measure a top-quark mass of mt = 174.94 +/- 1.49 GeV for a total integrated luminosity of 3.6 fb^{-1}.

## Authors to watch

### [arXiv:1106.0030](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.0030) — Light Stop NLSPs at the Tevatron and LHC (Yevgeny Kats et al.)

_Watchlist hit (David Shih) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

How light can the stop be given current experimental constraints? Can it still be lighter than the top? In this paper, we study this and related questions in the context of gauge-mediated supersymmetry breaking, where a stop NLSP decays into a W, b and gravitino. Focusing on the case of prompt decays, we simulate several existing Tevatron and LHC analyses that would be sensitive to this scenario, and find that they allow the stop to be as light as 150 GeV, mostly due to the large top production background. With more data, the existing LHC analyses will be able to push the limit up to at least 180 GeV. We hope this work will motivate more dedicated experimental searches for this simple scenario, in which, for most purposes, the only free parameters are the stop mass and lifetime.
