### [arXiv:2611.08573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08573) — Soft-gluon effective coupling for massive quarks (Luca Bason et al.)

_Watchlist hit (Simone Marzani) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

We present a generalisation of the soft-gluon physical coupling, defined in terms of the strength of soft radiation from a hard dipole, to massive hard emitters. We derive the relation between this coupling and the $\overline{\rm MS}$ coupling through two loops, encompassing all configurations in which both, one, or neither of the hard emitters are massive. We show that the presence of massive emitters introduces additional freedom in the definition of the physical coupling scheme, which we characterise at two-loop order. Our results provide new ingredients for precision QCD resummation in processes involving heavy quarks and for the development of higher-order parton-shower algorithms.

### [arXiv:2611.08606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08606) — Just CaloCHILL: fast hadronic shower generation in a learned latent space (Henry Day-Hall et al.)

_Watchlist hit (Gregor Kasieczka) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

Calorimeter showers are the most expensive part of detector simulation, and highly granular calorimeters, in which a single hadronic shower leaves thousands of energy deposits, make them more expensive still. We present CaloCHILL (Calorimeter Hits In Learned Latents), a generative model that does not produce these deposits one by one: instead it generates a compact summary of the shower in a learned latent space and expands it to full resolution only at the end. The complete shower, including the arrival time of every hit, is built in three stages: a flow-matching model first sets the number of hits and the earliest hit time in each calorimeter layer, a mean-flow model then generates the latent summary, and the decompressor of a variational autoencoder turns it into the full point cloud, giving every hit its position, energy and time. Sparse attention along the shower axis keeps this compression affordable for showers with thousands of hits. On positive-pion showers in the calorimeter of the International Large Detector, a proposed detector for the FCC-ee, CaloCHILL reproduces the main shower observables, including their time structure. This new model is 600 times faster than its predecessor, while improving on all accuracy metrics considered.
