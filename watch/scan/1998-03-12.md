## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:hep-ph/9803322](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/9803322) — The Cambridge Jet algorithm: features and applications (Stan Bentvelsen et al.)

_Watchlist hit (jet clustering, jet algorithm) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

Jet clustering algorithms are widely used to analyse hadronic events in high energy collisions. Recently a new clustering method, known as `Cambridge', has been introduced. In this article we present an algorithm to determine the transition values of y_cut for this clustering scheme, which allows to resolve any event to a definite number of jets in the final state. We discuss some particularities of the Cambridge clustering method and compare its performance to the Durham clustering scheme for Monte Carlo generated e+e- annihilation events.

### [arXiv:hep-ph/9803325](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/9803325) — Leading Particle Effect in the $J/Ψ$ Elasticity Distribution (F. O. Durães et al.)

_Watchlist hit (energy flow) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

Recent DESY-HERA data on $J/Ψ$ elasticity distribution show that it emerges mostly as a fast particle. Interpreting photoproduction as a collision between a pre-formed charmed hadron and the proton, the outcoming $J/Ψ$ is a leading particle of the collision. We analyse these data using a model formulated to describe energy flow in hadron-hadron reactions. The measured $J/Ψ$ spectrum can be successfully described in terms of this model. We conclude that the observed transparency of the charmed hadron-proton collisions arises because of the particularly small gluonic content of the initial $c - \bar c$ state.

## Muon collider

### [arXiv:hep-ph/9803326](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/9803326) — Physics with a Millimole of Muons (Chris Quigg)

_Watchlist hit (muon collider) — abstract only, not yet read for a briefing._

The eventual prospect of muon colliders reaching several TeV encourages us to consider the experimental opportunities presented by very copious stores of muons, approaching $10^{21}$ per year. I summarize and comment upon some highlights of the Fermilab Workshop on Physics at the First Muon Collider and at the Front End of a Muon Collider. Topics include various varieties of $μμ$ colliders, $μp$ colliders, and applications of the intense neutrino beams that can be generated in muon storage rings.
