## Muon collider

### [arXiv:2609.03648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03648) — Light Leptoquarks in a Dark Sector: Scalar Dark Matter, Neutrino Mass, and Collider Signatures (Priyotosh Bandyopadhyay et al.)

The muon collider is genuinely only the final probe in an otherwise DM/neutrino-mass model paper, so calibrate expectations: Sections 2–4 build a Z₂-odd dark sector (two scalar LQs S₁ and R̃₂, a VLQ doublet + down-type singlet, and a real singlet scalar φ as DM), generate Majorana neutrino mass at one loop via the T1-2 topology (requiring κ~10⁻³ GeV for TeV masses and O(10⁻²–1) Yukawas), and satisfy relic density through LQ–φ co-annihilation with compressed splittings ΔM~38–55 GeV. The Z₂-odd assignment kills the usual ℓq/νq LQ decays, so LHC pair-production bounds drop from ~1.5 TeV to a few hundred GeV; CheckMATE recasts of soft-lepton (CMS-SUS-16-048) and jets+MET searches leave sub-TeV LQ eigenstates viable for M_φ ≳ 500 GeV.

The muon-collider content (Sec. 5) is the motivated but not-yet-executed part: they argue a 10 TeV MuC is the right machine to measure the compressed S₁→ℓqφ system because M_T2 fails when invisible daughters aren't light relative to the parent, whereas the known √s lets you use visible-energy endpoints and the cusp technique. They set up the two-body-like kinematics S₁→V+φ with V=jℓ, giving the Lorentz-invariant endpoint m_jℓ^max = M_{S₁}−M_φ = ΔM as a robust handle on the splitting. Caveat: no signal-vs-background simulation, detector effects, or cusp-fit sensitivity study is actually presented — the MuC analysis is a proposal/kinematic sketch, and displaced-vertex R-hadron-like regions are explicitly excluded from scope. For MuC-tracking purposes, the takeaway is a concrete use-case for endpoint/cusp reconstruction of compressed colored-parent spectra at 10 TeV, not a new reach result.

## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2609.03072](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03072) — Energy-Energy Correlators in Z-Tagged Jets in pp Collisions (Jo\~{a}o Barata et al.)

This is squarely a jet-substructure paper and a clean methodological advance. They compute the collinear EEC inside forward Z-tagged jets by grafting an exclusive differential EEC jet function 𝒢_c^EEC onto the back-to-back Z+jet TMD factorization (replacing the unmeasured exclusive jet function, leaving TMD PDFs, hard, and soft functions intact). The EEC jet function is built from the inclusive one via one-loop anti-k_T matching coefficients, evolved with NLO timelike splitting functions from a b_χ-dependent boundary condition to μ_J, and the whole thing is run at NLL′ with a variable-flavor-number scheme. The key physics lever is retaining **separate quark and gluon non-perturbative scales**, a_q = 2.31 GeV (from e⁺e⁻ global fit, quark-dominated) and a_g = 3.8 GeV (from CMS inclusive-jet EEC, gluon-dominated).

The concrete result: predictions for LHCb-like forward kinematics (2.5<η_J<4.0, R=0.5) at √s=8 and 13 TeV in p_JT bins (20,30), (30,50), (50,100) GeV. The EEC shows a finite small-z_χ plateau (from the exponential large-b_χ suppression making the Hankel transform finite as z_χ→0) and, in angular representation, the characteristic confinement turnover, with the quark contribution turning over at smaller ⟨p_JT⟩χ than the gluon (reflecting a_q<a_g). The flavor decomposition shows quark dominance growing with p_JT and with √s. The physics case: because a_q is fixed independently from e⁺e⁻, measuring this quark-enriched sample tests universality of the confinement transition scale across collision environments. Caveat stated plainly: a_g is poorly constrained (extracted at LL with a single flavor-averaged parameter), and the factorization is unreliable for k_χ≥Q_J (z_χ≥0.063 at R=0.5); NP-parameter uncertainties are not included in the bands.
