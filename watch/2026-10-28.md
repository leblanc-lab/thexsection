## Muon collider

### [arXiv:2610.36030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36030) — A Practical Guide to Sensitivity Projections for Beyond the Standard Model Physics (Francesca Acanfora et al.)

**How central it is:** this is a statistics pedagogy paper. The muon collider is one worked example, not the subject. The core point is that BSM projections should use a hypothesis test, not a goodness-of-fit test. The authors show that on Asimov data both test statistics reduce to Pearson χ²_A. What differs is the reference distribution:
- **Goodness-of-fit:** χ² with k = n_bins degrees of freedom.
- **Hypothesis test:** ½χ²₁, using a μ-interpolated alternative H(μ) = μH_BSM + (1−μ)H_SM.

The practical recipe is to require χ²_A ≥ 2.71 for 95% CL, or ≥ 3.84 for 95% CLs.

**The muon-collider example:**
- Process: μ⁺μ⁻ → V⁰ → e⁺e⁻ at √s = 10 TeV with 1 ab⁻¹.
- Model: heavy vector triplet model A, tree level, with SM interference.
- Binning: six uniform |η_e| bins over [0, 2.5].
- Efficiency set to 1; no detector or beam-induced-background treatment.

At the benchmark (m_V, g_V) = (1.9 TeV, 1.9), the two tests disagree sharply:
- **Goodness-of-fit:** q_A = 9.06, p ≈ 0.16, so the point is not excluded.
- **Hypothesis test:** q_A = 9.62, p ≈ 0.0013 (Monte Carlo) or 0.00096 (asymptotic). χ²_A = 9.33 gives 0.0011. The point is excluded.

The (m_V, g_V) exclusion contours (thresholds 12.6 vs 2.71) differ substantially. The text describes them only qualitatively; no numerical reach is quoted. Using 3.84 plus systematics reproduces the CLs contours of the earlier muon-collider HVT study (Ref. [49]). Uncorrelated systematics added in quadrature give almost the same contours as a nuisance-parameter treatment.

**Takeaway for the topic:** there is no new muon-collider reach here. The useful part is a calibration warning: any published muon-collider projection that used a "χ² test" with n_bins degrees of freedom is conservative by a large factor in coupling reach. The available text truncates partway through the multinomial section, so the CLs and systematics sections were not seen.

## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2610.36073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36073) — Regression on high-dimensional phase space (Larkoski) (Andrew J. Larkoski)

Larkoski develops a 1/n expansion of uniform massless phase space through O(n⁻²) for m-particle correlators. At leading order, phase space factorizes into Boltzmann factors n²z e^{−nz} with isotropic angles. The main result is a proof that the covariance of any two EFPs on uniform phase space is O(n⁻²).

- **Why the O(n⁻¹) term vanishes:** the 9ml/2n, −6ml/n and +3ml/2n pieces cancel. This needs permutation invariance, momentum conservation and rotational invariance together.
- **What breaks it:** dropping momentum conservation (pure Boltzmann sampling) restores an O(1/n) covariance.
- **Numerical check with RAMBO:** Var(EFP₂⁽²⁾) = (2/5)n⁻² on Lorentz-invariant phase space versus (32/9)n⁻¹ for Boltzmann sampling.
- **Corollary:** EFP expansion coefficients of an IRC-safe observable are n-independent, so the n⁻² covariance extends to any IRC-safe pair.

**Consequence for regression.** Fitting |M_n|² with EFPs by mean-squared error forces coefficients c_β = O(n²). The normalization sum rule then requires the O(n²) and O(n) pieces to cancel exactly. That is a structurally ill-conditioned linear system at high multiplicity.

Larkoski proposes imposing these constraints order by order in n. He also suggests building non-analytic structure (e.g. Sudakov factors) into the basis. For the e⁺e⁻ → hadrons example he uses KNO scaling to keep only the leading ⟨n⟩ term, with O(α_s) EFP expectation values on the right-hand side.

**Caveats:**
- The measure is uniform phase space.
- No closed-form covariance exists for general EFP pairs; only numerical results are shown.
- The text truncates before the e⁺e⁻ numerical results.
- The minimal loss involves ⟨(|M_n|²)²⟩, which is not computable at fixed order.

This is relevant to anyone doing linear or EFP-based fits on high-multiplicity jets.

### [arXiv:2610.37137](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37137) — What the Primary Lund Plane Omits: A Model-Stitching Diagnosis (Saurabh Rai et al.)

The authors build Lund-ParT: the same ParT class (about 1.66M parameters) fed with primary-Lund declustering coordinates. They stitch frozen class tokens across networks through a single 128×128 linear map at depths 1–5. The data are 50% of the top-tagging reference set, with five seeds.

**Is the gap representation or reader?**
- Standalone background rejection at ε_S = 0.5: ParT 291, primary-branch Lund-ParT 136.
- Lund representation read by ParT's downstream: 128–136.
- Same representation read by Lund-ParT's own downstream: 120–134.
- So ParT's reader does at least as well, and the deficit is representational.
- AUC stays within about 1% throughout. The gap appears only in low-efficiency rejection.
- CKA rises from 0.76 to 0.93 with depth without tracking transfer.

**Where the deficit sits.** It is concentrated in about 0.6% of background: jets with m ≈ 180 GeV and about 67 constituents, but fewer primary splittings than average (11.3 vs 12.6). The relevant radiation sits off the primary branch.

**The repair.**
- Rebuilding on the full C/A tree lifts standalone rejection to 248, closing 72% of the gap. The leaked population drops by 2.9× at d = 5.
- Masking at inference shows the repaired network still reads mostly the primary branch. Dropping 12 primary nodes gives 9.2; dropping 12 secondary nodes gives 187.
- So secondary nodes mainly aid learnability rather than carrying inference-time information.
- The widest-angle primary splitting is worth +35% excess over a random node, near 2m_t/p_T.

**Quark/gluon contrast.** The primary branch alone retains 77.6% of ParT's rejection, and the full tree closes only 33% of the gap. Both tasks end with the full tree at about 85% of ParT.

**Caveats:**
- Rejection here is about 10× below full-dataset values.
- The reader-asymmetry result is not significant (p = 0.062).
- The hypergraph check (116 vs 112) is a single seed at 10% data.
- Results at d = 6 are excluded.
- Quark/gluon stitching results at shallow depth carry 20–55% seed spreads.

**Link to Larkoski.** Both papers diagnose why a physics-motivated basis underdelivers. Larkoski's is analytic (conditioning of an IRC-safe linear basis at large n); Rai and Ganguly's is empirical (which declustering content is missing).
