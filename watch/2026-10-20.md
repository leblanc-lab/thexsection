## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2610.24954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24954) — Ceci n'est pas un tagger: can an LLM flavour-tag a jet? (Andrea Coccaro et al.)

Claude Opus 5.5 was run zero-shot in an agentic harness. It was isolated from the web and filesystem and instructed not to write code or scoring formulas. Its task was to assign (b, c, light, τ) probabilities to 10k jets from the public ATLAS JetSet tt̄ sample at 13.6 TeV, with truth labels stripped and only jet- and track-level reconstructed inputs provided. The agent inspected about 40 jets, then farmed out batches of 60 to sub-agents. Processing took 4h28m (24h36m wall time including usage limits), versus 𝒪(ms) per jet for GN2.

Results, using the standard D_b with f_c = 0.2 and f_τ = 0.01:

| AUC | Opus 5.5 | DL1 | GN2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| b vs light | 0.928 | 0.963 | 0.971 |
| b vs c | 0.815 | 0.897 | 0.913 |

- **Correlation with trained taggers:** D_b Pearson r with DL1/GN2 is 0.880/0.870, versus 0.964 between DL1 and GN2.
- **c and τ:** both classes collapse largely into "light."
- **Rationale audit:** the natural-language rationales show heavy-flavour shortcuts. For c-jets, compared with true c-jets:
  - mass is cited for 65% of predicted versus 13% of true;
  - an S_d0 value is quoted for 73% versus 27%;
  - prompt tracks and missing inner hits are essentially absent for predicted c (versus about 23–24% of true c);
  - negative-side displacement is over-read as a light-jet signature.

For this topic it is a curiosity, not a technique. Its most usable content is the diagnosis that "c-jets as milder b-jets" is the literature-derived prior. The authors' hybrid "LLM prior + trained tagger" idea is speculative.

**Caveats:**
- No uncertainties are given on the AUCs.
- There is a single model and harness; robustness across sessions and prompts is only asserted.
- Outputs are coarse, human-rounded probabilities.
- Repeated inference is non-deterministic.
- Compliance with the no-algorithm constraint rests on instructions plus sandboxing.

### [arXiv:2610.25189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25189) — Quantum Veto Parton Shower Algorithm in the Conformal Window (Nongqiu Cai et al.)

**What it is.** The paper extends the hybrid quantum veto shower: classical proposals of (i, a, z) on a discretized q̃² grid, with a flavour-conditioned R_Y veto on an emission qubit. The setting is an SU(3)_D×U(1)_D dark sector with N_C = 3 and N_F = 12. It uses exact two-loop running via Lambert-W, with α* = 12π/50 ≈ 0.754, and 12 U(1)_D charges spanning 0.5–2.0. The mass basis is related to the interaction basis by an imposed DFT mixing ansatz; the masses are degenerate, so the mixing is not derived. Running conditions are parton level, E = 50 GeV, Λ = 0.2 GeV, t²_max = 100 GeV². The g→χχ̄ and γ_D→χχ̄ channels are omitted because of statevector memory limits.

**Results, quantum versus "classical":**
- Zero-dark-photon fraction is 14.54% versus 3.38%.
- ⟨n_γD⟩ is 3.344 → 3.216, while ⟨n_g⟩ is essentially unchanged (7.869 → 7.988).
- P(χ₀) is 72.17% versus 67.10%, with a U-shaped mass-eigenstate spectrum that the authors derive from Gaussian decoherence of off-diagonal ρ_kk′.
- The analytic check agrees well, except in the (0,0) sector: 0.6327 analytic versus 0.7308 simulated, from about 80 events, attributed to statistics.

**Relevance to substructure is limited.** The only jet observable is an all-pairs EEC, shown only for the QVPS sample. It looks approximately linear in log-log over 10⁻³ ≲ R_L ≲ 10⁻¹, with an M_q-dependent small-angle turnover. No exponent is fitted, and there is no hadronization.

**Caveat on the quantum claim.** Under the paper's own Kraus operators, which are diagonal in the interaction basis (Eq. 4.7), emission statistics depend only on the diagonal ρ_kk. The multiplicity effects would therefore also arise from a classical shower with a hidden, uniformly drawn interaction-basis flavour, i.e. Bayesian charge reweighting. Coherence matters only for the final mass-basis readout.
