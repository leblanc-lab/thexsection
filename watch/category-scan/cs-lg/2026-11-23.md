### [arXiv:2611.26986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26986) — Distributional Algebro-Geometric Persistence (dAGP): Wasserstein-Stable Polynomial-Relation Signatures (Edisy Kin Wai Chan et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: wasserstein) — original abstract; no AI summary._

A circle and a quartic superellipse have the same homology, but their defining equations have degrees two and four, respectively. We study this algebraic distinction for point-cloud distributions. For a probability measure $\mu$ on $\Omega=[-1,1]^d$ and an orthonormal polynomial feature map $\phi_m$ of degree $m$, the moment matrix $G_m(\mu)=\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\mu}[\phi_m(X)\phi_m(X)^\top]$ satisfies $c^\top G_m(\mu)c=\mathbb{E}_\mu[f_c(X)^2]$, so coefficient directions associated with its small eigenvalues represent low-degree polynomials that nearly vanish on $\mu$. Counting these near-relation directions across degree $m$ and the tolerance $\varepsilon$ gives a two-parameter integer signature. We call this representation distributional algebro-geometric persistence (dAGP), the population counterpart of the point-cloud algebro-geometric persistence (AGP) construction. For empirical measures, dAGP is the sample-normalised counterpart of point-cloud AGP. We also show that $\mu\mapsto G_m(\mu)$ is Lipschitz in the $1$-Wasserstein distance, so its ordered eigenvalues vary Lipschitz-continuously under transport of mass, while its threshold counts remain unchanged under perturbations satisfying a spectral-margin condition. We give an explicit support-localised Lipschitz constant and identify the regime in which the resulting guarantee is informative. Experimentally, the population noise-floor prediction agrees with the finite-sample mean to $0.1\%$, within sampling error. At a selected tolerance, relation onset orders the conic, quartic, and non-algebraic closed curves, while their count profiles distinguish them from the two-dimensional control. Calibration identifies an empirical tolerance range. A $100$-repetition sensitivity study quantifies onset recovery and shows that the count grid and two continuous spectral baselines nearly saturate the four-family discrimination task.

### [arXiv:2611.27261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27261) — Semiparametric Efficiency for Entropic Optimal Transport under Structured Distribution Shift (Yiming Ma et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: optimal transport) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Can structural information improve estimation of entropic optimal transport (EOT), and when is the gain zero? We study a fixed-dimensional exponential density ratio model (DRM) with a nonparametric baseline, compact support, and fixed positive regularization. At positive limiting sampling fractions, an exact projection identity quantifies the variance gain from the restriction. The pooled empirical-likelihood estimator attains the restricted bound and supports asymptotically valid confidence intervals when the bound is positive. Even under correct specification, the gain can be zero and can shrink as the tilt space expands. The same projection residual determines direction-specific first-order bias under local misspecification. When both samples grow and the target fraction vanishes, a zero first-order tilt derivative can leave source fluctuations and quadratic tilt error as the leading terms. Their balance gives a phase transition in the limiting distribution. A continuous example gives different convergence rates for two correctly specified bases at the same distribution pair. Controlled experiments assess the fixed-proportion theory. Natural-data comparisons distinguish variance reductions from improvements in reference RMSE driven by smaller reference bias.

### [arXiv:2611.27951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27951) — Topology Hub Optimal Transport: Eliminating Long Context Dilution at Flash Speed (Dylan Forde)

_Watchlist hit (title: optimal transport) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Long-context models use sparse or structured attention to avoid quadratic cost, yet retrieving a small set of relevant facts embedded among hundreds of thousands of distractors degrades as normalization mass disperses with length. Hub-and-spoke optimal transport (OT) replaces the dense $L \times L$ matrix with a linear-size graph over local windows and $K$ shared hubs balanced by Sinkhorn scaling. Building on block-wise tail refinement and fixed-support transport topology (arXiv:2605.08123, arXiv:2609.07954), we introduce Topology Hub Optimal Transport (THOT): (1) bounded-support capacity gating via simplex sparsemax ($O(L\log L)$) or sorting-free token-local thresholded ReLU ($O(L)$), driving excess distractor mass to zero; (2) a three-sweep $1+2$ annealed row-closed Sinkhorn schedule that damps bipartite residuals; (3) an exact rank-$(D+2R+1)$ One-Reference-Tile adjoint in batched split-factor Triton kernels (BF16 Tensor Cores, FP32 potentials); and (4) a chunked exclusive-prefix causal variant with verified zero future leakage. On NVIDIA L4, sparsemax THOT trained at $L=128$ reaches 97.40% zero-shot recall of fixed bindings at $L=262{,}144$ under pure task loss ($\alpha=0$) and 98.83% at $\alpha=0.05$ (matched by $O(L)$ thresholded ReLU at $\alpha=0.05$, vs. 80.60% at $\alpha=0$), while softmax-gated hub OT drops to 3.52%. In bidirectional operator microbenchmarks, our kernel runs forward+VJP in 100.1 ms at $H=8, D=64, L=262\mathrm{K}$ (2.2x faster with 4.0x smaller compiler temporaries than latent softmax) and 1.34 ms vs. 4.37 ms for cuDNN FlashAttention at $L=4{,}096$; multi-layer LM runs use the unfused path. Zero-shot WikiText-103 multi-needle retrieval hits 100.00% (128/128 queries at $L=65\mathrm{K}$) vs. 0.78% for short-trained RoPE FlashAttention, and compact 4-layer WikiText-4K runs show modest causal and masked LM gains.
