### [arXiv:2610.27078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27078) — LEAF: LUT-aware Efficient Attention on FPGAs (Arianna Cox et al.)

_Watchlist hit (jet tagging) — original abstract; no AI summary._

We introduce Lookup-table (LUT)-aware Efficient Attention on FPGAs (LEAF). LEAF is the first nanosecond-scale LUT-aware transformer for FPGAs. Deploying transformers that are large enough to perform well, small enough to fit on an FPGA, and fast enough to meet the sub-microsecond timescales required by real-time systems such as those at CERN remains difficult, since the same attention mechanism that gives these models their high expressivity also makes them costly to implement in hardware. LEAF converts the query, key, and value projections and the final output feed-forward layer into FPGA-native layers with LUT-aware training, creating the first LUT-aware transformer and linformer layers built for ultra-low-latency inference. Attention resists LUT-aware training because it multiplies two activations where the method needs a constant weight to tabulate; LEAF tabulates the attention block's constant matrix-vector multiplications and leaves the rest in arithmetic form. On a standard jet tagging benchmark this improves accuracy by 1.8 percentage points while using 2.9 times fewer LUTs than the previous state-of-the-art sub-microsecond transformer at the same input size. Our most efficient LEAF-based linformer with 64 input particles reaches 80.4% classification accuracy at 28k LUTs and 35ns.

### [arXiv:2610.27106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27106) — This is SParTA: A modular, efficient and scalable transformer architecture for jet-tagging (Rajneil Baruah et al.)

_Watchlist hit (jet constituents, jet tagging, boosted top, quark-gluon tagging, ParticleNet, particle transformer, colliders) — original abstract; no AI summary._

We present the Sparse Particle-interaction Transformer Architecture (SParTA), a modular, scalable, and efficient transformer for jet tagging at high-energy colliders. Exploiting the steeply falling transverse-momentum ($p_T$) spectrum of jet constituents, SParTA factorizes constituent processing into two topological regimes. Ordered by descending $p_T$, only the $N$ hardest constituents (the hard core) participate in quadratic $O(N^2)$ differential interaction attention driven by pairwise physical and learned relational features. The remaining soft constituents are integrated via linear $O(lN)$ cross-attention to the hard core, preserving soft-radiation and color-flow information while avoiding the $O(l^2)$ memory bottleneck of full self-attention over all $l$ constituents. Pairwise interaction features are computed dynamically in-network from constituent four-momenta and conditioned on global jet-level substructure observables. On the quark-gluon tagging benchmark, a 300K-parameter SParTA model achieves an AUC of 92.02% and accuracy of 85.06%, matching the 2.14M-parameter Particle Transformer (ParT) at one-seventh the parameter count and outperforming IAFormer by 21% in background rejection at 30% signal efficiency. On the boosted top-tagging benchmark, SParTA outperforms ParT and ParticleNet with 511K parameters, while we analyze the architectural trade-offs relative to fully dense multi-layer interaction networks.

### [arXiv:2610.27366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27366) — Trail-Weighted Influence Scores for Approximate Outcome Prediction in Bayesian Networks (Sahil Kumar Rajput et al.)

_Watchlist hit (collider, colliders) — original abstract; no AI summary._

We study trail-weighted pruning of evidence for Bayesian-network prediction. A traversal enumerates the active trails from each evidence node to the outcome, scores each trail by the product of its edge strengths, and drops evidence whose score falls below a threshold. For collider-free networks (chains and forks) over binary variables, we show that the trail score equals the exact correlation between a trail's endpoints, that a chain and a fork with equal edge strengths have identical joint distributions, and that the pruned posterior is within a computable sum of per-trail terms of the exact posterior when each dropped trail lies in its own branch of the outcome node. We prove this bound, check it exhaustively on all small trees, and show that it fails when dropped trails share a branch. In simulations on random trees, thresholding trail scores gives 1.4 to 13 times lower error than random pruning of the same size. We also apply the score, as an unproven heuristic, to seven bnlearn networks with colliders and loops. Keeping the top half of the active evidence by trail score gives lower error than random pruning on all seven, but it is not clearly better than a mutual-information ranking, and scoring costs more than the inference it saves on six of the seven, so we show no speedup. Models trained on trail, graph and information features and tested on networks they never saw do not beat the trail score or mutual information, but trail features carry most of what they use and clearly outperform generic graph features. A bound for general networks is left to future work.
