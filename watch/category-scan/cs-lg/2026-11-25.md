### [arXiv:2611.31403](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31403) — OmniX: A first jet and event model (Anna Hallin et al.)

_Watchlist hit (Gregor Kasieczka) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Foundation models are a new approach to building machine learning algorithms for particle physics. Their central idea is that a single model can be adapted to a variety of downstream tasks, amortizing training costs. So far, most foundation model prototypes have operated on the constituents of a single jet. This limits their usefulness for physics problems at the scale of full events, such as distinguishing different processes, and restricts their performance, as global correla- tions are invisible to the model. Here, we introduce OmniX, an architecture that operates on both single jets and whole events within a single model. Using the jet-level JetClass and Top-tagging datasets and the event-level Collide-2V dataset, we demonstrate that OmniX trained jointly on single jets and full events matches or exceeds otherwise equivalent jet-only and event-only models in generation and classification.

### [arXiv:2611.31525](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31525) — SOTS: Signature-based Optimal Transport Selection (Khalid Akhmouch et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: optimal transport) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Training data selection for machine learning models is a critical bottleneck in industrial applications where expert annotation is costly and time-consuming. Existing coreset selection methods for time series either ignore temporal structure entirely or cannot handle variable-length, irregularly sampled, multi-channel signals, the setting most common in industrial monitoring and testing. We introduce SOTS (Signature-based Optimal Transport Selection), an unsupervised coreset selection framework for irregular multivariate time series (IMTS) classification. Each sample is mapped to a truncated path signature, capturing the joint geometric structure of its channels, and the coreset is selected by greedily minimizing the Sliced Wasserstein distance between the empirical signature distributions of the candidate subset and the full dataset, a criterion for which we establish a convergence guarantee for random coresets. We validate SOTS on a real-world, highly imbalanced industrial I&C testing dataset, where SOTS-Greedy exceeds 95% of full-dataset accuracy using only 30% of the training data, outperforming standard coreset baselines at most budgets. This advantage generalizes to public UEA regular multivariate time series benchmarks specifically when class-discriminative information is purely geometric.

### [arXiv:2611.32402](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32402) — Optimal Transport Meets t-SNE: Integrated Visualization without Batch Effects in OT-tSNE (Azhar Akhmetova et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: optimal transport) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Visual exploration is an integral step in understanding the structure of datasets. However, systematic variation between datasets collected under different conditions, known as batch effects, can obscure shared structure and complicate their joint visualization. Existing methods often rely on cross-batch nearest-neighbor relations to integrate the batches. Yet, these local correspondences can be unreliable for challenging cross-batch geometries. We propose optimal-transport t-SNE (OT-tSNE), which replaces nearest-neighbor-based matching with globally coupled affinities derived from entropic optimal transport. Controlled synthetic experiments demonstrated that nearest-neighbor-based methods can deteriorate, while optimal-transport affinities and OT-tSNE embeddings remain robust. Across seven real single-cell datasets, we benchmarked OT-tSNE against a broad range of integration and visualization methods and found that it achieves a Pareto-efficient trade-off between batch mixing and biological conservation, and integrates batches particularly well.

### [arXiv:2611.32547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32547) — Contact Geometry for Generative Models: An Unbalanced Optimal Transport Formulation (Andrea Testa et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: optimal transport) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Real-world generative problems in biology, medical imaging, or robotics, rarely come with perfectly paired source and target distributions. While unbalanced Optimal Transport (uOT) provides a robust framework to bridge unpaired datasets via minimum-length paths in probability space, existing formulations based on the Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao (WFR) geometry introduce a structural bias toward high density regions, often triggering mode collapse. We introduce Contact unbalanced Optimal Transport (CuOT), a novel uOT formulation grounded in contact geometry that decouples density transport direction from mass growth to eliminate structural bias. This allows CuOT to adapt sampling steps to the local data structure, naturally reducing step sizes in high-density regions to prevent overshooting and ensuring stable convergence. The result is a stable, scalable path-length minimization solver that consistently outperforms WFR-based methods on biological dynamics reconstruction, unpaired image-to-image translation, and video generation.
