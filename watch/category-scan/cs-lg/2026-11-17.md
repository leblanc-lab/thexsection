### [arXiv:2611.19815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19815) — Conditional Optimal Transport and Triangular Maps for Imitation Learning (Mohammad Al-Jarrah et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: optimal transport) — original abstract; no AI summary._

We introduce a unified formulation of imitation learning from demonstrations as a conditional sampling problem solved by triangular transport: a policy is the second block of a triangular map that transports a reference distribution to the conditional distribution of the action chunk given the observation history. Unlike in Diffusion Policy, where it is a fixed Gaussian, the reference becomes a design variable; we take it to be the empirical distribution of the demonstrated action chunks and introduce three instances of the formulation: an optimal transport policy (OTP), a flow matching policy (FMP), and a stochastic interpolant policy (SIP). On the Push-T and robomimic Square tasks, the three policies are competitive in task performance with Diffusion Policy and Consistency Policy while using considerably smaller networks and shorter training, and the informative reference accounts for a substantial part of their performance when few network evaluations are used. The three instances offer different trade-offs rather than a single best method: OTP generates an action chunk with a single evaluation of the smallest network (0.23 million parameters on Push-T) and is suitable for settings in which inference time and model size matter, whereas SIP attains the highest scores among the proposed policies at a higher cost.

### [arXiv:2611.20602](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20602) — Quantum Computing-Based Wasserstein Generative Adversarial Networks for Airfoil Parameterization (Abdur Rahman Ovy et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: wasserstein) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Airfoil geometry parameterization is essential in aerodynamic design optimization. Optimization search in the large space suffers from low efficiency and modest feasible region, which could further incur failures to convergence. Generative adversarial networks (GAN), aiming to learn the statistical distribution of existing data, pave a new avenue for shape generation and airfoil parameterization. GAN manages to implicitly reduce design space by generating only smooth, realistic airfoils. Meanwhile, quantum computing has been emergingly exhibiting great potential for achieving superior performance in specific types of engineering problems but has not been introduced to airfoil parameterization. To fill this gap, this work develops quantum computing-based Wasserstein GAN (Q-WGAN) and investigates the performance for airfoil parameterization. The Q-WGAN adopts parameterized quantum circuits as the generator to promote the generative performance, while the discriminator remains classical. The developed Q-WGAN is demonstrated on a dataset of optimal airfoils parameterized by free-form deformation (FFD) control points, resulting from 992 airfoil aerodynamic design optimizations. Results show that Q-WGAN achieves 92.1% mean fitting accuracy on the optimal FFD control points and 99.2% accuracy on airfoil coordinates, comparable with the classical WGAN performance of 92.28% on FFD control points and 99.26% on airfoil coordinates. Q-WGAN achieves lower Jensen-Shannon divergence, Fr\'echet distance, and Wasserstein distance than the classical WGAN, indicating an improved agreement with the reference airfoil distribution. Therefore, this work presents a convincing evidence that Q-WGAN makes a viable option for parameterizing airfoils and possesses a great potential for other relevant engineering applications.

### [arXiv:2611.21594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21594) — Surrogate-Assisted Compact-Model Extraction for SKY130 CMOS at 77 K (Aaron Wang et al.)

_Watchlist hit (Nhan Tran) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Cryogenic circuit design requires compact transistor models that reproduce measured low-temperature behavior and run in standard circuit simulators. We fit BSIM4 parameters to measured I-V curves of SkyWater 130 nm (SKY130) transistors at 77 K using a neural surrogate of NGSpice. The surrogate maps compact-model parameters and device geometry to the complete set of output and transfer characteristics. Extraction is a bounded least-squares search: a bank of NGSpice simulations supplies initial candidates, finite differences of the surrogate supply the local Jacobian, and NGSpice re-simulates the resulting cards before selection. On 18 measured geometries, we compare a search over seven card parameters with a search over 43 parameters selected by NGSpice sensitivity. Relative to NGSpice re-simulations of the previously released cards, the two searches reduce the in-sample mean relative root-mean-square error (RRMS) by 17% and 26%, respectively. The 43-parameter search has the lower overall error, but the seven-parameter search has the lower mean on high-bias curves, and some individual curves have higher error than with the released cards under both searches.
