### [arXiv:2611.15607](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15607) — FADO: Learning Causal Explanations with Prior-Fitted Networks (Seth Flaxman et al.)

_Watchlist hit (colliders) — original abstract; no AI summary._

In regression and classification tasks with tabular datasets, most of the literature on explanation in ML (e.g. SHAP, permutation importance) has focused on predictive attribution: does feature $X_i$ help predict outcome $Y$? At its heart, predictive attribution captures correlation, not causation. We instead ask a causal question: would manipulating feature $X_i$ change outcome $Y$? We define a natural quantity to answer this question, the feature intervention effect: $\Delta_i = E[Y\mid do(X_i=+1)]-E[Y\mid do(X_i=-1)]$. In general, estimating this quantity from observational data alone would entail strong causal assumptions. We propose FADO (Feature-wise Amortized Do-Operators), an amortized inference method using prior-fitted networks trained on synthetic tabular datasets generated by structural causal models. During training, the synthetic data-generating process is known, so we can compute ground-truth intervention effects $\Delta_i$, and train a model to predict them at test time from a purely observational tabular dataset. As we demonstrate through extensive experiments, our new causal explainability method is easy to train and provides better explanations under a range of settings for which standard predictive attribution fails, including proxy variables, confounders, colliders, and placebos. We evaluate it on real, synthetic and semi-synthetic datasets, with and without hidden confounders, and compare it to existing predictive attribution methods and state-of-the-art causal inference methods.

### [arXiv:2611.15885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15885) — Reward-Driven Collider Physics Challenges for Self-Improving Agentic AI via Reinforcement Learning (Alexander Yue et al.)

_Watchlist hit (collider) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Recent advances in agentic AI have demonstrated the potential to automate complex high-energy physics (HEP) workflows. We focus on the regime of HEP tasks where frontier models still struggle to provide correct and complete solutions, and introduce an open-ended optimization task format with continuous, automatically evaluated rewards. Unlike conventional benchmarks focused on task completion, these tasks target reinforcement learning (RL) environments to generate agentic improvement on state- of-the-art HEP challenges. We benchmark frontier reasoning models on several HEP tasks and demonstrate an initial proof of concept in which a small language model improves through RL using automated feedback without human intervention. By connecting challenging HEP problems with verifiable scientific rewards and RL, we aim to advance agentic AI from reproducing established workflows to autonomously discovering better scientific solutions, paving the way toward self-improving scientific agents.
