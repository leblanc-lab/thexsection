### [arXiv:2610.29007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29007) — A Jump-Modified Wasserstein Gradient Flow for Maximum Mean Discrepancy with Energy Kernels (Ansh Desai et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: wasserstein) — original abstract; no AI summary._

We study a jump-modified Wasserstein gradient flow of the squared maximum mean discrepancy generated by the energy kernels $K(z)=-|z|^q$, $0<q<2$, in every dimension $d\ge2$ as well as the one-dimensional range $1\le q<2$. Under subcritical density and finite-moment assumptions, we prove global well-posedness. The added jumps transfer mass toward smaller values of the first variation and dissipate the same MMD energy as the transport term. Under our hypotheses on the target and jump rates, every Borel probability measure with finite MMD energy and zero jump dissipation is equal to the target. Combining this rigidity with compactness and a second-moment identity yields convergence to the target from finite-second-moment data, without additional uniform-in-time density or moment assumptions. We also construct a global collision-free particle process whose energy is nonincreasing along each path. A quantitative mean-field estimate applies to general initial empirical approximations, including deterministic configurations; propagation of chaos for exchangeable data and long-time target approximation follow as consequences.

### [arXiv:2610.29439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29439) — How Far Can Foundation Models Be Distilled for Efficient Jet Tagging? (Jay Agarwal et al.)

_Watchlist hit (jet tagging, jet tagger, Large Hadron Collider, collider) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Jet tagging is one of the most important applications of machine learning at the Large Hadron Collider. Recent models build in more physics knowledge or rely on large-scale pretraining, but the experiments place strict limits on their inference cost. Knowledge distillation can transfer their performance to small students that are cheap to run. We ask which properties of a teacher help the student, how small a student can get and how close it can come to the state of the art. On a jet tagging benchmark, we distil six teachers that differ in architecture, symmetry and pretraining, including two foundation models, into five small students. Every teacher improves every student, and the gain depends mainly on the strength of the teacher. We then shrink our two strongest distilled students, Transformer and L-GATr-slim, in width and in depth. With 20 thousand parameters, a distilled L-GATr-slim outperforms every published jet tagger trained from scratch at a fraction of the inference cost of every teacher. Even with two thousand parameters, it matches ParT, a model with a thousand times as many parameters. We also study what the students learn from their teachers. Our benchmark of inference cost shows that students of the same size can differ by almost an order of magnitude in speed. We release the code and the weights of all trained models.

### [arXiv:2610.29679](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29679) — Lifting PCA in the Wasserstein Space (J\'er\'emie Bigot et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: wasserstein) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is a fundamental tool for statistical dimension reduction, yet no canonical extension to distribution-valued data has emerged within the framework of optimal transport. While Wasserstein barycenters and Wasserstein least-squares regression provide canonical liftings of Euclidean averaging and linear regression in the Wasserstein space, an analogous principle for PCA has remained unexplored. In this paper, we introduce Lifted PCA, a canonical extension of Euclidean PCA to Wasserstein space, obtained through the framework of lifting functionals in the space of probability measures by convex extension. Starting from the classical variational formulations of PCA on the Stiefel and Grassmann manifolds, we derive two equivalent minimization problems over probability measures supported on these manifolds that correspond to Wasserstein barycenters with generalized costs. We establish an exact analogue of the equivalence between the Stiefel and Grassmann formulations of Euclidean PCA, preserving PCA's original variational structure under lifting. We further derive dual formulations and optimality conditions, and establish equivalences with multimarginal optimal transport and matching-for-teams problems. We also establish statistical consistency for empirical estimators and develop computational algorithms based on the Wasserstein barycenter formulation over the Stiefel manifold. Whereas Euclidean PCA summarizes vectors through projections onto an optimal low-dimensional linear subspace, Lifted PCA summarizes probability distributions through an optimal probability measure supported on multiple low-dimensional subspaces. Numerical experiments on distributional datasets illustrate the practical performance of the proposed methodology and its benefits for distributional data analysis.
