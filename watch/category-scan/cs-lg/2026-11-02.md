### [arXiv:2610.40734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40734) — Model-Agnostic Search for New Physics in the Presence of Imperfect Background by Smearing the Data (Soheun Yi et al.)

_Watchlist hit (LHC, Large Hadron Collider, collider) — original abstract; no AI summary._

In the search for new particles in high-energy physics, it is necessary to first define the signal region (SR) enriched in signal events, and then compare events in this region with the expected background distribution. While most existing search methods rely on prior domain knowledge of either the signal or the background, there has been less effort to relax both assumptions simultaneously, as methods that weaken one assumption typically rely on strong knowledge of the other. We propose Model-Agnostic Search with Smearing and Imperfect Background (MASSIB), a method that addresses this challenge by learning the density ratio of events relative to a misspecified background distribution using modern tools from machine learning, and defining the SR as the region with high density ratio. Recognizing that a naive application of this idea introduces significant bias in background estimation when interpolating from the control region to the SR, MASSIB mitigates this issue by introducing a "smeared" density ratio, which can be learned efficiently through data-level manipulation and used to define an SR that allows for accurate background estimation. By applying it to various types of simulated Large Hadron Collider (LHC) events with realistic background mismodeling, we demonstrate that MASSIB identifies a data-driven SR that achieves high signal concentration consistently across different types of signals while avoiding bias in background estimation. The method does this without making strong assumptions about either the signal or background distribution. We then show that this robustness extends to the validity and strong statistical power of the resulting hypothesis tests for signal detection, demonstrating the potential of this approach for future model-agnostic searches at the LHC.

### [arXiv:2610.41269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41269) — Coarse-Graining Optimal Transport for Rollout-Free Post-Training of Flow Matching (Soon Hoe Lim et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: optimal transport) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Flow matching depends critically on the coupling used to pair source and target samples. Optimal transport (OT) couplings can reduce velocity conflict and straighten trajectories; but, in practice, challenges remain: the source comes from a continuous distribution, only a finite number of target samples data are accessible, and the neural network has finite capacity. For these reasons, making the coupling more OT-like may not consistently improve sampling quality, in particular in the low number-of-function-evaluation (NFE) regime. Motivated by this, we introduce ReCouple, a controllable framework that constructs coarse-grained OT couplings, to better understand rollout-free OT-based post-training. We also consider extensions of this basic framework: ReCouple+ extends this toward the continuous-source setting by increasing source resolution, converging to semi-discrete OT in the appropriate limit; and ReCouple++ further adds spatial regularization, motivated by the observation that straight trajectories need not correspond to spatially simple velocity fields. Empirical validation demonstrates the effectiveness of these methods, and it reveals an NFE-dependent interplay between coupling optimality, neural realizability, and generation quality.

### [arXiv:2610.41540](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41540) — FOCUS: First-Order Control Using Optimal Transport Supervision for Imitation from Observations (Jo\~ao A. C\^andido Ramos et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: optimal transport) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Imitation learning from observations must recover behavior from state-only demonstrations without known temporal correspondence. Differentiable simulators provide pathwise policy gradients, but differentiating through long, contact-rich trajectories can make optimization unstable. We introduce FOCUS, which combines local optimal-transport alignment with short-horizon differentiable actor-critic learning. FOCUS matches agent rollout windows to multiple sampled expert segments using a soft best-of-$K$ objective that emphasizes locally compatible matches. The resulting transport-based costs provide differentiable imitation rewards, while a learned critic bootstraps value beyond the rollout horizon. FOCUS requires neither temporally aligned trajectories nor expert-aligned initialization or teacher forcing. Across contact-rich locomotion tasks in DFlex, including a musculoskeletal humanoid controlled by 152 muscle activations, FOCUS improves sample efficiency and training stability over adversarial imitation-from-observation methods and prior differentiable imitation baselines.

### [arXiv:2610.41599](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41599) — Underdamped Stochastic Optimal Transport with Landauer boundary conditions: machine learning and analytical solution (Julia Sanders et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: optimal transport) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Finding optimal transport protocols for underdamped stochastic processes is a difficult yet important problem in control theory. In this paper we develop a direct method, based on machine learning, to find the optimal solution of the problem in the presence of non-linear boundary conditions, including those modelling the Landauer bit-erasure problem. The dynamics is steered from a single-peak to a double-peak distribution in position space, by only acting on a controllable external potential. The problem is highly non-linear, and analytical solutions are not available in general. We benchmark our numerical results with a perturbative expansion, which is expected to hold in a suitable overdamped limit.

### [arXiv:2610.41958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41958) — WASABI: Wasserstein Sampling via Bridge Interpolants (Sebastian Salazar et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: wasserstein) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Sampling from Boltzmann distributions is a fundamental problem in physics, chemistry, and biology. We introduce WAsserstein SAmpling via Bridge Interpolants (WASABI), a method that trains a diffusion model to generate samples from a target Boltzmann distribution given access only to the energy function. WASABI minimizes the KL divergence between the distribution generated by its diffusion model and the Boltzmann target using Wasserstein gradient flows. This approach builds on a principle underlying classical sampling methods such as Langevin dynamics. However, applying Wasserstein gradient flows to diffusion models is challenging because the updates require the gradient of the model's own log density, which is generally intractable. We derive a practical training algorithm that avoids computing this gradient. Once trained, WASABI can generate new samples without further evaluations of the energy or its gradient. On alanine dipeptide, WASABI outperforms the best reported baselines using an estimated 3.3 times fewer energy evaluations. It also achieves competitive performance on interacting particle system benchmarks.

### [arXiv:2610.42109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42109) — Regret-Optimal Wasserstein-Robust Linear Regression (Sloan Nietert et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: wasserstein) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Distributionally robust optimization (DRO) minimizes worst-case risk under distributional uncertainty, but its decisions can be suboptimal in terms of their excess risk, or regret. We study this gap for multivariate least-squares regression, assuming that the observed source law and unknown target law differ by an adversarial 2-Wasserstein perturbation of size $\varepsilon$. We first identify the asymptotic complexity of robust decision-making in the $\varepsilon \to 0$ limit, characterized by a problem-specific condition number $\kappa$. Here, a wide spectrum of simple algorithms, including DRO, achieve the instance-optimal rate of $O(\kappa \varepsilon^2)$. For fixed $\varepsilon > 0$, we prove that DRO still achieves the minimax rate if the problem has bounded residual effective rank. On the other hand, we identify significant failure modes where DRO is provably suboptimal by dimension-dependent factors. Fortunately, we prove that two alternative procedures, minimax regret optimization (MRO) and spectral-norm regularization (SNR), attain the minimax rate. We derive an exact convex reformulation of MRO and an accelerated proximal algorithm for SNR, along with high-probability finite-sample guarantees. We support these theoretical results with numerical experiments separating the performance of MRO and SNR from DRO.
