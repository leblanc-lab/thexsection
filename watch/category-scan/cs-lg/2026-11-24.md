### [arXiv:2611.28194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28194) — HEP-Ex-Bench: Evaluating Agentic Performance in High Energy Physics Experiment Data Analyses with Sealed Worlds (Andrzej Nov\'ak et al.)

_Watchlist hit (Philip Harris, Michael Kagan) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Agentic systems are increasingly being explored for automating experimental high-energy physics measurements, but the correctness of their results can today be reliably evaluated only via extensive expert review. To address this, we propose a method to perform detailed quantitative evaluations of agent-driven analyses through the use of blind analysis and injection tests, which are critical in a typical expert review. Agents develop a data analysis pipeline on a public dataset, while the evaluation is performed on programmatically generated sealed private datasets where signals are injected and simulation settings varied within realistic detector uncertainties. By requiring agents to provide a single workflow that is evaluated across these "sealed worlds", we are able to test the accuracy and robustness of the workflows. We demonstrate the approach on four tasks built from CMS open data and present it as a way to reliably benchmark scientific work in experimental high-energy physics. At the time of writing, the best-performing model, Gemini 4 Argon, reaches 60% of the score of the expert reference analyses on average, ahead of GPT 6 Astra at 38% and Claude Opus 5.5 at 31%. In individual sessions on one of the tasks, Gemini 4 Argon exceeds the reference. The benchmark is hosted at https://hep-ex-bench.github.io

### [arXiv:2611.28355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28355) — Real-time event-level zero-shot learning at 150 ns: towards a foundation model for particle physics experiment triggering (Leo Rozanov et al.)

_Watchlist hit (LHC, Large Hadron Collider, collider) — original abstract; no AI summary._

The hardware-based Level-1 triggers of the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) experiments select collisions with algorithms designed for one signature each, acting on a coarse, low-latency view of every event. We present a learned event embedding for several trigger tasks and evaluate its transfer to processes absent from training. Using paired offline and Level-1 views of the same simulated collisions, we train an offline transformer with a supervised metric loss and distil it, event by event, into a quantised Level-1 transformer whose bit widths are learned under a fixed resource budget. Trained on 25 Standard Model processes, the embedding places 28 held-out processes next to their physical relatives. A nearest-neighbour selection against background collisions, which never enter encoder training, rejects 99% of them. It retains a median of 90% of seen and 96% of unseen Standard Model events and 88% of a set of 52 beyond-the-Standard-Model benchmark samples, the best-performing score across the three groups among the baselines evaluated on the same events. A second gate against the seen processes isolates high-mass dilepton signals into label-free clusters, and long-lived signals too when displaced-track information is available. With 7512 parameters and an FPGA latency of 147 ns, the encoder uses one event representation, learned once, to derive several selections, opening the door to a foundation model for the LHC's hardware-based triggers.

### [arXiv:2611.29363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29363) — Is the learner or the summary the limit? Auditing machine-learning energy reconstruction for air-shower arrays on KASCADE and GRAPES-3 (Mugdha Raje Ashiya et al.)

_Watchlist hit (LHC) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Machine-learning energy estimators for cosmic-ray arrays are usually trained on a few fitted shower parameters, not station signals. When their resolution stops improving, standard diagnostics cannot say whether a better model would help or the summary has run out of information. We use a five-step audit that compares trained models with a learner-independent estimate of the lowest error the summary allows (its squared-loss Bayes risk, or floor), tests which inputs add information and flags inputs merely computed from the fit. We apply it to public KASCADE simulations with three hadronic-interaction models (QGSJET-II-04, EPOS-LHC and SIBYLL-2.3d), to 13,848,509 measured KASCADE events and to simulated GRAPES-3 events. Within known energy decades below 10^16 eV, gradient boosting on nine reconstructed features is within 2.2% of the estimated floor under every estimator setting, so more capacity is not expected to gain more than about 2%. The floor is an estimate, accurate to about 1% in a semi-synthetic benchmark with intervals that under-cover, and not a bound on the true Bayes risk. Replacing reconstructed by true particle numbers removes 70 to 72% of the error variance below 10^15 eV and 24 to 26% between 10^16 and 10^17 eV on all three models, yet simulated station signals close about a tenth of that gap. On GRAPES-3, networks given images rendered from the fit do no better than the fit. In measured data, the mean muon size at fixed electron size lies within every model's proton-to-iron bracket and a label-free probe finds the muon channel used as in simulation; neither fixes the energy scale. Energy-labelled results are simulated.

### [arXiv:2611.29646](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29646) — JKO-GRPO: Augmenting GRPO with Wasserstein Proximal Steps for Flow-Matching VLAs (Jiyong Kwon et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: wasserstein) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Flow-based vision-language-action (VLA) models such as $\pi_0,\pi_{0.5}$ generate continuous action chunks by flow matching, and online reinforcement learning (RL) can improve them beyond supervised fine-tuning. Existing methods, such as Flow-GRPO and $\pi_{\mathrm{RL}}$, optimize likelihood ratios of stochastic denoising transitions, which reweight the sampled actions by their advantages but do not use the distances between them. We propose JKO-GRPO, which augments Flow-GRPO with Wasserstein proximal steps, with two contributions: (1) At each state, we treat the group of sampled actions as an empirical distribution and formulate policy improvement as a Jordan-Kinderlehrer-Otto (JKO) step. We use a soft discrete $c$-transform to construct transport targets from group-relative advantages and combine shared-noise regression of the deterministic flow sampler onto these targets and combined with the clipped Flow-GRPO objective without Kullback-Leibler (KL) regularization. (2) We extend this approach to sequential VLA control through random-time branching: we select one or multiple action-chunk indices along old-policy trajectories and independently apply group-level JKO updates using candidate continuations from the corresponding states.

### [arXiv:2611.30376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30376) — Low-Rank Tensor Representations for Efficient Multimarginal Optimal Transport (Johann Cl{\'e}ment-Cottuz (UNIROUEN et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: optimal transport) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Multimarginal Optimal Transport (MOT) suffers from exponential computational and memory costs in the number of marginals, as transport plans are represented by high-dimensional tensors. We introduce Low-Rank Multimarginal Optimal Transport (LRMOT), which restricts transport plans to tensors with bounded multilinear rank, providing a compact and structured representation of multimarginal couplings. This structure enables efficient algorithms with a low memory footprint. We establish approximation bounds for the resulting low-rank formulations and demonstrate experimentally that the proposed algorithms substantially reduce computational time while achieving solutions with low transport cost.

### [arXiv:2611.31011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31011) — Lorentz symmetry in self-supervised representation learning (Andreas Hermansen et al.)

_Watchlist hit (jet tagging) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Self-supervised learning (SSL) and explicit physical inductive biases have driven major advances in jet tagging, yet they are typically pursued along separate tracks. In this work, we unify both paradigms by integrating the Lorentz-equivariant Geometric Algebra Transformer slim (L-GATr-slim) architecture with the LeJEPA framework for representation learning. Pre-training on 100 million jets from the JetClass dataset, we optimize towards a latent space, which is invariant across physics-motivated jet augmentations. We demonstrate that enforcing Lorentz symmetry in the latent space is critical for representation quality and that this can be achieved either architecturally or learned via kinematic augmentations. With a standard non-invariant Transformer backbone, LeJEPA outperforms established autoregressive, masked particle modelling, and self-distillation baselines in linear- and MLP-probe accuracy, with L-GATr-slim providing further complementary gains. Across downstream benchmarks, our pre-trained models consistently outperform baselines trained from scratch or through different pre-training objectives.
