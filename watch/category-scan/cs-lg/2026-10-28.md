### [arXiv:2610.33922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33922) — Learning Population Dynamics with Variational Doob-Wasserstein Flow (Weizhu Qian)

_Watchlist hit (title: wasserstein) — original abstract; no AI summary._

This paper introduces a novel simulation-free framework for learning unbalanced population dynamics on the Riemannian Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao (WFR) manifold, termed Variational Doob-Wasserstein Flow (VDWF), built upon the theory of the unbalanced mean-field Schr\"odinger bridge (MFSB). First, by independently learning a geometry model equipped with a data-dependent metric, our model captures the intrinsic geometric structure of population dynamics. Furthermore, leveraging Doob's h-transform, the proposed method incorporates boundary information to improve flow-based trajectory inference. For unbalanced transport, a growth model is employed to learn the dynamics while constructing intermediate mass samples in the WFR space without simulation. Additionally, we analyze the convergence rate of the proposed framework using a uniform Log-Sobolev inequality (LSI). Finally, we validate the proposed approach on multiple real-world datasets, demonstrating competitive performance in learning both balanced and unbalanced population dynamics compared with state-of-the-art methods.

### [arXiv:2610.34120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34120) — Synthetic Data Augmentation for Functional Data: Wasserstein Risk Bounds and Diffusion-Based Conditional Generation (Yuhao Qi et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: wasserstein) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Functional data are costly to collect; samples of tens to hundreds of curves are common. Synthetic data augmentation can help, but its theory is developed for finite-dimensional data and relies on total variation, which is uninformative for functions. We study augmentation with a class-conditional functional diffusion generator and state guarantees in the Wasserstein-1 distance. For stable downstream estimators, we bound the risk of training on synthetic curves, from above and below, by the risk of training on as many real curves plus a generation penalty, and derive the best number of synthetic curves and a condition under which synthetic data outperform real data. For allocating synthetic curves under class imbalance, we bound the excess risk of training on real and synthetic curves together, without sample splitting. In applications to temperature, hyperspectral and gait curves, augmentation improved classification, function-to-scalar regression and function-to-function regression, and directing synthetic curves to the minority class gave the largest gain under class imbalance.

### [arXiv:2610.35314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35314) — Beyond Wasserstein Proximity: Finding Better Transfer Sources (Gordei Verbii et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: wasserstein) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Source selection often favors similar data, yet marginal proximity need not identify the most useful pretraining source. We motivate a conditional alternative through domain-adaptation theory and a regression example in which broader source variation improves estimation despite increasing Wasserstein distance. We examine five-day cryptocurrency volatility using ten Binance spot assets, six forecaster families, three seeds, and shared temporal cutoffs. Selecting the three farthest rather than nearest Gaussian-Wasserstein sources improves mean persistence-relative skill by 12.52 percentage points for MLP and 20.90 for Transformer, with positive post-hoc paired date-block intervals. Forward pilot selection improves mean skill over random transfer in all six families. Compact recurrent models achieve the lowest absolute mean errors, separating selection utility from forecasting quality. Retrospective five-candidate screening shows additional gains from farthest-first geometry before pilot ranking for MLP and Transformer. We distinguish across-target associations from within-target decisions and evaluate target heterogeneity, training seeds, and temporal-block sensitivity. Findings are exploratory for a fixed historical panel; the screening analysis measures no runtime saving.

### [arXiv:2610.35490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35490) — Minimax Rates for Conditional Generative Learning under Wasserstein Loss (Sungwon Park et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: wasserstein) — original abstract; no AI summary._

We study minimax rates for conditional generative learning under integrated Wasserstein loss, with H\"older continuous conditional laws and an unrestricted covariate distribution. We evaluate unrestricted generators through their conditional output distributions. For expected Wasserstein loss, the minimax rate agrees with the rate under uniform covariates and follows from balancing localization bias against unconditional distribution estimation error. Higher moments of the loss can instead be dominated by unobserved covariate regions, which force an additional risk contribution of order $n^{-1}$, where $n$ is the sample size. An adaptive localization argument gives matching upper bounds. The results cover unrestricted response laws and possibly vanishing Besov densities for $W_1$, and extend to higher Wasserstein orders under uniform response density bounds and integrability conditions. A multiscale lower bound closes the logarithmic gap in unconditional $W_1$ minimax rates in dimension two for both unrestricted distributions and Besov density classes. We also establish minimax rates for conditional Brenier maps under density, integrability, and uniform curvature conditions.
