### [arXiv:2611.06746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06746) — CMS ECAL VFE Production Qualification (C. Borca et al.)

_Watchlist hit (LHC) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Upgrading the CMS ECAL barrel for the HL-LHC requires replacement of all on-detector electronics, including 12240 Very-Front-End (VFE) cards employing CATIA and LiTE-DTU ASICs for amplification and digitisation of signals from ECAL's 61200 lead-tungstate crystals. All production VFEs undergo the same qualification procedure: functional testing, environmental stress screening, and calibration. We have received and functionally tested 13596 unique VFEs; two never passed our test criteria. Results for other VFEs are uniform and within expectations, demonstrating the excellent quality of the cards. Key metrics, such as a gain ratio of $10.21 \pm 0.07$ and power consumption of $1.805 \pm 0.013~\mathrm{W}$, demonstrate high consistency across the batch.

### [arXiv:2611.06807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06807) — CMS ECAL Phase-2 upgrade: characterisation and stability analysis of a three-stage low-voltage power-conversion system (Liv Anke Richter et al.)

_Watchlist hit (Large Hadron Collider, collider) — original abstract; no AI summary._

As part of the High-Luminosity upgrade of the Large Hadron Collider, the front-end electronics of the Compact Muon Solenoid electromagnetic calorimeter barrel are being replaced. A three-stage low-voltage power-conversion system supplies the upgraded electronics. As the cascade dynamics require characterisation to ensure stable operation, interactions between the three converter stages are assessed at their two interfaces using the Nyquist criterion. A validated shunt-injection setup using a Bode 100 frequency-response analyser provides in-situ impedance measurements under load to assess the accuracy of the averaged converter models. Results show that both interfaces are stable over the evaluated configurations, with modelled minimum distances to the critical point of 0.76 and 0.79, respectively. A turn-on study identifies capacitor charging at Stage 3 as the main cause of inrush currents triggering Stage 2 overcurrent protection. Two Stage 3 board variants stagger converter enables within and between boards, keeping the measured two-tower current below 5 A, within the 8.0 A protection threshold.
