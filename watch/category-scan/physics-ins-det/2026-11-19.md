### [arXiv:2611.23457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23457) — The Barrel Imaging Calorimeter of the ePIC detector at the Electron-Ion Collider (Bobae Kim (BIC) et al.)

_Watchlist hit (collider) — original abstract; no AI summary._

The Electron-Ion Collider (EIC) is a next-generation facility expected to address fundamental open questions in nuclear physics, including the structure and dynamics of gluons in nucleons and nuclei. The Barrel Imaging Calorimeter (BIC) is the electromagnetic calorimeter of the ePIC detector, designed to meet the stringent performance requirements of the EIC, providing precise measurements and particle identification of scattered electrons, photons, and neutral mesons. The BIC is a high-granularity sampling calorimeter based on lead absorbers interleaved with scintillating fibers, complemented by pixelated imaging layers based on the AstroPix monolithic active pixel sensor. This hybrid design enables both accurate energy and position measurements, as well as three-dimensional shower reconstruction, making it a key component for realizing the full physics potential of the EIC. The BIC has successfully completed its Preliminary Design Review last year and is currently progressing toward the Final Design Review. In this talk, I will present an overview of the BIC concept and focus on its current status and performance results from bench tests, system integration studies, and recent beam tests.

### [arXiv:2611.24061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24061) — The ALICE asynchronous software trigger processing for LHC Run 3 (Sara Pucillo for the ALICE Collaboration)

_Watchlist hit (LHC) — original abstract; no AI summary._

The detector and computing upgrades for LHC Run 3 enable ALICE to continuously acquire proton--proton (pp) and lead--lead (Pb--Pb) collisions at interaction rates of up to 1.5 MHz and 50 kHz, respectively. The resulting pp dataset is more than three orders of magnitude larger than the combined Run 1 and Run 2 samples, and its long-term retention in full would be impractical given the available storage capacity. To address this challenge, ALICE has implemented a physics-driven asynchronous software trigger designed to retain only collisions containing signals of physics interest. Following full asynchronous reconstruction, the Central Event Filter Processor applies more than 100 analysis-based selection channels spanning the ALICE pp physics programme. The selected collisions are then used to skim the original Compressed Time Frames (CTFs), preserving the relevant detector information within detector-specific time windows. This procedure reduces the retained CTF volume to approximately 4.5% of its original size, while preserving the possibility of future reconstruction with improved calibrations and software. This contribution describes the implementation and operation of the asynchronous software trigger, from collision selection to CTF skimming, and highlights how this strategy provides access to rare signals and processes across the ALICE physics programme.
