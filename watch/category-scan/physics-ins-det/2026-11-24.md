### [arXiv:2611.28180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28180) — Machine-learning-assisted photon pairing for neutral-pion reconstruction at future Higgs factories (Yi Wang et al.)

_Watchlist hit (ParticleNet, particle transformer, colliders) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Neutral-pion ($\pi^0$) reconstruction is important for precision measurements at high-energy colliders, particularly in flavor, $\tau$, and Higgs physics. We study $\pi^0$ candidate selection in four benchmark physics processes at electron--positron colliders, comparing an optimized diphoton mass-window (MW) baseline with four machine-learning methods: a boosted decision tree (BDT), a multilayer perceptron (MLP), ParticleNet (PN), and Particle Transformer (ParT). Performance is evaluated using the product of selection efficiency and purity. At the reference ECAL energy resolution, all four machine-learning methods outperform MW. PN and ParT achieve relative improvements of 53%--95% and 60%--128%, respectively, primarily through better rejection of incorrectly matched photon pairs. Their performance advantage is maintained across the investigated ECAL energy resolutions. Training-sample-size scans reveal two periods of rapid performance improvement separated by slower growth in most PN and ParT learning curves. The improved candidate selection provides a starting point for further studies of $\rho$ and hadronic $\tau$ reconstruction.

### [arXiv:2611.28355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28355) — Real-time event-level zero-shot learning at 150 ns: towards a foundation model for particle physics experiment triggering (Leo Rozanov et al.)

_Watchlist hit (LHC, Large Hadron Collider, collider) — original abstract; no AI summary._

The hardware-based Level-1 triggers of the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) experiments select collisions with algorithms designed for one signature each, acting on a coarse, low-latency view of every event. We present a learned event embedding for several trigger tasks and evaluate its transfer to processes absent from training. Using paired offline and Level-1 views of the same simulated collisions, we train an offline transformer with a supervised metric loss and distil it, event by event, into a quantised Level-1 transformer whose bit widths are learned under a fixed resource budget. Trained on 25 Standard Model processes, the embedding places 28 held-out processes next to their physical relatives. A nearest-neighbour selection against background collisions, which never enter encoder training, rejects 99% of them. It retains a median of 90% of seen and 96% of unseen Standard Model events and 88% of a set of 52 beyond-the-Standard-Model benchmark samples, the best-performing score across the three groups among the baselines evaluated on the same events. A second gate against the seen processes isolates high-mass dilepton signals into label-free clusters, and long-lived signals too when displaced-track information is available. With 7512 parameters and an FPGA latency of 147 ns, the encoder uses one event representation, learned once, to derive several selections, opening the door to a foundation model for the LHC's hardware-based triggers.

### [arXiv:2611.29331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29331) — Development of a Hybrid Scintillator Strip-Drift Tube Muon Detector for the FCC-ee (Jiajin Ge (on behalf of the University of Michigan FCC-ee R&D team))

_Watchlist hit (collider) — original abstract; no AI summary._

The Future Circular Collider (FCC-ee) is a proposed high-luminosity $e^+e^-$ collider for precision measurements at the electroweak, Higgs, and top-quark energy scales. A high-performance, cost-effective muon system is essential for muon identification, triggering, momentum measurement, and long-lived particle detection. We present the R&D of a hybrid muon detector concept combining fast extruded plastic scintillator strips with precision drift tubes. The scintillator strips were produced at Fermilab, while detector prototypes with SiPM readout and pressurized drift tubes were designed, constructed, and integrated at the University of Michigan. The hybrid detector was tested at the CERN test beam facility in 2025 and 2026. The scintillator strips achieved a position resolution of 1.6 mm and a time resolution of approximately 350 ps, while the drift tubes provided a single-hit spatial resolution better than 100 $\mu$m. These results demonstrate the feasibility of a high-performance hybrid muon spectrometer for the FCC-ee.

### [arXiv:2611.30394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30394) — FGPA-based advanced muon trigger algorithms for long-lived particle detection at the High-Luminosity LHC (Daniel Estrada (for the CMS Collaboration) et al.)

_Watchlist hit (LHC) — original abstract; no AI summary._

The CMS Level-1 trigger must reconstruct muons in real time under stringent latency and hardware resource constraints. Conventional algorithms lose efficiency for high-energy muons undergoing radiative energy losses and can be suboptimal for displaced trajectories, limiting sensitivity to exotic signatures such as long-lived particles (LLPs). The Phase-2 upgrade introduces advanced FPGAs that enable more sophisticated reconstruction algorithms. This work presents two complementary developments addressing these challenges. In the barrel region, a dedicated algorithm detects showers from Drift Tube hit patterns at the first processing stage. At the subsequent filtering stage, shower primitives are correlated with conventional trigger primitives, currently targeting radiatively showering high-momentum muons, with LLP signatures as a broader objective. Simulation studies yield approximately $80\%$ shower-classification accuracy and an estimated matching efficiency above $80\%$ for radiating muons. Firmware prototypes demonstrate a shower-detection latency of approximately $20\,\mathrm{ns}$ and sub-bunch-crossing latency for preliminary matching logic. In parallel, a hardware-oriented optimization methodology is investigated for future graph neural network based displaced-muon reconstruction in the barrel--endcap overlap region. Using a GraphSAGE proxy on a benchmark dataset, a workflow combining integer quantization, power-of-two scaling, and data-driven bit-width optimization achieves $(75.0 \pm 1.1)\%$ accuracy and a deterministic latency of $19$ cycles ($52.8\,\mathrm{ns}$ at $360\,\mathrm{MHz}$), with only $20\%$ digital signal processor utilization. These results establish a basis for implementation studies using CMS muon data.
