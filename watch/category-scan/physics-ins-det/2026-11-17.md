### [arXiv:2611.21011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21011) — Particle Identification Studies using a Highly Granular Crystal Electromagnetic Calorimeter (Jack Rolph et al.)

_Watchlist hit (collider) — original abstract; no AI summary._

A proposal for the Circular Electron Positron Collider electromagnetic calorimeter is a highly granular scintillating crystal design that uses silicon photomultipliers to measure physics signals from photons. The potential to develop particle identification methods using deep and traditional machine learning is explored. A contrastive machine learning technique was found to enable the separation of showering and 'punch-through' pions without explicit labels. A supervised particle identification method was then trained using the results, using both a graph neural network and a boosted decision tree to distinguish electron, showering hadron, and minimum-ionizing-particle-like events. The classifier achieved an area-under-the-receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.996 for both cases. A Shapley additive explanations analysis showed the boosted decision tree classifier matched expectations for each event type. The particle identification capability is then demonstrated on experimental data taken from the CERN Proton Synchrotron T9 beamline.

### [arXiv:2611.21101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21101) — Evaluation of Performance of the p-stop fabrication process variations in ATLAS18 strip sensors, Pre- and Post-Irradiation (J. Kozakova et al.)

_Watchlist hit (LHC, Large Hadron Collider, collider) — original abstract; no AI summary._

The High-Luminosity upgrade of the Large Hadron Collider (HL-LHC) requires the replacement of the ATLAS Inner Detector with a new all-silicon Inner Tracker (ITk). Radiation-hard n-in-p micro-strip sensors were developed for use in the ITk. During quality control (QC) and quality assurance (QA) procedures performed on ATLAS18 production sensors and test chips, ATLAS ITk strip sensor institutes identified instances of low p-stop density in certain production batches. To investigate the origin of these issues and to try some technological improvements, two dedicated sample batches with four variations of the p-stop fabrication process were produced. These process types (1-4) were systematically evaluated to determine their effectiveness and uniformity. In total, 56 full-size sensors, along with numerous miniature sensors and test chips, were tested. A number of samples were used in the irradiation campaign reaching the maximum expected levels for the HL-LHC operation after 10 years - with neutrons up to 1.6E15 neq/cm2, and with gamma rays from a 60Co source up to 66 Mrad. The goal of this evaluation program is to assess the impact of of p-stop fabrication process variations on interstrip isolation performance and to provide feedback for future sensor development. This paper describes the applied test procedures and presents the corresponding results, which demonstrate good sensor performance for all investigated p-stop types. The findings confirm the reliability of the evaluated designs and identify specific advantages that can inform further optimization of strip sensor fabrication.

### [arXiv:2611.21594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21594) — Surrogate-Assisted Compact-Model Extraction for SKY130 CMOS at 77 K (Aaron Wang et al.)

_Watchlist hit (Nhan Tran) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Cryogenic circuit design requires compact transistor models that reproduce measured low-temperature behavior and run in standard circuit simulators. We fit BSIM4 parameters to measured I-V curves of SkyWater 130 nm (SKY130) transistors at 77 K using a neural surrogate of NGSpice. The surrogate maps compact-model parameters and device geometry to the complete set of output and transfer characteristics. Extraction is a bounded least-squares search: a bank of NGSpice simulations supplies initial candidates, finite differences of the surrogate supply the local Jacobian, and NGSpice re-simulates the resulting cards before selection. On 18 measured geometries, we compare a search over seven card parameters with a search over 43 parameters selected by NGSpice sensitivity. Relative to NGSpice re-simulations of the previously released cards, the two searches reduce the in-sample mean relative root-mean-square error (RRMS) by 17% and 26%, respectively. The 43-parameter search has the lower overall error, but the seven-parameter search has the lower mean on high-bias curves, and some individual curves have higher error than with the released cards under both searches.
