### [arXiv:2611.31647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31647) — MSW: a Multi-Scale Sliced Wasserstein Distance Between Image Distributions (Youssef Haouchat et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: wasserstein) — original abstract; no AI summary._

We propose the multi-scale sliced Wasserstein (MSW) distance between image distributions, which operates in pixel space and requires no trained network. MSW convolves images with a fixed bank of discrete cosine filters at several scales, pools the responses over all positions, and compares the resulting one-dimensional distributions using the Wasserstein distance. We pair MSW with an exact two-sample test that gives calibrated p-values. Most metrics compare two image sets in the latent space of a pretrained network. We show that these metrics are insensitive to simple perturbations of the images, because the latent space is nearly invariant to them. In contrast, MSW detects smaller perturbations than latent-space metrics and needs far fewer images to do so. Although it uses no learned features, it still detects semantic changes of the image distribution. Finally, MSW matches or outperforms existing Wasserstein-based metrics in our experiments.

### [arXiv:2611.32220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32220) — FaceNet versus VGG Features in Wasserstein Distortion for Face Image Compression (Xinyu Wang et al.)

_Watchlist hit (title: wasserstein) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Wasserstein distortion (WD) compares local statistics of deep features and has been used to optimize learned image codecs for perceptual quality, but existing WD objectives rely on features from networks trained for generic image classification, such as VGG. We ask whether features from a face recognition network better reflect the perceived quality of compressed faces. In the Cool-Chic codec, we replace the VGG features in a WD-based objective with intermediate feature maps of FaceNet, and compare the two objectives on 20 LFW face images at a matched average bitrate of about 0.15 bits per pixel. In a preliminary pairwise human study, FaceNet-based reconstructions were preferred in 72.5% of judgments and by a majority of participants on 16 of the 20 images. These results suggest that the choice of feature network inside WD affects the perceived quality of face images, and that face recognition features are a promising direction for face-aware perceptual compression.
