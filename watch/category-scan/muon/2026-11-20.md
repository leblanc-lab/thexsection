## Muon collider

### [arXiv:2611.26518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26518) — Hydrodynamic modeling of liquid metal field emitter deformation under combined RF and DC magnetic fields (Yuan-Zhi Lim et al.)

_Watchlist hit (muon collider) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Radio-frequency (RF) breakdowns, also known as vacuum (or electrical) arcs and breakdowns, or electrical discharges, have been studied for over decades, but the mechanisms of the RF breakdown are still questionable. This phenomenon reduces the stability of the beam, damages the RF cavities, and thus limits the achievable accelerating field in the particle accelerator. In the case of Muon Collider, the RF cavities have to withstand an electric field of the order of multi tens of MV/m and a DC magnetic field of a few tens of teslas, with a minimum rate of RF breakdown. Different models have been proposed, and some important factors that could lead to the RF breakdown have been identified, such as the field emission current, irregularity of the surface , presence of hot particles on the surface and the role of the DC magnetic field. In this paper, we give insights to the mechanism of RF breakdowns using a hydrodynamic and a magnetohydrodynamic approach, focusing on understanding the forces that deform the liquid copper emitter. We found that if the local RF electric field is sufficiently high, it could provoke the emission of hot copper droplets into the cavity. Our simulations use the computational fluid dynamics module, the magnetic and electric field module, and the level set method in the COMSOL software to simulate the behavior of a copper liquid emitter with the Navier-Stokes equation under the influence of the pressure radiation, and the surface tension force.
