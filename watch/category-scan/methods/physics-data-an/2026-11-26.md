## Data & computational methods

### [arXiv:2611.33221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33221) — Continuous Universal Fake Factors for jet-to-tau background estimation (Lei Guo et al.)

_Watchlist hit (hadronic tau) — original abstract; no AI summary._

We study Continuous Universal Fake Factors (CUFF), a method for estimating backgrounds from jets misidentified as hadronic tau candidates. CUFF combines four reference-region fake factors for each target tau candidate failing Loose identification. Their weights depend on candidate features and target fail-Loose distributions after subtracting simulated contributions from genuine taus and electrons or muons misidentified as tau candidates. We use ATLAS 2015-2016 Open Data with tau candidates matched to jets with $20<p_T^{\rm jet}\leq60$ GeV. In eight mixtures of reference samples with known proportions, predicted total yields for the Medium identification selection differ from data after subtraction by $+0.7\%$ to $+4.3\%$, supporting the combination. In two validation regions, the corresponding differences are $-21.85\%$ and $-45.84\%$, with statistical uncertainties, including training fluctuations, of $1.58\%$ and $6.03\%$ relative to the data references. These discrepancies lie within broad empirical uncertainty ranges from simulation comparisons between regions, widened by limited simulation statistics. For Loose identification, CUFF and the Universal Fake Factor (UFF) method adapted to public data give similar inclusive yields in one validation region, while CUFF agrees more closely with the data reference in the other. CUFF also describes the shapes of several kinematic distributions more accurately than adapted UFF, including that of an observable not used as a model input. Across nine identification thresholds, relative yield differences vary gradually from $-26.4\%$ to $-19.7\%$ and $-50.2\%$ to $-40.6\%$ in the two regions, respectively, demonstrating consistent application of CUFF at continuously variable tau-identification thresholds without retraining.

### [arXiv:2611.34888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34888) — The Look-Elsewhere Effect in Searches for New Physics: from Classical Methods to Machine Learning (Gaia Grosso et al.)

_Watchlist hit (colliders) — original abstract; no AI summary._

The look-elsewhere effect (LEE) arises when the location of an effect in parameter space is not specified in advance: scanning many locations inflates the significance of the largest excess, and the local $p$-value must be corrected into a global one. A well-established framework, rooted in the work of Davies and operationalised for particle physics by Gross and Vitells, provides this correction for scans over continuous parameters. Some aspects, however, remain unsettled. The definition of "elsewhere" is ambiguous and ranges from a single distribution to an entire experimental programme. Moreover, the analytical shortcuts of the classical framework do not carry over to searches based on machine learning (ML), which are affected by an implicit post-selection bias that depends on the model's resolution and expressiveness. This review covers the LEE in searches for new physics at colliders, from its statistical foundations to these modern challenges. We discuss the corrections for continuous scans and for multiple discrete tests, illustrated with simple numerical examples, and the interaction between ML and the LEE, which we connect to the broader literature on selective inference. We highlight open problems, including the computational cost of empirical calibration for complex models and the development of analytical shortcuts for ML test statistics, and offer recommendations for reporting discovery significance in both classical and ML-based searches in particle physics.
