### [arXiv:2611.31403](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31403) — OmniX: A first jet and event model (Anna Hallin et al.)

_Watchlist hit (Gregor Kasieczka) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Foundation models are a new approach to building machine learning algorithms for particle physics. Their central idea is that a single model can be adapted to a variety of downstream tasks, amortizing training costs. So far, most foundation model prototypes have operated on the constituents of a single jet. This limits their usefulness for physics problems at the scale of full events, such as distinguishing different processes, and restricts their performance, as global correla- tions are invisible to the model. Here, we introduce OmniX, an architecture that operates on both single jets and whole events within a single model. Using the jet-level JetClass and Top-tagging datasets and the event-level Collide-2V dataset, we demonstrate that OmniX trained jointly on single jets and full events matches or exceeds otherwise equivalent jet-only and event-only models in generation and classification.

### [arXiv:2611.32261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32261) — A quantum-inspired spiking variational autoencoder for robust anomaly detection at the LHC (Dean Brand et al.)

_Watchlist hit (LHC, Large Hadron Collider, collider) — original abstract; no AI summary._

Model-agnostic searches for new physics at the Large Hadron Collider utilise unsupervised anomaly detection, where quantum and neuromorphic detectors represent potential approaches. We develop the first fully spiking variational autoencoder, a quantum-inspired spiking variational autoencoder (QSVAE), built on the quantum-inspired leaky integrate-and-fire (QLIF) neuron. In this work we recast the neuron from a measurement-based circuit to a differentiable computational unit. This neuron is benchmarked against other classical, spiking, and quantum models on an LHC di-jet latent space. We show that under noise perturbations the QLIF network retains $0.95$ of its clean accuracy, while accuracy retention for twelve classical models falls to $0.74$-$0.85$. The QLIF anomaly scores respond $3.5$-fold less to the noise perturbation than any of the control model's scores. We compare against a periodic classical control that reproduces the QLIF effect, isolating it to the $\sin^2$ membrane map. This membrane map is a native regulariser that decouples robustness from model capacity supplied by the qubit state space. We demonstrate this through several benchmarks and ablation studies to characterise the performance landscape of the quantum-neuromorphic model space.
