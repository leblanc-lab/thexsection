## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2611.15274](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15274) — Benchmarking Machine-Learning Top Taggers from JetClass to a Full-Simulation $Z'\to t\bar{t}$ Search (Manisha Kumari et al.)

Seven taggers (XGBoost BDT on 19 R=0.2 subjet observables, a 40×40 CNN, ResNeXt-50, ParticleNet, ParT, UParT, GloParT) are trained on JetClass (TTBar vs ZJetsToNuNu, 500–1000 GeV). They are then applied without retraining to CMS Open Data UL16 Geant4 samples: $Z'$ at 1.2–2.5 TeV, HT-binned QCD and hadronic $t\bar t$. Jets are clustered from raw PF candidates with no CHS or PUPPI, at $\langle n_\mathrm{PV}\rangle=17.9$.

- **AUC drop:** mean AUC falls by 0.065–0.085 for every tagger (GloParT 0.928, ParT 0.927, BDT 0.887). UParT, best on JetClass at 0.9959, falls behind ParT and GloParT in the search.
- **Fully merged jets:** the fully merged fraction rises from 44% to 74% with $m_{Z'}$. Restricted to fully merged tops, every tagger degrades with mass. Transformers lose 0.01–0.02 in AUC, the others 0.03–0.05.
- **Common pT bin (750–850 GeV), 1% background efficiency:** ParT keeps 37–47% of signal versus 12–21% for the CNN.
- **Pileup:** after per-$n_\mathrm{PV}$ pT reweighting, pileup effects are minor for all taggers.
- **Significance (Asimov, jet-level):** the transformer/BDT ratio grows from 1.3 at 90% rejection to 3.3 at 99.9%. The jet-level 5σ reach is 1800 GeV for transformers, 1600 GeV for ParticleNet and the image taggers, and 1400 GeV for the BDT.
- **Latency per jet:** 0.15 ms for transformers versus 0.014 ms for the CNN. ResNeXt gives no gain over the CNN at 21× the cost.

The useful message for the field is that in-window benchmark AUC understates both the high-rejection gaps between taggers and the out-of-range extrapolation behaviour.

Caveats:
- The abstract's "similar AUC, 3× significance" is really transformer versus BDT, which differ by about 0.04 AUC in-search.
- ParticleNet receives no PID or displacement inputs, so the gap between ParticleNet and the transformers is confounded with input features.
- The BDT lacks jet mass and $\tau_{32}$.
- The 50-constituent truncation differs from training for the BDT and image taggers.
- Training and application backgrounds differ (Z+jets vs QCD multijet).
- Cross sections are LO, there are no systematics, and there is no mistag calibration. The last matters because the claimed gains sit at 99.9% rejection, exactly where ATLAS's accuracy-vs-precision study found the largest uncertainties.
- The ranking within each tagger group is not stable across seeds.

### [arXiv:2611.16454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16454) — Performance of the ATLAS tau trigger in Run 3 and future prospects (ATLAS Collaboration)

*Abstract only.* This paper is tangential to substructure: it covers hadronic-τ trigger performance, not boosted-jet tagging.

- **Plateau efficiency:** 90–98% for single-τ triggers at $\sqrt{s}=13.6$ TeV.
- **Scale factors:** data/MC factors from 2022–2023 τ-enriched data are 0.95–1.05, with 2–3% uncertainties over a wide $p_T$ range.
- **Upgrades:** improved trigger-level τ identification is evaluated on 2026 data in test chains. The abstract does not say whether these use constituent-level ML.

The relevance here is mainly as a calibration reference point for narrow-jet ID at trigger level.
