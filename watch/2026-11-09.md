## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2611.08579](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08579) — Observing Transverse Momentum Broadening from TEECs in Heavy Ion Collisions (Jo\~ao Barata et al.)

**Setup.** The authors compute the back-to-back TEEC (τ→0) for a colour-singlet qq̄ pair moving through a static, homogeneous medium. They use e⁺e⁻→γ*→qq̄ at c=0 as a proxy, which deliberately removes initial-state and cold-nuclear effects. The vacuum part is NLL′ TMD resummation (b* prescription, Fit-1 non-perturbative factor).

**Medium modifications.** These enter only through a parent-resolved TMDFF. The jet function becomes J = D_F[b; L⁺] J^vac + J^(1),med.rad.
- **Broadening:** D_F is the harmonic-oscillator dipole.
- **Radiative term:** the BDMPS–Z q→qg correction, computed at large N_c, from the in–in and in–out vertex-time regions. The out–out region reduces to broadening followed by vacuum splitting.

**Technical results.**
- The radiative term obeys an energy sum rule, J^(1)(0) = 0.
- Energy weighting softens the (1−z)^−3/2 BDMPS endpoint to an integrable (1−z)^−1/2, so no soft divergence enters the jet function.
- It carries no scale dependence at O(α_s), so it is counted as an NLL′ constant.
- The large-|p| tail combines as [D_F³ − D_F] × vacuum splitting. This tail is only conditionally convergent, so they use an η_UV regulator with the time integrals done first.
- Medium response is added as a mean energy flow.

**Headline (abstract-level).** The extracted text cuts off in Sec. IV, so I have no numbers or (q̂, L) values.
- Classical broadening only lowers the peak.
- Radiative corrections suppress the peak further and populate larger δφ.
- Medium response is flat across the back-to-back window, negligible at the peak and growing away from it.

**Why it matters.** Earlier back-to-back heavy-ion decorrelation studies kept only broadening. This paper argues that extracting q̂ from the peak requires the radiative piece.

**Caveats.**
- They drop quark recoil against soft medium-induced gluons. That term is rapidity-divergent, needs a medium-modified soft function, and contains the known radiative p_T-broadening double logs.
- The two legs are treated as propagating independently.
- Large-N_c and harmonic-oscillator approximations throughout.
- No realistic AA geometry.

### [arXiv:2611.08656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08656) — Higher Order Component Attribution via Learned Surrogates in High Energy Physics (Lauri Laatu et al.)

**Method.** This is a workshop-length interpretability study.
- For each jet (≤32 constituents), they fit a B-spline KAN surrogate (n→16→8→1, ≤5832 parameters) to the target model's logit shifts under 50k local Gaussian perturbations.
- The surrogate's mixed partials (2nd- and 3rd-order tensors) serve as pair and triplet interaction scores.
- These are Spearman-correlated with the top-256 pair (triplet) contributions to e₂^(1) (e₃^(1)).
- Targets are ParT and vanilla MHA trained on 10-class JetClass. Best 8-block accuracies are 82.8% and 81.9%.

**Results.**
- **Pairs:** ParT gives R^(2) ≈ 0.3 on 2-prong jets; MHA gives ≈ 0.
- **Triplets:** correlations are weaker, but ParT's error bars exclude zero.
- **Depth:** adding blocks does not improve alignment for ParT and degrades it for MHA. The authors read this as motivation for explicitly higher-order (e.g. hypergraph) architectures.

**Caveats.**
- **Surrogate fidelity confounds the comparison.** For ParT the KAN captures R² ≈ 0.99, but for 8-block MHA it is ~0.85 median and ~0.75 mean. MHA's low alignment at depth may partly reflect a worse surrogate.
- **Second-order alignment is partly built in.** ParT's pair features include ΔR_ij, which the authors acknowledge.
- **Small and unexplained pieces.** Only 50 jets per class per network. The Z-jet behaviour is "anomalous" and not explained. Per-jet spreads are large.

**Fit with the TEEC paper.** The only overlap is the energy-correlator language. This is a substructure-observable-as-probe for ML, not substructure physics per se.

## Muon collider

### [arXiv:2611.09775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09775) — Uncovering TeV-scale dark matter with Z-fusion at a muon collider (Anindya Datta et al.)

**Analysis.** They study μ⁺μ⁻ → μ⁺μ⁻χχ via neutral VBF at √s = 10 TeV with 10 ab⁻¹, tagging both forward muons out to |η| < 7 (MUSIC-style forward coverage).
- Simulation: MG5 + Delphes muon-collider card with 1% muon p_T resolution.
- Only background considered: μμνν, 1.44 pb after preselection.
- Key discriminant: η_invisible, reconstructed from the dimuon system using the known initial state. In the EFT cutflow, |η_inv| < 0.5 alone cuts background from 4.1×10⁶ to 5760.

**Results.**

| Scenario | Benchmark | Signal / background events | Significance (S/√B) |
|---|---|---|---|
| EFT: c₁BBχ² + c₂WWχ², Λ = 12 TeV | c₁ = c₂ = 2, m_χ = 1 TeV | 511 / 5760 | 6.7σ |
| Inert doublet model | m_H = 1 TeV, m_A = m_H± = 1.2 TeV, λ₃₄₅ = 10⁻³ | 507 / 8640 | 5.5σ |

In the IDM, H is relic-underabundant. Destructive H–A interference collapses the rate for small Δm, which is visible in the fixed Δm = 100 GeV curve.

**Relevance.** This is squarely on-topic. It adds a concrete Z-fusion invisible benchmark to the forward-muon-detector physics case, alongside the Ruhdorfer–Salvioni–Wulzer studies.

**Caveats.**
- **No systematics.** S/B is only ~9% (EFT) and ~6% (IDM).
- **Forward resolution assumed.** A flat 1% p_T resolution is applied up to |η| = 7.
- **Not simulated:**
  - beam-induced background;
  - beam energy spread (argued to be harmless, but η_inv depends directly on the fixed-√s assumption);
  - any background beyond μμνν.
- **EFT validity is marginal.** Λ = 12 TeV with c = 2 at √s = 10 TeV.
- **Internal inconsistency.** Table 4 lists Δm = 230 GeV at m_H = 1 TeV, while the stated benchmark has Δm = 200 GeV.
