## Optimal transport

### [arXiv:2609.28661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28661) — Cross-geometry transfer and model collapse in point cloud calorimeter shower generation (Thorsten Buss et al.)

**Relevance to this topic is weak.** Optimal transport enters only as the evaluation metric: the Wasserstein-1 distance to Geant4 on six shower observables, combined by geometric mean. There is no OT-based training, coupling, or methodology.

The paper is a proceedings summary of JINST 21 (2026) P07037. The authors pre-train CaloClouds II on 524k ILD ECAL photon showers (10–90 GeV) and adapt it to CaloChallenge DS3. The target differs in several ways at once:
- electrons instead of photons;
- a cylindrical rather than planar geometry, with 45 instead of 30 layers;
- a log-uniform 1 GeV–1 TeV energy range;
- point clouds more than three times larger at high energy.

At 10² target showers, full fine-tuning reaches a geometric-mean W₁ of 0.064±0.006, against 0.132±0.018 from scratch (about 51% lower). The advantage fades with more data: at 10⁵ it is 4.7 vs 4.5, i.e. no gain.

Among parameter-efficient methods:
- **BitFit:** updates 17% of the diffusion parameters and lands within 5% of full fine-tuning (mean 6.2 vs 5.9 ×10⁻²).
- **LoRA at rank 106:** underperforms (8.4). The authors attribute this to high-rank weight updates, with some layers needing rank above 200.

A self-consuming retraining study shows progressive collapse in both ShowerFlow and the diffusion model. It is reported only qualitatively via figures.

Caveats:
- There is no comparison to multi-detector pre-training such as CaloDiT-2.
- A residual per-observable mismatch at 10⁴ showers is hidden in the geometric mean.
- The claim of adaptation to arbitrary geometry is explicitly not demonstrated here.

## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2609.29025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29025) — Impact of soft QCD effects over intrajet azimuthal anisotropies (Jesus Alberto V. Corral et al.)

This is a generator-level reinterpretation of the CMS intrajet v₂ measurement ([arXiv:2312.17103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17103)), using PYTHIA 8.309 with the Monash, CP5 and QCD-scheme CR tunes. The setup is anti-kT R=0.8 jets with pT>550 GeV and |η|<1.6, particles with 0.3<j_T<3 GeV, and a long-range cut |Δη*|>2.

Tune spectra: CP5 gives a softer N_ch spectrum (inclusive ⟨N_ch⟩ 27.3, vs 31.2 for Monash and 30.0 for QCD-CR), with fewer jets at high N_ch.

v₂ agreement with data (χ²/ndof):

| Model | N_ch < 80 | N_ch ≥ 80 |
|---|---|---|
| QCD-scheme CR | 0.32 | 10.21 |
| Sherpa (from CMS) | 1.02 | 21.93 |
| Monash | 2.28 | 10.20 |
| CP5 | 4.69 | 23.55 |

The high-N_ch excess persists for all tunes. The authors frame QCD-CR and Monash as "consistent at 2.3σ" in the top bin. That framing sits awkwardly with χ²/ndof ≈ 10 over those two points, and should not be read as agreement.

The new ingredient is rotating into the WTA-axis frame:
- WTA lowers v₂ at the lowest N_ch for all tunes.
- At high N_ch, Monash and CP5 shift away from their E-scheme values, while QCD-CR is the most frame-stable.
- The authors tie the Monash shift to ⟨η*⟩ differences (2.08 for E-scheme vs 2.12 for WTA, at N_ch>80).

This argues that part of the high-N_ch "signal" is sensitive to soft-recoil effects on the jet axis, and it motivates a WTA-frame measurement.

Caveats:
- No detector simulation.
- The WTA predictions have no data to compare against.
- The fit uses only three Fourier terms.
- Differences between tunes are attributed to colour reconnection, but CP5 also differs in its ISR, FSR, MPI and α_s settings.

### [arXiv:2609.29620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29620) — Event-Level Classification of Boosted Multi-Tau Signatures in scNMSSM Di-Higgs Production (Marwa Telba)

The signal is h₁h₁→4a₁→8τ with m_a₁=8.2 GeV (γ≈7.5, ΔR_ττ≈0.27), with Z(→ττ)+jet at pT^ττ>100 GeV as the main background. It is simulated with MG5+Pythia8+Delphes, using R=0.8 fat jets with the threshold lowered from 200 to 30 GeV. Without that change only 18% of signal events contain any fat jet.

**Negative result for jet substructure.** Standard two-prong discriminants fail against a background that is itself a ditau jet:
- The separation power is S=0.016 for m_J and 0.09 for τ₂₁.
- Neutrino losses (Z) and soft contamination (a₁) push both jet-mass distributions to about 18 GeV, despite parents at 8.2 and 91 GeV.
- The best single variables are z_sub (S=0.36) and τ₁ (0.33). Normalising into τ₂₁ throws away information.
- Energy correlation functions were not available from Delphes.

**Performance** (AUC):

| Configuration | AUC | ε_S at ε_B=10% |
|---|---|---|
| Single-jet BDT vs Z | 0.759 | — |
| Event-level (32 inputs) vs Z | 0.941 | 82% |
| Event-level vs tt̄→bb̄ττ | 0.855 | — |
| Cross-section-weighted combination | 0.908 | 71% |

- The event-level gain is driven by fat-jet multiplicity and the soft-drop masses of the second and third jets.
- tt̄ mimics the multiplicity and subleading masses; discrimination against it relies on N_τ and E_T^miss instead.
- tt̄ makes up 66% of the surviving background at ε_B=1%, despite being under a quarter of the combined background cross section.
