## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2609.36523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36523) — Non-global logarithms and fiducial transverse-momentum-dependent observables in deep-inelastic scattering (Shuo Lin et al.)

Lin and Zhou replace the lepton–jet imbalance with a jet-free fiducial imbalance, **q**_T = **l**′_T + Σ_{h∈|η|<Δη/2} **p**_hT. By momentum conservation this equals minus the total p_T outside the window, so the rapidity window plays the role of the jet boundary. At one loop, the jet-radius coefficients c₀(R), c₁(R) are replaced by analytic B₀(Δη) = −Δη − 2ln(1−e^{−Δη}) and B₁(Δη) = 4 atanh(e^{−Δη/2}) − 2e^{−Δη/2}. For reference, inclusive-k_T at R=1 gives c₀ = −0.253 and c₁ = 0.782.

The non-global treatment has three parts:
- They derive closed-form two-loop angular coefficients I₀ and I₁ (dilogarithm expressions).
- The azimuthal-average NGL U₀ is resummed at LL, large-N_c, with a BMS dipole-shower Monte Carlo using a 1 − J₀(bk_T) weight.
- The cos φ harmonic NGL is kept only at O(α_s²). It is single-log, U₁⁽²⁾ ~ ᾱ_s² I₁ ln(P_T/μ_b), whereas U₀⁽²⁾ is double-log.

Headline numbers:
- ⟨cos φ⟩ at q_T = 1 GeV roughly halves from Δη = 1 to Δη = 2. For HERA it goes 0.280 → 0.134 → 0.074 for Δη = 1, 2, 3.
- At the EIC, |A_UT| and |A_LT| grow with Δη and peak near q_T ~ 1–1.5 GeV.
- A_LT at Δη = 1 and q_T = 1 GeV is ≈0.25 at EicC versus ≈0.02 at EIC.

For this topic, the paper gives an algorithm-independent recoil-control knob with analytic boundary dependence. It is a direct comparator to the H1 ML-unfolded lepton-jet measurements, and scanning Δη within one dataset tests the global and non-global contributions together.

Caveats:
- NGLs are LL and large-N_c only, and the first-harmonic NGL is not resummed.
- The S_NP inputs are borrowed from Drell–Yan fits (halved).
- Twist-3 evolution of T_F and g̃ is approximated by unpolarized DGLAP.
- The calculation is at parton level, with no hadronization or detector acceptance effects.

### [arXiv:2609.36933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36933) — Flavor-resolved four-fermion probes at the FCC-ee Z pole (Syuhei Iguro)

This paper is only tangential to jets. Jet flavor tagging is an enabling assumption, but the analysis is parton-level and has no jet definition; the author notes the ΔR_min = 0.2 cut must eventually become an IR-safe jet definition.

The physics: using Z → QQ̄ℓℓ (Q = b, c; ℓ = μ, τ) at 200 ab⁻¹, the author sets tree-level, interference-only 95% CL reaches on vector contact operators of (2.5, 2.7, 3.5, 3.6) TeV for bμ, bτ, cμ, cτ. These assume unit efficiency, and the reach scales as ε_rec^{1/4}. The main point is flavor resolution. The projected EWPO likelihood in the (C^{bτ}, C^{cτ}) plane has correlation ρ = −0.9999 and effectively constrains only C^{bτ} + 0.058 C^{cτ}. The four-body channels constrain the flat direction, giving combined reaches of Λ^{bτ} > 11.8 TeV and Λ^{cτ} > 3.2 TeV.

What matters for jets:
- The optimal hardness cut is only 3–5 GeV, so this needs b/c tagging on soft jets in busy four-body topologies. The author explicitly flags this as unvalidated.
- b↔c migration cannot be absorbed into a common efficiency factor.

## Muon collider

### [arXiv:2609.37117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37117) — Input to the SCAC Facilities Subcommittee from the U.S. Muon Collider Collaboration (S. Jindariani (on behalf of the US Muon Collider Collaboration) et al.)

This is a policy and strategy input to DOE's SCAC with no new technical results. It asks SCAC to recognize muon-collider R&D as a gap in the 2026–2036 facilities portfolio and proposes a staged program:
1. Distributed micro-demonstrators covering HTS solenoids, RF in strong magnetic fields, high-power targets, fast-ramping magnets and power systems, detector timing, and integrated cooling-cell prototypes.
2. Parallel design of a macro-demonstrator. This is now framed as a higher-intensity front-end facility (proton driver, target and capture, production and cooling) with its own neutrino, dark-sector, flavor and nuclear physics program, rather than the earlier cooling-cell-only concept.

Other points relevant to tracking the program:
- Cooling is restated as the dominant luminosity risk. MICE and g−2 demonstrated the principle, but a practical six-dimensional channel (~10⁶ phase-space reduction) with adequate transmission has not been shown.
- The document states there is no dedicated DOE funding; current support comes from universities and private foundations (Simons, RCSA, Sloan).
- It cites the 2026 ESPP explicit call for cooperation with the U.S. initiative.
- It names AI4MuC target areas: cooling-channel optimization and beam-induced-background simulation.

There are no cost or schedule figures; the collaboration says the demonstrator is not yet mature enough for them.
