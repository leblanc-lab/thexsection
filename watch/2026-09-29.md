## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2609.31862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31862) — Prompting Particle Physics: Tokenized Multi-modal Foundation Models for Combinatorially Many Tasks (Nilotpal Kakati et al.)

The authors tokenize seven per-jet modalities in COCOA single-jet events (88.9M train, median truth-jet p_T 46 GeV). Content features use residual VQ-VAEs and positions use 1024-bin (η, cos φ, sin φ); calorimeter cells enter as 8×8 patch images. The modalities are tracks, topoclusters, cells, HGPflow particles, truth particles, truth jet and per-cell charged fraction. One model is trained over all 1302 input→output subsets with ≤3 modalities on each side. There are two decoders: nanoHEP (GPT-2-style, autoregressive) and HEP4M (4M-style, parallel with cardinality heads).

The jet-relevant benchmark is particle flow (TK+CL→TP), scored against truth particles:

| Model | Jet p_T IQR | Median response | C2ST AUC |
|---|---|---|---|
| HGPflow | 0.0754 | 0.986 | 0.902 |
| nanoHEP-pflow | 0.0733 | 0.978 | 0.691 |
| HEP4M-pflow | 0.0836 | 0.994 | 0.940 |

The C2ST is an event-conditioned set-transformer two-sample test. Its point is that HGPflow matches on aggregate jet metrics but its constituent cloud is easily separable from truth, which argues for particle-cloud-level validation beyond jet response.

- **Extra inputs help.** Adding cells (TK+CL+CE) gives IQR 0.0704 and AUC 0.650.
- **Unseen four-input combination.** TK+CL+RP+CE, never presented in training, reaches 0.0688.

Caveats:
- **Not substructure.** The paper is about reconstructing and simulating jet constituents; no substructure observables are shown.
- **Multi-task gain is small.** On the benchmark itself, nanoHEP-multi (0.0781) is slightly worse than nanoHEP-pflow; at matched steps, from-scratch training gives 0.0758.
- **Truth-jet-input row is not a fair comparison.** The 0.0305 IQR uses the truth jet as an input.
- **Refinement fails.** RP→TP does not improve on the HGPflow input.
- **Cell generation fails.** Generated cells are fully separable from truth (AUC 0.998), and the cell tokenizer round trip alone is separable at 0.96.
- **HEP4M artefacts.** It needs cos/sin renormalisation and under-produces high topocluster multiplicities.
- **Simplified setup.** The detector is simplified, tracks are parameterised fast simulation, and pileup is not discussed.

### [arXiv:2609.32054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32054) — The era of charmed resonance searches at the LHC (Daniel Whiteson)

This is a flavour-tagging projection. The author starts from the ATLAS 139 fb⁻¹ untagged dijet spectrum and builds c-tagged background spectra using:
- Pythia dijet flavour-pair fractions binned in (p_T, p_T), validated against ATLAS 7 TeV flavour-fraction data with a small +0.0073 bℓ correction.
- GN2 c-tag efficiencies, interpolated log-linearly in p_T between the two published p_T samples.
- A multi-hadron efficiency enhancement with α = 1.96, fitted to the observed b-tagged spectra.

Exclusive 1-tag and 2-tag categories are fitted jointly with four-parameter dijet functions. The limit-setting machinery reproduces ATLAS limits to within 10%.

Results:
- **Z′→cc̄.** At ε_c = 0.6, the tagged search beats the inclusive one (gain G > 1) over the whole 2–4 TeV range, with roughly ±10% band dominated by the tagging model.
- **T→cg.** The gain appears only at lower masses.
- **Doubling the inclusive sensitivity** needs ε_ℓ = 0.12 at ε_c = 0.6 (current 0.15, a factor 1.3). At ε_c = 0.3 it needs 0.016 (current 0.083, a factor 5).
- **Perfect-tagger ceilings** are G ≈ 8 (Z′) and G ≈ 2.2 (T).

The conclusion for tagger development is to optimise the loose working points.

Two points are of interest for jet physics:
- **Gluon splitting in the background.** The multi-hadron fraction from g→cc̄ rises with p_T and is larger for charm than for bottom, and FSR scale variation moves the charm fraction f_c by +22/−14% at about 1.5 TeV.
- **IRC-safe flavour.** Under IRC-safe interleaved flavour neutralisation (IFN), about 50% of cone-labelled charm jets become light at low mass. At 4–5.4 TeV only 17% stay charm. This reclassifies which jets count as charm but does not change the predicted tagged spectrum.

Caveats: there are no published p_T-differential c-tag efficiencies, so these are extrapolated. The 13 TeV flavour fractions are extrapolated from simulation (7 TeV data only reach p_T < 500 GeV). The analysis uses binary thresholds only.

### [arXiv:2609.35653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35653) — Factorization and virtuality evolution of jet functions in heavy-ion collisions (Carlos Lamas et al.)

The provided full text is truncated at the start of Sec. III, so only the formalism is covered here; the numerical results come from the abstract only.

For γ+jet production in AA collisions, the authors model the nuclei as Glauber ensembles of uncorrelated nucleons. They derive an impact-parameter-dependent factorized cross section: thickness beam functions, times the on-shell 2→2 partonic cross section, convolved with an in-medium jet function 𝒥_k(p_I; p_J, X). The jet function explicitly retains the initial virtuality m_I² = p_I², defined in a physical gauge, with quark and gluon projectors fixed by Ward identities.

They then set up BDMPS-Z Feynman rules for this object:
- dressed propagators as path integrals with Wilson lines;
- a harmonic-oscillator dipole approximation;
- a soft-gluon emission vertex;
- a new single-line color-survival factor, exp(−½∫dt/λ_R), controlled by the light-cone mean free path λ_R.

Per the abstract:
- Integrating over m_I² recovers standard BDMPS-Z p_⊥ broadening and the soft medium-induced spectrum.
- The ratio of medium formation time τ₀ to jet formation time controls which initial virtualities contribute. Vacuum radiation dominates when the jet forms well before τ₀.
- As τ₀ → 0, the mean free path becomes the controlling scale instead.

This matters for pre-equilibrium quenching and small systems, and offers a formalism-independent object for comparing energy-loss models. The authors frame it as a common ground for the spread in q̂ extractions.

Caveats:
- **Neglected couplings.** Medium couplings to initial-state partons are dropped, justified only at lowest order; couplings to the hard vertex are argued to be subleading.
- **Limited order.** The hard process is LO, and the NLO part is soft-gluon only, for quark jets.
- **Approximations.** The calculation uses the eikonal limit for the leading parton and the harmonic-oscillator approximation.
- **No dynamical evolution.** The authors state that the full virtuality evolution of the shower through the medium is not described dynamically.
