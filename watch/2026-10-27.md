## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2610.33757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33757) — Dissecting flavor tagging with graph neural networks (Ankita Budhraja et al.)

The authors train ParticleNet (three EdgeConv blocks, k=16, no block concatenation or input batch-norm) on Pythia 8.318 Z(→νν)+jet samples, R=0.4, with 80–400 GeV jets matched cell-wise in (p_T, |y|). Per-bin AUC rises from 0.842 to 0.904. Two baselines run alongside it:
- **Multiplicity alone:** the smoothed likelihood ratio L_N gives AUC 0.787–0.841.
- **Six-observable BDT** (N, p_T, p_T^D, λ_1, λ_0.5, Q_0.5): AUC 0.807–0.865.

ParticleNet's relative gain over L_N (7–8%) is about three times the BDT's (2–3%). Standard observables therefore capture only about a third of what the network extracts beyond N.

The main contribution is a decomposition, M = L_N + Δ, where Δ is a residual log-likelihood ratio at fixed (N, p_T).
- **Calibration at fixed N:** the mean P(g) matches the gluon fraction, and the equal-odds multiplicity shifts from N≈23 to N≈35 across p_T.
- **Spread of Δ:** σ_Δ is 0.5–1.6, against ≤0.28 variation of L_N within a bin.
- **Overturning multiplicity:** for jets whose N favours the wrong class at ≥2:1 odds, Δ recovers 24–34% of gluon jets and 20–32% of quark jets, rising with p_T.

They probe Δ by rescaling constituent angles by e^δ at fixed momenta and PID:
- The response S_R is positive for most jets and peaks at N≈18–27, where 80–90% of jets become more gluon-like when broadened.
- At high N, the gluon response goes to zero; quark jets keep a median S_R of 0.3–0.5 in the top two p_T bins.

**Caveats:**
- Single generator, no detector simulation, and no hadronization-model comparison. This matters because N and p_T^D are IRC-unsafe.
- Calibration is tested only as a fixed-N mean, so identifying Δ with Δ* is approximate.
- Inputs are truncated at 100 constituents.
- The authors flag that the misID-vs-correct comparison is selection-biased (their toy, App. B).
- They also flag that the high-N falloff may be a distance-from-decision-boundary effect rather than physics.

## Muon collider

### [arXiv:2610.35154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35154) — LHμC: A high energy, high luminosity muon-hadron collider at CERN (Krzysztof Piotrzkowski)

This paper is only tangential to the topic. It is a μ⁺p collider proposal (a conference status report), relevant mainly as a near-term test-bench for cold-muon and Muon Collider technology. The machine collides a 1 (or 2) TeV μ⁺ beam with the 7 TeV LHC beam, giving √s = 5.3 (7.5) TeV.

Example parameters:
- **Luminosity:** 2.1×10³⁴ cm⁻²s⁻¹ peak.
- **Timing:** 4 MHz collision rate, 12.5 Hz repetition, 80 μ⁺ bunches at 250 ns spacing.
- **Beams:** σ_x,y = 4.8 μm, with 7×10⁹ μ⁺ and 2.2×10¹² p per bunch.

These numbers assume μ⁺ yields at 5% of μTRISTAN, a 4 MW proton driver, and enhanced LHC bunch populations (an order of magnitude above HL-LHC nominal) enabled by fewer bunches.

The technology relevant to muon colliders:
- **Source:** the J-PARC/KEK ultra-cold μ⁺ scheme (thermal muonium from aerogel, laser ionisation; μ⁺ only) rather than ionisation cooling. Hot tungsten wires, two-step cooling and a hybrid source are under study.
- **Acceleration:** the LHeC ERL plus vertical FFAG beamlines, e.g. one vFFAG taking 70→500 GeV, avoiding fast-ramping RCS limits.
- **Machine-detector interface:** reuses Muon Collider work on decay-background shielding (a nozzle) and TeV-muon detection.

The outlook explicitly frames LHμC as a test-bench for the Muon Collider. It also mentions longer-term options: μp at FCC-hh (30 TeV) and μ⁺e⁻ at FCC-ee/LEP3.

**Caveats:** no simulation results are presented. The physics reach (Higgs couplings, di-Higgs, top, PDFs) is deferred to workshop contributions and a white paper in preparation. The luminosity rests on the source-yield and bunch-population assumptions above.
