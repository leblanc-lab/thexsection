## Muon collider

### [arXiv:2611.07056](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07056): Searches for electroweak singlets at future plasma wakefield colliders

This is a wakefield-collider paper. The muon collider is a benchmark, not the subject. The authors project 95% CL reach on sin²γ for a Higgs-portal real singlet using S→hh→4b. Events are split into three categories (fully resolved, partially merged, fully merged b-pairs, ΔR = 0.4 boundary) and combined in a three-bin Poisson likelihood using Asimov datasets with no systematics. Detector assumptions are 10% jet energy resolution (~7.5% m_bb), 90% b-tag efficiency, and a double-b tagger with 80% efficiency and a 2% single-b fake rate. Additional cuts are 0.92 < m_4b/m_S < 1.1 and 0.7 < H_T/m_S < 1.09. The dominant background is VBF hh.

The muon-collider entry (10 TeV, 10 ab⁻¹) is not taken from an external study. It is the identical analysis with beam–beam effects switched off, so all luminosity sits at √s = 10 TeV. Relative to the e⁺e⁻ wakefield options, it:
- performs better at high m_S and slightly worse at low m_S;
- does so because the VBF cross section grows only logarithmically with √s, which rewards luminosity concentrated at the endpoint. Pair production of charged states (σ ∝ 1/M²) prefers broad spectra instead.

The headline is that e⁺e⁻ and γγ wakefield options reach "qualitatively similar" sensitivity to a 10 TeV muon collider and FCC-hh. γγ wins if e⁺e⁻ geometric luminosity falls short by more than ~3×. The intro quotes the muon collider's indirect hWW sensitivity as sin²γ ~ 10⁻³ for context.

**Caveats:**
- The muon proxy omits beam-induced background and any muon-specific detector effects.
- The authors call EW PDFs negligible relative to beam–beam effects; that comparison has no content for a muon machine.
- Numerical limits appear only in Fig. 7 and are not in the text provided.
- The pair-production section (λ_HS-driven, 4j+4b channel) is truncated in the available text.

## Optimal transport

### [arXiv:2611.07465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07465): Optimal-Transport Contrastive Learning for Centrality Determination in Heavy-Ion Collisions

This is the one paper today where OT is the method. The pipeline:
- **Distance:** each UrQMD Au+Au event (√s_NN = 3.0, 5.2, 19.6 GeV) is a point cloud in 3-momentum. The distance between events is an exact Hungarian-assignment W1 with a Euclidean ground metric.
- **Unequal multiplicities:** surplus particles are left unmatched at zero cost and the cost is averaged over the min(N_A, N_B) matched pairs. This partial-transport convention deliberately removes multiplicity from the distance.
- **Landmarks:** each event is embedded by its W1 to 100 landmark events sampled uniformly in N_ch, computed for 2000 training events only.
- **Network:** a PointNet (3→64→64→32, max+mean pooling, 32-d unit embedding) is trained with InfoNCE, using the k = 16 F-space nearest neighbours as positives (τ = 0.15). It then runs inference on 5–7M events.
- **Classes:** size-constrained k-means gives 20 equal 5% classes.

Using all final-state particles, the classes align with N_part, giving narrower N_part bands and better σ/⟨N_part⟩ than N_ch slicing at all three energies. Under realistic STAR acceptance (−2.4 < η_lab < 0 fixed-target; |η| < 1 collider), "OT, charged, η" is worse than N_ch slicing. The authors attribute this to spectator protons and peripheral activity falling outside acceptance, leaving multiplicity as the main central/peripheral discriminant. The transport distance has, by construction, discarded multiplicity.

**Caveats:**
- The unbalanced-OT choice is the crux and was not varied. A mass-penalizing unbalanced W1 is the obvious next test, and the min-normalized cost is not a metric.
- The method is "unsupervised" only partially: landmark selection and class ordering both use N_ch.
- Resolution values are figure-only.

### [arXiv:2611.07826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07826): Estimating Spectrum-Free Information Bounds for Generative Surrogates Beyond Conditional Collapse

OT is tangential here. W1 appears as one of the 1D marginal divergences the paper argues against. On CMS open-data parton→reco-jet regression (~10.8M pairs, four residual targets), a condition-blind marginal sampler beats a trained MDN on all targets, by 3.75–18.02× under W1 and 3.65–22.39× under KL. It also cannot be stably separated from a conditional flow-matching model on two of four targets. The lesson for OT users: pooled-marginal W1 is blind to whether the conditioner is used at all.

The constructive content is a per-event CRPS skill score:
- **Floor:** the exact marginal, i.e. half the Gini mean difference, equal to Hersbach's uncertainty term, computed in O(N log N).
- **Ceiling:** a k-NN analog-ensemble estimate, using fair-CRPS scores at k ∈ {5…100} extrapolated to zero neighbourhood radius.

Both surrogates land within ±0.005 of the ceiling ratio. A 17,280-cell lookup table recovers 98.8% of CFM skill on jet mass, 84.6% on Δφ, and 60.8% on Δη. Global versus p_T-binned skill on jet mass drops from 28.7% to 10.1%, showing how strongly global skill depends on the breadth of the test spectrum.

**Caveats:**
- The zero-radius intercept is explicitly not a bound.
- All scores are per-target 1D.
- The k-NN search omits φ, which explains a 0.0014 gap on momentum response.

One observation of mine, not made by the authors: 1D CRPS is the integrated squared CDF difference against a step function, the L2 analogue of the CDF-area form of W1.
