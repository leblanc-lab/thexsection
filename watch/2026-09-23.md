## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2609.25153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25153) — Search for boosted VBS $W^\pm W^\pm H$ production with same-sign leptons and $b$-jets (ATLAS, 140 fb⁻¹) (ATLAS Collaboration)

First ATLAS search for EW $W^\pm W^\pm Hjj$, targeting the quartic $WWHH$ coupling via $\kappa_{WW}$. The physics result is a null: observed (expected) 95% CL exclusion of $\kappa_{WW}$ outside $[-2.1,4.1]$ ($[-1.9,3.9]$), statistically limited (data stat impact 0.22 vs total 0.26 on $\mu$ for the $\kappa_{WW}=-2$ benchmark). Weaker than the recent CMS inclusive $VVH$ result ($\kappa_{2V}\in[0.4,1.6]$), but a different channel.

For substructure, the interesting content is the analysis architecture. The boosted $H\to b\bar b$ candidate is a single anti-$k_t$ $R=1.0$ jet built from UFOs (Track-CaloClusters), groomed with Soft-Drop + Constituent Subtraction + SoftKiller, $250<p_T<3000$ GeV, $m_J>40$ GeV. Tagging uses **GN2X**, the transformer $H\to bb$/$cc$/QCD tagger, with the discriminant in Eq. (1) at $f_{Hcc}=0.02$; the working point is a *mass-flat 1.55% multijet mistag*, giving ~80% $H\to bb$ efficiency. Notably, GN2X calibration is a leading systematic — flavor tagging (signal) contributes 0.050 to the $\mu$ impact, second only to the fake-factor uncertainty (0.084) and well above jet energy/mass scale (0.014). The VBS forward-jet topology is captured by an RNN tagger on small-$R$ four-momenta (VBF-RNN) rather than cut-based $m_{jj}/\Delta\eta_{jj}$, retaining ~80% signal at score >0.2, and explicitly excludes small-$R$ jets within $\Delta R<1.4$ of the large-$R$ Higgs candidate. This is a clean demonstration that mass-decorrelated boosted-$X\to bb$ taggers plus ML VBS tagging are now the default toolkit for rare EW processes; the caveat is that at these yields (9 observed, 12.5 expected background) the boosted approach is entirely statistics-limited and the resolved topology added negligible sensitivity.

## Muon collider

### [arXiv:2609.25903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25903) — Spacetimes of maximal proper acceleration and muon mean lifetime (Ricardo Gallego Torrom\'e)

Tangential match — this is a classical-geometry theory paper, and "muon collider" enters only as a proposed testing ground. The claim: in a spacetime with a maximal proper acceleration $A_{\max}$, the proper-time functional acquires a factor $(1-a^2/A_{\max}^2)^{1/2}$, reducing the muon lifetime by $\Delta \approx -\tfrac12 a^2/A_{\max}^2$. Given the measured lifetime precision ($\sim10^{-8}$), an observable effect requires $\tfrac12 a^2/A_{\max}^2 \gtrsim 10^{-8}$.

Among the $A_{\max}$ candidates, only identifying $A_{\max}$ with the muon Schwinger acceleration $A_S[\mu]\sim10^{27}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ gives a testable window: a $10^4$ GeV circular muon collider with $R\sim10^4$ m yields proper acceleration $a\sim10^{23}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, hence a $\sim10^{-8}$ lifetime shift. High-order-jet electrodynamics ($A_{\max}\sim8\times10^{34}$) and Caianiello ($\sim10^{31}$) give far-too-small effects. Caveats are self-flagged and severe: the Schwinger identification is a heuristic effective model, sits exactly at the acceleration scale where the classical geometric framework breaks down, and the whole construction presumes a well-defined classical worldline for the muon. Of no practical relevance to muon collider design or accelerator physics — file under exotic-physics phenomenology that happens to name-check the machine.
