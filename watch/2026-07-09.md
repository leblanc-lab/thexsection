## Optimal transport

### [arXiv:2607.08723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08723) — Optimal-Transport-Based Cell Resampling for Negative and Pathological Event Weights

The authors employ Optimal Transport (OT) metrics—specifically the Energy Mover’s Distance (EMD) and its 1D spectral projection, the Spectral EMD (sEMD)—to drive cell resampling algorithms that eliminate negative and pathological weights in NLO Monte Carlo simulations. Because EMD and sEMD are inherently infrared and collinear (IRC) safe, this approach bypasses the intermediate jet clustering required by previous Euclidean methods. Using $10^5$ simulated $\sqrt{s} = 13\text{ TeV}$ $ZZ$+jets and $t\bar{t}$ events generated with `MadGraph5_aMC@NLO` + `Pythia 8`, the authors test resampling at three generation stages: Born-level Hard Scattering (HS), Parton Shower (PS), and Hadronization (HAD). HAD-level resampling yields the most stable performance across all monitored observables ($H_T$, $N_{\text{jets}}$, $\Delta R(j_1, j_2)$, and $m_{b,l}$), while also minimizing the overall sample-level distortion. 

A systematic scan of the EMD angular parameter $\beta$ shows that the first Wasserstein distance configuration ($\beta = 1$) provides the optimal balance of accuracy and efficiency. Extremes like $\beta=0$ and $\beta\to\infty$ discard the spatial distribution of energy and induce kinematic biases of up to 50% in the $H_T$ spectrum. Comparing metric performance at a high reweighted fraction ($f_{\text{rw}} = 0.75$), both EMD and sEMD significantly outperform the standard Euclidean-based metric of Andersen et al., which exhibits large biases in $H_T$ and $N_{\text{jets}}$. 

To evaluate these biases in an unbinned, projection-independent manner, the authors introduce the Cross-Section Mover's Distance ($\Sigma$MD) as a sample-level metric of distortion. To circumvent the mathematical breakdown of standard OT solvers under negative measures, they apply a constant offset $c = |\min_{i}\sigma_i|$ to the event weights of both samples. This maps the problem to a non-negative space while leaving the transport cost invariant due to the cancellation properties of the generalized Wasserstein distance under shared support.

**Caveats:** The key bottleneck remains computational complexity; exact EMD calculations scale as $\mathcal{O}(N^3 \log N)$, and although the 1D sEMD approximation reduces this to $\mathcal{O}(N^2 \log N)$, evaluating high-multiplicity final states remains intensive. Additionally, the authors note that the Euclidean benchmark was designed for larger, denser datasets; its degraded performance here may be partially driven by the sparseness of the $10^5$ event test samples.
