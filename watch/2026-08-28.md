## Muon collider

### [arXiv:2608.27258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27258) — An Axial $U_A(1)_{L_\mu-L_\tau}$: UV Completion and Experimental Searches (Rundong Fang et al.)

Be aware up front: this is primarily an intensity-frontier / UV-completion paper. The muon collider appears only in Sec. IV as one of several complementary probes; the bulk is trident production, muon beam dumps, invisible meson decays, $(g-2)_\mu$, and four-muon resonance recasts for a *purely axial* $L_\mu-L_\tau$ gauge boson (axial charged-lepton current, LH neutrino current, masses via a universal-seesaw with $M_E>10$ TeV vector-like leptons). Under their benchmark ($Q_\ell=1$, $y_{\mu\tau}=0$, $\theta\le10^{-2}$, scalar decoupled $m_s\gtrsim10$ GeV) the phenomenology collapses to $(m_{A'},g_X)$ with $v_s=m_{A'}/2g_X$.

The collider-relevant point: for FSR-type observables ($e^+e^-,Z\to\mu^+\mu^-A'$, and muon-collider radiative return $\mu^+\mu^-\to\gamma A'$) the vector/axial difference is only the longitudinal mode, $\propto m_\mu^2/m_{A'}^2$, so for $m_{A'}\gg m_\mu$ axial limits are obtained by simple branching-ratio rescaling of published vector limits — total rate does *not* discriminate chirality. Their proposed discriminator is the two-body $\mu^+\mu^-\to\tau^+\tau^-$ ($s$-channel $\gamma/Z/A'$ only) angular distribution at $\sqrt{s}=3$ TeV. Using massless-external-lepton chiral amplitudes (Eq. 65–66, chiral couplings in Table 3), for $m_{A'}=1$ TeV, $g_X=0.05$: the vector case shifts the $|\cos\theta|<0.9$ rate by ~5% vs ~2% axial, but the axial case shifts $A_{\rm FB}$ by $\Delta A_{\rm FB}\simeq-2.9\times10^{-2}$, exceeding the vector shift. So $A_{\rm FB}$ retains direct sensitivity to the axial current where rates wash it out.

Caveat, stated explicitly: no detector-level Monte Carlo; they use $\Gamma_{A'}=g_X^2 m_{A'}/4\pi$ and analytic amplitudes, and defer a real exclusion to a follow-up modeled on Ref. [62]. This is a "here is a viable observable" argument, not a sensitivity contour.
