## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2611.31311](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31311) — Machine learning for trigger, reconstruction and generation (Marco Giacalone)

This is an LHCP2026 review of public ML results from ALICE, ATLAS, CMS and LHCb. It contains no new analysis. The jet-relevant items it collects:

- **CMS Phase-2 L1 jet tagger.** A quantised Deep Sets network takes up to 16 pT-sorted PF candidates per jet, each with 20 features. It outputs eight flavour classes plus a pT regression. It fits within the L1T FPGA budget and gives about 15% better HH→4b efficiency.
- **LHCb jets.** A GBR jet-energy correction targets the b/c energy underestimate. A DeepJet-like tagger (1D conv + LSTM) improves on the secondary-vertex tagger by 9.8–27.3% for b-jets and 23.6–53.4% for c-jets. On 1.6 fb⁻¹ this gives observed limits of 6.64×SM for H→bb̄ (11.1 expected) and 1003×SM for H→cc̄.
- **ATLAS flavour-tagger scaling laws.** In a single-epoch regime the fits give N*∝C^0.10, D*∝C^0.90 and L*∝C^−0.07. The 12M-parameter tagger trained on 417M jets sits off the compute-optimal curve. An 86M-parameter model trained on 7.7B jets gains up to 1.8× c-rejection and 1.4× light-rejection at low b-efficiency.
- **GN3 vs GN2.** Adding neutral PF inputs and attention pooling gives 1.8× c-, 2.3× light- and up to 5× τ-rejection in tt̄. b-efficiency rises by up to +0.10 absolute in high-pT QCD.
- **MaskFormer tracking in jet cores.** Efficiency stays at about 0.8 above 1 TeV in Z′→qq̄, where standard tracking drops below 0.6, because shared clusters are assigned to multiple tracks.
- **CMS MLPF.** Jet response resolution is up to about 25% better than standard PF at intermediate pT, and per-event time drops from 0.11 s (CPU) to 0.02 s (GPU).

**Relevance:** the paper is only partly about jets. Its most consequential message for substructure and tagging is that taggers are data-limited rather than architecture-limited.

**Caveats:** most items are PUB notes or R&D, and the review covers them only at summary level.

### [arXiv:2611.31445](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31445) — Quarkonium Energy Correlators in pp Collisions (June-Haak Ee et al.)

The authors give the first full LO (α_s³) analytic J/ψ energy correlator: the energy-weighted recoil flow in the J/ψ rest frame. It covers all six NRQCD channels and keeps the full recoil kinematics instead of the fragmentation limit.

Key technical results:
- **Non-monotonic angle map.** The map pT→cosχ is non-monotonic. Inverting it requires solving a quartic in pT² (via Ferrari's method), which can have up to three physical roots and produces integrable square-root singularities.
- **Fragmentation limit fails.** Keeping only the large-pT root misses up to about 40% near cosχ→−1. It underestimates the integrated Σ by about 12% at sPHENIX and about 30% at ATLAS/CMS.
- **Validation.** The result agrees with HELAC-onia in both shape and absolute normalisation.
- **Angular-momentum hierarchy.** Channel shapes group by the total angular momentum J of the cc̄ pair. This follows from the tree-level rule that two on-shell gluons cannot form J=1, which dynamically suppresses ³P₁^[8] below ³P₀^[8].
- **Acceptance.** sPHENIX's |y|≤1.1 caps the hard contribution at cosχ ≈ 0.14, which compresses the hierarchy. Wider LHC acceptances expose it.
- **Soft hadronisation model.** An isotropic soft-gluon model adds 3–9% to the QEC and 14–23% to the pT-weighted variant (QpTC). It dominates for cosχ > 0.32.

**Relevance:** this is an energy correlator anchored on a quarkonium rather than a jet observable. It complements the J/ψ-in-jet LDME programme.

**Caveats:**
- Half of the correlator comes from pT ≲ 1.4–2.2 GeV, below the pT > 7 GeV range where the LDMEs were fitted.
- The scale band spans ×0.5 to ×2.4.
- The LDME degeneracy largely survives in the central-acceptance total.
- The text is truncated before the LHC discrimination results (Sec. VII).

### [arXiv:2611.31587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31587) — All the Positive Energy Correlators (Lorenzo Ricci et al.)

The paper systematises positivity constraints on N-point energy correlators for sources of any spin.

- **Constraints.** Positivity is expressed through operators 𝓔^(N,k), k = 0…⌊N/2⌋, built as mode-space tensor products of the one-point positivity matrix 𝓔^(1,0) and the hermiticity matrix 𝓔^(2,1).
- **Outer bounds** come from requiring these operators to be positive semidefinite. **Inner bounds** come from a column-generation linear program over explicit massless event ensembles. The two are shown to converge.
- **Example results.** For a 3D stress-tensor source they derive H₄² ≤ c₄ and |d_n|² ≤ c_{n−2}c_{n+2}, plus the genuinely three-point constraint c₂+c₃ ≤ 1.
- **Global positivity is emergent.** Imposing only 𝓔^(N,0) gives c_ℓ ≥ −1/(N−1), which approaches non-negativity as N→∞.

**Relevance:** the key use for jet physics is that measured lower-point correlators bound higher-point ones. The abstract states an application bounding three-point EC multipoles in QCD from LEP one- and two-point data, but that section is not in the provided text.

**Caveats:**
- Massless final states are assumed.
- The LP stopping criterion is not a certified upper bound on the remaining error.

### [arXiv:2611.33057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33057) — Boosted energy-energy correlators in the small-acoplanarity limit (Benjamin Jobilal et al.)

The authors study the EEC from a boosted boson decay, differential in the opening angle z_θ and the acoplanarity z_φ = [1+cos(φ₁−φ₂)]/2.

**Main observation:** a boost smears the rest-frame back-to-back Sudakov region across z_θ but preserves coplanarity with the boost axis. The singular region therefore lives entirely at small z_φ, as z_φ ~ (η̃−η)², while z_θ stays non-singular.
