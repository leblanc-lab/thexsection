## Jets and jet substructure

Both papers matched on substructure machinery: jet z-sharing in one, energy correlators in the other. Neither is primarily about jets. Today's actual jet-physics content is one retrospective check on LHCb X(3872)-in-jet data and a port of EEC technology to bulk hadrons.

### [arXiv:2610.22424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22424) — Causal-horizon scaling of prompt X(3872) formation lengths (Yi Yang)

The main result is not about jets. A Gaussian source–channel overlap model, with a phase field averaged over an exponential causal-horizon ensemble, gives a formation loss linear in the sampled length s_i. Taking the ratio λ = ln C_{3872/ψ} / ln C_{ψ/J} cancels both the field strength and the exposure. From matched LHCb 13 TeV prompt data, the 12–14 vs 4–6 GeV/c endpoint at 2<y<3 gives λ ≈ 0.94. The indicative uncertainty is δλ ≈ 0.34, a diagonal estimate without covariance. Calibrating against potential-model radii (r_J = 0.50 fm, r_ψ = 0.90 fm) yields r_3872 ≳ 1.28 fm and a source size w ≳ 0.65 fm. A fixed χ_c/ψ(2S) feed-down mixture shifts these to 1.18 fm and 0.40 fm.

The jet content is a side test in Sec. III.2:
- **Input:** LHCb X(3872)/ψ(2S)-in-jet z distributions (anti-k_T, R = 0.5, 2.5<η<4), reconstructed from vector figure panels.
- **Observable:** a double ratio C_jet of high-z to low-z sharing preference, with z = 1 excluded.
- **Fit and test:** the first tag-p_T interval sets the normalization and the second fixes the slope. The proper-exposure rule g(p_T) ∝ 1/E_T (m₀ = 3.1 GeV) then predicts the 15–20 GeV bin.
- **Agreement:** predicted 2.01 vs 1.98 at z_c = 0.85, and 2.66 vs 2.79 at z_c = 0.75.

This is one predicted point per selection. The input and test points are correlated, and the two z_c selections share events. The author concedes that jet shapes are sensitive to hard production (NRQCD fragmentation), which the model does not compute. For substructure purposes, treat this as a consistency check, not a constraint on X(3872) fragmentation.

### [arXiv:2610.22477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22477) — Exploring QCD Matter through Energy Correlators of Soft Hadrons (Yiyang Peng et al.)

The authors apply the EEC to all soft hadrons in |η|<1 and split it into two pieces:
- a disconnected piece, built from event-averaged energy flow;
- a connected piece, holding correlations beyond the mean energy flow.

They also define EEC harmonics dΣ_n/dcosχ relative to an energy-weighted event plane Ψ_n^E taken from a non-overlapping acceptance.

**Smooth-initial-condition hydro (VISH2+1):** the full and disconnected results agree. The single-angle EEC is set by the longitudinal mean energy-flow profile (∝ 1/sin³θ) and is insensitive to ε_n and η/s. Σ₂ and Σ₃ track their eccentricities (ε₂ or ε₃ = 0.2) and decrease with η/s across 0, 0.08 and 0.16.

**iEBE-VISHNU vs PYTHIA/Angantyr (Monash tune), pp/OO/PbPb:**
- In hydro, disconnected ≈ full everywhere; the largest deviation is <5% at small χ in pp.
- In PYTHIA, the connected piece grows toward small angles and small systems, attributed to minijets, strings and decays.
- PYTHIA's Σ₂ falls by about an order of magnitude from pp to PbPb, while hydro's stays comparable across systems.

**Longitudinal dynamics:** MUSIC vs VISH2+1, after a three-subevent resolution correction R_n, differ by ≲5%.

**Relevance:** this extends the connected/disconnected EEC formalism from jet-wake studies (Barata et al.) to the bulk. The small-angle connected region is effectively a minijet-substructure probe in pp and OO.

**Caveats:**
- The study is model-only, with no data comparison.
- The disconnected piece is defined as a product of ensemble averages. Event-by-event fluctuations of the mean energy flow therefore count as "connected" by construction.
- pp hydro uses parameters calibrated on PbPb/pPb.
- Key details are deferred to a paper in preparation.
