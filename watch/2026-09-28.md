## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2609.30357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30357) — Optimizing Cell-Based Negative Weight Mitigation with Optimal Transport (Lauren Hay et al.)

This paper replaces the modified-Euclidean metric in Andersen–Maier cell resampling with IRC-safe OT metrics: event-level EMD in (η,φ) with p_T weights, and the spectral EMD (SEMD). A negative-weight seed grows a hypersphere in metric space until the enclosed net weight is positive, then weights are redistributed as w_i → (Σw/Σ|w|)|w_i|.

The test sample is MG5_aMC@NLO+Pythia 8.3 Z+jets at 13 TeV, with 37.6% negative weights and O(10⁵) events. The reweight fraction is set deliberately high at f_RW = 0.75. Findings:
- **β choice:** β = 1 and 0.5 give the least bias. β = 1 was chosen on cost grounds.
- **Stage:** resampling after hadronization beats parton-shower and Born-level resampling.
- **Metric comparison:** EMD ≳ SEMD, both better than the original metric, most visibly in H_T and leading-jet p_T.
- **ΣMD:** the distance to the original sample stays below the sample's statistical uncertainty (δσ/σ = 0.0127).
- **Statistical gain:** all variants take 1/f_ESS from 16 to 6. The metric therefore controls bias, not the statistical gain.

For jets specifically, the relevant change is that IRC safety comes from the metric itself. The previous jet-clustering step, and its dependence on algorithm and R, is no longer needed. Jets otherwise enter only as anti-k_t R = 0.4 validation observables, so this is not a substructure paper.

Caveats:
- This is proceedings-length and the evidence is mostly histogram comparisons.
- Only one low-statistics sample is tested.
- The ΣMD figure of merit ([arXiv:2609.30418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30418)) uses the EMD as its ground metric. That may flatter the EMD-based resampler relative to the Euclidean baseline; this is my inference, not addressed by the authors.

### [arXiv:2609.30775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30775) — Parnassus for the CLD Detector: A Generative ML Surrogate for Detector Simulation and Reconstruction at the FCC-ee (Umar Sohail Qureshi et al.)

**Model.** A two-stage conditional flow-matching surrogate for CLD_o2_v07 Geant4 + PandoraPFA, trained on Z→qq̄ (u,d) and bb̄ at 91.2 GeV:
- An event-level MLP models N, H_T and missing-momentum components.
- A cross-attention DiT (~5M parameters) generates the particles, each with species and d₀, z₀ and their significances.
- Conditioning includes the truth production vertex, which is what lets it reproduce displaced heavy-flavour tails.
- cc̄ and ss̄ are held out as out-of-distribution tests.

**Jet-level results.** After Durham exclusive 2-jet clustering, Parnassus beats Delphes on every observable and every metric. Examples:
- Jet mass: W₁ = 0.247 vs 1.886 for Delphes.
- e₂^(β=2): VLC 0.076 vs 0.865.
- m_jj: VLC 0.52 vs 58.6.

**Flavour tagging.** A transformer tagger (b/c/s/q) trained on surrogate output gives:
- Accuracy 0.684 vs 0.693 for full simulation.
- b-vs-c mistag at 80% efficiency: 1.58% vs 1.00%, a ~60% relative excess.
- Delphes overestimates separability (accuracy 0.732; b-vs-c mistag 0.24%).
- A full-sim-trained tagger drops only 0.012 in accuracy when applied to Parnassus.

**Speed.** 1.3 ms/event on an A100 and 39 ms on a CPU core, vs 6.0 s for full simulation and reconstruction.

This is a credible e⁺e⁻ jet-flavour benchmark. It shows directly that Delphes-based tagging projections are optimistic.

Caveats:
- Coordinates and substructure definitions (p_T, ΔR girth) are carried over from the CMS setup rather than e⁺e⁻-native variables; the authors acknowledge this.
- Hyperparameters are untuned from the CMS configuration.
- bb̄ reproduces worse than the held-out flavours.
- The HitPF extension is qualitative and shows impact-parameter deviations.
- No boosted or multi-prong substructure is tested.

## Optimal transport

### [arXiv:2609.30357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30357) — Optimizing Cell-Based Negative Weight Mitigation with Optimal Transport (Lauren Hay et al.)

See the jets entry for numbers. From the OT side, this puts OT distances to work as a neighbourhood definition: EMD and SEMD serve as the metric for cell growth, not as an evaluation metric. The practical findings for OT users:
- EMD β = 1 is the sweet spot for this job; the β = 0 and β = ∞ extremes are worst, per the companion paper.
- SEMD (1D spectral representation) performs nearly as well as the full EMD, which matters for cost at scale.
- Cost scaling for growing hyperspheres in EMD space over O(10⁵) events is not reported. Implementation is in OT-CReS and in [arXiv:2607.08723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08723).

### [arXiv:2609.30418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30418) — A Phase-Space Inclusive Figure of Merit Based in Optimal Transport for Validating Monte Carlo Reweightings (Rishabh Jain et al.)

This paper proposes the Cross-Section Mover's Distance (ΣMD, an OT distance between weighted event sets with EMD as ground metric) as an unbinned, IRC-safe global measure of bias from reweighting. The OT-technical content is how it handles signed weights:
- It adopts the Piccoli–Rossi–Tournus construction, W_p(μ,ν) = W_p(μ⁺+ν⁻, μ⁻+ν⁺), which satisfies the triangle inequality only for p = 1.
- In practice it uses the translation invariance of W₁: shift all weights by a constant K ≥ |min w|, then run standard OT.
- The shift is valid only on shared support, which is trivially true when comparing the same events with different weights; otherwise zero-weight ghost events are added.

**Results** on cell-resampled samples (Z+jets with 37.6% negative weights; tt̄ with 22.8%; 10⁵ events each):
- ΣMD grows monotonically with f_RW.
- EMD β = 0 and β = ∞ resampling metrics are the most biased.
- Hadron-level resampling is least biased for both processes.
- Normalized by total cross section, tt̄ shows a smaller ΣMD, which the authors attribute to its lower negative fraction.
