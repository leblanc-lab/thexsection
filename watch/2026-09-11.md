## Muon collider

### [arXiv:2609.11386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11386) — Connecting Vector-like Muons, pNGB Dark Matter and Electroweak Phase Transition through Collider and Gravitational Wave (Jaydeb Das et al.)

The muon-collider content (Sec. 3) is genuinely central, not incidental. The model adds two complex singlets (Z₂, Z₃) plus a muon-philic SU(2) singlet vector-like lepton ψ sharing the Z₃ charge of S₃, so the only renormalizable new Yukawa is ψ̄_L μ'_R S₃. The stable pNGB η₂ (from S₂) is DM; η₃ decays to muons. For a multi-TeV μ⁺μ⁻ collider they argue the discovery channel is VLM pair production μ⁺μ⁻→vl⁺vl⁻, since associated production is suppressed by tanθ_L = (m_μ/m_vl)tanθ_R. The novel handle is the scalar-mediated *t*-channel (via h₃, η₃), which does *not* carry the sinθ_L suppression and, for sinθ_R=0.3, dominates the gauge s-channel by orders of magnitude; there is visible destructive interference with γ/Z for sinθ_R=0.1. LO cross sections (m_vl=1.4 TeV) reach 17.3 fb at 3 TeV (BP9) vs ~2.2 fb at 10 TeV — the 3 TeV rate is larger because the t-channel grows with m_vl despite phase-space loss.

The collider study is a full Delphes + XGBoost BDT analysis of the 2-opposite-sign-muon + E̸_T signature (vl→μh₃, h₃→η₂η₂), with seven observables (M_eff, L_T, ΔR_μμ, p_T^{l1}, E̸_T, M_μμ, M_T^{μμ}), a jet veto, and dominant irreducible background μ⁺μ⁻→μ⁺μ⁻ν ν̄. They quote test AUC = 0.982 and, at D_BDT>0.924 with 500 fb⁻¹, statistical significance Z∼12 for BP9. Caveat stated explicitly: Z is purely statistical, no background systematics; Δa_μ from the new states remains negligible across their benchmarks, so no g−2 tension or motivation is claimed.

## Jets and jet substructure

### [arXiv:2609.10679](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10679) — Computationally Efficient Description of Medium Response to Jets in Heavy Ion Collisions (Jorge Casalderrey-Solana et al.)

Only tangential to jet substructure per se: the paper computes the hydrodynamic wake (medium response) that contaminates the *soft* component of reconstructed jets in heavy-ion collisions, not substructure observables directly. Its relevance is that this soft contribution cannot be groomed away for most observables, so a correct wake description is a prerequisite for computing jet/substructure observables in AA. The concrete advance is an "Efficient Wake" procedure: precompute a library of 34 template (Green's-function) solutions to (1+1)D linearized hydro on a Bjorken background for unit energy-momentum deposition (deposition times τ_temp^d = kΔτ, Δτ=0.4 fm/c, τ₀=0.4 to τ_f=13.6 fm/c, T_f=145 MeV ⇒ T₀=446 MeV), then reconstruct wakes in a radially expanding droplet by superposing templates with suitable rotations/boosts to fold in transverse radial flow at freezeout.

Benchmarked against full nonlinear (3+1)D MUSIC hydro for 50 representative partons at two energies, they find reasonable agreement and — the headline — a speedup of "at least tens of thousands" up to a factor 200,000, while giving a much better p_T spectrum (harder, matching data's demand for more particles above ~2 GeV) than the constant+cosine Hybrid-Model treatment. Caveats stated: the approximations are *not* controlled by smallness of the perturbation (validity assessed only empirically via the 50-event comparison), and implementation in a jet Monte Carlo (where 10⁵–10⁶ wakes per analysis are needed) remains future work.

### [arXiv:2609.11692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11692) — Hunting the Unseen: Deep Learning Analysis for Semi-Visible Jet Tagging (Miguel A. Avenda\~no-Bernal et al.)

Squarely a jet-substructure paper. It benchmarks five DL classifiers for SVJ (Z′→dark quarks, Hidden Valley) vs QCD dijet discrimination, combining global kinematics (MET, Δφ(MET,jet), M_jj, M_T) with substructure: primary Lund Jet Plane, two-point EEC e₂^(β=1), angularity τ_{α,1} for α=0.1,0.8,1.5, and charged-hadron multiplicity. Architectures: a ViT on 50×50 LJP images (patchified 10×10, 6 encoder blocks, 4 heads), JetLOV (RegNet learning node features on the C/A tree, feeding LundNet EdgeConv), an MLP on 15 high-level observables, and two fusion networks (ViT+MLP, JetLOV+MLP). Training on 300k+300k events across 18 BPs (m_Z′=2.5, 4.5 TeV; scans of m_ψd, r_inv, Λ_dark), with three held-out 3.5 TeV BPs to test interpolation across parameter space.

The key benchmark result for the field: global kinematic observables *outperform* both jet-representation networks individually; among substructure representations the hierarchical clustering-tree (JetLOV) beats LJP images; and fusing structured representations with global kinematics gives the best overall performance. This quantifies how much discrimination lives in radiation-pattern structure beyond MET/global variables — modest but complementary. Caveats visible in text: no pileup in the samples (angularity especially would need mitigation), Pythia default hadronisation parameters held fixed, and r_inv is not an explicit Pythia parameter but tuned via dark-hadron branching fractions. Full AUC numbers are in tables not reproduced in the provided text.
